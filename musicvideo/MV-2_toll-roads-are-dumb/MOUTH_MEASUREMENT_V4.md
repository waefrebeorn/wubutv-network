extracted every 0.5 s (0.0–9.5, plus final frame labelled 10.0) for each
active take, viewed as labelled contact sheets in /tmp/mouth/ (never in the

sample; profile/back-to-camera and distant frames where the mouth cannot be

*sampled* open frame. Cartoon mouths at 320 px-wide frames: a smile line vs.

## What this says about the v3 hypothesis

It supports it, strongly. Only a minority of takes show a mouth open across
most of their length. The dominant pattern is a short visible-singing burst
followed by a closed-mouth gesture, turn-away, or smile-hold — so audio-word
warping routinely stretches song words across stretches where the picture is
visibly not singing. That is exactly the shape that would read as "speech
happens in the audio, then the mouth moves late / not at all" rather than a
single global shift.

## v4 rule derived from this measurement

For each scene, let audio_first = first matched song anchor's take t0, and
vis_first = first clearly open above. If vis_first > audio_first, the take's
mouth lags its own audio, and v3's audio-word anchor puts a closed mouth on
the song's first word. v4 shifts that scene's first anchor take t0 to
vis_first (all other anchors unchanged; the assemble script's monotonic
filter drops the pre-burst profile anchors that fall before it). Scenes
where vis_first <= audio_first keep v3 anchors unchanged (mouth already
open when audio starts).

Scenes shifted in v4 (lag = vis_first - audio_first):
- S01: audio 0.00 -> vis 1.00, lag +1.00 s
- S02: audio 0.00 -> vis 5.00, lag +5.00 s (profile section dropped)
- S05: audio 2.76 -> vis 3.50, lag +0.74 s
- S10: audio 1.72 -> vis 5.00, lag +3.28 s (profile section dropped)
- S12: audio 0.00 -> vis 2.00, lag +2.00 s (back-to-camera section dropped)
- S23: audio 1.54 -> vis 2.00, lag +0.46 s

S08 is unshiftable (no visible open) and is left as in v3, disclosed as a
known failure, and flagged for a refire — no warp can put singing on a
take that never opens its mouth.
