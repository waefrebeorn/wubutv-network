## Method lessons (volley 1)

1. **MOUTH RULE for MV-12 (new, from 3 rejects): the generator invents speech on every take with a prominent open mouth** (S01 "Oh yeah", S02 "We'll call it Apple", S05 "We rock the night" — all high-confidence). A visible mouth saying invented words while the song chants the hook is a G5 fail. **Montage scenes must not show a prominent open mouth**: mouths closed, backs to camera, silhouettes, wide shots. The chant lives in the kinetic type + master audio, not in take mouths. Singing mouths only where the vehicle quotes the exact short line (verse S19–S22) — quoted speech sometimes lands verbatim.
2. **G2 text is the other volley-1 killer**: the generator puts garbled titles on magazine covers and posters even when told "no text" — S01's magazine title + "RAANDOT" poster. Vehicles must demand covers/posters/signs COMPLETELY BLANK, and QC must read full-res frames (contact sheets hide garbled text at small size).
3. **Two-image morph shape confirmed on this tool**: the take opens ~0.5s on the first image (the lock) and arrives in the second image's world by ~1.0s (S01, S06). Budget for it: the gag must be readable by 1.0s, and assembly may trim the morph head. A take whose gag needs the first frame should use a SINGLE image (S04/S05 opened in-world immediately).
4. **"Exactly 5 seconds" lands exactly**: all 6 takes 5.000s. Slot-length requests work as measured; assembly trims to ~1.83s slots.
5. **Service restart kills parallel batches**: the first 6-call parallel batch died on a service restart with 0/6 landed. Pairs survived. Fire in pairs from now on.
6. **Frame 0 is never the scene still on two-image calls** — it is a re-render of the lock. Never promise frame-exactness; the lock is an identity carrier, not a frame source.

## Method lessons (volley 2)

1. **STAGING axis works**: back-to-camera (S02) and singer-from-behind (S05) both removed the mouth-speech mismatch.
2. **SILENT take audio is now the norm on closed-mouth vehicles** — 4 of 6 takes transcribed to near-silence, and the two with invented audio (S02's confident line, S09's tail mumbles) were harmless because no talking mouth was on screen / they sat past the slot.
3. **Single-image calls open IN the world immediately** (S05's front-facing band at frame 0) — a staging that must hold from frame 0 needs the still itself to carry it, not the vehicle text.
4. **Fixed laughing grins read as CUTAWAY-correct** (S09, S03 precedent) — open mouth ≠ talking mouth when the shape never changes across samples.

## Method lessons (volley 3)

1. **A CROWD is a mouth risk, not a safe extra**: "fixed closed mouths" in the vehicle did not hold — the S10 crowd sang its own "Hey, hey, hey!" (p=1.00) with visibly articulating mouths. A crowd of visible faces is a prominent mouth × N. Crowd scenes need the STAGING axis (backs to camera / silhouettes) just like solo singers.
2. **The fixed-laugh call, refined**: S14's wide open laughing mouth to camera KEEPS because the mouth holds ONE shape across 0.5/1.0/1.5/2.0s AND the take audio is laughter, not words. The test is two-part: (a) mouth shape fixed across samples, (b) audio is non-word (laugh / "Hmm") or silent. Either part failing = G5.
3. **Non-word vocalizations are not speech**: S15's "Hmm." (p=0.92) and S14's laughter are the take's own sound matching the picture's gesture — they read as the gag, not as invented dialogue.
4. **Tail audio is free**: S13's invented "Point at the wall" (p=0.98) sits entirely past the 1.83s slot — usable-span verdicts ignore the surplus tail when no mouth on screen says it.
5. **Morph-head world can be a THIRD world**: S14 frame 0 opened on the lock's curtain-stage world — neither the lock still nor the scene still. Budget the head: usable spans start ~0.5s on two-image calls.
6. **Slow is a motion number, not a verdict**: S12's crane (usable mean 6.95) is a calm CUTAWAY by design — no near-still runs, motion live throughout. The eye decides; the numeric floor (0.5) is a tripwire, not a target.

## Method lessons (volley 4)
