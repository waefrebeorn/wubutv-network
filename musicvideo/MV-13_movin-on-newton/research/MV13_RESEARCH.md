## THREAD 1 — NEWTON + THE FOURTH LAW (the parody's spine)

### The three laws (verified)

| Law | Statement | The gag it hands you |
|---|---|---|
| **First — Inertia** | A body at rest stays at rest, a body in motion stays in motion, unless acted on by an external force. | The Mover NEVER stops. He glides through every scene without ever visibly pushing off anything — inertia as a superpower. Newton keeps trying to find the force that started him. There isn't one. |
| **Second — F = ma** | Force = mass × acceleration. Push harder, accelerate faster; heavier things need more push. | Newton's instruments keep reading impossible numbers: the Mover's mass reads zero, his acceleration reads infinite, F = ma divides by zero and the calculator smokes. |
| **Third — Action / Reaction** | For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction. | Every time Newton pushes the Mover, Newton himself flies backwards. The Mover doesn't move at all. Newton's cradle is the visual: drop a ball on one side, the other side swings out — except here the Mover's side never moves. |

### The apple (legend, flagged)

- The story: Newton sitting under an apple tree in his mother's garden (Woolsthorpe, 1665–66, escaping plague London), sees an apple fall, wonders why it always falls toward the earth's centre. The account comes from William Stukeley's memoir of a conversation with Newton; the Royal Society published the manuscript in 2010. **There is no evidence the apple hit him on the head** — that part is exaggeration. Use the fall, never the bonk.
- **The fourth-law gag:** in this video the apple falls UP. Once per section, somewhere in the background, an apple rises off the ground and floats away. Newton notices. Nobody else does. That is the fourth law working in the world.
- Newton developed the laws at 23 (1666) and published them in the *Principia* (*Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica*) in 1687, with Edmund Halley's support. He was 44 at publication — the video's Newton is the older scholar (white wig, frock coat, quill, pocket watch), not the 23-year-old.

### The REAL fourth law — there isn't one

- **There is no fourth law of motion.** The song invents it: "he'd add a fourth law of motion" is the hook's fantasy — Newton himself would rewrite physics to account for the Mover. The video's comedy engine is the CONTRAST: the dry real laws (three of them, published 1687, still taught) vs. the song's invented fourth (a glowing tablet that breaks all three at once).
- **The tablet:** a stone/metal slab etched with "FOURTH LAW" and a glowing equation. In the video's fiction it is the law itself, made physical — the Mover carries it, leans on it, slides on it, and it appears in every scene while the world changes. The tablet's text is POST-RENDERED in assembly (generated in-clip text stays banned); in generation it is a blank glowing slab.
- **Newton got the pen but the paper stay blank** (breakdown line) — the video's thesis in one line: Newton has the instrument, the Mover has the motion, and the paper can't hold it. Stage it literally at least once: Newton at a desk, quill poised, the page blank, the Mover gliding past the window behind him.

### Physics set-pieces (free gags)

- **Newton's cradle:** five steel balls on strings. Drop one, the other end swings out. Gag: the Mover taps the first ball and ALL FIVE swing the wrong way, or the middle balls start dancing.
- **The pendulum:** a brass bob swinging in a clockmaker's shop. Gag: the Mover rides the pendulum like a swing, or the pendulum freezes mid-swing while the Mover walks past it.
- **The textbook page:** equations as physical objects — F=ma as a street sign, a formula as a road the Mover drives on, a page that folds itself into a paper plane and flies away.
- **The stopwatch:** Newton's pocket watch. Gag: it always reads the wrong time — it runs backwards, or shows a time that hasn't happened yet, or its hands spin like a propeller when the Mover passes.
- **The ruler / measuring tape:** Newton measures the Mover's stride. The tape stretches, snaps, or measures in impossible units (the Mover's step = "one fourth law").
- **The apple tree:** one tree, in a park, at dawn. Apples hang UP from the branches. Newton stands under it with a notebook. An apple detaches and rises.

### Hype-era visual language (verified, 1990s–2000s rap video)

- **Fisheye lens** — the signature: distorted, magnified centre, the subject feels closer than they are, everything pops like a cartoon. Hype Williams shot most of his videos with cinematographer John Perez on a fisheye; it was dropped by ~2003. For MV-13: fisheye-ish WIDE low angles in the breakdown (a camera language, not a lens claim — the generator doesn't have a fisheye, so the vehicle says "extreme wide low angle, subject looming large in the centre, edges bending").
- **Low angles + shiny surfaces** — the subject shot from below, looming; gold pillars, wet streets, chrome, sun-gun lighting rigs gleaming off shades. The early-2000s rap video is AFROFUTURIST gloss: bold hues, futuristic clothing, sets that look expensive.
- **Doubled frame rates / stutter** — Hype's videos feel faster than they are; the edit doubles the motion. For MV-13: the breakdown cuts on the 2-beat grid (1.24s shots) to get that doubled feel without touching the frame rate.
- **Split screens + letterbox play** — a second shot split into the upper and lower bars. For MV-13: an assembly-layer option (the kinetic .ass can run a split lower-third), never a generation request.

- **The shiny-suit era** — the look is a period signifier, not a costume to copy literally. The Mover's varsity jacket is the WuBuTV-native version of it: bold, flat, graphic, readable at 1.2s.

## THREAD 3 — MONTAGE-FIRST CRAFT + BETTER FIRING (MV-12's rules, carried forward and sharpened)

### Why MV-12 worked (the lesson this build extends)

MV-12 proved the montage method: 57 scenes, fresh first frame every cut, one gag per shot, the carry as the only continuity device — and it delivered with all gates passing. MV-13 extends it under the test directive: MORE scenes (70 vs 57), MORE cuts (chant pile-ups at 2 beats), MORE events (the apple-up gag once per section, Newton's stopwatch, the tablet's equation gaining terms). The risk the test directive names is the opposite failure from MV-11: not monotony but NOISE — 70 scenes of unconnected gags. The guardrails are the carry (the tablet in every scene), the two character locks, and the density audit at assembly.

### Cut density — the numbers (carried from MV-12's research)

- Cut-rate framework (datafield.dev, cutscore.io, Jun 2026): **30+ cuts/min = hyperspeed**; 15–30 = fast; 8–15 = moderate. Short-form runs 30–60+/min.
- Beat-cutting arithmetic: at 120 BPM, cutting every 2 beats = 1s shots = 60 cuts/min; every 4 beats (one bar) = 2s = 30/min; every 8 beats = 4s = 15/min.
- **MV-13 grid (97.0 BPM):** 1 beat 0.619s · 2 beats 1.237s · 1 bar 2.474s · 2 bars 4.949s. Target: **choruses 1 bar/shot for hook lines (24/min) + 2 beats/shot for the "Fourth law" chant pile-ups (48/min)** — blended ~30/min per chorus; **verses 1 bar/shot (24/min)** so the rap lines land; **breakdown 1 bar/shot (24/min)** — the barked lines are ~2.5s each, one line per shot, no splitting; **bridge slow by design** (the sanctioned slow shots, 2 bars+); **outro 2 bars/shot, settle**.
- **One readable gag per shot.** A 2.47s shot holds exactly ONE idea. A 1.24s chant shot holds exactly ONE chant hit. If a shot needs two ideas, it needs two shots.

### Cutting-room rules (the user's test directive, made concrete)

- **Trim and use the good, trim the bad.** Every take is judged on its USABLE window, not its full length. A 10s take with a 2.5s good window is a 2.5s take — the ledger records the usable span, assembly uses only that span. A take with NO usable window is a REJECT, not a "maybe".
- **Triple devil's advocate on new material.** Every new scene idea gets three counter-reads before firing: (1) does it serve the line it covers? (2) does it repeat a gag already used? (3) would it read to a cold viewer in one sentence? A scene that fails any of the three is redesigned before the vehicle is written.
- **Recursive learning.** Every volley close records what worked and what didn't, and the next volley's vehicles apply it. The research doc's firing rules are updated at each volley close if a new lesson lands — the plan coordinator owns the update, not the parent.
- **Montages for parts you can't lipsync.** The hook is a chant — it lives in kinetic type + the tablet + the crowd, never in a single mouth. The breakdown is barked ad-libs — it lives in 10 fast cuts, never in a talking head. A sung line gets a SUNG picture only where a visible mouth event can carry it (the Mover's "What?" bark, once per section at most).

### FIRING RULES FOR THE PLAN COORDINATOR (concrete, not generic)

1. **FRESH FIRST FRAME EVERY CUT.** No chaining between scenes. Each scene's vehicle names its own start still (world + characters + one prop). The ONLY continuity devices are the tablet (post graphic) + the two character locks. A coordinator that chains S14's last frame into S15's first frame has misunderstood the brief — flag it.
2. **CAMERA MOVE IN EVERY VEHICLE, AND NEVER THE SAME MOVE TWICE IN A ROW.** Rotate: whip-pan in · crash zoom out · low-angle push-in · overhead drop · dolly past foreground prop · handheld wobble · extreme wide low angle (fisheye-feel) for the breakdown · static wide ONLY as a punchline (max 1 per section). "Static wide" as a default is a REJECT reason on this project.
3. **ONE GAG PER SHOT, READABLE IN 2.5s (1.2s for chant shots).** Vehicle = [world] + [ONE action] + [ONE camera move] + [ONE prop/gag]. If the vehicle text needs a semicolon to list a second action, split the scene.
4. **CUT DENSITY BY SECTION (97.0 BPM grid):** choruses 1 bar/shot for hook lines (2.47s, 24/min) + 2 beats/shot for the "Fourth law" chant (1.24s, 48/min) · verses 1 bar/shot (2.47s) · breakdown 1 bar/shot (2.47s, one barked line per shot) · bridge slow by design (2 bars+, the sanctioned slow shots) · outro 2 bars/shot. Slots are 1.2–5s, NOT 10s. Plan 70 scenes for 185.6s.
5. **CHORUS WORLDS, NOT REPEATS:** chorus 1 = dawn street + textbook world · chorus 2 = clockmaker's lab + measuring world · chorus 3 = full-tilt street parade. Same words, new world each time — that IS the joke.
6. **THE VERSES ARE THE STORY.** Verse 1 tells the lane-switch (wake-up → lane → formula → equation dies, 8 shots, 2 per line). Verse 2 tells the textbooks (old books → pages → textbooks wrong → gone before play, 4 shots, 1 per line). Do not montage the verses into noise; the story needs to land.
7. **THE BREAKDOWN IS THE RAP SHOWCASE.** 10 shots, 1 bar each, one barked line per shot, blue-hued night city, extreme wide low angles. No lip-sync chase — the Mover barks "What?" at most once; the rest is motion + kinetic type. The line "Newton got the pen but the paper stay blank" gets the breakdown's biggest shot (Newton at the desk, the Mover gliding past the window).
8. **RUNNING GAGS, LAYERED IN POST/ASSEMBLY (not in generation):** (a) an apple falls UP once per section (background, post composite if needed); (b) Newton's stopwatch always reads the wrong time (post graphic on the watch face); (c) the tablet's equation gains a term per chorus (post-rendered); (d) the "FOURTH LAW" lower-third in the section's typeface.
9. **KINETIC TYPE IN POST:** "FOURTH LAW" / "I'M MOVING" / "YOU CAN'T MEASURE THIS" rendered in post (kinetic_ass.py; SLAM on the hook line, CASCADE on the chant). The hook line is a CHANT — the crowd/tablet carries it, never a single mouth. ASR garbles are NEVER used as kinetic text — the sheet words only ("clock", never "cock"; "If Newton saw the way", never "gluten's"/"Putin saw").
10. **SINGING RULE (unchanged):** the hook is a chant — the crowd/tablet carries it; NO real-person lip-sync of any line; NO photoreal likenesses. Sung lines get sung pictures only where a visible mouth event is on-beat; otherwise montage. The Mover's "What?" bark is the only direct-to-camera mouth in the video, once per section at most.
11. **QC GATES, MONTAGE EDITION:** (a) motion audit per take as usual — but ALSO a **density audit at assembly**: count cuts/min per section vs rule 4; a section 30% under its target density FAILS; (b) **gag-readability check**: every shot's gag must be describable in one sentence by a cold reviewer from the contact sheet — if it needs explaining, it isn't a gag; (c) **vantage audit**: no two consecutive shots from the same angle/distance; (d) **carry audit**: the tablet must be present (as prop or post graphic) in every scene — a scene without the tablet FAILS the carry audit.
12. **VOLLEY SHAPE:** ≤6 videos per volley as always; a volley here is ~6 SCENES (1.2–5s each), not 6 ten-second takes. Cap is per generation, not per scene — the coordinator may fire 6 short takes per volley and the plan budgets 12 volleys for 70 scenes. Do NOT pad takes to 10s to fill the old shape; slot-length requests (1.2–5s) are the correct ask.
13. **ANTI-TEXT CLAUSES MUST NAME THE ARTIFACT.** "No text" alone does not work — the generator invents captions, signs, and book covers. Every vehicle names what it must NOT contain: "no comic-book captions, no text bursts, no words on any sign, poster, book cover, or screen; all signs/posters/screens blank". The tablet is ALWAYS blank in generation; the "FOURTH LAW" text is post-rendered.
14. **INTEGRITY BLOCKED CATEGORIES (learned so far — BLOCKED means redesign, never re-fire as designed):** (a) two caricatures lifted overhead by a crowd (crowd-surf) — remove the category (crowd surfs an OBJECT, not people); (b) people lifted overhead by a crowd generally; (c) exam-room / medical-procedure depictions (MV-9/10 lesson — move the scene out of the room); (d) face depictions at large scale (giant face holograms, giant face on screen — use a LOGO or OBJECT instead); (e) two-face depiction risks generally (two faces laughing/shaking hands — hands-only redesign). The sanctioned fix is always a redesign that REMOVES the depiction category, never a re-fire of the same vehicle.
15. **MOUTH RULE + CROWD RULE (unchanged):** no prominent open mouths in montage — fix via STAGING (backs to camera, wide shot, face turned away). A visible crowd is mouths × N — stage the crowd from behind or at distance, never a wall of open mouths.
