# UNIVERSE EXPANSION v1 — plotlines, people, arcs

WuBu-authored, 2026-10-18. **Additive canon**: nothing here contradicts the
received compendium (UNIVERSE, LORE, VOICES, PREMISE_BANK 1–78, CONTINUITY_WEB,
THE_100). It fills in the world the wheel has been building all week.

**The expansion thesis:** the network now has a library of ten-second moments.
A library needs librarians. The new characters are all, in one way or another,
librarians of the impossible: they count it, fix it, stock it, dispatch it,
audit it. They take it completely for granted. That is the whole engine —
the universe is beginning to form because people now *work* in it.

## PART I — NEW RECURRING CHARACTERS

Every character carries an identity lock in the same format the wheel already
uses for UNC and the Colonel. Two classes:

- **LOCKED** (full body): appearance, costume, job, voice, rule. Staged like
  any persona. Two-denial drift rule applies — a LOCKED character that fails
  its identity lock twice is shelved, never rewritten.
- **DRIFT-PROOF** (voice-only or hands-only): no face to drift, so they can
  recur forever at zero continuity cost. Prefer these for high-frequency roles.

### R-1 THE NIGHT AUDITOR — LOCKED
- **Appearance:** woman, late 50s, silver hair in a tight bun, green eyeshade
  visor, long black wool coat, brass clipboard held in both hands.
- **Job:** audits the MEGA's units at night. Counts things that do not need
  counting. Writes in a small book that is never shown.
- **Voice:** flat, unhurried, administrative. She reads numbers the way other
  people breathe.
- **Rule:** she never looks at the camera. She looks at paperwork, padlocks,
  and the middle distance in that order. She has never once been surprised by
  a discrepancy; a discrepancy is just a number with a future.
- **Channels:** ORIGINALS, ARCHIVE.
- **NEVER:** explain what she audits, show the book's contents, or let her
  hurry.

### R-2 STAN (MAINTENANCE) — LOCKED
- **Appearance:** man, 60s, grey stubble, navy coveralls with a white name
  patch reading STAN, red canvas tool bag that is never opened.
- **Job:** preventative maintenance of the impossible. Tightens bolts that are
  already tight. Checks the warm fluorescent tube. It is always warm.
- **Voice:** the quiet optimism of a man whose work is never done because it
  was never started. Sentences always finished; repairs never finished.
- **Rule:** he carries the bag everywhere and opens it nowhere. The bag is the
  joke's container. If the bag were ever opened, the show would end.
- **Channels:** ORIGINALS, SATIRE (background), NETWORK (background).
- **NEVER:** fix anything successfully. A successful repair is a plot hole.

### R-3 THE RESTOCKER — LOCKED
- **Appearance:** woman, 40s, red uniform vest over a white shirt, hairnet,
  wheeled crate of snacks and bottles.
- **Job:** restocks the vending machine. Stocks exactly what was taken,
  one-for-one, in the same slots. The machine is never wrong because she is
  never late.
- **Voice:** methodical. Counts under her breath. The count is always right.
- **Rule:** she never buys anything from the machine herself. She is staff of
  the ordinary; the ordinary is her jurisdiction, not her appetite.
- **Channels:** ORIGINALS, PUBLIC ACCESS.
- **NEVER:** let her be surprised by a selection. The machine dispenses; she
  nods; that is the whole relationship.

### R-4 THE SCOREBOARD CUSTODIAN — LOCKED
- **Appearance:** short man, 70s, grey cardigan, bow tie, wooden step stool,
  feather duster.
- **Job:** maintains the ∞:0 board. Polishes the ∞. Never touches the 0.
- **Voice:** speaks only to the board. "There," he says, when it is clean. It
  is always clean.
- **Rule:** the ∞ changes itself when he looks away. He never notices. This is
  not a mystery; it is maintenance. Polishing is a kind of looking away.
- **Channels:** SATIRE, NETWORK.
- **NEVER:** let him alter the score. The score is not his. The dust is.

### R-5 THE DISPATCHER — DRIFT-PROOF (voice only)
- **Form:** a voice on a wall speaker in the control room. Never a person.
- **Job:** announces the schedule of a network that has no schedule. Assigns
  UNC nothing, Stan nowhere, the Auditor no units.
- **Voice:** calm, recorded-sounding, slightly too close to the microphone.
  Always says "stand by." Nothing ever stands by.
- **Rule:** no one ever answers the speaker. Answering would imply the
  schedule was real.
- **Channels:** NETWORK, ARCHIVE, ORIGINALS (background).
- **NEVER:** give the Dispatcher a body. A body would have a shift, and the
  shift would end.

### R-6 CALLER 6 — DRIFT-PROOF (voice only)
- **Form:** the same caller on the Colonel's line, every night. Never on
  camera.
- **Job:** reports where he found a penny. Street level, exact change, exact
  location. He is the network's field correspondent for the ground giving
  things back.
- **Voice:** earnest, unhurried, faint traffic behind him. He has never once
  been put on hold; the Colonel takes his call the way other anchors take
  weather.
- **Rule:** the Colonel never says his name on air. The audience knows it
  anyway. (Continuity: this is premise 58's "every caller is a fan," kept —
  the fandom is the format.)
- **Channels:** NEWS, NETWORK.
- **NEVER:** let him find anything larger than a quarter. The economy of the
  bit is small-denomination.

### R-7 THE CHICKEN KEEPER — DRIFT-PROOF (hands only)
- **Form:** hands through a unit-door gap, carrying a metal feed scoop. Never
  above the waist, never a face.
- **Job:** feeds the Day 47 chicken. Maintains the unexplainable on a schedule.
- **Rule:** the scoop is always full going in and always empty coming out. The
  chicken is never shown eating. (LORE rule holds: the chicken is never shown
  cooking, and now also never shown eating — it is maintained, not depicted.)
- **Channels:** ORIGINALS.
- **NEVER:** show which unit, show the chicken, or let the hands hurry. The
  hands are the calmest hands on the network.

### PAIRING RULES (multi-character discipline)
1. **UNC never reacts and never acknowledges.** Others may orbit him — audit
   around him, restock beside him, maintain near him. He is weather; they
   dress for it.
2. **Laura is the only reactor.** If a scene needs someone to notice, Laura
   notices. Nobody else does.
3. **The Colonel never meets anyone.** He is behind the desk, behind the
   static. Caller 6 reaches him by phone; that is the maximum proximity.
4. **The Auditor and Stan share the corridor but never speak.** Two jobs, one
   hallway, zero conversation. Their scenes rhyme; they do not connect.
5. **DRIFT-PROOF characters can pair with anyone** — a voice on a speaker
   costs nothing to place.

## PART II — NEW PLOTLINES

Each is plot-shaped: a **want**, an **obstacle**, a **turn**. Each fits one
10s unit (setup 0–3s, beat 4–6s, turn/tagline 7–10s) and is generatable as
written. Channel-tagged.

1. **THE INSPECTION** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* the Auditor announces an
   inspection. *Obstacle:* there is no schedule, no checklist, no criteria.
   *Turn:* the units inspect themselves; she nods and writes "pass." The pen
   does not pause.
2. **THE LOST CLAIM** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* UNC files a lost-and-found claim.
   *Obstacle:* the form requires a description of what was lost. *Turn:* he
   describes the form itself, in full. The claim is accepted.
3. **THE SHIFT HANDOVER** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* Stan ends his shift.
   *Obstacle:* no relief shift exists; no one is coming. *Turn:* he hands the
   shift to the building — a door closes, gently, on its own. Handover
   complete.
4. **THE DOUBLE BOOKING** (SATIRE) — *Want:* two contestants, one podium.
   *Obstacle:* the podium is singular and the rules are four hundred readings
   old. *Turn:* the orb pulses once; one contestant becomes audience. The
   show continues. It always continues.
5. **THE RESTOCK AUDIT** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* the Auditor audits the
   Restocker. *Obstacle:* every count matches exactly. *Turn:* she writes
   "suspiciously correct" in the book and moves on. The Restocker nods; she
   has heard this before.
6. **THE PENNY ARBITRAGE** (NEWS) — *Want:* Caller 6 finds two pennies.
   *Obstacle:* the Colonel's register has one slot per call. *Turn:* filed as
   "the ground, overperforming." The static takes the second half of the
   sentence.
7. **THE WRONG DOOR** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* the Dispatcher sends Stan to unit
   47. *Obstacle:* unit 47 is the chicken's. *Turn:* he tightens a bolt on the
   door instead and logs "torque nominal." A single feather falls past the
   frame. Approval, of a kind.
8. **THE QUIET COMPLAINT** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* someone files a noise
   complaint about the hallway breathing. *Obstacle:* the hallway stops the
   moment it is inspected. *Turn:* the complaint is refiled against the
   complainant's hearing. Case closed.
9. **THE KEY RETURN** (ORIGINALS) — *Want:* a stranger returns a key UNC never
   gave out. *Obstacle:* no record of the loan. *Turn:* UNC accepts it and
   adds it to the ring. The ring is heavier. He does not notice. (Callback:
   THE_100 #57, THE HANDOFF — the ring is the ledger.)
10. **THE WEATHER EXCEPTION** (NEWS) — *Want:* the Colonel files a hallway
    under weather. *Obstacle:* the weather disagrees; it is clear.
    *Turn:* the hallway is filed under hallway. A new category is opened. It
    has one entry. Filed under weather anyway.
11. **THE SECOND COUNT** (ARCHIVE) — *Want:* the day counter is audited.
    *Obstacle:* it is counting something, and no one will say what.
    *Turn:* the Auditor's count matches the counter exactly. She does not say
    what she counted. The counter keeps counting.
12. **THE STANDING ORDER** (NETWORK) — *Want:* a standing order arrives for
    the network. It reads: "continue." *Obstacle:* no one remembers issuing
    it; the paperwork has no origin. *Turn:* the Dispatcher reads it twice.
    Nothing changes. That is the point, and the point is filed.

## PART III — MULTI-CHARACTER STORIES (SERIALIZATION)

### Serialization mechanics
- **Arc IDs:** A-001, A-002, … registered here before the first unit ships
  (CONTINUITY_WEB rule: a new bit gets an ID before it ships).
- **Arc shape:** 4–6 units. Each unit is standalone — the first-time viewer
  gets the joke; the returning viewer gets the thread. Max one arc beat per
  unit; arcs never crowd out the bumper's own beat.
- **Deadpan-safe cliffhangers:** the only permitted suspense is
  administrative — a form half-filled, a key unreturned, a count
  unreconciled, a correction pending. Never peril, never emotion.
- **Ledger tagging:** episode/bumper ledger rows carry the arc ID
  (e.g. `arc:A-002/5`) so the web stays auditable.
- **Rotation:** no arc runs two units in a row on the same channel. The wheel
  interleaves; the audience assembles.

### A-001 THE KEY RING (ORIGINALS, 6 units)
The ring from THE_100 #57 grows, one key per unit.
1. UNC accepts a key from off-camera hands. Adds it to the ring.
2. The Auditor walks past mid-handoff. Does not look. Writes something.
3. Stan tightens the hasp the new key belongs to. Logs "torque nominal."
4. The Keeper's hands pass the scoop through the same door the key opens.
   The door is not opened.
5. UNC counts the keys. The count does not change. (Premise 56's inventory,
   serialized.)
6. He adds one more key. The ring is heavier. He does not notice. Arc closes;
   the ring remains open.

### A-002 THE NIGHT AUDIT (ORIGINALS/ARCHIVE, 5 units)
One discrepancy per unit, filed without emotion.
1. The Auditor finds a unit numbered in the wrong sequence. Files it.
2. She finds a padlock of a different brand. Files it. (LORE: the padlock is
   always the same brand — this is the one exception, and it is filed, not
   explained.)
3. She finds a unit that is clean. Files it as "suspiciously correct."
4. She finds the cabinet the files go into. It is also a unit. It has a
   number. There is a gap before it.
5. She files the audit of the audit. The book closes. The corridor is longer
   from this end.

### A-003 THE RESTOCK LOOP (ORIGINALS/PUBLIC ACCESS, 4 units)
The same ten minutes from both sides of the glass.
1. The Restocker stocks slot C4. Counts under her breath. Correct.
2. UNC buys from slot C4. The machine dispenses. It is never wrong.
3. The Restocker returns. Stocks exactly what was taken, one-for-one.
4. Laura watches the loop from the red curtain and says, "It does that."
   UNC does not respond. The machine hums. Loop closed.

### A-004 THE PENNY LEDGER (NEWS, 5 units)
Caller 6's findings escalate, one denomination at a time.
1. A penny, Elm and 3rd. Filed.
2. A penny, same corner, heads up. "The ground," the Colonel says, "is
   consistent."
3. Two pennies. Filed as "the ground, overperforming."
4. A nickel. A pause — the longest pause in the broadcast. Filed under
   weather.
5. A quarter. The Colonel corrects the week's reports: "rates have moved."
   [STATIC]. (Callback: premise 38 — the ground still gives things back.)

### A-005 THE STANDING ORDER (NETWORK, 4 units)
The order "continue" propagates through the building.
1. The order arrives. No origin. The Dispatcher reads it twice.
2. Stan receives it via speaker. Tightens a bolt in acknowledgment.
3. The Auditor receives it. Writes "continue" in the book, underlines it
   once.
4. UNC receives it. He was already continuing. Nothing changes. The order is
   filed. Arc closed; the network continues.

## PART IV — NEW PREMISES (79–84)

79. Every institution in this world has exactly one employee, and the employee
    has exactly one task, and the task is always complete.
80. A callback is a form of paperwork: it must be filed (registered with an
    ID) before it ships.
81. Two characters can share a frame but never a plot; the plot belongs to
    the world, not to them.
82. The cliffhanger is administrative: a form half-filled, a key unreturned,
    a count unreconciled.
83. The new person is never new to the building. The building has always had
    exactly this many people in it.
84. Maintenance is a point of view: from the building's perspective, Stan is
    the one being maintained.

## PART V — THE RECURSIVE LOOP (canon pipeline)

This document is v1. The universe keeps forming because the wheel feeds it.
The loop:

1. **PROPOSE** — Q0 research banks [CANON-CANDIDATE] items: new characters,
   arcs, plotlines, premises. Candidates are never staged as canon; they are
   staged as one-offs in PREPRO if at all.
2. **PROMOTE** — the showrunner (WuBu) promotes candidates into this file's
   next version (v2, v3…). Only the showrunner edits canon. Factories never
   write canon; they read it.
3. **STAGE** — Q1 drafts from the latest promoted version only. Promoted
   characters get rotation: every Q1 batch must include ≥2 drafts using a
   promoted non-UNC character. The UNC monoculture ends here.
4. **MEASURE** — ledger audits report character appearances per week. A
   LOCKED character that drifts twice is shelved per the two-denial rule.
   An arc whose units underperform the channel median gets closed early, on
   an administrative beat — arcs end the way they live.
5. **LOOP** — Q0 reads this file every run (it lives where the compendium
   lives) and must bank ≥1 item per run that extends a promoted arc or
   character into a new setting. The world expands or the run is incomplete.

**Versioning:** revisions append; nothing is rewritten. A retired character is
struck in place with cause, never deleted. The file's history is the
universe's history.

**Mirror discipline:** the canonical copy lives at
`~/workspace/wubutv-network/canon_inbox/UNIVERSE_EXPANSION_v1.md` (versioned,
backed up to GitHub). The read-mirror at
`~/workspace/freeq/canon_inbox/UNIVERSE_EXPANSION_v1_mirror.md` is refreshed
from it on every revision so Q0/Q1 pick it up with no job-body changes.
