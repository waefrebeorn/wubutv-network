# SHOW 4 — THE COUNT

**Status:** founding treatment, new era. STATION is closed canon (585 pieces,
ep079, ST-079; piece 585 'The Essay the Millpond Keeps for No Reader' landed after shutter as archive only). THE COUNT
continues STATION's ARC-3 ("The Count") as its own show — it does not
continue STATION's plots.

## Premise

Super Number Head counts. He has always counted — the broadcasts, the
viewers, the chairs, the culls — and the count is always underway and never
finished. But the count has started misbehaving: it passes a round number and
nothing happens; it skips a number and will not say which one; the thirteenth
count arrives twice. Each episode is one counting: a number the station needs
(audience, chairs, seconds, names) pursued through a world that rebuilds
itself every ten seconds and keeps changing the total. The show is absurdist
comedy with a spine of dread: if the count ever completes, whatever it was
counting gets revealed — and nobody, including the counter, is sure they want
to know what it was.

## The 10-second transition device — THE NUMBER ITSELF

Every 10-second unit ends on a number spoken aloud — counted, announced,
stamped, tallied. The next unit opens on that number ALREADY VISIBLE
somewhere new: chalked on a wall, stamped on a parcel, lit on a board, worn
on a badge. The cut is a number handoff: spoken → shown → spoken again, one
higher. The craft problem is arithmetic: the number must increment exactly
once per unit, in order, or the audience catches the show cheating — and the
characters know the audience counts too. Rule: the number is the transition;
no unit ends without counting; no unit opens without the counted number in
frame. A skipped number is a visible mistake, and a skipped number is the
show's central mystery: the count skips, and the counter will not say which
one.

## Cast (WuBu-native originals only)

- **SUPER NUMBER HEAD** — counts toward something. Numbers-obsessed, exact,
  deadpan about the impossible. The only character who can say which cull
  number this is — until the day he skips one. (STATION canon: ARC-3.)
- **THE TALLY CLERK** — new. Keeps the second count — the audit count — in a
  ledger the counter never sees. Her numbers disagree with his by exactly
  one, every time, and neither will concede.
- **PETER** — sells the count to the advertiser as an audience. The
  advertiser asks what is being counted. Peter cannot answer. His promises
  are the station's promises, one desk removed. (STATION canon: VERMILION.)
- **UNC** — manages the station. Needs the count for the roster, the
  schedule, the promise. Short certain sentences; the count is the one thing
  he cannot manage by refusing. (STATION canon: INK + CADMIUM, `~ WuBu ~`.)
- **LAURA** — asks the question everyone avoids: what happens when the count
  completes? (STATION canon: FOREST.)
- **DISPATCHER** — logs every count the building hears, including the ones
  the counter never said aloud. (STATION canon: INK + BONE.)

## The world rule (what the characters know about the cull)

Everyone knows the world culls every ten seconds and that the count is the
one thing that is supposed to survive it — numbers don't rebuild, they
accumulate. What they do NOT know is what the count is counting TOWARD. The
counter's working theory: "the count completes when the station has earned
its viewer." The clerk's working theory: "the count completes when the
station runs out of things to count." Both theories agree on one thing: the
day the count completes is the day the show changes forever. A skipped
number is the count protecting itself — or protecting them.

## Episode shape

- One episode = 3–4 units of 10s (30–40s), one counting across the whole
  episode, exactly one spoken line per unit at 7–9s, ≤9 words, named
  character. (STATION grammar, kept.)
- Unit structure: 0–1.5 HOOK (the number, shown — chalk, stamp, board) /
  1.5–4 STATE (the counting, in this version of the world) / 4–7 TURN (the
  world changes the total — a chair appears, a viewer leaves, a cull eats a
  digit) / 7–9 LINE / 9–10 CLIFF (a number spoken aloud — the next unit's
  first frame shows it, one higher).
- Units chain by the number: the cliff's spoken number IS the next unit's
  shown number, incremented once. A chain's final cliff is the next chain's
  first frame. Every episode's count starts where the last episode's count
  ended — the show's numbers are continuous across episodes, and the
  audience can check.
- Comedy register: the joke is always the arithmetic — the world refuses to
  add up, and the counter refuses to round down.

## Pilot episode chains (dialogue-first — one conversation each)

### Chain C1-1 — "The Round Number" (OPEN)
Stake: the count's credibility — it passes a round number, nothing happens,
and the Dispatcher logs it anyway.
Setting: the counting desk, count 1,000.

- SUPER NUMBER HEAD: "One thousand. The count passes one thousand."
- DISPATCHER: "Logged, Counter. One thousand, nothing happened."
- SUPER NUMBER HEAD: "Something always happens at a round number. Count
  it again."
- LAURA: "Then count me, Counter. I am standing right here."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: LAURA offers herself as the thing
that happened — the count needs a witness, and she volunteers. (Poached
move: objects and environment as subtext — the count is the character that
wants something.)

### Chain C1-2 — "The Skipped Number" (DEVELOP)
Stake: the audit — the Tally Clerk's ledger disagrees with the counter by
exactly one, and UNC needs one true number for the roster.
Setting: the counting desk; two ledgers, one number apart.

- TALLY CLERK: "Your count is short by one, Counter. Mine says nine."
- SUPER NUMBER HEAD: "Mine says eight. Eight is the count."
- TALLY CLERK: "Then say the ninth number, Counter. Say it aloud."
- SUPER NUMBER HEAD: "I can't. The ninth number isn't mine anymore."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: the counter has LOST a number — the
count is leaking, and the leak has a direction. (Poached move: silence as
subtext — the number he will not say does the work.)

### Chain C1-3 — "Sell the Count" (DEVELOP → next question)
Stake: the advertiser's money — PETER tries to sell the count as an
audience, and the advertiser asks the one question Peter cannot answer.
Setting: PETER's desk; the contract with the empty signature line.

- PETER: "One thousand viewers, signed and counted. Buy the count."
- UNC: "Peter. The count is not an audience. The count is a count."
- PETER: "Then what is it counting, UNC? The buyer is asking."
- UNC: "Count the buyer, Peter. The buyer is the audience now."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: UNC turns the sale inside out — if
the count must be an audience, the only audience it can prove is the person
paying for it. Cliff opens the next question: who is the one advertiser, and
why are they buying a count?

## The viral bet

**Count with us.** A 10-second clip that ends on a number, and the audience
counts the next one — a duet chain where thousands of strangers keep the
same count going, one number each. It is the show's premise as a
participation format. Secondary bet: **the skipped number** — a clip where
the count jumps and the audience hunts the missing digit frame by frame.

## What it inherits from STATION vs what it breaks

- INHERITS: ARC-3 whole ("The Count," 5 chains, OPEN → DEVELOP → PAYOFF);
  the count, the missing number, the thirteenth count arriving twice, the
  count-as-currency as established lore beats; SUPER NUMBER HEAD's voice and
  PETER's sales patter; the dialogue-first chain grammar; the rule that lore
  is shown (a tally, a stamp, a skipped digit), never explained; the one
  viewer / one advertiser as background pressure.
- BREAKS: the station setting shrinks to one desk and two ledgers — no
  studio, no theatre, no backstage; the sitcom register gives way to
  absurdist counting comedy with a dread spine; the engine is no longer
  promise-failure but accumulation — the joke is that the numbers refuse to
  behave, and the dread is that one day they will.
