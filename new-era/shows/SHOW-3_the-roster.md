# SHOW 3 — THE ROSTER

**Status:** founding treatment, new era. STATION is closed canon (585 pieces,
ep079, ST-079; piece 585 'The Essay the Millpond Keeps for No Reader' landed after shutter as archive only). THE ROSTER
continues STATION's ARC-2 ("The Roster That Keeps Everyone") as its own show
— it does not continue STATION's plots.

## Premise

Somewhere under the closed station there is a room with a roster in it, and
the roster keeps names. People who left are still on it. Shifts that ended
are still kept. A keeper returns after years away and finds his name was
never erased — circled, in fact, in a hand he does not recognize. Each
episode is a name on the roster: who they were, what shift they kept, why the
roster will not let them go. The show is a gothic mystery told in kept
shifts: the roster is a memory, a promise, and a trap, and the keeper's job
is to read it aloud, one name at a time, until it gives up the first name —
whoever wrote it, and why.

## The 10-second transition device — THE NAME SPOKEN ALOUD

Every 10-second unit ends with a name spoken aloud — by a keeper, by the
Dispatcher, by the roster itself through a machine that reads. The next unit
opens with that name ALREADY WRITTEN somewhere new: on a different page, in
a different hand, on a door, on a shift board. The cut is a name handoff:
spoken → written → spoken again. The craft problem is handwriting and voice:
the written name must be legible and the spoken name must be audible, or the
audience cannot follow the chain. Rule: no unit ends without a name; no unit
opens without that name already visible in frame. A name the audience cannot
read is a transition that failed — and the keeper notices, because a name
that cannot be read is a name the roster is hiding.

## Cast (WuBu-native originals only)

- **THE KEEPER** — new. Returned after leaving. His name stayed circled on
  the roster the whole time. Reads the roster aloud; refuses to skip a name,
  even the ones that frighten him. Quiet, stubborn, keeps the shift.
- **THE FIRST NAME** — new, never seen whole. Whoever wrote the first name on
  the roster. Appears only as handwriting, as a circled entry, as a voice on
  the building's machines. The show's long question.
- **UNC** — manages the station and its roster. Asked once to erase a name;
  refused, and the refusal cost the station its advertiser for one night. He
  will not be asked twice. (STATION canon: INK + CADMIUM, `~ WuBu ~`.)
- **LAURA** — asks the question everyone avoids: what did the people on this
  roster leave behind that the roster already knows? (STATION canon: FOREST.)
- **DISPATCHER** — reports what the building knows: which names the building
  still answers to, which rooms still expect their keepers. (STATION canon:
  INK + BONE.)
- **PETER** — sells the roster's names to the advertiser as an audience. The
  advertiser keeps asking what is being counted. Peter cannot answer.
  (STATION canon: VERMILION.)

## The world rule (what the characters know about the cull)

The keepers know the world culls every ten seconds and that the roster does
NOT cull: names survive every reset, shifts survive, the circled name
survives. What they do NOT know is whether the roster survives because it is
outside the cull — or because the cull IS the roster, reading itself aloud
one name at a time. Working rule of the room: "a name spoken is a name kept;
a name erased is a shift unkept." Nobody has ever erased a name and lived to
file the report. The Dispatcher logs every reading; the log is itself a
roster.

## Episode shape

- One episode = 3–4 units of 10s (30–40s), one name across the whole episode,
  exactly one spoken line per unit at 7–9s, ≤9 words, named character.
  (STATION grammar, kept.)
- Unit structure: 0–1.5 HOOK (the name, written — show the page, the hand,
  the circle) / 1.5–4 STATE (the keeper reads; the room answers) / 4–7 TURN
  (the roster contradicts the record — a second keeper, a kept shift, a name
  in the wrong hand) / 7–9 LINE / 9–10 CLIFF (a name spoken aloud — the next
  unit's first frame shows it written).
- Units chain by the name: the cliff's spoken name IS the next unit's written
  name. A chain's final cliff is the next chain's first frame. Each episode
  ends with the roster's next question named in the cliff.
- The room is the set: one room, one roster, one lamp, one chair that is
  always occupied on the monitor and always empty in the room.

## Pilot episode chains (dialogue-first — one conversation each)

### Chain R1-1 — "The Keeper Who Returned Twice" (OPEN)
Stake: the Keeper's own name — if the roster kept him, he was never free;
if it didn't, he came back for nothing.
Setting: the roster room, first reading.

- KEEPER: "My name is circled, UNC. I never circled it."
- UNC: "The roster doesn't circle itself, Keeper. Someone kept you."
- KEEPER: "Then who kept me? I left. I was gone three years."
- UNC: "Read the next name, Keeper. The roster answers in order."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: UNC will not answer out of order —
the roster is the only authority he still obeys. (Poached move: status
negotiation — UNC answers a question with a command, keeping the high ground
by refusing the conversation.)

### Chain R1-2 — "The Night With Two Keepers" (DEVELOP)
Stake: the shift itself — two keepers listed for one night, both showed up,
and the roster will not say which one kept it.
Setting: the roster room; two names, one night, one chair.

- LAURA: "Two keepers, one night. Which one kept the shift, Dispatcher?"
- DISPATCHER: "The building logged both. The building keeps both."
- LAURA: "Then ask the building which one it remembers, Dispatcher."
- DISPATCHER: "It remembers the one who stayed. They both stayed."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: the paradox is the point — the
roster keeps everyone, even when that is impossible. (Poached move:
contradiction as subtext — the Dispatcher answers truthfully and the truth
doesn't resolve.)

### Chain R1-3 — "Read It Aloud" (DEVELOP → next question)
Stake: the whole roster — PETER wants it read as an audience list for the
advertiser; the Keeper refuses to sell names.
Setting: the roster room; PETER with a contract, the Keeper with the book.

- PETER: "Read them all, Keeper. The advertiser pays per name."
- KEEPER: "These aren't an audience, Peter. They're keepers."
- PETER: "Then sell me the keepers. A kept name is still a name."
- KEEPER: "Read it yourself, Peter. The first name is yours."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: PETER's own name is on the roster —
he was kept before he ever sold anything. Cliff opens the next question:
how long has PETER's name been circled, and who circled it?

## The viral bet

**The circled name.** A 10-second clip: a hand circles a name on a roster
and the name is YOURS — the audience duets with their own name circled, the
roster that keeps everyone. It is a participation format built on the
uncanniest object in the lore. Secondary bet: **read-it-aloud** — keepers
reading one name each, a chain of strangers keeping the same shift.

## What it inherits from STATION vs what it breaks

- INHERITS: ARC-2 whole ("The Roster That Keeps Everyone," 5 chains, OPEN →
  DEVELOP → PAYOFF); the roster, the circled name, the kept shift, the night
  with two keepers as established lore beats; UNC's refusal to erase; the
  dialogue-first chain grammar; the rule that lore is shown (a circled name,
  a kept shift), never explained; the one viewer / one advertiser as
  background pressure.
- BREAKS: the station setting shrinks to one room — no studio, no theatre,
  no backstage; the sitcom register gives way to gothic mystery; the engine
  is no longer promise-failure but custody — who keeps whom; episodes no
  longer need a joke or a sale — they need a name the audience cannot forget.
