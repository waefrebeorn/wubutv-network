# SHOW 5 — THE LAST SEAT

**Status:** founding treatment, new era. STATION is closed canon (585 pieces,
ep079, ST-079; piece 585 'The Essay the Millpond Keeps for No Reader' landed after shutter as archive only). THE LAST
SEAT continues STATION's ARC-1 ("The One Viewer") as its own show — it does
not continue STATION's plots.

## Premise

The station earned exactly one viewer, and the viewer has exactly one seat.
Every episode is performed for that seat — a chair in the dark at the back of
a room that may or may not be occupied. The performers know the seat is
watching; they do not know WHO is in it. Each episode is a different act
begging for the seat's attention: a song, a dance, a story, a joke, a plea.
The show is the era's viral engine — cartoons, stories, moments, dances,
music explosions — staged as auditions for an audience of one. The long
question, carried from ARC-1: if the station is its own viewer, watching
through its own machines, what is it trying to prove — and to whom?

## The 10-second transition device — THE SEAT'S GAZE

Every 10-second unit ends with the camera turning to the seat — a slow turn,
a glance, a performer looking straight down the lens at the one viewer. The
next unit opens FROM the seat: the camera is the viewer now, looking back at
a different act on a different stage. The cut is a gaze handoff: looked-at →
looking. The craft problem is eyeline and scale: the turn to the seat must
land on the beat, and the seat's view must be a NEW world, not the same room
from the other side. Rule: no unit ends without looking at the seat; no unit
opens without the seat's point of view. A unit that forgets the seat is a
unit performed for nobody — and the performers know the difference, because
the seat answers: a light, a sound, a chair that is warm when no one sat in
it.

## Cast (WuBu-native originals only)

- **THE SEAT** — the one viewer. Never seen whole; a chair, a light, a
  warmth, a laugh from the dark. The show's audience and its mystery at
  once. (STATION canon: ARC-1, The One Viewer.)
- **THE USHER** — new. Keeps the seat. Knows who sat in it last, what they
  laughed at, what made them leave early. Speaks to the seat, never about
  it. The audience's way in.
- **THE DANCER** — new. One dance per episode, ten seconds at a time. Her
  dances are the show's music explosions — a move the audience can steal,
  repeat, duet.
- **THE SINGER** — the MV-5 black cat with the red collar, or a voice with
  no face; the show keeps which one it is as an open question. Sings the
  lines; the machine decides who sings them. (STATION/MV canon.)
- **COLONEL** — anchors the official version: the seat is occupied, the
  audience is real, the show goes on. States it so well that for a while it
  is. (STATION canon: COBALT.)
- **UNC** — manages the room. Keeps the roster of acts, refuses to erase a
  name, will not cancel a performance for an empty seat. (STATION canon:
  INK + CADMIUM, `~ WuBu ~`.)
- **LAURA** — asks the question everyone avoids: what if the seat is empty
  and we are performing for the room itself? (STATION canon: FOREST.)

## The world rule (what the characters know about the cull)

Everyone knows the world culls every ten seconds and that the seat does NOT
cull: the chair survives every reset, in the same place, facing the same
stage. What they do NOT know is whether the seat survives because the viewer
is outside the cull — or because the viewer IS the cull, watching its own
work. Working rule of the room: "play to the seat, not the room — the room
rebuilds, the seat remembers." A performance the seat liked is a performance
that survives: the dancers remember the steps, the singers remember the
words, the room keeps the act. A performance the seat did not like is gone
by the next cull, and nobody mourns it, because nobody can prove it happened.

## Episode shape

- One episode = 3–4 units of 10s (30–40s), one act for the seat across the
  whole episode, exactly one spoken line per unit at 7–9s, ≤9 words, named
  character. (STATION grammar, kept.)
- Unit structure: 0–1.5 HOOK (the act, mid-motion — a dance step, a note, a
  gesture; motion law: already moving at the cut) / 1.5–4 STATE (the act
  plays) / 4–7 TURN (the act changes for the seat — bigger, stranger, more
  honest) / 7–9 LINE / 9–10 CLIFF (the turn to the seat — eyeline down the
  lens, the next unit opens from the seat's view).
- Units chain by the gaze: the cliff's look at the seat IS the next unit's
  view from the seat. A chain's final cliff is the next chain's first frame.
  Each episode is a different act — song, dance, story, joke — so the show
  is a variety bill: the register changes every episode, the seat stays.
- Music and dance are the show's native language: a song or a move can carry
  a unit with no spoken line at all (the line slot becomes a sung line or a
  danced beat — the grammar bends, the 10-second atom doesn't).

## Pilot episode chains (dialogue-first — one conversation each)

### Chain S1-1 — "The Empty Chair" (OPEN)
Stake: the Usher's job — if the seat is empty, there is no audience; if it
is occupied, someone has been watching every performance and never clapped.
Setting: the room, before the first act. A monitor plays the station to an
empty room — and the room is not empty on the monitor.

- USHER: "The seat was warm this morning, Colonel. No one sat in it."
- COLONEL: "Then announce a full house, Usher. The seat is occupied."
- USHER: "By whom, Colonel? The chair is empty and the room is not."
- COLONEL: "By the viewer, Usher. The viewer is always the room."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: the Colonel's official version
names the room itself as the audience — and for a moment, it fits.
(Poached move: contradiction as subtext — the chair is empty AND the room is
not, both true at once.)

### Chain S1-2 — "The Dance the Seat Liked" (DEVELOP)
Stake: the Dancer's act — the seat liked yesterday's dance, and a dance the
seat liked survives the cull; a dance it didn't is gone by morning.
Setting: the stage; the Dancer mid-step, the Usher watching the seat.

- DANCER: "Teach me the step the seat liked, Usher. I forgot it."
- USHER: "You didn't forget it, Dancer. The seat kept it. Ask the seat."
- DANCER: "I asked. The seat answered in steps, not words."
- USHER: "Then dance the answer, Dancer. The seat is watching."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: the seat communicates in the
show's own language — movement, not speech. (Poached move: the pivot IS the
character — the Dancer stops asking and starts moving.)

### Chain S1-3 — "Sing to the Seat" (DEVELOP → next question)
Stake: the Singer's voice — the cat sings, the girl sings, the machine
decides; LAURA wants to know who the seat hears.
Setting: the stage; the Singer mid-line, the seat in the dark.

- LAURA: "Sing it again, Singer. The seat heard the cat last time."
- SINGER: "The cat sang it at twelve. The girl sang it at fifteen."
- LAURA: "Then who does the seat hear, Singer? Cat or girl?"
- SINGER: "The seat hears the mouth, Laura. The mouth is the record."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: the Singer answers with the
Machine's own rule — the mouth is the record, and the record doesn't care
whose face it is. Cliff opens the next question: if the seat hears only
mouths, is the seat a mouth too — is the viewer a singer?

## The viral bet

**The dance the seat liked.** A 10-second dance move, shot from the seat's
point of view, built to be stolen — duet it, repeat it, teach it to the
seat. It is the era's dance format: one move per episode, one move the
audience can own. Secondary bet: **sing to the seat** — the wrong-singer
flip as a sing-along, cat vs girl, the audience picks a side.

## What it inherits from STATION vs what it breaks

- INHERITS: ARC-1 whole ("The One Viewer," 6 chains, OPEN → DEVELOP →
  PAYOFF); the lobby monitor, the empty chair, the tower that answers, the
  contract with the empty signature line as established lore beats; the
  dialogue-first chain grammar; the rule that lore is shown (a warm chair, a
  monitor that disagrees with the room), never explained; the one viewer /
  one advertiser as the show's literal subject, not background pressure.
- BREAKS: the station setting is abandoned for a room with one seat and a
  stage that changes every episode; the sitcom register gives way to variety
  — song, dance, story, joke, one act per episode; the engine is no longer
  promise-failure but audition — every episode performs for a judge who never
  speaks, and the verdict is survival: acts the seat liked persist, acts it
  didn't are culled.
