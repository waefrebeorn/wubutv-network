## PREMISE

Sentence: "Two neighbours race to fill the big jar before the fair.
The loser carries the winner's jar home on his head."
Schema: eatsimple_x contract-on-the-counter + Adi fill-level stakes.
Frame 1: a giant empty glass jar between two houses, a painted line
near the top reading FAIR DAY, both neighbours already dropping
things in, mid-motion. Whatever each puts in came off his own house
or body — roof tiles, coat buttons, dinner — so the houses visibly
empty as the jar fills. Serial engine: each episode a new thing both
want in the jar first; the fill level resets only when somebody pays
for it on screen.

## CAST

- **MARO** — the tall neighbour (male, HOME). Costume token: a
  chalk-white-dusted work shirt; everything he contributes is marked
  chalk-white (his roof tiles carry the white mark).
- **PELL** — the short neighbour (male, HOME). Costume token: a red
  string tied at his wrist; everything he contributes is tied with
  red string.
- **Widow BRAN** — the fair judge (female, HOME). Costume token: the
  paint pot and brush she carries everywhere; she paints the running
  count and the FAIR DAY line on the glass, and her rulings are
  pictures, not speeches.
- **The street (crowd, named):** TILO lends tools, SARA gossips the
  count, UMBER bets on the outcome. One crowd member changes sides
  per unit — carries a thing to the other house, moves their chair
  to the other side of the jar — so the street's seating plan is a
  second, silent scoreboard next to the fill level (ANGLES #9,
  Annex Part I).

Home vs invader: both neighbours and the judge are HOME characters.
The invaders never enter the race — see TEXTURE.

## ENGINE

The fill-level race under a printed contract: the terms are painted
in the world from frame 1 (Annex Part A #7 — "FIRST TO THE LINE"
banner / law on the jar's stand), the stake is the fill level
against the FAIR DAY line, and the proof beat is always a produced
object or body event (see RULINGS).
RESERVE: escalation by recruitment (Part G BIG JAR #1 — the dispute
scaling by allies and courts) as a later-episode raise; the loser's
victory condition (winning by making the race never legally end,
Part A) as a one-episode twist, never the standing engine.

## LEDGER RULE

Whatever enters the jar came off a man's own house or body, marked
in his colour — the jar's fill and the houses' emptiness are the
same number read twice.

## TEXTURE

Fruit characters move into the emptied houses — one open door per
unit, a fruit family carrying its things in behind the race, shown
in the background and never explained. The race empties the street;
the takeover fills it.

## FORMAT + COMPRESSION

6 units × 10 seconds, 16:9 landscape, one 60-second episode.
The source forms run minutes of fill/haggle escalation across a
fair-day. The compression device is the THRESHOLD CEREMONY plus
BRAN's painted count (Annex Part G BIG JAR #3; Part I): every unit
crosses a visible threshold — the banner names the round's class,
BRAN repaints the count, one crowd member changes sides — so 60
seconds carries the escalation the sources spread over a whole
fair, with the premise re-spent in every unit.

## RULINGS

- **Count (G12.1):** the painted count is a SCOREBOARD, never the
  proof. The proof beat is always a produced object or a bodily
  event (the twice-used tile held up, the jar tipping, the carry).
  "Call the count" items (Part A #1, Part D #1, Part B eatsimple_x
  #1) are scoreboard dressing only.
- **WORN NUMBER placement (G12.2):** the default is the count
  painted ON THE GLASS by BRAN, beside the FAIR DAY line. RESERVE:
  the count chalked on chests (Part A #17); the forehead stamp
  FULL / HALF / CHEAT with a FAIR DAY re-stamping (Annex Part E).
- **Title/caption:** per the template ruling (2026-11-27) — TITLE =
  the question the episode answers; CAPTION = the episode SENTENCE.
- **Endings adopted:** E1's button is THE KOAN BUTTON (Annex Part
  I #15) — after the carry-home is set, MARO, jar on head, asks
  "What will you put in it first?" and PELL's answer is a held face
  at poster size, no line. E2 ends on THE FALSE-VICTORY FACE
  (Part I #16). END ON THE NEXT CHALLENGER (Part I #18) is RESERVE.

## E1 SCRIPT-STAGE RULINGS (G5 — post-verdict ammunition)

Recorded for the script stage, AFTER the user's verdict on
THE_BIG_JAR_E1_PLAIN_STORY_DRAFT.md. The draft itself is untouched
by this bible. When the parent writes the E1 script:

(a) **The key stays in.** The door key STAYS IN the jar: the
    episode ends with PELL knocking on his own locked door while
    MARO stands beside him with the full jar on his head — the
    loser is the only one who can free the winner's key, by
    tipping the jar he lost with.
(b) **Scoring rule, stated in beat 1.** Each man's contributions
    are marked in his own colour — MARO's chalk-white, PELL's
    red-string-tied — and the winner is the man whose marked thing
    first TOUCHES the FAIR DAY line. Beat 1 shows the marks and
    the rule (BRAN paints it / the banner states it), so a cold
    viewer can referee the race themselves.
(c) **The cheat is shown before the accusation.** One night beat
    in U1/U2: the paint-marked tile going in twice — the audience
    is ahead of the town before anyone accuses (synthesis §3
    item 10; the colour-match proof, Annex Part H BIG JAR #4).
(d) **Reaction beats.** Every beat closes on a face changing state
    at poster size (synthesis Rule 10); the payment beats land on
    MARO's face, not only on the jar.
