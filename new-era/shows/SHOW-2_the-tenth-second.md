# SHOW 2 — THE TENTH SECOND

**Status:** founding treatment, new era. STATION is closed canon (585 pieces,
ep079, ST-079; piece 585 'The Essay the Millpond Keeps for No Reader' landed after shutter as archive only). THE TENTH
SECOND mines STATION's cull premise and its countdown grammar — it does not
continue STATION's plots.

## Premise

Every ten seconds the world ends and starts again — same street, same people,
same light, one detail changed. Everyone knows. Nobody panics anymore; panic
wastes a whole unit. Each episode follows one person with one job that takes
longer than ten seconds: finish the sentence, deliver the parcel, cross the
street, say the name. They have learned to work in ten-second shifts, leaving
notes for the next version of themselves, trusting a stranger who is also
them to pick up where they left off. The show is a thriller about continuity:
what survives a cull is what somebody was holding on to.

## The 10-second transition device — THE CARRY

Whatever a character is physically holding, touching, or mid-word on when the
cull hits is CARRIED into the next unit — same object, same hand, same
sentence fragment, new world around it. The transition is a match cut on the
carried thing: a parcel held at 9.9s is a parcel held at 0.0s, but the street
behind it has changed. The craft problem is explicit: the carried object must
be legible in both units or the audience loses the thread. If nothing is
carried, the unit is a cold open and the character has to re-earn the
audience from zero — the show treats an uncarried cut as a failure of
planning, and the characters know it. Rule: every unit names its carry in
the first 1.5s (show the hand, show the object, show the half-sentence).

## Cast (WuBu-native originals only)

- **THE COURIER** — new. Delivers parcels across culls. Knows every shortcut
  that survives a reset and every one that doesn't. Talks in addresses and
  deadlines; never says a name twice.
- **THE WATCHER** — new. Keeps the log of what changed between culls — one
  detail per unit, written on the wall in chalk. The wall is the show's
  memory; the cull cannot erase chalk, and nobody knows why.
- **LAURA** — the new hire who asks the question everyone avoids: if the
  world rebuilds every ten seconds, which version of us is the real one?
  (STATION canon: FOREST.)
- **SUPER NUMBER HEAD** — counts the culls. The count is always underway and
  never finished; he is the only character who can say which cull number
  this is. (STATION canon: ARC-3, The Count.)
- **UNC** — manages the depot the courier runs out of. Deadpan; keeps the
  roster of deliveries, refuses to erase a name. (STATION canon: INK +
  CADMIUM, `~ WuBu ~` hoodie.)
- **DISPATCHER** — reports what the building knows: which doors survived
  this cull, which rooms moved. (STATION canon: INK + BONE.)

## The world rule (what the characters know about the cull)

Everyone knows the cull comes every ten seconds and that it keeps one thing
per person: whatever they were holding. They do NOT know who chooses what
survives, or whether the cull is a machine, a weather, or an audience
changing the channel. They have a working theory — "the cull keeps what
matters" — and a working fear: that the cull keeps what matters TO IT, not
to them. Chalk survives. Paper doesn't. A name spoken aloud survives in the
mouth that spoke it; a name written down survives only if someone is holding
the pen.

## Episode shape

- One episode = 3–4 units of 10s (30–40s), one job across the whole episode,
  exactly one spoken line per unit at 7–9s, ≤9 words, named character.
  (STATION grammar, kept.)
- Unit structure: 0–1.5 HOOK (the carry, shown — hand, object, half-word) /
  1.5–4 STATE (the job, in this version of the world) / 4–7 TURN (the world
  changes under the job) / 7–9 LINE / 9–10 CLIFF (the cull hits mid-gesture
  — the hand is still holding at 10.0s).
- Units chain by the carry: the cliff's held object IS the next unit's hook
  object. A chain's final cliff is the next chain's first frame.
- The countdown is visible: a chalk tally, a parcel stamp, a number called
  by SUPER NUMBER HEAD — the audience always knows which second it is.

## Pilot episode chains (dialogue-first — one conversation each)

### Chain T1-1 — "The Parcel" (OPEN)
Stake: the courier's record — 400 culls, zero lost parcels; this parcel is
addressed to someone who no longer exists in this version of the street.
Setting: the depot door, cull 412.

- COURIER: "This address doesn't exist anymore. The street moved."
- WATCHER: "The street moves every ten seconds. The parcel doesn't."
- COURIER: "Then who do I give it to? The name on it is mine."
- WATCHER: "Then you carried it to yourself. Sign for it, Courier."

Narrator test: passes — each line is a speech act (accuse / rule /
counter / command). Final line lands: the job was never delivery; it was
custody. (Poached move: 2+2 — the audience assembles "the courier is the
recipient" from the pieces, never told.)

### Chain T1-2 — "The Chalk Wall" (DEVELOP)
Stake: the wall — the only memory the cull cannot erase — and LAURA wants to
write the one thing the Watcher refuses to record.
Setting: the chalk wall, cull 413.

- LAURA: "Write it down, Watcher. The cull took my name last time."
- WATCHER: "Chalk keeps what matters, Laura. Your name didn't matter."
- LAURA: "Then write this instead: the cull keeps what IT wants."
- WATCHER: "I already did. Read the wall, Laura. It's your handwriting."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: LAURA has been here before, and the
wall remembers what she doesn't. (Poached move: objects as subtext — the wall
is the character that knows.)

### Chain T1-3 — "The Count Skips" (DEVELOP → next question)
Stake: the count itself — SUPER NUMBER HEAD refuses to say which cull number
this is, and UNC needs the number to file the delivery.
Setting: the depot, cull unknown.

- UNC: "What number is this, Counter? The roster needs a number."
- SUPER NUMBER HEAD: "Four hundred and fourteen. Or fifteen. I skipped one."
- UNC: "You don't skip numbers. Numbers are the only thing that holds."
- SUPER NUMBER HEAD: "Then hold this one, UNC. It isn't mine anymore."

Narrator test: passes. Final line lands: the counter has started handing the
count away — the count is looking for a new keeper. Cliff opens the next
question: who counts when the counter stops?

## The viral bet

**The carry challenge.** A 10-second clip that ends mid-gesture with an
object in hand, and the audience duets the next ten seconds — what do YOU
carry across the cut? It is a participation format: same object, new world.
Secondary bet: **the chalk wall** — one detail changed per episode, and the
audience hunts it frame by frame.

## What it inherits from STATION vs what it breaks

- INHERITS: the 10-second grammar and the cliff-chaining; the roster, the
  count, and the dispatcher as lore systems; the dialogue-first chains; the
  rule that lore is shown (chalk, parcels, tallies), never explained; UNC,
  LAURA, DISPATCHER, SUPER NUMBER HEAD in their established voices.
- BREAKS: STATION never let the characters know the world was artificial —
  here everyone knows and plans around it; the sitcom register gives way to
  a countdown thriller; the station setting is abandoned for a street, a
  depot, and a wall; the engine is no longer promise-failure but
  continuity-under-erasure — the joke is replaced by the question of what
  survives.
