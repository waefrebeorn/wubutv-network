DANCER: "I want this stage open; shall I dance again?"
USHER: "Landlord's test: dance for fifty cash before last bar."
USHER: "House of one."

[Picture: A dancer lands from a leap in front of a shadowed woman seated in a theatre's front row; a man in a grey coat slaps a paper notice on a door and shakes a cash box whose tally card reads 32.]

USHER: "I want that test passed without begging him."
OCCUPANT: "I want a reason to stay."

[Picture: The cash box sits open showing the tally 32; the seated woman's hand stops conducting and she does not applaud while coins are counted out in a short row; a couple in peach and lime costumes enters arm in arm, points at the seated woman's seat and then at themselves to claim it as theirs for tonight, and waits in the aisle, the lime pointing accusingly at the peach and turning away from her.]

DANCER: "Empty your pockets for your job — would you?"
OCCUPANT: "Forty-one is not fifty, even in a pretty dress."

[Picture: The dancer empties her wage envelope into the cash box; the tally now reads 41; the man in the grey coat chains the theatre door shut; the fruit couple waits in the aisle, watching.]

USHER: "Forty-one short, he adds: critic stays to last bar."
OCCUPANT: "A phrase is leap, turn, land — land clean and I'll stay."

[Picture: The man padlocks the chain on the door; a white envelope slides under the chained door and stays there unopened; the fruit couple attempts the leap-turn-land phrase together in the aisle and lands in a heap in each other's arms, not clean, and the seated woman does not tap and the lamp does not warm for them, so their claim to the seat is lost; the seated woman's foot then taps a rhythm three times and a small lamp beside her seat warms with each tap.]

DANCER: "Take the box and my flat key — nine, and my home."
OCCUPANT: "No begging, then: she chose the price herself."

[Picture: The fruit couple watches from the aisle floor where it landed; the dancer performs the leap-turn-land phrase three times cleanly, the seated woman steadies her elbow on the third landing, then the dancer hands the cash box and her flat key to the man in the grey coat, who tears the notice in half, unchains the door, and leaves carrying the box, the key, and the chain; the dancer's wrist key ring is now empty and the white envelope slips fully inside the unchained door; the fruit couple gets up and sits together in two ordinary seats at the back.]

USHER: "The letter says the real critic comes final night."
OCCUPANT: "This house of one owes him its seat, darling."

[Picture: The letter that slid under the door while it was chained is held up to the seat lamp's light; the torn notice halves lie on the floor by the open door and the three people hold still, looking at the seated woman; the fruit couple sits together in two ordinary seats at the back, its claim to the critic's seat lost.]
