## BOARD HEADER

- **Skeleton named (Pass 1): S1 — THE DOOR TEST.** Keeper states
  the test aloud before it is tested (ST-4); claimant fails on the
  stated evidence, adapts exactly one thing, tries again; keeper's
  side upgrades the protocol aloud; the third attempt resolves at
  the door. Here the "door" is literal: the theatre's own door,
  which the landlord's man chains.
- **Episode engine (F-6 rotation): a want-vs-want CONTEST with a
  winner** — Dancer vs the Landlord, fought in the present tense
  through his man on screen. This is NOT the B1 record-procedure
  (proof → lie → authority reveal about a book) that spined E1.
  The guest book appears only as furniture; no reveal is read out
  of it. B1 does not spine two consecutive episodes. ✓
- **Before/after pair (ST-1):** *Open for tonight but unpaid, with
  the door to be chained at dawn → paid and unchained for tonight,
  at the cost of the Dancer's home above the theatre.* End state
  ≠ start state, and the difference is visible in picture (chain
  off, notice torn, key gone).
- **One-sentence argument (ST-2):** A dancer must pass her
  landlord's cash-and-stay test before the last bar ends, or the
  theatre locks at dawn. Every line pulls this rope: the test,
  the count, the stay, the payment.
- **Working title (ST-10):** "Night Two: The Landlord's Test" —
  states the setup (a test set by the landlord) and the promised
  turn (a test can be passed or failed). Frame one is the
  contrast: the Dancer mid-leap in vermilion at a shadowed seat,
  a notice already being slapped on the door behind her —
  conflict in motion, not setup being assembled.
- **Persisted situation + one new element (SR-4):** Persisted —
  the seat, the woman in it, the encore she commanded, the lease.
  The single new element: **the Collector**, the landlord's man,
  and the cash test he brings. No second system is added.
- **Audience secret (ST-5):** By the end of U1 the viewer has
  seen, in a picture insert the Dancer cannot see while dancing,
  that the cash box holds only **32** against the test's 50. The
  characters learn it at the U2 count. A number, picturable in
  U1 — no lore.
- **Relationship inversion (ST-7), in words before spectacle:**
  The judged becomes the judge's rescuer — the Occupant, who was
  on trial for her seat in E1, is the person whose *staying*
  decides whether the Dancer passes in E2; and the Landlord's
  man, who came to close the room, leaves carrying its rent and
  tearing his own notice.
- **Function phrases (CH-4, unique):** DANCER — *the payer*;
  USHER — *the keeper who will not beg*; OCCUPANT — *the stay
  that decides*; COLLECTOR (silent) — *the hand that chains*.
- **State-carrier (SR-5):** The Dancer's **flat key on a wrist
  ring** — worn and visible from U1, handed over in U5; from
  then on the **empty ring** is worn for the rest of the episode
  (and into E3). Secondary carrier: the landlord's notice —
  whole on the door (U1–U4), torn in half on the floor (U5–U6).

## ESCALATION LIST (ST-6 — itemized, one step per unit, settled in order)

1. **U1** — The notice is slapped on the door; the test is stated
   aloud: fifty cash before the last bar ends.
2. **U2** — First count: the box holds 32. Attempt 1 FAILS the
   cash test on the stated evidence.
3. **U3** — The Dancer adapts exactly one thing: her own wages go
   in. Second count: 41. Attempt 2 FAILS — and the Collector
   chains the box-office door (the stake, in picture, worsening).
4. **U4** — The protocol is upgraded aloud, before it is applied
   — and caused, in the Usher's line, by the 41 shortfall: the
   critic must also stay to the last bar, or the cash counts
   nothing. The Occupant states her price for staying: three
   clean landings. A letter slides under the chained door and
   is left unopened (plant, P-204).
5. **U5** — Attempt 3: three clean landings (she stays) + the box
   and the flat key handed over (the nine, and her home). The
   Collector tears the notice and unchains the door. Both tests
   settled, in the order they were set.
6. **U6** — Consequence, not revocation: the room is paid and
   open — and the letter planted under the chained door in U4
   is read, booking the next debt (the real critic, the final
   night; the house owes him its seat).

## CHARACTER CARDS (CH-1 — complete before dialogue; Pass 2)

### THE DANCER — function: the payer
- **Want (series, spoken this episode by U1):** keep this stage
  open — tonight, by passing the test herself. (Gate E repair:
  spoken as her own first-person want + question in U1 —
  "I want this stage open; shall I dance again?")
- **Stake:** her stage AND her home above it; if she fails, both
  go at dawn. Tonight she pays with the second to save the first.
- **Wound / wrong belief:** believes if she dances well enough,
  no one she loves will ever have to pay for this room — the
  episode proves the dance alone is nine short.
- **Rule she will not break:** she never stops a dance once the
  seat has asked for it — the encore runs, whatever enters.
- **Tell:** she touches the flat key on her wrist ring before
  she spends anything — planted U1 (a glance/touch mid-leap
  recovery), U3 (touch before the wages), paid U5 (the key
  itself leaves the ring).
- **Voice rule:** plain and physical — states wants and prices
  in concrete nouns; when cornered, she talks to the room
  itself (direct address) rather than pleading with a person.
- **Signature line (nobody else would say it):** "Empty your
  pockets for your job — would you?"

### THE USHER — function: the keeper who will not beg
- **Want (spoken by U2):** the test passed without begging the
  landlord for time.
- **Stake:** forty years of keeping ends with a chained door he
  could have prevented by asking — asking is the one price he
  has never paid (carried plant P-102).
- **Wound / wrong belief:** the shared wrong belief — the
  founder vanished, the real critic is still to come, every
  occupant is a rehearsal. The U6 letter starts its crack.
- **Rule he will not break:** he never asks anyone for anything.
  (He holds it all episode — at visible cost, which is the point.)
- **Tell:** he reads paper before he reads people — every turn
  of his starts with his eyes on a page, a notice, a tally.
- **Voice rule:** SHORT, deadpan, 3–8 words; states rules and
  facts, never feelings. Refrain (TW-6, one mouth, word-identical):
  **"House of one."**
- **Signature line:** "House of one."

### THE OCCUPANT (the woman with the white stub) — function: the stay that decides
- **Want (spoken by U2):** a reason to stay. (Gate E repair:
  "and sign nothing" cut — signing was never asked for or used
  in the episode.)
- **Stake:** if she leaves early, the room dies and her seat —
  the only place her promise lives — dies with it; if she stays
  for the wrong reason, she becomes the landlord's instrument.
- **Wound / wrong belief:** believes staying is a neutral act —
  that she can watch without deciding. U4's upgrade makes staying
  the verdict itself, and she must own it.
- **Rule she will not break:** she never applauds a dance she
  has not chosen to judge.
- **Tell (the show's signature, CH-5):** her foot taps / her
  gloved hand conducts the first-night rhythm — planted U1
  (conducting the encore she commanded, exactly as E1 ended),
  reminded U4 (taps under the phrase lesson), paid U5 (she taps
  all three landings and steadies the Dancer's third landing
  with one gloved hand — the tell becomes a deed).
- **Voice rule:** MEDIUM, deadpan from the dark; verdicts built
  on numbers and one dry joke; calls the Dancer "darling" only
  when delivering a verdict.
- **Signature line:** "Forty-one is not fifty, even in a pretty dress."

### THE COLLECTOR (silent — the episode's one silent principal, F-3)
- The landlord's man. Want, shown never spoken: the fifty, or
  the chain on the door. He ACTS in the present tense in U1
  (notice slapped, box shaken), U3 (door chained), U4 (chain
  padlocked), U5 (takes box + key, tears notice, unchains —
  the payoff's other half). His decisive physical actions carry
  visible cost both ways. He has no lines; the room's lines
  about him are the record.

## DOCTRINE LAYER (msg 524 — on top of E2 law)

- **TEACH:** U4 — the Occupant teaches how a dance phrase is
  built, inside the bargain: *"A phrase is leap, turn, land —
  land clean and I'll stay."* The viewer learns the unit of the
  dance and immediately watches it tested three times in U5.
- **WHIMSY SET-PIECE:** U5 — the three landings land as a
  **seat-wave**: each clean landing slams one row of house seats
  down in a wave, programmes flap up like startled birds, and
  the Collector must duck each wave while clutching his chain —
  the third duck drops the chain at the Dancer's feet.
- **QUOTABLE LINE:** "Forty-one is not fifty, even in a pretty dress."
- **DIRECT ADDRESS (1 beat):** U3 — the Dancer, wage envelope in
  hand, turns straight to camera: *"Empty your pockets for your
  job — would you?"* A question meant for the comments. Never
  in the BLAM second; placed at the cost beat, where it bites.
- **WORLD TEXTURE (set dressing only, zero lore terms in
  dialogue):** the house is an afterlife for old shows — rows of
  seats hold programmes from productions nobody alive performed,
  which flap like birds in the seat-wave; the theatre cat sleeps
  on the cash box in GAP 1 and is entirely unbothered. The
  surface story needs none of this explained.
