## CHARACTER CARDS (A4 — pasted into the dialogue pass)

**THE CLERK** — keeper of the counting office; the book is hers.
Response Style **Medium** (C6). Carrier line (audio logo):
*"We count what's brought."* Catchphrase: *"The book doesn't
blink."*
- History: has kept this office's book for years; no count on her
  watch has ever closed even; she once charged for the book's
  opening and the debt was never paid.
- Goal this episode: close the book even by closing time, and
  never once be the one who was wrong.
- Entering emotion: proprietorial calm — the office is hers.

**THE COUNTER** — counts the loads aloud, floor side. Response
Style **Short** (C6). Catchphrase: *"Again."* His first line of
any episode is a number.
- History: once lost count (the back-step) and ordered it kept
  secret — his one professional shame, known only to him.
- Goal this episode: finish one count — just one — that comes
  out even.
- Entering emotion: flat; already counting something small.
- **QUESTIONING JOB (A4):** he notices by U2, names by U4, acts
  in U6. He rides the accusation chain as its accuser-of-the-
  office from inside it (X8).

**THE PLANNER** (Ep. 1 customer; wedding planner, deadline trait
turned up, everything else deadpan — S8/P4). Response Style
**Medium** (C6).
- History: packed the hire umbrellas herself this morning; her
  own wet umbrella stands dripping by the office door (visual
  plant only — see DECISION_LOG).
- Goal this episode: thirty-one umbrellas confirmed before the
  noon wedding, or her fee dies.
- Entering emotion: deadline panic held flat.

**Shared wrong belief (A4, declared):** the Clerk and the
Counter both believe — and run the office on the belief — that
*customers* always miscount by one. The viewer can see it is
wrong: one item from every load ends up on the shelf behind the
Clerk, in frame, unremarked. The cast never looks at it. The
audience counts it.

**Unsaid sentences (Pass 3, legibility-test reference):**
- CLERK: "One from every load is on my shelf, and I put it
  there." (Cost of saying it plain: her authority dies.)
- COUNTER: "I think the office takes it. I think she takes it."
  (Cost: his job — and his own hidden back-step surfaces.)
- PLANNER: "If it's thirty, I packed wrong and the bride fires
  me." (Cost: her fee and her name.)

## THE SCRIPT

Subtotal chain (joint grammar): U1 CLAIMED 31 flips on the slam
→ U2 subtotal 20 spoken at the cliff → U3 board flips 30 at the
tail → U6 board settles COUNTED 30 | CLAIMED 31. The office
persists across joints; the pile shrinks; the shelf grows.

### U1 — FULL-TALK · 18 words · 3 lines

**Picture:** FRAME 0 IS MID-SLAM. The door is already swinging,
the bell ringing, the umbrellas already crashing across the
counter — slam, bell, crash stacked inside 0.5s. Split Total
flips at ~0.3s on the slam: CLAIMED 31. No fade-in, no locked
frames; the camera pushes in *with* the load. The Planner is
mid-stride, mid-sentence. Her own wet umbrella drips by the
door, unremarked. The Counter is floor-side, righting fallen
umbrellas, already counting them.

> **PLANNER** *(want-statement, B6 — her want is her first line;
> accusation-in-flight energy, B2 Hook)*:
> "Thirty-one umbrellas. Count them before noon. My fee rides
> on it." — 11 words
>
> **CLERK** *(carrier line — the office's audio logo)*:
> "We count what's brought." — 4 words
>
> **COUNTER** *(his first line is a number — he never looks
> up)*:
> "Nine, ten, eleven—" — 3 words

*Physical moment (C7):* the crash itself — one umbrella slides
off the counter and he catches it by the handle without
breaking count.

### U2 — MIXED · 12 words · 2 lines

**Picture:** Counter-wide settles to the pile. Batch one is
counted in frame, umbrellas laid in rows of five — honest
piles, countable-looking, never framed as an exact-count
promise (the board carries the arithmetic; see Production
Notes). At the unit head the Planner slaps the hire receipt
onto the counter — **the proof object is shown before anyone
explains it (B8)** — and only then speaks.

> **PLANNER** *(accusation-with-proof, B5)*:
> "The hire receipt says thirty-one umbrellas, paid in full."
> — 9 words
>
> **COUNTER** *(batch tail; the subtotal is the cliff, spoken
> as the board flips to 20)*:
> "Eighteen, nineteen, twenty." — 3 words

**GAP 1 (A2, after U2's turn) — carrier: the Counter's stopped
hand.** His hand stops over the remaining pile — barely ten
umbrellas left and the board already says twenty. He has felt
it run light (A4 notice, unspoken). 2–3s, no dialogue; the
board holds one behind the pile. ~1s reaction hold after the
Planner's receipt line precedes it (A2 micro-structure).

### U3 — QUIET · 8 words · 2 lines

**Picture:** Batch two, counted in frame. **The accusation
lands INSIDE the batch (slate §3 ruling):** the Counter is
mid-batch, hands moving, count audibly running, when the
Planner speaks over it — this is the episode's ONE declared
interruption (C8/X5), placed on the count itself, the
sacrificial surface. He does not stop counting. He does not
look up. The pile, not the talk, carries the turn: the last
umbrella is laid down in silence and the board flips to 30 at
the unit's tail.

> **PLANNER** *(accusation, mid-batch, over the count)*:
> "Street took one." — 3 words
>
> **COUNTER** *(lie/denial, B5 — flat, still counting)*:
> "I don't lose. I count." — 5 words

### U4 — FULL-TALK · 19 words · 2 lines

**Picture:** The recount — faster, both of them on the pile,
the Planner counting alongside him to catch him (her hands in
his rows). The board climbs again and stops at 30. Beat. Then
he says the thing he has never said in front of a customer.

> **COUNTER** *(A4 — he NAMES it, U4; the episode's peak
> line)*:
> "Every pile comes up one light. Every one." — 8 words
>
> **PLANNER** *(entrance-that-contradicts, B5 — she steps
> around the counter to his side of the pile as she says it;
> her accusation changes address)*:
> "Then it's the office doing it. Not my packing. Not me."
> — 11 words

**GAP 2 (A2, after U4's peak turn) — carrier: the shelf, CCTV
corner angle.** On the shelf behind the Clerk stands ONE
UMBRELLA. Nobody looks at it. Nobody remarks it. 2–3s, no
dialogue. The viewer does the arithmetic the cast won't.

### U5 — MIXED · 14 words · 2 lines

**Picture:** Book close angle. The Clerk's hand rests on the
book. She names the price before she opens it — **the pricing
beat is the price tag on the authority reveal (slate fix
F4.1).** The Planner pays in the only concession she has: she
lays the hire receipt face-down on the counter — her proof,
surrendered. The book opens: page after page of counts, every
one short, in the Clerk's hand. After her line she writes
today's shortfall into the book, on screen: the umbrellas,
claimed 31, counted 30.

> **CLERK** *(authority reveal, B10 — she keeps the book, so
> she delivers the deepest reveal; price fused to it)*:
> "The price: this office has never closed even." — 8 words
>
> **PLANNER** *(the concession, paid out loud)*:
> "Then write me in. One short." — 6 words

~1s reaction hold on the Planner's face after the reveal line
(A2 micro-structure) before she pays.

### U6 — SILENCE-BEAT · 12 words · 3 lines

**Picture:** The board settles: **COUNTED 30 | CLAIMED 31**
(the device supplies the fact). The Counter acts (A4, U6): he
turns away from the pile and counts the SHELF instead — one
item. He stops. He doesn't say it. The Clerk answers the
silence with doctrine. Then the customer supplies the cliff
(X2: a character, never the device, carries the ending).

> **COUNTER** *(counting the shelf; stops at the umbrella)*:
> "One umbrella." — 2 words
>
> **CLERK** *(catchphrase, flat, to the Planner)*:
> "The book doesn't blink." — 4 words
>
> **PLANNER** *(the cliff — spoken AND on screen, B11)*:
> "Then where does the one GO?" — 6 words

**GAP 3 (A2, final 2–3s of U6) — carrier: the Clerk's face.**
Freeze on the Clerk, book open in front of her, the question
on screen. Nobody moves. Cut at the peak (B3) — consequences
are Episode 2's setup.

## PRODUCTION NOTES (binding on the build)

1. **ANCHOR LAW (morph inheritance, non-negotiable):** the
   combined two-character anchor panel rides EVERY unit, and
   the Clerk's CHAR sheet rides every call she appears in.
   This is the proven fix for this show's identity failure
   (THE COUNT pilot) and is not traded away for any take.
2. **THE BOARD CARRIES THE ARITHMETIC (slate §5, this show's
   defining production risk):** the split-flap board
   (COUNTED | CLAIMED) is a Method C post graphic, keyed to
   measured beats — flip at ~0.3s on the U1 slam, subtotal 20
   at the U2 cliff, 30 at the U3 tail, settle 30|31 in U6.
   FOREST #1F6B3A accent, furniture bottom-left, bug
   bottom-right, legible at phone size. **Do NOT ask the
   generator for exactly-countable piles.** Piles are honest
   piles, laid in rough rows; the count-along promise is
   cashed by the board plus the spoken subtotal chain, never
   by asking a viewer to count 31 rendered umbrellas. The
   script was written so the promise survives this: every
   number a viewer needs is spoken and board-shown.
3. **SHELF CONTINUITY (slate §5):** the shelf is shot from a
   dedicated CCTV-corner angle; each episode needs its own
   shelf-angle PEAK panel. Ep. 1 shelf state: ONE UMBRELLA,
   standing, handle up, legible at phone size in the U4 gap
   and in U6. Shelf state is ledger-tracked (LEDGER.md) and
   cumulative across episodes; production verifies the shelf
   contents against the ledger before assembly, every episode.
4. **BLAM OPENING (the E2 A1 fix, by design):** frame 0
   mid-slam; three sound events (door slam, bell, load crash)
   stacked inside 0.5s; the want-line onset at ~0.5s,
   mid-stride; board flip at ~0.3s. No fade-in. No locked
   first frames. Takes are gated on the standing BLAM bars
   (A1 t50 ≤0.5s / t90 ≤1.5s, E1 named impact ≤1.0s).
5. **FRONT-LOADING DEFENSE (Method B A3):** turn 1 and turn 2
   are separate quoted blocks with the picture turn written
   between them; every unit's tail carries a speech-independent
   event (board flip, hand stop, shelf hold, freeze). Lines
   are written to read wherever they land. The U6 cliff is
   ALSO an on-screen post-graphic card ("Then where does the
   one GO?") so the ending survives speech-placement drift.
   Generated in-take text is never requested — the card, like
   the board, is post.
6. **GAP DEFENSE (E2 lesson — the generator compresses
   scripted silence):** all three gaps are carried by action
   or picture (stopped hand / shelf hold / freeze on a face),
   never by a bare pause; if a take compresses a gap's
   seconds, its carrier still reads. Assembly protects the
   U6 freeze's full 2–3s.
7. **CAMERA UNIFORM (bible §6, P6/X7):** counter-wide master
   angle; CCTV corner owns the shelf; one close angle owns
   the book. One grain/grade pass over everything. 16:9.
8. **PALETTE:** INK + BONE + CADMIUM; COBALT is the Clerk's
   accent only (ink on her fingertips); FOREST reserved for
   the board. Wordmark law applies as standing (glyph-spelled
   "~ WuBu ~", small / shadowed / frame edge) + corner bug.
9. **FORMAT GATES:** 1280×720, 24fps, exactly 1440 frames,
   CRF 18 (proved by size differential, never the SEI
   string), AAC, faststart. One assembly pass owns the joined
   intermediate; probe before the master encode; re-probe
   the finished master end to end.
10. **COVERAGE (A5):** shoot units as speaker → object/hand
    insert → silent listener reaction where the plan names
    it (U2 receipt insert, U4 hands-in-rows insert, U5 book
    insert); do not assume a 10s two-person dialogue master
    carries a unit alone.
