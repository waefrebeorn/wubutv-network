## GATE E — COLD-READER ANSWERS (Critic, on the locked draft)

1. **Who wanted what:** "A wedding planner wanted the
   counting office to confirm her thirty-one umbrellas before
   a noon wedding, because her fee depended on the count
   being right."
2. **What blocked her:** "The office counted thirty. And
   their own book shows every count they have ever run came
   up one short — so it isn't just her."
3. **What changed by U6:** "Her accusation collapsed. First
   she blamed the street, then the office — then the counter
   himself said out loud that every pile comes up one short,
   and he went and counted the back shelf: one umbrella
   standing on it. She left asking where the missing one
   goes."
4. **Why it matters to someone who knows no lore:** "A woman
   is being told she miscounted the thing her pay depends
   on, by an office whose own book proves the office is
   wrong by exactly one, every time. It's about being blamed
   for somebody else's pattern."

**Verdict: PASS** — no answer requires a lore term; the
relatability clause is answered with a job and an accusation.
Title-as-contract checked: "Thirty-One Umbrellas" + U1's
first line names a visible object and a want in plain words.

## CRITIC — LOCATED OBJECTIONS AND RESOLUTIONS

**O-1 (U3, slate §3).** *Objection:* the board as first
broken had the accusation landing after batch two finished —
a coda, not a turn; the slate names exactly this the show's
swipe point. *Resolution:* the accusation was re-staged
INSIDE the batch — the Planner speaks over the running count
at its midpoint (the episode's one declared interruption,
C8/X5), the Counter denies without stopping, and the pile
completes in silence after her line. Located fix, dialogue
unchanged in wording. **Resolved.**

**O-2 (A1 line cap).** *Objection:* draft v1 ran 16 lines
against the ≤14 cap. *Resolution:* three merges, all logged
as D-06 below; recount at Pass 4 verified 14. **Resolved —
and flagged as the episode's barely-passed gate** (see the
lock note in SCRIPT.md).

**O-3 (A4 shared wrong belief).** *Objection:* the belief
("customers always miscount by one") is declared on the
board but never spoken in Ep. 1 — the cast's error is shown
(the shelf) but not heard. *Resolution:* accepted as staged,
on two grounds: (a) A4 requires the belief *declared* and
visible to the viewer — the shelf discharges the visible
half in Ep. 1's gaps; (b) the spoken half is assigned, not
dropped: Ep. 2's B12 recap (the Clerk's complaint —
"a woman swore blind she'd brought thirty-one umbrellas") IS
the belief in her voice, and it is written into the lock
anchors. The room judged a fourth Clerk line in Ep. 1 a
worse trade than the deferral (see O-2). **Resolved, with
the deferral on record.**

**O-4 (Repetition audit, A5).** *Objection:* "one" recurs in
nearly every unit (thirty-one / one light / one short / one
umbrella / the one). *Resolution:* exempt per A5 — repetition
WITH a change of meaning: claim → subtotal → pattern → book
entry → physical object → open question. Each recurrence
moves the word's referent closer to the shelf. No phrase or
fact repeats at the same meaning twice. **Resolved.**

## DECISIONS (pitched / chosen / killed)

- **D-01 — Load and skeleton.** Pitched: three Ep. 1 loads
  (umbrellas / wedding chairs / a landlord's keys). Chosen:
  **thirty-one umbrellas** — the bible's own outline, the
  short-count skeleton (S3), and the only load whose Ep. 1
  plant (one umbrella on the shelf) reads at phone size in a
  2–3s gap. Chairs move to Ep. 2 per the season projection;
  keys held for Ep. 3. Killed: opening the series on keys —
  the back-door twist spends a reveal the premiere hasn't
  earned.
- **D-02 — The count-along is board-carried.** The slate's
  §5 flag is adopted as a scripting rule, not just a
  production note: no line, direction, or beat asks the
  viewer to count rendered objects; every number the story
  needs is spoken in the subtotal chain AND shown on the
  post board. The promise is "count with us" cashed through
  the board — the strongest form the pipeline can honestly
  ship. (SCRIPT.md Production Note 2.)
- **D-03 — Accusation inside the batch.** See O-1. The
  interruption is the accusation; the denial rides the count.
  This is also the episode's single C8 interruption —
  declared, singular, on the count itself.
- **D-04 — The pricing beat is fused to the reveal (F4.1).**
  The slate moved the book-pricing beat from U3 to U5; the
  room fused it into the reveal line itself ("The price:
  this office has never closed even.") so U5 holds two lines
  and the price lands as a tag ON the authority beat, per
  the fix's own wording. The Planner's payment is physical
  (the receipt, face-down) plus one spoken concession.
- **D-05 — The Counter's U6 act is a count, not a speech.**
  Pitched: a U6 line where he says the shelf is short of
  nothing / accuses the Clerk outright. Killed: he is one
  episode into a season-long A4 arc (T-2); an accusation in
  E1 spends E8's turn. Chosen: he counts the shelf and stops
  — the act is legible (B9), the conclusion withheld, and
  F-008 records observation-without-conclusion so no later
  room can pretend he already knows.
- **D-06 — The line-cap merges (the barely-passed gate).**
  Three merges took the draft from 16 lines / 87 words to
  14 lines / 83 words: (i) the Planner's U1 deadline line
  folded into her want-line (one 11-word line — fee pressure
  now rides the demand itself); (ii) the Clerk's U2
  receipt-skepticism line cut — her authority is carried by
  U1's carrier line and U5's reveal, and U2 keeps proof +
  subtotal only; (iii) the Counter's U4 "Again." held for
  Ep. 2 — the recount opens in action; the catchphrase is
  seeded where the line budget can afford it, logged here
  so Ep. 2's room owes it. **Standing flag for this show's
  future rooms: count lines before words — this cast of
  three hits the 14-line cap before it hits the word cap.**
- **D-07 — Two-hander declaration (A5).** The one-talker
  default is formally excepted for this show: the premise IS
  a two-hander plus a customer (bible §4, three chairs).
  Declared here so no future Pass 2 flags it as drift; the
  tensest unit (U4) is where all three voices concentrate,
  per the amendment's intent.
- **D-08 — The door umbrella stays visual in E1.** The
  bible's mistaken-identity seed (the Planner's own umbrella,
  from the door) is planted as picture only (P-005). Giving
  it a line in E1 would open a second explanation thread
  competing with the shelf in the premiere; its remind (E2
  recap dispute) and payoff (E4) are assigned in the plant
  table instead.
