## The case

Ansel the cook's coin tin has ridden in the Courier's hands
for four hundred bells — holding is her trade — and this
morning it came back with his nine coins gone. He wants the
theft written on the wall. The audience sees what the street
does not: down the street, a fruit princess presses her
bouquet closed over a glint of coins hidden inside it. The
accused Courier never speaks. She opens her hands to the
bell and lets them testify. The law of the wall is stated
whole in U3, before it rules in U5: at the bell, what's held
stays held — and what's loose, changes.

VALUE CHANGE (ST-1, stated before dialogue): BEFORE — the
Courier stands accused on the wall; her four-hundred-bell
record counts for nothing against an empty tin. AFTER — the
keeper strikes the accusation with his own hand and slides
the false claim's fee back across the counter, the true
line is chalked (HANDS OPEN. COINS LOOSE.), six coins go
back to Ansel and three stay in the drain, and the only
name the wall still owes is the thief's — unwritten,
waiting on the street's vote at the freeze.
ONE ROPE (ST-2): the tin, and what was — and was not — held
in it.
SKELETON: the bible's own docket chain (Hook+Setup / Proof+
Lie / Twist / Clash / Reveal / Consequence), five-beat spine
Hook → Denial → Evidence → Turn → Hanger carried on it.

## Cast of this episode

his deed (the slammed tin).
THE LIME (silent; the Peach's other half, named in her claim) —
holds the bouquet with her, copies her, until he doesn't.

## U1 — HOOK + SETUP · LAURA · 19 words

PICTURE: Frame 0 is mid-motion: Ansel's tin SLAMS onto the
wall's stone counter and spins, clattering — the named
first-second impact (BLAM: no dead head, no ramp-from-rest;
the queue is already moving). Ansel points at the Courier,
who stands with her hands open and empty. The Watcher,
keeper of the wall's word, stands at the wall with his
chalk. Down the street at Ansel's stall, unremarked, the
Peach presses her bouquet closed over a glint of coins
hidden inside it (AUDIENCE SECRET, ST-5: the viewer holds
it from this unit). Laura chalks the docket line on the
blank wall (the chalk text itself is composited in post).

LAURA: Ansel says the Courier held his tin. His coins are gone.
LAURA: I write one true line today, or none.

## U2 — PROOF + FIRST LIE · THE PEACH · 18 words

PICTURE: The proof is shown, not argued: the Courier turns
the tin over above her open palm — nothing falls. Ansel
presses a coin into the Watcher's palm to have his line
heard first; the Watcher pockets it without looking down
and turns back to the wall (his flaw, shown once, never
explained). The Peach steps into the docket queue ahead of
the theft, bouquet high in both hands, grinning, and states
her claim — the episode's first lie, told to the wall.

THE PEACH: Write us next: the Peach and the Lime, holding this bouquet since dawn.
THE PEACH: Both hands stayed on it.

## U3 — TWIST · LAURA · 15 words · MIXED (quiet-dominant)

[DIGESTION GAP 1 — after U2's turn: 2 seconds held on the
empty tin in the Courier's open hands, bell ticking, no
words. Carried at this unit's head.]

PICTURE: The bell has rung in the reset and one detail of
the street has changed: the stall awning's stripe, cobalt
now where it was bone, nothing more (world-swap is a spice,
not a metronome — and the change itself is the loose-things
rule, shown in the same unit that speaks it). The Courier
crosses to the wall, sets the tin down open on the counter,
and holds both hands up, open, turned over once — she
offers her hands to the bell to clear her name: the one act
that could clear her, volunteered. The Peach points at her
and laughs; the Lime copies her, letting go of the bouquet
with one hand to point too, and the bouquet sags. Laura
states the whole law of the wall — the test every hand in
the street is about to be judged by.

LAURA: At the bell, what's held stays held. What's loose changes. The Courier's hands are open.

## U4 — CLASH · LAURA · 17 words

PICTURE: Ansel jabs a finger at the tin, then at the
Courier's hands, still raised from her offer to the bell.
The Peach still holds her bouquet out toward the wall,
waiting for her line. Laura steps between the accusation
and the accused and names what she noticed in U1 — the
bouquet, heavier than flowers — against the tin's bell
count, the episode's worn stake: the Courier's record
made object.

LAURA: The Courier kept that tin safe four hundred bells.
LAURA: And the Peach's bouquet is heavier than flowers.

## U5 — THE BELL BREAKS THE LIE · LAURA · 14 words

[DIGESTION GAP 2 — after U4's peak turn: 2 seconds held on
the Peach's face, the bouquet in frame, no words. Carried
at this unit's head.]

PICTURE: The noon bell cracks. The Peach points at the
Courier and laughs; the Lime copies her with both hands —
the habit that sank them in E2, completed — and lets go of
the bouquet entirely. It drops: nine coins bounce across
the cobbles, and three of them roll into the storm drain
at the counter's base and are gone. Nothing that was held
moves an inch: the Courier's hands are still raised, as
they have been since she offered them. The Peach grabs at
the coins with both hands, too late. Laura gives the
verdict, then the cost.

LAURA: No hand held those coins at the bell.
LAURA: Three coins in the drain. Gone.

## U6 — CONSEQUENCE · LAURA · 11 words · SILENCE-BEAT

PICTURE: The Watcher strikes through the morning's
accusation line on the wall with his own chalk — his wall
keeps only what's true; the record corrected by the
keeper's hand, irreversible — slides Ansel's fee coin back
across the counter to him (the hearing was bought; the
verdict was not), and puts the six recovered coins into
Ansel's palm. The drain keeps its three. Ansel looks at
the Courier and nods, once. Laura chalks the true line
herself beneath (post-composited): HANDS OPEN. COINS
LOOSE. Her chalk then hovers over the blank space where a
name would go. She does not write it. The Peach and the
Lime stand over their dropped bouquet; nothing is written
for either of them. Below, the docket board's next line
already stands (post-composited): TOMORROW: THE NAME HE
WON'T WRITE.

LAURA: I wrote one true line today. The Peach's name stays unwritten.

[DIGESTION GAP 3 — the final 2–3 seconds: freeze on the
hovering chalk, the Peach's face, the struck line. No
words. The freeze carries this part's viewer vote as a
post-composited question: SHOULD LAURA WRITE THE PEACH'S
NAME? The majority verdict is chalked onto the wall in
Part 2's U1 background.]
