## The case

Ansel the cook's coin tin has ridden in the Courier's hands
for four hundred bells — holding is her trade — and this
morning it came back with his nine coins gone. He wants the
theft written on the wall. Laura will not chalk a verdict on
a say-so: she makes the Courier hold her hands up to the
bell, and the bell counts hands. The audience sees what the
street does not: down the street, a fruit princess presses
her bouquet closed over a glint of coins hidden inside it.
The accused Courier never speaks. She opens her hands to
the bell and lets them testify.

VALUE CHANGE (ST-1, stated before dialogue): BEFORE — the
Courier stands accused on the wall; her four-hundred-bell
record counts for nothing against an empty tin. AFTER — the
keeper strikes the accusation with his own hand and slides
the false claim's fee back across the counter, the true
line is chalked (HANDS OPEN. COINS LOOSE.), six coins go
back to Ansel and three stay in the drain, and the only
name the wall still owes is the thief's — unwritten,
waiting on the street's say at the freeze.
ONE ROPE (ST-2): the tin, and what was — and was not — held
in it.
SKELETON: the bible's own docket chain (Hook+Setup / Proof+
Lie / Twist / Clash / Reveal / Consequence), five-beat spine
Hook → Denial → Evidence → Turn → Hanger carried on it.

## Cast of this episode

## U1 — HOOK + SETUP · LAURA · 19 words · PROOF OBJECT: the tin

PICTURE: Frame 0 is mid-motion: Ansel's tin SLAMS onto the
wall's stone counter and spins, clattering — the named
first-second impact (BLAM: no dead head, no ramp-from-rest;
the queue is already moving). Ansel points at the Courier,
who stands with her hands open and empty. Laura tips the
tin over the counter as she speaks — nothing in it. The
Watcher, keeper of the wall's word, stands at the wall with
his chalk. Down the street at Ansel's stall, unremarked,
the Peach presses her bouquet closed over a glint of coins
hidden inside it (AUDIENCE SECRET: the viewer holds it from
this unit — planted shown, not said). Laura chalks the
docket line on the blank wall (the chalk text itself is
composited in post).

LAURA: Nine went in, Ansel. Count what's left.
LAURA: Empty. Not chalking 'thief' off that.
LAURA: Hands up, Courier — show me empty.

## U2 — PROOF + FIRST LIE · THE PEACH · 17 words · PROOF OBJECT: the bouquet

PICTURE: The proof is shown, not argued: the Courier turns
the tin over above her open palm — nothing falls. Ansel
presses a coin into the Watcher's palm to have his line
heard first; the Watcher pockets it without looking down
and turns back to the wall (his flaw, shown once, never
explained). The Peach steps into the docket queue ahead of
the theft, bouquet high in both hands, grinning, and makes
her claim — the episode's first lie, told to the Watcher.

THE PEACH: Us next, Watcher.
THE PEACH: Us two have held this bouquet since dawn.
THE PEACH: Both hands on it. All morning.

## U4 — CLASH · LAURA · 11 words · PROOF OBJECT: the bouquet

PICTURE: Ansel jabs a finger at the tin, then at the
Courier's hands, still raised from her offer to the bell.
Laura steps in between Ansel and the Courier. The Peach
still holds her bouquet out toward the wall, waiting for
her line — and it hangs low, its stem bending, heavier
than flowers hang. Laura turns on the Peach and throws
the contrast at her sworn morning — the Courier's hands,
still raised, against the bouquet's two claimed hands.
The Courier's record stands on the wall itself, unspoken:
row on row of tally marks beside the docket line, one for
every bell the tin came home (four hundred, the episode's
worn stake, where the street can count them) — and Laura
asks the question the contrast entitles her to ask, at
the bouquet.

LAURA: Her hands stayed up, Peach — so what's in yours, besides flowers?

tomorrow. Below, the docket
board's next line already stands (post-composited):

LAURA: Not yet, Peach. Not on the board.
LAURA: It goes to the street tomorrow.
