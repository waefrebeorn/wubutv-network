## The case

Marta, who sells bread at the depot corner, paid the
Watcher one coin yesterday — a coin with her notch cut in
it, like every coin she earns — to write her name on the
wall as PAID, so the wall stops saying she owes the rent.
The Watcher pocketed the coin and wrote nothing. After
her coin went in, the landlord's boy put two coins in
the same pocket: her name was to stay down. This morning
the wall still calls her owing, and the Watcher chalks
for everyone in the queue but her. Laura will not chalk
on a say-so — she reads the proof the street can see:
a notched coin says its owner's name, and the keeper's
hand has not left his pocket all morning. At the bell
she orders his hand up like everyone he judges. It
stays buried — and the bell's crack swings his coat:
three coins in the pocket's mouth, one of them notched.

VALUE CHANGE (ST-1, stated before dialogue): BEFORE —
the wall's keeper takes coins from both sides of a name
and the wall says what it is paid to say; Marta stands
owing on the record though she paid. AFTER — Laura
strikes the owing line and chalks MARTA PAID in her own
hand (her second true line), the keeper walks away from
his own wall with his hand still in his pocket, and the
only name left unwritten is his — given to the street's
vote tonight.
ONE ROPE (ST-2): the notched coin, and the hand that
won't come out of the pocket.
SKELETON: the bible's docket chain (Hook+Setup / Proof+
Lie / Twist / Clash / Reveal / Consequence) — the mirror
of Part 1: there the accused never spoke and her OPEN
hands cleared her; here the accused never speaks and
his CLOSED hand convicts him.
