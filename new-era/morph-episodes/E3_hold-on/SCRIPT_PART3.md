## The case

Last night the street voted on the question Part 2 froze:
should Laura write the Watcher's name? The street voted YES —
the tally marks went up on the wall under the question, chalk
on brick, for anyone to read this morning. Before dawn the
Watcher came alone to his own wall and scraped the tallies off
with his coat sleeve. THE AUDIENCE SEES HIM DO IT (U1). By
sunrise the wall carries his chalk instead: THE STREET SAID
NO. The town believes the wall — the wall is the town's only
record — and the morning queue forms as if the vote never
happened. But the Courier was at the wall before dawn too,
holding the dawn delivery, and he saw the scraping. And the
scraped vote never left the street: it is on the Watcher's
sleeve, white chalk dust to the elbow, which he has been
holding behind his back all morning. Laura reads what the
street can see — a sleeve cannot scrape a wall clean and stay
white by accident — names it at U4, and at U5 the Courier's
unsigned box forces the sleeve up into the light to sign.
Laura chalks the name the street voted for: WATCHER — OWES
THE STREET A TRUE WALL (her third true line). The episode
ends on the Watcher's face, his name above him, his hand
finally open — holding the chalk she offered, holding
nothing back, saying nothing.

VALUE CHANGE (ST-1, stated before dialogue): BEFORE — the
keeper can un-vote the street at his own wall before dawn and
the record will lie for him by breakfast; the street's yes is
gone and no one alive can prove it happened. AFTER — the
vote is found ON the keeper's own sleeve, Laura executes it
on the wall in front of the queue, and the only man in town
whose name is written as OWING is the keeper himself; his
hand ends open and empty of everything except her chalk.
ONE ROPE (ST-2): the chalk dust on the sleeve — what he is
holding without knowing he is holding it.
SKELETON (post-gutting): Cheat shown (U1) / The town believes
the wall (U2) / The witness prices himself in (U3) / The
naming (U4) / The proof forced into the light (U5) / The
verdict executed, end on the loser's face (U6). Sustained
irony replaces the reveal: the audience is never behind the
characters — it is ahead of the town, from frame one.
THE TURN (one per episode, slate constraint): the unsigned
box — a delivery cannot be refused twice; signing forces the
hidden sleeve into the light.

## The units

### U1 — THE SCRAPE (FULL-TALK for the cheat; speaker: THE COURIER)
Dawn, the street empty, the wall in cold light. On the wall
(post layer, visible before the scrape): the freeze question
from Part 2 and a row of YES tallies under it. The Watcher
alone, dragging his coat sleeve down the tallies, hard —
chalk dust puffs, the marks smear away. Behind him, unheard,
the Courier comes in holding a plain delivery box with both
hands. He stops. He watches. The Watcher turns, sees him,
and waves him off with the same dusty sleeve.
- COURIER: Dawn delivery.
- COURIER: Sign for the box, keeper.
(The wave-off refuses the signature. The Courier looks at
the sleeve — white to the elbow — and holds his box and
says no more. GAP: none scripted; the scrape's sound and
the dust carry the tail.)

### U2 — THE WALL SAYS NO (FULL-TALK; speaker: LAURA)
Sunrise. The queue at the wall. The wall now reads (post
layer): THE STREET SAID NO — fresh chalk, the tallies gone.
Marta in the queue, her PAID line up from Part 2. The
Watcher chalks a morning name one-handed. Laura reads the
wall, then looks — not at the wall — at the Watcher's
hidden arm.
- LAURA: The wall says the street said no.
- LAURA: The vote was last night.
- LAURA: Who held the chalk last?
(The Watcher chalks on. Nobody answers. GAP 1 (digestion,
~2s): the queue looks at the wall; Laura looks at the man.)

### U3 — HOLDING AT DAWN (QUIET; speaker: THE COURIER)
The Courier sets his box down square on the counter stone
between the queue and the wall. The Watcher chalks the
morning names; each pass, the hidden sleeve brushes a faint
white smear at the wall's edge. The Courier watches the
smears land.
- COURIER: Four hundred bells. Never dropped one.
- COURIER: At dawn I was holding this box. Here.
(He says it to the queue, plain, like weather. The audience
knows what he is really saying. The Watcher's chalk stops
for one beat — then goes on.)

### U4 — THE SLEEVE (FULL-TALK; speaker: LAURA)
Laura steps out of the queue's line and faces the Watcher.
She is not looking at the wall at all now.
- LAURA: Your sleeve's been holding chalk all morning.
- LAURA: The street voted yes.
- LAURA: You scraped their yes off the wall.
(The Watcher backs to the wall itself, the dusty arm going
behind him, hidden against the brick. GAP 2 (digestion,
~2s): the queue turns from Laura to the keeper's hidden arm.)

### U5 — SIGN FOR IT (speaker: THE COURIER)
The bell. Down the queue, every hand opens in the light —
the town's habit at the bell, shown not taught. The
Courier picks up his box and holds it out to the Watcher,
steady, at arm's length.
- COURIER: Sign for the box, keeper.
- COURIER: There's the vote. On your sleeve.
(The Watcher must sign the wall's slate to take the box —
and signing means the chalk hand, and the chalk hand means
the sleeve comes up into the light: white to the elbow,
in front of the whole queue. He signs. The dust is the
vote, carried on him all morning.)

### U6 — HIS NAME (SILENCE-BEAT; speaker: LAURA)
Laura takes the chalk and writes on the wall, large, in
her true-line hand (post layer): WATCHER — OWES THE STREET
A TRUE WALL. The queue reads it. The Watcher stands under
his own name.
- LAURA: Your name's up, Watcher.
(She holds out the chalk to him — the same offer she made
in Part 2, when he walked away from it. This time he takes
it. FINAL BEAT (silence, ~2.5s): END ON THE LOSER'S FACE —
the Watcher looking up at his name, his hand open at last,
holding her chalk, holding nothing else, saying nothing.
No freeze question. The name is the ending.)
