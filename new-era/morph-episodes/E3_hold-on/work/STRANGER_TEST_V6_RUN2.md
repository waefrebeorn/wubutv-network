## Q4 — The gossip retell

### Retell (exactly two sentences)

Laura is running some kind of hands-up checkpoint count, finds a container empty after nine went in, and refuses to call anyone a thief on empty alone. She then turns on Peach, who is begging to be next and swearing she held her bouquet with both hands all morning, accuses her of only pointing, finds the nine with her flowers, says three of them are lost down a drain, and still won't put Peach's name to it because she says that's the street's decision, not hers.

### (a) Facts my retell NEEDED that are not actually in the dialogue

- That this is a checkpoint / inspection procedure at all — the dialogue gives orders (count, hands up) but never says what the procedure is or why it exists.
- Who Ansel, the Courier, the Watcher, and Lime are, and whether Laura is the "Watcher" Peach addresses in line 4 — none of these roles or identities are explained.
- What "the nine" are (coins, objects, people?) and whose container/tin was found empty in lines 1–2.
- What a "bell" is as a unit (of time? a signal?) and why a bell would "count hands."
- That the nine in line 11 ("There's your nine") are the same nine that "went in" in line 1, and that they were with Peach's flowers — line 10–11 imply a location but never state where they were found.
- That "three of them in the drain" (line 13) refers to three of the nine, and how Laura knows they are there and gone.
- What "your name" in line 14 means — naming Peach as the thief, reporting her, registering her? The dialogue never says what putting a name to it would do.
- Why "the street" gets to make the call in line 15, and what call the street would make.

### (b) Things I am unsure I got right

- Whether Peach actually pointed instead of holding, or whether Laura is merely accusing her of it — line 7 and line 12 assert it, Peach never confirms or denies it after line 6.
- Who "keep 'em up" (line 8) is addressed to — the Courier from line 3, Peach, or everyone.
- Whether the empty container in line 2 belongs to Ansel, the Courier, or someone else entirely.
- Whether Peach and Lime are two people presenting together, and where Lime is or why Lime never speaks.
- Whether Laura found the nine in Peach's bouquet, in a tin of Peach's, or somewhere else she was pointing.
- Whether "that's the street's call" (line 15) is a refusal, a deferral, or a threat that the street will name Peach even if Laura won't.
