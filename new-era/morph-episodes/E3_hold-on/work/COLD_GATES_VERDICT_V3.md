## GATE 1 — HEAR-BEFORE-IT-MOVES (cold read-aloud)

Each line judged on first hearing, at performance pace, with no staging knowledge.

- **L1 — FLAG.** "Write Ansel's words:" trips. Spoken aloud this is a dictation heading, not a sentence a person says, and the addressee is unnamed (L3 names "Watcher"; L1 does not). The listener must stop and work out who is being told to write, and whether Laura is quoting Ansel or asserting the claim herself. The second half — "the Courier held his tin. His coins are gone." — lands only after that re-parse.
- **L2 — PASS.** "I write one true line today, or none." Lands immediately as a vow/standard.
- **L3 — FLAG.** "Write us next" trips: "write us" does not land on first hearing as "inscribe our claim next." "a couple" also trips — the speaker is one person, THE PEACH, and on first hearing the listener cannot say who the second member of the couple is; no line names a partner.
- **L4 — PASS (in immediate context).** "Both hands stayed on it." Heard straight after L3, "it" reads as the bouquet and the claim (hands never left it) lands, though "both hands" sits oddly against a "couple" holding (see Gate 2, Q1).
- **L5 — PASS.** "At the bell, what's held stays held. The Courier's hands are open." Rule plus application; lands first time.
- **L6 — PASS.** "The Courier held that tin safe through four hundred bells." Lands first time as a defence claim.
- **L7 — FLAG.** "The Peach's bouquet hangs too heavy for flowers." The comparative is bent: on first hearing "too heavy for flowers" parses as too heavy *for the flowers* (i.e. it would crush them), and the intended sense — too heavy to be only flowers — needs a second parse.
- **L8 — FLAG (worst line in the script).** "Held coins stay coins. Her coins turned to caps." Two trips. (1) "Her" has no clear antecedent on first hearing — the women in play are Laura, the Courier and the Peach, and the line does not say whose coins these are. (2) "turned to caps" lands as a literal transformation that the first sentence appears to contradict ("held coins stay coins" — so were these held or not?), and nothing heard so far explains coins becoming bottle caps. A viewer hearing this once cannot say what it means.
- **L9 — FLAG.** "No fist held them at the bell." "Them" has no stable referent on first hearing (the coins of L8's first sentence, or the caps of its second?), and "fist" is a new word for the thing every earlier line called "hands" — the listener must convert fist→hands and guess the referent before the ruling lands.
- **L10 — FLAG.** "One true line today. The Peach's name waits on the street's vote." The first half is a fragment that only works as an echo of L2. The second half introduces an entirely new mechanism at the last moment: a "street's vote" has never been mentioned, and "the Peach's name waits" does not say what the vote decides — whether her name is written, cleared, or condemned. It does not land as sense on first hearing.

### GATE 1 VERDICT: **FAIL**
Lines L1, L3, L7, L8, L9 and L10 stumble on first hearing. L8 and L10 are the decisive failures: L8 ("Her coins turned to caps") and L10 ("The Peach's name waits on the street's vote") sound like nonsense spoken — a first-time viewer cannot say what either means.
