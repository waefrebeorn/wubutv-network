# BANTER DRAFT — HOLD ON E3 Part 1, SCRIPT v6 (re-dialogue room, 2026-11-18)

Method: DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.1 as amended (A-10). Each unit's
scene is played as ONE CONTINUOUS TWO-VOICE TRANSCRIPT — Laura and
the Peach in role, in order, interruptions / wrong answers /
overlaps in place. The silent cast (Ansel, the Courier, the
Watcher, the Lime) act in stage directions; they have no banter
lines, because they have no lines. Proof object per unit was
assigned BEFORE the scene was played (A-2):

  U1 → the tin · U2 → the bouquet · U3 → the tin ·
  U4 → the bouquet · U5 → the bouquet · U6 → the coins

Harvested lines are marked ▸ with their locked-script ID; the
transcript line number is recorded in BRIEF.md's line ledger
(Pass 3.2). A locked line with no banter origin is presumed a
ledger line — there are none in v6.

## U1 — the slam and the count (proof object: the tin)

[Morning at the wall. The queue is already moving. Ansel crosses
from his stall with the tin in his hand.]

TU1-01  [The tin SLAMS onto the stone counter and spins,
        clattering. Ansel's finger is out at the Courier before
        it stops.]
TU1-02  LAURA: Ansel— Ansel. Down easy, it's— no. Too late.
TU1-03  LAURA: Yeah, I see her. I see the hands too. Hands are
        empty, Ansel.
TU1-04  LAURA: [tipping the tin over the counter] Nine in it
        yesterday, wasn't it. Nine of yours.
        ▸ U1 L1 (compressed at harvest; the count is Ansel's
          answer, made with the tin)
TU1-05  LAURA: Count it with me. …That's the count. Nothing.
TU1-06  [Down the street at the stall, unremarked: the Peach
        presses her bouquet closed over a glint of coins inside
        it. The Lime holds the stem with her.]
TU1-07  LAURA: [to Ansel, whose finger is still talking] No —
        no. I'm not chalking 'thief' because you slammed a tin.
        That's not how my wall works.
        ▸ U1 L2 (compressed at harvest)
TU1-08  LAURA: You want a line, we do it proper. [to the
        Courier] Hands, love. Up where everyone can look at
        them.
        ▸ U1 L3 (compressed at harvest; "Bell's watching" is
          TU1-09, folded in)
TU1-09  LAURA: Bell's watching, same as us.

## U2 — the claim (proof object: the bouquet)

[The Courier tips the tin over her open palm — nothing falls.
Ansel presses a coin into the Watcher's palm; the Watcher
pockets it without looking down and turns back to the wall.]

TU2-01  THE PEACH: [stepping out of the queue, bouquet high]
        Us! Us next!
TU2-02  LAURA: [not looking up from the wall] The theft's
        still open, Peach.
TU2-03  THE PEACH: The theft, the theft — we've been standing
        here since— Watcher. Watcher, look at me. Us next.
        ▸ U2 L1 (harvested: "Us next, Watcher.")
TU2-04  THE PEACH: Peach and Lime. A couple. Holding this
        bouquet since dawn, since before dawn—
        ▸ U2 L2 (harvested whole, minus the "before dawn"
          overreach — she corrects herself down to the lie she
          can carry)
TU2-05  LAURA: Since dawn.
TU2-06  THE PEACH: Both hands! His hands, my hands, all the
        hands, all morning. Ask the street, the street saw.
        ▸ U2 L3 (compressed at harvest: "Both hands on it.
          All morning.")
TU2-07  THE PEACH: [to the Lime] Smile. Hold it up. Up.

## U3 — the offered hands (proof object: the tin)

[REACTION — GAP 1: two seconds held on the empty tin in the
Courier's open hands, bell ticking, no words. The street has
changed in exactly one detail: the stall awning's stripe is
cobalt where it was bone. The Courier sets the tin down open
on the counter and holds both hands up, turned over once.]

TU3-01  [silence — the tin, the raised hands, the ticking]
TU3-02  THE PEACH: [pointing, laughing] Look at her! Look at
        the hands, like she's holding the sky up—
TU3-03  LAURA: [to the Peach, not loud] Point while you can,
        love.
        ▸ U3 L1, first half
TU3-04  LAURA: Bell's coming for every hand on this street.
        Loose ones lose. That's the whole of it.
        ▸ U3 L1, second half (harvested: "— loose hands lose

TU3-05  THE PEACH: [still grinning] She's not holding

TU3-06  LAURA: [to the Courier] Keep 'em up. Right there.

        ▸ U3 L2 (harvested: "Keep 'em up.")

## U4 — the naming (proof object: the bouquet)

[Ansel jabs a finger at the tin, then at the Courier's raised
hands. The Peach holds her bouquet out toward the wall; it
hangs low, its stem bending.]

TU4-01  LAURA: [stepping between Ansel and the Courier]
        Enough pointing, Ansel. Enough.
TU4-02  LAURA: Four hundred bells. Say it with me — four
        hundred bells that tin's come home safe in her fist.
        ▸ U4 L1 (harvested: "Four hundred bells that tin came
          home safe.")
TU4-03  THE PEACH: [bouquet out] We're still waiting! A
        couple, holding—
TU4-04  LAURA: [turning, eyes on the bouquet] Are you. Then
        tell me something, Peach.
TU4-05  LAURA: Flowers don't pull a stem down like that.
        What's in yours — besides the flowers?
        ▸ U4 L2 (harvested: "What's in yours, Peach — besides
          flowers?")
TU4-06  THE PEACH: [grinning wider] Holding! We're holding
        it!

## U5 — the bell (proof object: the bouquet)

[REACTION — GAP 2: two seconds held on the Peach's face, the
bouquet low in frame, bell ticking, no words.]

TU5-01  [silence — her face, the bouquet, the ticking]
TU5-02  [The noon bell cracks. The Peach points at the
        Courier and laughs; the Lime copies her with both
        hands and lets go of the bouquet entirely. It drops:
        nine coins ring out across the cobbles, three roll
        into the storm drain at the counter's base. The
        Courier's raised hands do not move. The Peach grabs
        at the coins, too late.]
TU5-03  THE PEACH: [grabbing] No — no no no, those are—
        hold on, those—
TU5-04  LAURA: [quiet, to Ansel, nodding at the cobbles]
        Count them, Ansel. There's your nine.
        ▸ U5 L1 (harvested: "There's your nine.")
TU5-05  LAURA: [to the Peach] The bell counts hands, Peach.
        It counted yours. Pointing.
        ▸ U5 L2 (harvested: "Bell counts hands, Peach. Yours
          pointed.")
TU5-06  LAURA: And three of them went in the drain. In.
        Those don't come back, not for anyone.
        ▸ U5 L3 (compressed at harvest: "Three in the drain.
          Gone.")

## U6 — the strike (proof object: the coins)

[The Watcher steps to the wall and strikes through the
morning's docket line with one stroke of his chalk. The
Courier lowers her hands. He slides Ansel's fee coin back
across the counter, gathers the six coins from the cobbles
and puts them into Ansel's palm. Ansel looks at the Courier
and nods, once. Laura chalks the true line beneath the
struck one — HANDS OPEN. COINS LOOSE. (post-composited) —
and her chalk hovers over the blank space below it.]

TU6-01  [deeds, no words — the strike, the fee sliding, six
        coins counted into a palm]
TU6-02  THE PEACH: [watching the hovering chalk] That's—
        write it. Go on. Write it, it's my—
TU6-03  LAURA: [chalk still hovering] Not yet, Peach.
        ▸ U6 L1 (harvested whole)
TU6-04  THE PEACH: But the wall—
TU6-05  LAURA: The street hasn't had its say. When it does,
        I'll chalk it. Till then it stays blank.
        ▸ U6 L2 (harvested: "The street hasn't had its say.")
TU6-06  [REACTION — GAP 3: the chalk hovers; nobody moves;
        the picture freezes for the final 2–3 seconds. The
        freeze carries the viewer vote as a post-composited
        question: SHOULD LAURA WRITE THE PEACH'S NAME?]

## REPAIR PASS — after STRANGER TEST RUN 1 (FAIL: 4 STAGE lines; three doubly-orphaned exchanges)

The Stranger Test's first cold reader (dialogue only) failed
the draft above: the bell rule was stated as a maxim TWICE
(U3's warning and U5's verdict — "recites rulebook at him…
again"), the record line arrived unprompted, the cost line
read as three MORE coins, and the U6 refusal answered a
request that exists only in the pictures. Per the method,
the repair is here, at Pass 3.1 — the exchanges re-played,
not line-polished. The re-played beats:

TU-R1 (U1, re-played): the count has to ANSWER before the
  refusal can land — the refusal now reports the count first.
  LAURA: [tipping the tin] Nine went in, Ansel. Count what's
  left. … [the count happens — nothing] Empty. And 'thief'
  needs more than empty. [to the Courier] Hands up, Courier.
  ▸ U1 L1 (unchanged in substance), U1 L2 NEW, U1 L3
    (the bell's first naming moves to U3; the test is set
     here as a plain instruction)

TU-R3 (U3, re-played): the warning stops being a proverb and
  becomes a taunt about the exact thing she is doing.
  THE PEACH: [pointing, laughing] Look at her! Look at the
  hands, like she's holding the sky up—
  LAURA: Go on, point, Peach — pointing's not holding.
  ▸ U3 L1 NEW (the rule's substance in plain words: what
    she is doing is not the thing she swore to)

TU-R4 (U4, re-played): the record is no longer read to the
  room — it is thrown at the rival claimant, answering her
  sworn morning with the Courier's four hundred bells.
  LAURA: [turning on the Peach] Peach — that tin's come
  home safe four hundred bells. So what's in yours —
  besides flowers?
  ▸ U4 L1 NEW (target: the Peach; the number is the payload
    and lands last), U4 L2 (hinge "So" added at harvest)

TU-R5 (U5, harvest correction): the cost line is made
  partitive so the three are heard as OF the nine.
  LAURA: Three of them in the drain. Gone.
  ▸ U5 L3 (wording at harvest from TU5-06)

TU-R6 (U6, re-played): the refusal now speaks the request it
  refuses — Laura echoes the Peach's pictured grab for the
  name before she refuses it, and the deferral is a plain
  fact about whose call it is, not an aphorism.
  THE PEACH: [watching the hovering chalk] That's— write
  it. Go on. Write it, it's my—
  LAURA: Your name? Not yet, Peach. That's the street's
  call. Not mine.
  ▸ U6 L1 NEW, U6 L2 NEW

Unchanged by the repair: all of U2; U3 L2; U5 L1; U5 L2
(the verdict — now the rule's ONLY maxim-form naming,
arriving after the event has taught it, as a judgment on
one person's hands). The repaired script is re-gated cold
by fresh readers (RUN 2) on all three gates.

## REPAIR ROUND 2 — re-bantered beats (TU-R2), after RUN 2

RUN 2 evidence (fresh cold readers, repaired text): hear-before
PASS (0 stumbles); Gate E 11/12 — item 4 FAIL: the name
deferral names no future event, so the settlement audit

person, the U4 record recited, the U5 verdict maxim flagged

calls her Peach") and 9 FILED pairs with three doubly
orphaned exchanges. Repair at Pass 3.1 again — the flagged
beats re-played below; nothing annotated toward the test.

TU-R2.1 (U1, the refusal re-played): Ansel jabs at the
Courier and wants the word up. Laura has just counted the
tin empty in front of him. Played flat, the refusal comes
out first-person — not a principle about proof, a clerk
declining an act: "Empty. I'm not chalking 'thief' off
that." The count is answered ("Empty"), the demand refused
in the same breath, and the word she won't write sits
inside the refusal where the street can hear it.

TU-R2.2 (U2, the claim re-played): asked who's "us", the
Peach names her couple the way people name themselves —
first person, object in hand: "Us two have held this
bouquet since dawn." Then the topper stands: "Both hands
on it. All morning."

TU-R2.3 (U4, the clash re-played as one breath): Laura
turns on the Peach and the record and the question come
out together — the record as the unbroken run, the count
itself left on the wall's tallies where it belongs:
"That tin's come home safe every bell till today, Peach —

the proof and the accusation it entitles.

TU-R2.4 (U5, the verdict re-played): the maxim is retired.
Two cold readers, independently, heard "Bell counts hands"
as the rulebook recited; a clerk giving a finding says
what happened: "Bell rang, Peach. Yours pointed." Two

in the U3 taunt, in this finding, and in the pictures —

TU-R2.5 (U6, the refusal and deferral re-played): the
Peach reaches at the board's blank space — write it.
Laura's refusal names the thing refused: "Your name on
the board? Not yet, Peach." And the deferral names its
appointment, answering the Gate E settlement failure in
dialogue rather than annotation: the name is decided at a
named future event the episode has already posted on its
own board — "Tomorrow's docket decides — the street's
call." The docket is the occasion; the street is the
decider; the vote (Part 2) is the mechanism, as designed.

## REPAIR ROUND 3 — re-bantered beats (TU-R3), after RUN 3

RUN 3 evidence: Stranger FAIL for the third time, but the
flags have moved onto the repairs themselves — the record
flagged in its third spoken form ("Peach already knows
tin's history"), the event-form finding flagged ("Peach
already heard bell ring"), the echo-refusal flagged ("she
answers her own question"), the named appointment flagged
("nobody recites docket procedure aloud"); FILED orphans at
the two Courier orders and the verdict chain. Three runs of
evidence now say the same thing from three directions: the
record is ARCHIVE — any spoken form of it reads as
recitation to a stranger. Round 3 therefore changes WHERE
facts live, not just how lines sound.

TU-R3.1 (U1, the test set re-played): the refusal clips to
chair shorthand — "Empty. Not chalking 'thief' off that." —
and the order to the Courier takes up the count's own word,
so the turn between parties is spoken, not just pictured:
"Hands up, Courier — show me empty." The test is now the
refusal's consequence in words: his tin showed empty; her
hands will show empty.

TU-R3.2 (U3, the settle re-played): after the taunt at the
Peach's hands, the instruction to the Courier is spoken as
the contrast it is: "Courier — yours stay up." Her hands
are the counter-fact to the pointing; the line says so in
the scene's own key word (yours/hers hands), payload "up"
last, as the order has always landed.

TU-R3.3 (U4, the clash re-played WITHOUT the record): the
record leaves the dialogue. In its place, present evidence
— the fact standing in the frame, not in the archive:
"Her hands stayed up, Peach — so what's in yours, besides
flowers?" The Courier's record itself moves to the wall:
row on row of tally marks beside the docket line, one for
every bell the tin came home, where the street can count
them without anyone reciting them. Laura still acts on
the record — it is why she steps in — but nobody says it.

TU-R3.4 (U5, the finding re-played at minimum): the bell
clause goes too — the bell is in the picture and in
everyone's ears; the finding is the one fact the Peach
cannot have watched herself do: "Peach — yours pointed."
Three words, payload last. The count and the cost stand
as they were.

TU-R3.5 (U6, the refusal and deferral re-played plain):
no question echo — a flat refusal with its object named:
"Not yet, Peach. Not on the board." And the deferral in
the plainest words that still name the appointment: the
decider and the day, no procedure noun — "It goes to the
street tomorrow." The docket itself stays where the
episode posts it: on the board, in the end card, in
picture.
