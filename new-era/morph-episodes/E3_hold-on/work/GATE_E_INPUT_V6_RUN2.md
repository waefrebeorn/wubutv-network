# GATE INPUT — HOLD ON E3 Part 1 (cold gates, v6 — repaired text, RUN 2)

## Transcript (spoken order)

[U1]
LAURA: Nine went in, Ansel. Count what's left.
LAURA: Empty. And 'thief' needs more than empty.
LAURA: Hands up, Courier.

[U2]
THE PEACH: Us next, Watcher.
THE PEACH: Peach and Lime — holding this bouquet since dawn.
THE PEACH: Both hands on it. All morning.

[U3]
[Silence: two seconds held on the empty tin in the Courier's open hands, a bell ticking, no words.]
LAURA: Go on, point, Peach — pointing's not holding.
LAURA: Keep 'em up.

[U4]
LAURA: Peach — that tin's come home safe four hundred bells.
LAURA: So what's in yours — besides flowers?

[U5]
[Silence: two seconds held on the Peach's face, the bouquet low in frame, a bell ticking, no words.]
LAURA: There's your nine.
LAURA: Bell counts hands, Peach. Yours pointed.
LAURA: Three of them in the drain. Gone.

[U6]
LAURA: Your name? Not yet, Peach.
LAURA: That's the street's call. Not mine.
[Silence: the final two to three seconds freeze on the hovering chalk, no words. A question appears on the frozen picture: SHOULD LAURA WRITE THE PEACH'S NAME?]

## Pictures (one line per unit)

[U1] Ansel slams his tin onto the wall's stone counter and it spins; he points at the Courier, whose hands are open and empty; Laura tips the tin over the counter and nothing is in it; the Watcher, keeper of the wall's word, stands at a tall brick wall with his chalk; down the street, the Peach presses her bouquet closed over a glint of coins hidden inside it; Laura chalks on the wall.
[U2] The Courier tips the tin over her open palm and nothing falls out; Ansel presses a coin into the Watcher's palm to have his line heard first, and the Watcher pockets it without looking down and turns back to the wall; the Peach steps forward holding a bouquet high in both hands, grinning.
[U3] The bell has rung in the reset and one detail of the street has changed: the stall awning's stripe is cobalt now where it was bone; the Courier stands at the wall with the tin set down open on the counter and holds both hands up, open, turned over once, offering them to the bell to clear her name; the Peach points at her and laughs while the Lime copies her, letting go of the bouquet with one hand to point too, and the bouquet sags.
[U4] Ansel jabs a finger at the tin, then at the Courier's open hands, still raised from her offer to the bell; the Peach still holds her bouquet out toward the wall, waiting for her line, and it hangs low, its stem bending; Laura steps between Ansel and the Courier and turns on the Peach.
[U5] A bell rings; the Peach points at the Courier and laughs, and the Lime copies her with both hands and lets go of the bouquet entirely; it drops, spilling nine coins across the ground, three of them rolling into the storm drain at the counter's base; the Courier's hands are still raised, as they have been since she offered them; the Peach grabs at the coins with both hands.
[U6] The Watcher strikes through a chalk line on the wall — his wall keeps only what's true — slides Ansel's fee coin back across the counter to him, and puts six coins into Ansel's palm; Ansel nods at the Courier; Laura chalks a new line on the wall — HANDS OPEN. COINS LOOSE. — and her chalk hovers over a blank space below it while the Peach watches it, reaching for the moment; the Peach and the Lime stand over their dropped bouquet, nothing written for either of them; the picture freezes.
