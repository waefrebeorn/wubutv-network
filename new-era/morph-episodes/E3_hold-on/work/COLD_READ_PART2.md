# COLD READ — HOLD ON E3 Part 2 (dialogue only)

Reader: independent cold reader. Source: the 14 MARTA:/LAURA: lines and the
U1–U6 unit headers only. No picture descriptions, no case summary, no other
files read. Premise given: a town whose chalk wall is its public record; a
bell every ten seconds resets the world in one detail; what you hold at the
bell stays, what is loose changes.

## 1. RETELL

Marta wants one thing: the wall to say she paid her rent. She asks Laura to
write it. Yesterday Marta's coin went into the pocket of the man who writes
the names — the Watcher — but the wall still says she owes the rent. Laura
backs Marta's claim with a proof: every coin Marta earns is cut with a
notch, and the coin sitting in the Watcher's pocket has her notch on it.
Marta says she has now paid him twice, and he writes names on the wall for
everyone except her. Laura turns on the Watcher: his hand has been in his
pocket all morning, and yesterday the landlord's boy also paid two coins
into that same pocket. Laura orders him to bring the hand out. He does not
answer in words. The bell rings — the moment when what you are holding is
fixed and seen — and Marta calls it: her coin is out in the light, his is
still hiding. End state: Laura gets Marta's name up on the wall anyway, and
announces that tonight the street will vote on the Watcher's own name —
the man who decides what the wall says is now the one being judged.

Who wanted what: Marta wanted her payment on the record. The Watcher
blocked her by taking her coin and simply not writing. What changed: her
name is up despite him, and his name is now the one at risk.

Where I had to guess (the story never fully stops making sense, but three
things are inferred, not told):

- **Who writes?** Marta asks *Laura* to write (U1), but says "*He* writes
  for everyone but me" (U3). By U6 Laura says "Your name's up," so Laura
  clearly can get a name onto the wall — but the dialogue never states
  what Laura's authority at the wall is, or how she can override him.
  I guessed she is his deputy or the keeper of the record. Inferable,
  not stated.
- **The hand never comes out — I think.** U4 ends on Laura's order
  "Bring it out." No later line says he obeyed or refused. From U5's
  "Yours is hiding" I inferred the hand stayed in the pocket through
  the bell. The confrontation's immediate outcome is skipped; the
  story jumps to the consequence.
- **Why did the landlord's boy pay two coins?** The line lands as a
  reveal, but the motive is never spoken. I guessed bribery — the
  landlord profits if the wall keeps saying Marta owes. A cold viewer
  gets "someone else paid him too" and must supply the reason.

Also noted: "Paid him twice now" (U3) — the second payment is never
shown in the dialogue; I took it as "I have paid twice and still have
nothing," which reads fine as grievance.

## 3. EAR TEST

- **FLAGGED — U5 L2: "My coin's in the light. Yours is hiding."** The
  neatest line in the script, and the one that sounds most written.
  The balanced antithesis (light / hiding) reads like a slogan or a
  poster line; a person mid-argument more likely says "Mine's right
  here — where's yours?" It still performs an accusation, but it is
  the only line I heard an author inside of.
- **Borderline — U5 L1: "Bell rang, Watcher."** An announcement of an
  event rather than words aimed at anyone's want. It survives because
  in this world it works like "Time's up" — a challenge — but it is
  one step from narration.
- **Borderline — U6 L2: "The street votes on his name tonight."** Has
  a cliffhanger/trailer flavour — a pronouncement to the audience as
  much as to him. It does declare a real consequence, so it passes,
  but it is pitched at the house.

No line is nonsense. Nothing needs the picture to be understood as
speech.

## 4. QUOTE-BACK

Quoted from memory after one read:

> "My coin's in the light. Yours is hiding."

Standout character: **Marta.** Her signature move: she cuts a notch
into every coin she earns, so her own money can testify for her — she
doesn't argue that she paid, she makes the coin in his pocket say it.

## 5. JOINTS

- **U1 → U2: THEREFORE.** Marta accuses and demands; therefore Laura
  answers with the proof that the coin in his pocket is Marta's.
- **U2 → U3: BUT.** The coin is proven hers — but Marta has paid
  twice and he still writes for everyone but her. Proof exists; the
  wrong continues.
- **U3 → U4: THEREFORE.** Marta names the refusal; therefore Laura
  stops arguing Marta's case and confronts him directly — the hand,
  the second payment, the order.
- **U4 → U5: BUT.** Laura orders the hand out — but instead of
  compliance, the bell rings with the hand still buried. The test
  arrives before the obedience does.
- **U5 → U6: THEREFORE.** At the bell his coin stays hidden, which
  settles it; therefore Laura puts Marta's name up herself and puts
  his name to the street.

**"And then" joints: none.** Every unit is caused by the one before
it, by therefore or by but.

## VERDICT: PASS

The whole story — want, block, proof, confrontation, outcome — is
retellable from the dialogue alone, all 14 lines perform an action,
and all five joints are causal. Flags for the record, none of them
fatal: (1) Laura's authority to write on the wall is never stated
in dialogue, only implied by the ending; (2) whether the Watcher
ever brings his hand out is implied, never spoken; (3) one line —
"My coin's in the light. Yours is hiding." — sounds crafted for
the poster rather than spoken in an argument.
