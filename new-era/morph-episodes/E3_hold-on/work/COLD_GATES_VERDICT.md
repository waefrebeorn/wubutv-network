## GATE 1 — HEAR-BEFORE-IT-MOVES (cold read-aloud, first hearing)

- **L1 — STUMBLE.** "the Holder" and "coins gone" trip the ear. "Holder" is a capitalised role heard before it is explained — held what, for whom? "Write it: the Holder held his tin, coins gone" compresses an accusation, a container and a loss into one fragment; on first hearing I cannot say whose tin it is or who is being accused.
- **L2 — LANDS.** "Today I write what's true. Not what's shouted." Plain spoken sense on first hearing.
- **L3 — STUMBLE.** "Write us coupled next" trips on "coupled" and "next". Coupled to what, next in what list/order? The key demand of the scene is carried by an undefined word. "We held all morning" is clear in isolation, but what was held is not said.
- **L4 — STUMBLE.** "Both hands, every bell" is two fragments that need re-parsing: both hands on what, and is a "bell" a time, an event, or a test? "Ask anyone down this street" lands, but it does not repair the fragments before it.
- **L5 — STUMBLE.** "Her hands stay open" — the pronoun "Her" has no safe antecedent on hearing. The immediately preceding speaker was THE PEACH talking about "us" and "Both hands", so the ear first attaches "Her" to the Peach, while the pictures point to the Courier. A viewer hearing it once cannot reliably say whose hands are meant.
- **L6 — STUMBLE, worst line.** "Four hundred bells that tin crossed, inside her fist" does not parse as a spoken image on first hearing: a tin crossing bells, inside a fist. The number, the undefined "bells", the tin, and "her fist" collide. I had to re-read to guess it means the tin was carried for a long time by someone.
- **L7 — LANDS.** "Her bouquet hangs too heavy for flowers, Watcher" is the one concrete clue that lands spoken: something heavier than flowers is in the bouquet.
- **L8 — STUMBLE.** "Those coins crossed no bell in any fist" is inverted, abstract negation. "Those coins" — distinct from the tin's coins in L6? — and "crossed no bell in any fist" again depends on the undefined "bell" and on the crossing image that already failed in L6.
- **L9 — STUMBLE.** "Held things stay where they're held" lands as an aphorism, but "Hers fell" trips: hers *what* fell — hands, bouquet, coins, claim? The decisive verdict is carried by an ambiguous pronoun and an unnamed object.
- **L10 — STUMBLE.** "The wall takes her name when the street says write it" — "her" is again unanchored, "the street says" is a personification whose meaning (who decides, and how?) is not audible, and the line sounds like a condition, not an outcome. On first hearing I cannot say whether a name is being written, refused, or postponed.
