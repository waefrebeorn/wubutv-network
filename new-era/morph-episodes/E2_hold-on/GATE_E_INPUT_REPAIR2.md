WATCHER: Then: one ring, both hands, at the bell.
COURIER: Both hands? I want it believed, no wall.

WATCHER: 'Believed'? I want couples written — believed isn't.
PEACH AND LIME: Couples? Write us first.
WATCHER: First? First written keeps the room.
COURIER: Keeps the room? Drop it — my backed couples held.
[U2 picture: the keeper taps the ring on the woman's palm and points down the queue at the peach and lime — the same test for every pair. JUNE: Dropped all — opening. Bags inside till noon.
COURIER: Bell-snap startles fingers. Lock thumbs.
[U3 picture: the bride's bouquet lies on the ground and her hands are bare; the woman folds the couple's thumbs over each other's wrists; at a third bell the whole queue freezes holding its proofs — a ladder upright, a fish tank, a frog in a morning suit with a tiny ring box — and only the bride's hand moves, her bare fingers flying open at the bell's snap; the peach and lime stay frozen with both hands on their stem.]
JUNE: Loaded, bare, opened — thumbs next: write us married.
WATCHER: 'Next'? By noon or rooms empty — first written wins.
COURIER: Rooms empty? Stems don't count — who else can?
[U4 picture: through the doorway a porter sets the couple's two bags down outside the room door and steps away, emptying the unwritten couple's room as the keeper states the rule; the peach and lime glance from the bags to the door; Eli, the groom, crosses, carries both bags back inside the threshold, and stands in the doorway; at the wall the bride practises locking her thumbs over her own wrist, jaw set; the woman turns and speaks straight into the camera.]
COURIER: Hands off — believe theirs, wall and all.
WATCHER: I believe it. Wall writes married.

COURIER: Nobody holds your life for you — my couple held.
WATCHER: Nobody? Tomorrow's docket reads thefts.
