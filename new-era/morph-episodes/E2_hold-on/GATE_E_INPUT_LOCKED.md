WATCHER: Then hold at the bell. Both hands, one ring.
COURIER: I want that hold believed without the wall.

WATCHER: I want couples written. Not hired hands.
PEACH AND LIME: Write us coupled first.
WATCHER: One room. First written keeps it.
COURIER: Bouquet, veil, sleeve — drop them, be first.
[U2 picture: the bride attempts the hold at a second bell with her arms loaded — a bouquet wedged in one elbow, a veil over one forearm — and the load drags her hand open off the ring while the groom's hand stays flat; the peach and the lime keep both hands clamped on their shared bouquet stem, grinning.]
JUNE: Bouquet's down. Why's it still opening?
COURIER: The snap startles fingers. Lock thumbs.
[U3 picture: the bride's bouquet lies on the ground and her hands are bare; the woman folds the couple's thumbs over each other's wrists; at a third bell the whole queue freezes holding its proofs — a ladder upright, a fish tank, a frog in a morning suit with a tiny ring box — and only the bride's hand moves, her bare fingers flying open at the bell's snap; the peach and lime stay frozen with both hands on their stem.]
JUNE: Loaded, then bare, I opened — write us married, thumbs locked.
COURIER: Hold yours with both hands. Tell us — who else can?
[U4 picture: a porter sets the couple's two bags outside the ROOMS door and the peach and lime glance at the door; the groom carries both bags back inside the threshold and stands in the doorway; the bride practises locking her thumbs over her own wrist; the woman turns and speaks to camera.]

COURIER: Nobody holds your life for you.
WATCHER: Nobody? Next: the cook. Who brought his tin back empty?
