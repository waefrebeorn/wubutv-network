## 1. THE FOUR QUESTIONS

### Q1: Who wanted what? — **FAIL (speaker assessment impossible)**

- **An unnamed couple-voice wants to be written married in order to keep a room at noon.** Citation: "Write us married, or noon loses our room." The pictures identify the couple only visually: "a bride and groom slam both hands down onto a gold ring resting in a blue-coated woman's open palm as a bell rings" (U1 picture), and the wall result names them: "a fresh span, JUNE — ELI, draws on" (U1 picture); "the word MARRIED drawing on beneath it" under "JUNE — ELI" (U5 picture).
- **The same or another voice wants the hold itself believed, independently of the wall.** Citation: "I want that hold believed without the wall." No speaker is named for this line, and no other line repeats or answers it.
- **A second, distinct first-person voice wants a gatekeeping rule, not hired substitutes.** Citation: "I want couples written, not hired hands." The picture nearest to it shows only that "The chalk-handed older man steps in, wipes chalk dust from his fingers, and rules" (U2 picture) — "rules" is not a speech attribution, so this want cannot be credited to the older man on this input.
- **A rival claim to be written first exists in the words.** Citations: "Write us coupled first..." and "One room, first written keeps it." The pictures show a rival pair acting like claimants: the peach and lime are "both pointing at the wall" (U1 picture) and in U2 "The peach and lime push to the front of the queue, both hands clamped on their shared bouquet's stem, grinning hugely." But no transcript line is attributed to the peach or the lime, so their want is inferred from an unattributed line plus behaviour, not stated by them.
- **A speaker in U4 issues instructions/challenges whose owner is unknown.** Citations: "Hold yours with both hands." and "Tell us, who else can?" The only speaking act described in the pictures is: "The blue-coated woman turns and speaks straight into the camera" (U4 picture). The input does not say which U4 line she speaks, so even this one visible speaker cannot be assigned a want.

Every speaker, assessed:

- **Speakers cannot be enumerated from this input.** The transcript is six blocks of unattributed text (U1–U6). There are at least two different first-person wants in U1–U2 ("I want that hold believed without the wall." / "I want couples written, not hired hands."), plus plural claimants ("Write us married..." / "Write us coupled first..."), plus later voices ("Tell us, who else can?" / "Who brought his tin back empty?"). The input never maps any of them to the bride, groom, blue-coated woman, older man, peach, or lime.
- **Who does not speak (as far as the input shows):** the green-shirt woman is explicitly "A silent woman in a green work shirt" (U5 picture). No speaking is described for the groom individually, the man with a ladder, the woman holding a glass fish tank, the frog in a morning suit, the porter, or the cook; the peach and lime are shown in U6 "arguing and pointing at each other" (U6 picture), which is behaviour, not a transcribed line. The bride is never individually described as speaking either.

**Result: FAIL.** A cold viewer can recover *that* a couple wants marriage-writing and a room; they cannot do what Q1 requires — say *who* (every speaker) wanted what.

### Q2: What blocked them? — PASS on the central block, with one unexplained rival block (see §5a)

### Q4: Why does it matter — what is concretely won or lost? Is every claim settled inside the episode? — **FAIL (unsettled claims)**

Concretely won/lost, where supported:

- **Won by June–Eli: the written status and the room.** Citations: "MARRIED standing finished beneath JUNE — ELI on the wall" (U6 picture); "puts it in the bride's hand" [the brass room key] (U6 picture); "One room, first written keeps it."
- **Lost by the peach and lime: any writing at all.** Citation: "nothing written for them" (U6 picture), with their bouquet "lying dropped on the ground" (U6 picture).

Claims **not** settled inside the episode:

1. **"I want that hold believed without the wall."** Never settled — the outcome is in fact delivered *by* the wall: "the older man chalks on the wall in long strokes" (U5 picture) and "MARRIED standing finished beneath JUNE — ELI on the wall" (U6 picture). Nobody states that the hold was believed without the wall; the wall is the mechanism of the win.

3. **"Who brought his tin back empty?"** (U6). Raised in the final unit about the cook — U6 picture: "a cook holding up an empty tin" — and the episode ends on a freeze ("The picture freezes: the key in the bride's hand, the cook's empty tin raised," U6 picture). No answer, no outcome.

**Result: FAIL** — the central room/marriage claim is settled; at least three spoken claims/questions are not.

## 5. STORY SENSE

### (a) Causality — **FAIL**

Turns/beats that cannot be answered with "because the previous turn…":

1. **The rivals lose despite succeeding.** The peach and lime do exactly what the test requires: "The peach and lime keep both hands on their stem" (U2 picture); in U3 they are "frozen with both hands on their stem"; in U5 they are "still clutching their shared bouquet behind the counter." Yet U6: "nothing written for them," bouquet "lying dropped on the ground," "arguing and pointing at each other." No line or beat states a rule they broke, a moment they failed, or why June–Eli's later success outranks their earlier, sustained success under "first written keeps it." Their loss, and the dropping of their bouquet between U5 and U6, are uncaused.
2. **The porter reversal.** U4 picture: "a porter sets the couple's two bags down outside the room door and steps away" — because of what? No prior line evicts them or orders the bags out. The groom then "carries both bags back inside the threshold" with no stated consequence; the beat arrives and is undone without cause or effect.
3. **The green-shirt woman's chalk.** U5 picture: "A silent woman in a green work shirt stands at the wall's edge holding a stub of white chalk, then hands the chalk to the older man." Uncaused arrival; the older man has had chalk since U1 ("An older man with chalk dust on his hands stands at the blank brick wall, chalk raised"), so why he needs her chalk to write MARRIED is unstated.
4. **The ring's transfer to the bride's finger.** U5 picture has the ring "on her palm" [the blue-coated woman's]; U6 picture: "the ring is now on the bride's finger." No beat shows anyone placing it, choosing to, or earning that specific change.
5. **The street-edge door beat.** U1 picture: "At the street's edge a man knocks on a door and a woman opens it." No connection to any prior or later turn is stated.
6. **The cook.** U6 introduces "a cook holding up an empty tin" (U6 picture) and the question "Who brought his tin back empty?" with no prior setup — an uncaused arrival carrying an unexplained object and an unsettled accusation/question.
7. **Rule contradiction, unexplained.** U1 wants the hold "believed without the wall," yet every settlement (writing, key) flows from the wall and its keeper. No turn reconciles the stated desire with the mechanism that actually decides.
