## CHARACTER CARDS (Pass 2 — CH-1 complete before any dialogue)

### JUNE — guest accuser (archetype: the bride; does not return
within three episodes, Bible §4)
- **Function phrase (CH-4):** *the accuser who is the obstacle.*
- **Want (never changes):** to be believed when she says a
  thing is true. Episode form, spoken by her in U1: the wall
  writes that she's married, today.
- **Stake:** the wedding is *today* — cake, guests, the boat
  of a morning spent holding things across bells. † Gate E
  repair (concrete, named in her U1 line): if the line is
  not written by the noon bell, June and Eli lose their
  room. And from U4, the stake is physical: the ring
  itself, unheld at a bell, is gone — and the room with it.
- **Wound / wrong belief:** she believes a steady hand means
  a steady heart, so she reads Eli's stillness as coldness
  and her own flinch as the bell's fault. (The town's shared
  wrong belief — the wall can't be wrong — is hers too: she
  wants the wall's word because she trusts it more than her
  own eyes.)
- **Unbreakable rule:** she will not take a line she didn't
  earn in the hold. She never asks the Watcher to just write
  it, and never offers payment — the demand is always "write
  it because it's true," which is why U5's earned line lands.
- **Tell (CH-5, planted U1, fires before the U3 turn):** she
  clutches the bouquet in the hand that should be holding the
  ring — U1 picture (bouquet in her ring hand at the slap),
  U2 picture (the clutch tightens as the bell ticks). What
  she's holding instead is what gives her away.
- **Voice rule (CH-2):** speaks in demands and short lists;
  when caught, she over-specifies — times and counts nobody
  asked for (her denial in U3 carries "Twice. At noon.").
- **Signature line:** "Write us married." (her demand, U1 —
  the title in her own mouth.)
- **Deed (CH-3):** U5 — she puts the bouquet down herself and
  keeps her hand on the ring through the bell, flinch and all.
  The situation changes because her hand stays.

### THE COURIER — co-lead (even episode, Bible §4); the Holder
for hire; the ring is in HER custody when the episode opens
- **Function phrase:** *the witness who becomes the holder of
  the secret.*
- **Want (never changes):** to be believed without the wall —
  a held thing should speak for itself. Episode form, spoken
  by her in U1: one hold believed without walls.
- **Stake:** her record. "Four hundred bells. Never dropped
  one." is town gospel (canon F-002) and it is her whole
  livelihood. In U5 she stakes her hands on the couple's
  hold — she arranges it and holds the bouquet down; if
  it fails, it fails on her record ([GE-R2]: at the bell
  her hands are off the ring; the hold is the couple's
  two hands alone). In U6 the record
  becomes the price († repair): keeping the bell-flower
  costs her a KEPT strike chalked through her record tally
  on the wall, in public — the record survives as words and
  is marked as fact, because she is now holding something
  she cannot account for.
- **Wound / wrong belief:** she believes words are decoration
  and only held things tell the truth. The episode corrects
  her gently: it is her *words* — the taught count — that save
  the hold her hands alone couldn't.
- **Unbreakable rule:** she never drops, and she never holds
  what wasn't freely given. U6 breaks the second clause
  without her choosing it — the bell-flower persisted in her
  fist because her hand was closed on the couple's hands at
  the bell. The hanger is her rule meeting its exception.
- **Tell (planted U1, fires before her U3 naming):** she
  counts bells on her thumb, silently, one tap per tick —
  U1 picture during the flicker (she is watching hands, not
  faces, while she taps). The audience learns she *saw*

- **Voice rule:** short, deadpan; prices, counts, and teaches
  in imperatives. She never explains a feeling; she states a

- **Signature line (canon):** "Four hundred bells. Never
  dropped one."
- **Deeds:** U3 — she names the slip (speech) *and* teaches
  the count with her own fingers on June's (action). U5 —

## THE IRREVERSIBLE PAYOFF (F-2 — stated for the record)
**Want:** June's, spoken in U1 — the wall writes that she's
married. **Fought over:** three hold attempts across U2–U4,
against the Watcher's rule, his deadline, and June's own hand.
**Won, irreversibly, on screen, in U5:** the hold holds
through the bell and the Watcher chalks MARRIED himself; in U6
the line stands on the wall and the ring is on June's finger.
U6 does not revoke, qualify, or re-open it — it opens a
*different* debt, the Courier's, on a different character.
U1 world state (unmarried, ring in the Courier's custody,
bouquet all flowers) vs U6 world state (married on the wall,
ring worn, bouquet plain with one bell missing) differ in
picture, not only in knowledge.
