LAURA: Your log's ten beds, eleven names—
KEEPER: I want quiet: name off ends arguing.
LAURA: I want it for my brother, Elias.
LAURA: Without it, Elias sleeps in rain.
KEEPER: Door test: spoken and carried, same hands.
DISPATCHER: I want my log believed.
BLOB PRINCE: I want a dry bed.
UNC: I want it sold. Peter!
KEEPER: Spoken. Not carried. Bag outside.
LAURA: Carrying is not speaking, boss.
KEEPER: Carried it. He never spoke.
UNC: Eleven names, ten beds — Peter's, test or none.
KEEPER: Last rule: carrying hands hold no money.
LAURA: Elias — spoken, carried, same hands.
KEEPER: No held tag till ropes are tightened nightly.
DISPATCHER: Ten names, ten beds. Keeper's downstairs.
KEEPER: Staying after refusal earns dry floor.
KEEPER: Eleven names, ten beds — which bed's mine?

U1: The clerk strikes his own name off a board in red; a woman says the log lists ten beds but the board carries eleven names, claims the freed bed for her brother, says without it he sleeps in the rain, and the clerk states a test about names spoken and bags carried by the same hands.
U2: A woman clerk arrives with a log and wants it believed; a small damp blob creature wearing a bottle-cap crown arrives at the door, says he wants a dry bed, tries to carry his wet sack through the door himself, drops it outside the door, and the clerk refuses him with a shake of his head; beside the door a painted house sign states the house's floor rule — a refused claimant who stays earns the dry floor, no bed and no name; the owner then calls the name Peter at the door to sell the freed bed, but Peter's bag stays outside the door and the clerk rules the claim failed; the blob waits in the doorway's outer corner.
U3: The owner hauls Peter's bag inside the door himself, past the blob waiting in the doorway's outer corner; the woman says carrying is not speaking, and the clerk rules on her words — the bag was carried, but Peter never spoke.
U4: The owner strips the clerk's bed — blanket bundled, mattress rolled and shouldered toward the door — to ready it for Peter and says the count aloud — eleven names, ten beds; the clerk states a last rule that carrying hands hold no money.
U5: The woman carries her brother's bag through the door herself and speaks his name; the clerk says no held tag till the bed's ropes are tightened nightly and tightens them, the tag is hung, the count is entered as ten names and ten beds with the clerk sleeping downstairs, and the clerk states the house's floor rule — staying after refusal earns dry floor; through the doorway, under the house sign by the door, the blob who asked for a dry bed at the door and was refused by the test is still claiming it — he lifts his bottle-cap crown off and holds it toward the board and points from his wet corner at the bed as the held tag goes up, but he gets no bed.
U6: The clerk sleeps at his desk; on his way to the board he looks at Peter's bag still unclaimed inside the door — its man never came — then at the blob, who stayed in his wet corner all night after being refused, and he taps the painted house sign by the door and names its rule — staying after refusal earns the dry floor — as the rule he is applying because the blob stayed, and gives the refused claimant who stayed the house's floor place: he drags the worn doormat to the dry corner under the stair beside the door, where the eave's drip does not reach — dry floor, no bed and no name — and the blob rolls onto it, his split sack under his side, and sleeps there, settling for dry floor instead of the wet street; the clerk then takes the chalk himself, pauses over it, looks back at the desk he now sleeps at, and writes his own name fresh at the board's foot so he will be counted tomorrow, with no bed assigned to it; the brother's name stays on the board, held, with his bag on the made bed, as the clerk asks which bed is his.
