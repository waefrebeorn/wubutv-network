## Q1 — RETELL: FAIL (not coherent without outside knowledge)

### Retell in my own words

At night in a lodging house with ten narrow iron beds, the clerk (Keeper) has struck a name off the roster board in red chalk. Laura, holding her brother Elias's carpet bag, demands the name be put back on and wants a bed for Elias, warning that without it Elias sleeps wet in the rain. An older man (UNC) brings a bag in through the door; the Keeper carries it back outside. A damp, round blob creature with one huge eye and a slipping gold crown oozes in dragging a soaked sack it cannot lift, trailed by more round shapes in the wet dark, and asks for a dry bed. Laura says Peter never stood at the board. UNC offers money for Peter's bed; the Keeper pushes the envelope back and refuses it. Laura says Peter's hands carried but never spoke, so Peter fails the Keeper's door test — spoken, carried, same hands. Laura then speaks Elias's name at the door, carries his bag inside in her own hands, and tightens the ropes of the tenth bed; the Keeper re-makes the bed, hangs a HELD tag on its rail, and counts ten names for ten beds, leaving whoever is refused to the dry floor. In the final unit Elias is asleep on the held bed, the creature sleeps on a mat in the dry corner under the stair, a man in a wet tan overcoat arrives too late and turns away into the rain, and the Keeper writes his own name at the foot of the board as the eleventh name and asks which bed is his.

### What breaks coherence

The broad arc above is followable. The load-bearing links are not — a cold reader has to guess at every turning point:

1. **Who was struck, and who is "him"?** Laura opens: "You put him back on, Keeper. Eleven names—" — "him" is never named in U1, and the U1 picture says only that the Keeper "strikes through a name in red chalk at the foot" of the board. Only the U6 picture retroactively implies the struck name was Elias's ("ELIAS fresh at the foot over the remnant of the red strike"). The reader must wait until the last unit to resolve the first line.
2. **Who is UNC, and what is his relation to Peter?** UNC is never identified. He carries a bag in (U2 picture), says "Peter's bed — pays tonight," offers an envelope of money (U4 picture), and says "These hands weren't at that board." Is he Peter's agent, a buyer, a lodger, a stranger? The material never says. He speaks of "Peter's bed" as someone else's, so he is apparently not Peter — but that is an inference, not a statement.
3. **Who is Peter, and what happens to him?** Peter is named only, never appears, never speaks. His bag sits alone at the foot of the staircase (U3 picture); his bed is the tenth (UNC, U4); "Peter's never stood here, boss" (Laura, U3). Whether Peter is on or off the board at the end, and whether he loses his bed, is never stated.
4. **How does Elias get into the bed?** Laura carries Elias's bag and speaks his name (U5). Elias himself never speaks, is never shown arriving or entering, and is simply asleep on the held bed in U6, "wet from the rain." The arrival of the episode's central beneficiary is skipped entirely.
5. **The proxy problem.** Laura claims a bed for Elias although Elias himself does neither of the acts the test names. No line states that a proxy may claim for another person. (See Q4.)
6. **The man in the wet tan overcoat (U6 picture).** He appears only in the final wide shot, "stands in the rain looking in, too late, then turns away." He is unnamed, speaks no line, and "too late" for what is unstated. He could be Peter, UNC returned, or a twelfth person; the material gives no way to tell.
7. **The Keeper's own bed.** Nothing before the final line establishes that the Keeper has a bed, or gives one up. The U5 picture underlines the gap: the Keeper "turns toward a desk in the hall's corner — a chair, a lamp, no bed." So his closing question, "Eleven names, ten beds — which bed's mine?", has no established referent — it reads as a sacrifice the material never set up.

**Q1 verdict: FAIL.** An arc is visible, but the whole story — who is who, who was struck, how Elias arrives, who the last man is, what the Keeper loses — cannot be retold without guessing.

## Q3 — PAYOFF + COST: PAYOFF MET; COST ONLY PARTLY SHOWN

**Payoff — won on screen.** Laura's want (a bed for Elias) is contested across U1–U5: the name is struck, UNC bids money for the bed, the door test is invoked. It is won visibly: the Keeper re-makes the tenth bed and "hangs a tag reading HELD on its rail himself" while Laura tightens the ropes (U5 picture), and the U6 picture shows Elias "asleep on it" in the settled wide shot. For the night in question, that is irreversible as shown.

**Cost — shown for the contest losers, but not tied to the bed Elias takes.** The blob loses the bed it wanted: it "points at the bed, then lets its hand drop" (U5 picture) and ends sleeping on a mat under the stair (U6 picture). UNC's bid is refused: the Keeper "pushes the envelope back into the older man's chest" and UNC "clutches the refused envelope to his own chest, beaten" (U4 picture). Both losses are on screen. But the specific cost of Elias's win — *whose* bed he takes — is never tied to a shown person: "Peter's bed" is named (UNC, U4) yet Peter never appears and no line says he loses it; the Keeper's closing question hints that he pays, but no Keeper bed was ever established (U5 picture: desk corner, "no bed"). So the material shows people losing bids, but never shows the displaced bed-holder paying for Elias's win.

**Q3 verdict: the enumerated requirement (3) — a payoff won/lost irreversibly on screen — is MET** (Elias wins the held bed on screen). The cost half is only partly established and is flagged as a repair note, not counted as a failing condition under the stated PASS criteria.

## Q4 — THE STRANGER + THE RULE: FAIL

- **What the creature wants is clear.** BLOB PRINCE: "Mine's outside. I want a dry bed." (U2)
- **Where the creature ends up is clear.** U6 picture: "the creature sleeps in the dry corner under the stair on a mat with its sack for a pillow." That matches the U2 picture's sign (a figure "sleeping dry on a floor under a stair") and the Keeper's U5 line, "Full ten — who stays refused: dry floor."
- **The rule that decides its fate is NOT stated.** It arrives only as fragments, never as one plain sentence:
  - "Door test: spoken, carried, same hands." (Keeper, U1)
  - "Spoken, not carried. Bag outside." (Keeper, U2)
  - "Last rule: carrying hands hold no money." (Keeper, U4)
  - "Elias — spoken, carried, same hands." (Laura, U5)
  
  No line says what must be spoken (a name? a want?), what must be carried (whose bag?), by whom, or what "same hands" binds together. The best reconstruction a reader can assemble — the same person must speak the name, carry the bag, and make the bed with the same hands, and hands holding money are disqualified — is assembled from wordplay across three units ("Tight ropes, same hands: sleep tight," Keeper, U5). A reconstruction is not a statement.
- **Equal application cannot be verified — on its face, it is not applied the same way.** Laura passes as a proxy although Elias himself neither speaks nor carries; no line states a proxy exception. UNC, handling Peter's matter, fails ("These hands weren't at that board"). Peter "carried, never spoke" (Laura, U4) fails. The blob speaks its want but drags a sack "it cannot lift" (U2 picture) and fails. The distinction that lets Laura claim for Elias while UNC cannot claim for Peter is never explained — same posture (a third party handling another man's bag/bed), opposite outcomes, no stated difference. Whether the blob failed the "spoken" prong, the "carried" prong, or both is also never said.
- **Endpoint vs. rule:** the creature's endpoint is clear, but whether the dry floor is the rule's prescribed outcome for the refused, the Keeper's mercy, or the creature's own choice is never stated.

**Q4 verdict: FAIL.** The creature's want and endpoint are clear; the deciding rule is not stated, and its equal application cannot be verified from the material.

## Q5 — THE COUNT: FAIL

- U6 picture: "ELIAS fresh at the foot over the remnant of the red strike" — and then, "a new name, KEEPER, stands fresh at the very foot, the eleventh name."

These do not add up to one consistent count:

1. Start 11 → U5 10 → U6 11. What removed a name between the opening and U5 is never stated (the red strike? Peter? someone else?).
2. If Elias restored makes 11, then the Keeper adding himself makes 12 — contradicting "the eleventh name." If the Keeper is the 11th, Elias must be the 10th — meaning someone counted in the opening 11 is gone without any line saying so.
3. The end state leaves more than one bedless person for a single excess name: the Keeper (by his own question), the blob (on the floor), the overcoat man (outside), and possibly Peter. The count does not map to bodies.
4. "What changed" therefore cannot be stated in one consistent sentence: was it Elias back on, the Keeper on, Peter off, or all three? The material supports each reading in one place and contradicts it in another.

**Q5 verdict: FAIL.** Start count, end count, and what changed are not all clear; the arithmetic contradicts itself.

## DELETION TEST — readings the material invites but does not support

1. **UNC is Peter.** Invited by UNC handling a bag and talking about Peter's bed; unsupported — UNC speaks of "Peter's bed" as another person's, and is never named Peter.
2. **The man in the wet tan overcoat is Peter, arriving too late.** Invited by his lateness and wetness; unsupported — he is unnamed, speaks no line, and "too late" for what is unstated.
3. **The Keeper gave up his own bed for Elias.** Invited by "which bed's mine?"; unsupported — no Keeper bed is ever shown or mentioned (U5 picture: desk corner, "no bed").
4. **Elias takes Peter's bed specifically.** Invited by "Peter's bed" (U4) plus the tenth bed being held for Elias (U5–U6); unsupported — no line says the held bed is Peter's or that Peter loses it.
5. **The blob is owed a bed because it is a prince / wears a crown.** Invited by the speaker label BLOB PRINCE and the "tiny gold crown slipping over" its eye (U2 picture); unsupported — no rule gives royalty a claim; only its want is stated.
6. **Elias spoke his name or carried his bag.** Invited by "Elias — spoken, carried, same hands" (Laura, U5); unsupported — the U5 picture shows Laura speaking and carrying in her own hands; Elias never speaks and is never shown carrying.
7. **The dry floor is a win, or what the blob wanted.** Invited by the blob ending dry under the stair; unsupported — it wanted "a dry bed," and the floor is dry but not a bed; no line says it accepts this as a win (its hand dropping, U5, is gesture only).
8. **UNC wanted to sell Peter's bed.** Invited by "I want it sold" (U2); unsupported — "it" is unresolved, and his pictured act is offering money (buying), which points the other way.
9. **Peter was the name struck off the board.** Invited by Peter's absence and his bag sitting alone (U3); unsupported — the U6 picture shows ELIAS written over the red-strike remnant, implying the struck name was Elias's.
10. **Laura may claim for Elias because she is his sister.** Invited by the U1/U6 pictures ("her brother's carpet bag," "the woman's brother himself"); unsupported — kinship is shown, but no rule says kinship confers a claim; the passing act is her hands, not her kinship.
11. **The Keeper's final question means he has decided something (e.g. he will take the dry floor).** Invited by the held shot on him looking to camera; unsupported — he only asks; no answer, no bed assigned, no decision shown.
