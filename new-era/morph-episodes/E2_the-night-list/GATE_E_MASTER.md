# THE NIGHT LIST — E2 "The Keeper Night" — GATE E (MASTER) — COLD READ

Basis: only the master's transcript and the six picture summaries in
GATE_E_MASTER_INPUT.md. No other material was read. The transcript has
no speaker labels, so speaker attributions below are inferences from
line order and the pictures — and where that inference cannot be made,
that is stated, because it is itself a finding.

## Q1 — RETELL

A clerk (the "Keeper") is in charge of a roster board and a room of
beds for the night. At the start a name has been struck off the board —
"A hand strikes through a name in red chalk at the foot of a dark
roster board" (U1) — and a woman at the door argues with the clerk
about it: "a woman at the open door holds a carpet bag in both hands
and argues with him" (U1); "You put him back on, Keeper. Eleven
names!" The clerk wants the argument over: "I want quiet. Name off
ends arguing." The woman wants the place for someone else: "Quiet? I
want it for Elias." A test for getting in is stated: "Door test:
spoken, carried, same hands." Without passing it, the consequence is:
"Without it, Elias sleeps wet."

Bags become the battleground. A manager brings a carpet bag in —
"An older manager in a black hoodie carries a carpet bag in and sets
it down just inside the door" (U2) — but the clerk reverses that:
"The woman speaks from the door; the clerk takes the bag and sets it
outside the door himself." (U2). Someone objects to a bag being
outside and claims a bed in the first person: "Spoken, not carried.
Bag outside." / "Mine's outside. I want a dry bed." A small creature
arrives in this same stretch: "A small, damp, grey-green blob
creature with one large eye and a tiny crown oozes under the door
dragging a soaked sack; more round shapes wait in the wet dark
behind it." (U2).

A second fight opens over a man named Peter and money. One speaker
says "Wet? I want it sold," another reports "Peter's never stood
here, boss," and a price is asserted: "Peter's bed — pays tonight."
A further rule is stated: "Last rule: carrying hands hold no money."
The manager then tries to pay: "The manager crosses to a bed, works
at its bedding, and holds out a paper envelope of money." (U4). The
clerk refuses it: "The clerk pushes the envelope back into the
manager's chest." (U4); "The manager clutches the refused envelope
to his own chest, beaten." (U4).

The test is then applied by name to two men: "Elias wants it.
Peter's hands carried, never spoke." / "Elias — spoken, carried,
same hands." The woman then performs the test herself: "The woman
speaks at the door and carries the carpet bag through it in her own
hands, setting it down inside." (U5). A bed is reserved: "A bed is
re-made; a tag reading HELD hangs on its rail." (U5). The creature
loses out at that bed: "A small creature points at the bed, then
lets its hand drop." (U5).

How it ends: an unnamed man takes the held bed — "A man comes in out
of the rain carrying nothing and lies down on the held bed, his bag
on the floor beside it." (U6). The creature does not get a bed: "A
small creature sleeps in the dry corner under the staircase on a
mat, its sack for a pillow." (U6). Another unnamed man is shut out:
"Outside the door, a man in a wet tan overcoat stands in the rain
looking in, then turns away into the wet." (U6). The clerk is left
without an obvious bed — earlier he "turns toward a desk in the
corner — a chair, a lamp, no bed" (U5) — and closes the episode by
asking the camera: "Eleven names, ten beds — which bed's mine?"

That is the broad shape. But the retell is **not fully coherent
without outside knowledge**: the transcript/pictures never name the
man who ends up on the held bed, never name the man turned away
outside, and never state in words why the creature ends up on the
floor (see Q3–Q5). A viewer has to guess those identities and links.

## Q2 — WANTS

Note first: no transcript line is labelled with a speaker. The
following attributions are the best inference from order/pictures;
two important wants cannot be attributed at all.

- **The Keeper / clerk — quiet, and the name staying off.** Spoken by
  him (in reply to being addressed as "Keeper"): "I want quiet. Name
  off ends arguing." That want is plain and in his own words. His
  later apparent want of a bed for himself is **never** spoken by him
  in plain words — it exists only as a question: "Eleven names, ten
  beds — which bed's mine?"
- **The woman at the door — "it" for Elias.** "Quiet? I want it for
  Elias." She speaks this want herself, but "it" is never named in
  that line; the nearest clarification is someone else's line,
  "Without it, Elias sleeps wet." So she wants Elias not to sleep
  wet / to have the place, but she never says the plain words "I want
  a bed for Elias."
- **The would-be seller (the manager's side) — "it" sold.** "Wet? I
  want it sold." This is spoken in the first person by its holder,
  and the pictures support that "it" is a bed: "The manager crosses
  to a bed, works at its bedding, and holds out a paper envelope of
  money." (U4); "Peter's bed — pays tonight."
- **An unidentified speaker — a dry bed.** "Mine's outside. I want a
  dry bed." The want is plain, but the speaker is **not identifiable**
  from the material: it follows "Spoken, not carried. Bag outside,"
  and both the woman (whose bag is outside — U2) and the creature
  (whose sack is wet, U2) are candidates. The material does not let a
  viewer assign this line with confidence.
- **Elias — never speaks his want himself.** His want is only
  reported by someone else, in the third person: "Elias wants it.
  Peter's hands carried, never spoke." No line in the transcript is
  identifiable as Elias speaking.
- **Peter — never speaks at all.** No line is identifiable as Peter's.
  Things are said about him — "Peter's never stood here, boss" and
  "Peter's hands carried, never spoke" — but he states no want.
- **The small creature — no want spoken by it is identifiable.** No
  transcript line is assigned or assignable to the creature. If
  "Mine's outside. I want a dry bed" is meant to be the creature's,
  the material does not establish that (see above).

**Q2 requirement fails:** at least Elias's want is spoken only by
someone else, and the creature's want is never identifiably spoken
by the creature.

## Q3 — PAYOFF + COST

**The bed sale — a clear on-screen loss.** The want "Wet? I want it
sold" / "Peter's bed — pays tonight" is fought over and lost on
screen: "The clerk pushes the envelope back into the manager's
chest." (U4); "The manager clutches the refused envelope to his own
chest, beaten." (U4). The governing line is given: "Last rule:
carrying hands hold no money." Nothing later in the transcript or
pictures shows a sale going through. That sub-payoff is won/lost on
screen.

**The main payoff — Elias and the held bed — is not verifiable.**
A bed is held: "A bed is re-made; a tag reading HELD hangs on its
rail." (U5), after "Elias — spoken, carried, same hands." But the
person who finally occupies it is never named: "A man comes in out
of the rain carrying nothing and lies down on the held bed, his bag
on the floor beside it." (U6). The material does not say this man is
Elias. And the ending reopens the allocation rather than sealing it:
"Eleven names, ten beds — which bed's mine?" So the central want
(the woman's "I want it for Elias") cannot be confirmed as won, and
the count question at the end leaves who is bedless unresolved.

**Cost — partly shown, partly only implied.**
- The creature pays a visible cost: it goes from pointing at the bed
  — "A small creature points at the bed, then lets its hand drop."
  (U5) — to "A small creature sleeps in the dry corner under the
  staircase on a mat, its sack for a pillow." (U6), matching "Full
  ten — who stays refused: dry floor."
- An unnamed man pays a visible cost: "Outside the door, a man in a
  wet tan overcoat stands in the rain looking in, then turns away
  into the wet." (U6). Who he is, and which failed claim this cost
  belongs to, is not stated.
- A cost for the clerk is suggested but never stated: he "turns
  toward a desk in the corner — a chair, a lamp, no bed" (U5) and
  asks "which bed's mine?", but no line says he gave up his own bed
  or for whom.
- The manager's cost is only defeat, not a material loss: he keeps
  the money — "The manager clutches the refused envelope to his own
  chest, beaten." (U4).

## Q4 — THE STRANGER + THE RULE

**The creature's arrival.** "A small, damp, grey-green blob creature
with one large eye and a tiny crown oozes under the door dragging a
soaked sack; more round shapes wait in the wet dark behind it."
(U2). It then waits: "A small damp shape waits in the doorway's
outer corner." (U3).

**What it wants — not established.** No line states the creature's
claim in words attributable to it. The only first-person dry-bed
claim, "Mine's outside. I want a dry bed," cannot be assigned to the
creature rather than the woman (see Q2). Its wanting a bed is shown
only in gesture: "A small creature points at the bed, then lets its
hand drop." (U5).

**The rule that decides its fate — not stated.** No transcript line
applies the door test to the creature: nothing says whether the
creature spoke, or whether dragging a sack counts as carrying. The
test lines are applied by name only to others: "Elias — spoken,
carried, same hands" and "Peter's hands carried, never spoke."

**Where it ends up — clear.** "A small creature sleeps in the dry
corner under the staircase on a mat, its sack for a pillow." (U6) —
i.e. the dry floor, not a bed, matching "Full ten — who stays
refused: dry floor."

**The house rule for a bed.** As stated: "Door test: spoken,
carried, same hands." Plus: "Last rule: carrying hands hold no
money." In words: whoever claims a bed must have spoken and must
have carried (the bag) themselves, the same hands doing both, and
carrying hands must not hold money.

**Is it applied the same way to everyone? Not demonstrably — the
material shows apparent inconsistencies:**
- Elias is passed on a spoken verdict only: "Elias — spoken,
  carried, same hands." No picture shows Elias speaking or carrying.
- Peter is failed on the test: "Peter's hands carried, never spoke."
- The woman passes by performing it in picture: "The woman speaks at
  the door and carries the carpet bag through it in her own hands,
  setting it down inside." (U5) — yet in U1 she was already shown
  holding the bag and arguing, with no pass resulting then.
- The manager carried a bag in (U2) and the clerk simply "takes the
  bag and sets it outside the door himself" (U2), with no test
  verdict stated for him.
- Most damagingly, the man who finally gets the held bed is pictured
  doing the opposite of carrying at entry: "A man comes in out of
  the rain carrying nothing and lies down on the held bed, his bag
  on the floor beside it." (U6).

**Q4 requirement fails:** the creature's fate (dry floor/mat) is
clear, but what it claimed and which rule decided that fate are
never stated for it, and the rule's application is not shown to be
consistent.

## Q5 — THE COUNT

**At the start:** names = eleven — "You put him back on, Keeper.
Eleven names!" One name is being struck: "A hand strikes through a
name in red chalk at the foot of a dark roster board." (U1). A bed
total is **never stated at the start**; the pictures only show "rows
of narrow iron beds stand beyond the doorway" (U1).

**At the end:** two different totals are given back to back:
"Held tight: ten names, ten beds." and then "Full ten — who stays
refused: dry floor." and finally "Eleven names, ten beds — which
bed's mine?"

**What changed — not clearly established.** If the struck name
stayed off, the count would be ten names / ten beds and everyone
named would have a bed; but the final line restores "Eleven names"
against the same ten beds, leaving one person over. Whether that
eleventh person is the restored Elias, the clerk counting himself,
or someone else is never stated. The episode therefore ends on two
contradictory name counts (ten in "Held tight: ten names, ten
beds," eleven in "Eleven names, ten beds — which bed's mine?") with
no line resolving which is the final state.

**Q5 requirement fails:** the start bed count is unstated and the
end name count contradicts itself.

## VERDICT: FAIL

PASS requires all of: (1) a retell coherent without outside
knowledge; (2) every speaker's want spoken by them; (3) a payoff
won/lost irreversibly; (4) the creature's fate and the deciding rule
clear; (5) the count change clear. The following requirements fail:

- **Q2 fails** — Elias's want is spoken only by someone else:
  "Elias wants it. Peter's hands carried, never spoke." No line is
  identifiable as Elias (or Peter, or the creature) speaking a want;
  the creature's want is never spoken by it at all, and the clerk's
  own bed-want exists only as the question "which bed's mine?"
- **Q4 fails** — the creature ends on a mat (U6), but no line states
  its claim or applies any rule to it; and the door test is not
  shown applied consistently — the man who gets the held bed is
  pictured "carrying nothing" (U6).
- **Q5 fails** — no bed count is given at the start, and the ending
  contradicts itself: "Held tight: ten names, ten beds." versus
  "Eleven names, ten beds — which bed's mine?", with no resolution
  of who the eleventh name is.
- **Q1/Q3 fail on the main thread for the same reason** — because
  the man on the held bed (U6) is never named, the central payoff
  (the woman's "I want it for Elias") cannot be confirmed as won,
  and the story's ending depends on the viewer guessing identities
  the transcript and pictures never supply. (The one clean payoff is
  the refused sale in U4, which is not the episode's central want.)

The broad situation — a night clerk, a list, ten beds, a money
attempt refused — is legible. The specific story the gate asks about
— whose want was won, why the creature was refused, and what the
final count is — is not established by the transcript and pictures
alone.
