## PASS 1 — STORYLINE (Storyliner)

**Premise choice.** The bible's §8 outline ("The Sol Night") was
adopted as the episode: a man struck at noon accuses the night
clerk; the log clears the pen and names the money. It is the only
candidate that teaches the engine (one bed contested, one chain),
fires the premiere's escalation ladder at its lowest rung (a
stranger's name, S7), and puts the questioning job on the accuser
(X8). Alternatives pitched and killed at storyline:

- *Open on Sol knocking at the door, unanswered, then entering.* —
  **Killed.** BLAM requires the decision already in flight at frame
  0 (the strike + the accusation mid-flight); a knocking cold open
  is an atmosphere beat, and B2 spends no beat on atmosphere.
- *UNC speaks in U4, defending the sale.* — **Killed before it was
  pitched twice.** Slate fix F3.1 already moved his lecture to E2
  and capped the speaking cast; re-importing it would re-break the
  cap the slate imposed.
- *The Dispatcher reveals by intercom/voice.* — **Killed.** E2's
  GATE_LOG precedent: a lone name from her disembodied voice failed
  7 of 7 fires. She enters in-scene or she does not reveal.

**Gate A — connector test.** The six unit summaries, joined only by
*but / therefore*:
1. Sol accuses the Keeper of striking his name for money →
2. Laura backs the accusation with eyewitness proof and the wet
   strike, **but** the Keeper denies taking any money →
3. **therefore** the Dispatcher opens her log, which shows the
   strike was logged at noon, when the Keeper is never there →
4. **but** noon points at UNC, who enters silently with the
   receipts, and Laura names the sale →
5. **therefore** the Keeper restores Sol's name under his own rule
→ 6. **but** restoring Sol makes eleven names for ten beds, with
the Keeper's own name at the bottom — **therefore** Laura dares
him to strike his own name or hers.
No "and then" joints. **PASS.**

**Gate B — turns.** Every unit opens and closes on different
values: quiet room → accused clerk (U1); accusation → suspicion
with physical proof (U2); clerk suspected → clerk cleared,
suspicion moved (U3); suspicion → culprit named in his presence
(U4); rule stated → name restored (U5); restored → a worse
shortage and a dare (U6). **PASS.**

**Delta forecast (Pass 1, for the ledger):** ≥2 state changes —
Sol's status (off the list → on it) and a power delta at the desk
(Laura's dare). Recorded; reconciled at lock (see Pass 7).

**Anti-repetition pre-check (G2).** Board archive for this show is
empty (premiere), but the predecessor's shapes were searched: THE
ROSTER's E2 board was a six-name procession — this board is one
contested bed and one chain, the killed construction is not
re-imported. Used-events check: no prior episode exists to repeat.
**Clear.**

## PASS 6 — CRITIC (fresh context; located objections only)

**Cold read (reported, not objected):** no stumbled lines; the four
voices are tellable apart blind; the chain is followable without
the bible. The cold reader's one confusion is filed as Objection 1.

**Round 1 — three located objections:**

1. **U3, the reveal (Gate E / B9).** *"Nobody struck him at night.
   It was noon"* is a fact a viewer must take on hearing; the
   slate's §3 named exactly this the show's swipe point. If the log
   page doesn't read, U3 is a woman saying a time. — **Resolution
   (production note, dialogue unchanged):** the reveal was already
   staged picture-first at breakdown; the Critic's objection is
   answered by making the beat map explicit and binding in
   SCRIPT.md's Production Notes (beat 5: the page turned to camera
   *before* she speaks; "12:00" sized for a phone screen; the
   blank NIGHT column beside it). The line now names what the
   picture has already stated (B8). **Closed.**
2. **U4, Sol's line (legibility).** *"I knocked at noon. The desk
   was empty"* could read as contradicting the sale — if the desk
   was empty, who sold the bed? — **Resolution:** no rewrite. The
   line is the alibi's corroboration and the sale's shape: no
   *clerk* was at the desk; the owner let himself in. The log's
   noon entry carries an initial, not a clerk's hand (Production
   Notes beat 5), and UNC's silent, unhurried entrance in U4 is
   staged as a man who has a key. The stumble is the intended one:
   Sol is mid-realization as he says it. **Closed, with the
   staging note binding.**
3. **U1, Sol's first line (B6).** The accusation states a grievance;
   the want is implicit. — **Resolution:** accepted as a pass, not
   a fix. B5 licenses the accusation-with-proof as a first line,
   and a cold viewer can repeat what Sol wants (his name back, his
   bed back) without help. The Keeper's first line states *his*
   want outright, so the unit carries one explicit want-statement.
   **Closed.**

**Legibility test:** the Critic restated each speaker's unsaid
sentence from the dialogue alone; all four matched Pass 3's
recorded unsaid sentences (the Keeper's cost is audible in U5's
rule; Laura's in what she does not ask for; Sol's in the grievance
he leads with; the Dispatcher's in what her flatness withholds).
**PASS.**

**Round 2:** the Critic re-read the amended production notes
against Objections 1–2 and the timing sheet against the Pass 3

## GATE E — COLD-READER ANSWERS (lock gate, A3; no lore terms)

- **Who wants what:** Sol wants his name back on the list so he
  can sleep in his bed tonight. The Keeper wants one quiet night
  and his rule kept.
- **What blocked them:** the list is full and somebody with a key
  has been selling beds off it — Sol's bed was sold at noon by the
  owner, and the night clerk got the blame.
- **What changed by U6:** Sol is back on the list — and now there
  are eleven names for ten beds, the Keeper's own name is at the
  bottom, and Laura has dared him to strike his own name or hers.
- **Why it matters to someone who knows nothing:** somebody sleeps
  in the rain tonight. The only question left is who — and it is
  on the screen, in those words.

Every answer is statable without a single lore term: list, bed,
name, rain, money, noon. **GATE E: PASS.** (The premise had already
passed Gate E on its face at the slate pass — cleanest on the
slate, with ONE SHORT.)
