**Show:** THE NIGHT LIST (morphed from THE ROSTER; bible
`new-era/morph/MORPH_SHOW-3.md`, as fixed by slate commit 62fa65d3 —
law). **Format:** 60s, 6 units × 10s, 16:9, 1280×720, 24fps, house
CRF 18, AAC. Palette INK + BONE + CADMIUM lamp, VERMILION device.
Register: deadpan — the wronger the night, the flatter the clerk.

**North star (msg 490):** best, most engaging. This show's stakes
are the rawest on the slate — a bed inside, or the rain. A cold
viewer must *feel* the shortage in the first ten seconds: the
episode stages beds and doorway first, and demotes the list to
scoreboard (SLATE §2 ruling).

**Premise (bible §1, Gate E on its face):** *A night clerk keeps
the list of who gets a bed in his building — and every night there
is one bed too few, and somebody fights the list on his desk for
it.*

**Episode premise (one breath):** A man struck off the list is at
the door in the rain, saying the Keeper struck him for money. The
log says nobody struck him at night at all.

**Shared-context brief (C2, ~600 chars):** *A night desk in a
gothic lodging house, after midnight. Ten beds; the list on the
board decides who sleeps in them. The Keeper holds the pen and
wants one quiet night. Laura, a long-stay guest, watches the desk
the way family watches a will reading. The Dispatcher keeps the
entry log and believes it the way other people believe weather.
Tonight's arrival, Sol, has a wet coat, a bag, and a name that was
struck off the board at noon. Somebody in this building is selling
beds.*

**The shared wrong belief (A4):** Everyone in the room believes the
Keeper holds the power because he holds the pen. The viewer can
see the pen only records what money already decided.

**The chain (B1, one chain only):** proof shown (the wet strike,
the midnight money) → lie told (the denial) → entrance that breaks
the lie (the Dispatcher's log) → authority reveal (noon; the
receipts; the naming) → consequence begun (a name restored, a worse
shortage, a dare). Keeper = accused · Laura = accuser and
questioning-job holder (notice U2 / name U4 / act U6, riding the
chain per X8) · Dispatcher = authority · UNC = the lie's owner,
present and silent (F3.1).

## THE SCRIPT

Dialogue is verbatim. Delivery notes and tactic verbs are part of
the lock. Picture turns are written between quoted blocks (A3
anti-front-load construction): lines are written to read wherever
the generator's front-loading lands them; every unit's tail carries
a speech-independent event.

### U1 — HOOK (MIXED, 15 words)

*Picture, frame 0:* the board, tight — SOL struck through in
vermilion, the strike stroke landing inside the first half-second
to the **chalk-snap** (the first-second sound event; the door does
not bang on this show — F3.4). This frame is the unit's PEAK panel:
the decision already in flight. *Cut wide:* the doorway, the row of
beds beyond the desk — ten beds, one standing empty with its tag
still on (BED 7). Sol is already inside and already talking,
mid-stride, wet coat dripping; his bag goes down **by the empty
bed**, not at the desk (F3.4). The Keeper at the desk, pen up.

**SOL** *(mid-accusation, to the Keeper, not sitting)*: "You struck my name. I slept in the rain."
`tactic: ACCUSE · 9 words · his want is the accusation: the name back, the bed back`

*Picture turn:* the Keeper looks at the empty bed, then at Sol. He
does not stand.

**KEEPER** *(flat, pen still up)*: "Sit down, Sol. One quiet night."
`tactic: DEFLECT · 6 words · his want stated plain (B6): one quiet night`

*Tail event (speech-independent):* Sol does not sit. He stays
standing by the empty bed, hand on his bag. The rain keeps coming
off his coat onto the floor.

*Physical moment (C7):* the bag set down by the empty bed.
*Joint out:* Sol's hand stays on the bag — U2 opens on the room
still arranged around that refusal.
*Interruption beat (C8, declared point):* Sol's entrance line is
delivered over the room's quiet as he enters — an unprompted
entrance at the unit boundary, the episode's one interruption-class
beat (X5-compliant; no mid-line overlaps are scripted anywhere).

### U2 — PROOF → LIE (FULL-TALK, 19 words)

*Picture:* Laura's chair, the corner she has clearly sat in for
months — a blanket, a cup. She has been watching the desk the whole
time. The board behind the Keeper: SOL struck, and the strike
**glistens** — it is still wet.

**LAURA** *(from her chair, even, an eyewitness not a gossip)*: "I watched Peter pay you at midnight."
`tactic: ACCUSE (with proof) · 7 words · relationship line (C3): only the woman who pays on time and watches this desk can say it`

*Picture turn:* the Keeper caps his pen. Slowly.

**KEEPER** *(flat denial)*: "UNC takes Peter's money. Not me."
`tactic: DENY · 6 words`

**LAURA** *(leaning forward — the questioning job, NOTICE — and pointing at the board, at what the picture already shows)*: "Then why is it still wet?"
`tactic: CHALLENGE · 6 words · the proof object is named only after it is seen (B8)`

*Tail event:* the Keeper uncaps the pen, caps it again. Nothing
else moves.

**DIGESTION GAP 1** *(2–3s, carrier: the Keeper's held reaction —
he stares at the wet strike and says nothing).*

*Joint out (name-handoff, device):* the wet strike fills the last
frame — U3 opens on a different book: the log.

### U3 — ENTRANCE → REVEAL (MIXED, 13 words)

*Picture:* the Dispatcher enters from the day office with the entry
log under her arm — in-scene, a person, not a voice. She sets the
log on the desk and **slams it open** to the day's page, turning it
toward the room before she speaks: the page fills the frame — the
entries in columns, the **NOON column carrying the strike entry
("12:00 — SOL struck — U.")**, the **NIGHT column blank**. The
picture states the reveal first (B8/B9, SLATE §3: the noon strike
must be *seen*, not only heard).

**DISPATCHER** *(flat, reading the page, not the room)*: "Nobody struck him at night. It was noon."
`tactic: REVEAL (authority, B10) · 8 words`

*Picture turn:* the Keeper looks at the noon column. Not at her.

**KEEPER** *(quieter than anything so far)*: "I'm never here at noon."
`tactic: ALIBI · 5 words`

*Tail event:* the Dispatcher closes her hand on the log's edge and
leaves it open. The noon column stays in frame.

*Physical moment (C7):* the log slammed open.
*Joint out:* paper on the desk — U4 opens on more paper: the
receipts.

### U4 — THE NAMING (FULL-TALK, 19 words)

*Picture:* UNC enters — **silently** (F3.1) — carrying the day's
receipts. He lays them on the desk one by one, unhurried, while
Sol speaks (the entrance overlaps the unit's opening line; UNC does
not speak, interrupt, or react). Two of the receipts, legible in
close-up, are for the same bed: **BED 7, twice** (post-composited,
see Production Notes). The empty chair beside the desk stays empty.

**SOL** *(to the receipts as much as to the room; he is done accusing the wrong man and doesn't know it yet)*: "I knocked at noon. The desk was empty."
`tactic: TESTIFY · 8 words · corroborates the alibi from the picture's side: he came at noon; no clerk was there`

*Picture turn:* Laura stands. First time in the episode.

**LAURA** *(to UNC, aimed at the receipts and the empty chair — the questioning job, NAME)*: "You sold his bed at noon. Let him blame the pen."
`tactic: NAME · 11 words`

*Tail event:* UNC finishes laying down the last receipt. He looks
at it, not at her. He does not deny it.

**DIGESTION GAP 2** *(2–3s, carrier: held reaction on Sol's face —
he stops looking at the Keeper and looks at UNC).*

*Physical moment (C7):* the receipts laid down one by one.
*Joint out:* the desk is covered in paper — U5 clears it: the
Keeper alone at the board.

### U5 — CONSEQUENCE BEGUN (QUIET, 7 words)

*Picture:* the Keeper alone at the board. Sol's bag still by the
empty bed. The board: ten names, SOL struck through. The Keeper
takes up the chalk. One line only, his rule said plain for the
first time — the line that costs him, because the board is about
to make it arithmetic.

**KEEPER** *(to the board, not to Sol)*: "One on. One off. That's the rule."
`tactic: DECLARE · 7 words`

*Tail event (the unit's whole back half, speech-independent):* he
writes SOL back on, in full, stroke by stroke, while Sol watches
from the doorway. The name completes by the unit's tail (post
graphic keyed to the pen strokes — Production Notes).

*Physical moment (C7):* the writing itself.
*Joint out (name-handoff, device):* the spoken name of U1 is now
the written name — U6 opens on the full board.

### U6 — THE DARE / FREEZE (SILENCE-BEAT, 11 words)

*Picture:* the board, full — **eleven names, ten beds** — and the
name at the bottom of the board is **KEEPER** (post graphic; the
reveal is the pan down the board, a picture, not a line). The
Dispatcher, at the door with her log, states the arithmetic the
viewer has just seen.

**DISPATCHER** *(flat, closing the log)*: "Eleven names. Ten beds."
`tactic: STATE · 4 words`

*Picture turn:* Laura steps to the board. She looks at the bottom
name for a long beat before she speaks.

**LAURA** *(quiet, not kind — the questioning job, ACT)*: "Then strike yours, Keeper. Or strike mine."
`tactic: DARE · 7 words · the cliff is a social beat carried by a person (X2); the device name rides inside it`

**DIGESTION GAP 3 / FREEZE** *(final 2–3s):* the Keeper's pen
hovers over KEEPER on the board. It does not come down. The
question card over the freeze (B11, post graphic): **WHOSE NAME
COMES OFF?** Cut. The consequence — who loses the bed — is Episode
2's setup and is not played here (B3).

*Physical moment (C7):* the pen, hovering, tapped once against the
book's edge — and held.

**BLAM self-check (U1):** A1 onset — action and accusation from
frame 0, no dead head; E1 named impact ≤1.0s — the strike landing;
V1 — Sol enters mid-stride, in medias res, no ramp from rest; no
fade-in; the door does not bang (F3.4 — ONE SHORT owns the slam).
**Front-loading:** lines are written to land anywhere; turn 1 and
turn 2 are separate quoted blocks with a picture turn between them
in every two-line unit; every unit's tail is a speech-independent
event. **Anchoring:** board/book/log close-ups are PEAK anchors;
faces ride CHAR panels per the standing panel workflow; the two
two-handers at the desk (U3, U4) anchor on the paper, not the
faces, when both cannot be carried.
**Silence defense:** the three gaps have named carriers (a stare,
a face, a freeze) — none is a bare pause the generator can compress
away unnoticed; if a take compresses a gap, the carrier shot is the
QC check, not the word count.
