- **D-1 — The bible's 15th line is cut (A1 line cap).** The
  bible's §8 outline runs 15 lines; A1 caps an episode at 14.
  The room cut the Occupant's U3 line ("Ask the book.", 3w). The
  chain survives intact — U3's turn is carried by the book's
  entrance and the Usher's correction — and her silence in U3

  the room's one structural deviation from the bible outline,
  made under A1, which outranks an outline (the outline is the
  starting point; A1 is adopted law).
- **D-2 — U6's whisper extended by two words.** The Dancer's U6
  line runs "She knows it, Usher. Every step." (6w), lifting U6
  to 12w, mid-band for SILENCE-BEAT (10–13), and the episode to
  82. "Every step" is repetition-with-a-difference (A5 audit
  exempt): the claim grows from the rhythm to the whole dance in
  the same breath that confesses it.
- **D-3 — The test dance is one danced pair across U5→U6.** The
  bible's coexistence law caps danced units at two per episode,
  never adjacent. The room rules the law's unit is the *pair*
  (the room method's THE LAST SEAT clause: a danced pair is
  TURN 1 + a person's answering TURN 2). Night One runs two
  danced pairs — U2's challenge pair and the U5→U6 test pair —
  non-adjacent as pairs. Splitting the test pair at the unit
  boundary would break B3 (the answer *is* the cliff) and the
  bible's own §8 stages it exactly this way. Logged so the
  ruling is on record, not silent.
- **D-4 — The slate's U5–U6 swipe point is answered by staging,
  not by lines.** SLATE §3 names the back half the slowest five
  seconds on the slate: a viewer who hasn't bought
  dance-as-verdict leaves there. Fix order per Gate E doctrine

## GATE E — COLD-READER ANSWERS (on the locked draft)

1. **Who wanted what?** The Dancer wanted the stranger out of
   the critic's seat — or proven — and the theatre's verdict to
   stay hers. The stranger wanted to be allowed to stay in it.
2. **What blocked them?** The stranger's claim, and the theatre's
   own guest book, which backed the claim's category (white
   stubs were given, and this one was given to the woman who
   built the room).
3. **What changed by U6?** The stranger passed the Dancer's own
   test — she knows the theatre's oldest dance from memory — and
   the Usher let her stay. The intruder is now a guest, and the
   room owes her a dance.
4. **Why does it matter to someone who knows no lore?** A woman's
   job and home — the theatre is both — hang on a stranger's
   verdict, and the stranger may be the one person alive who has
   a right to give it.

No answer requires a lore term. The word "cull" appears nowhere
in the episode. **Gate E: PASS.**
