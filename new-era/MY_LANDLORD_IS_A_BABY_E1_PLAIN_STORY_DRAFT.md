# MY LANDLORD IS A BABY — E1 plain story (draft for user verdict)

Written by the parent, 2026-11-27. Bible: new-era/shows/MY_LANDLORD_IS_A_BABY.md
(rulings applied: opens ON THE DESTINATION, then "ONE MONTH EARLIER" on the
rent book; the baby grows one visible size each time he is paid and shrinks
when he is not; Elias narrates, every narration sentence is a move and the
baby answers it with an act in the same shot; exactly one aside per episode;
one pictured wish, re-shot by the finale; the night-light is the baby's
beloved thing and Elias's one power, always given back).

Premise: My landlord is a baby. He grows every time I pay him. This month
he took everything I have — it started with one coin.

Cast: ELIAS (the tenant, home character, narrator), BABY BOSS (the landlord,
an invader baby — landlord's hat, teething-ring crown — he never speaks, he
acts), THE COLLECTOR (an impostor with an identical rent book, one detail
wrong).

1. The destination. Elias's room is empty. No bed, no table — Elias sits in
   the bathtub, fully dressed, holding his pillow. In the corner sleeps
   Baby Boss, grown huge, as big as a man, hugging a small night-light.
   An empty coin glass stands on the shelf. Elias, to us: "My landlord
   took everything. It started with one coin." On the floor, the rent book
   lies open. Its page reads: ONE MONTH EARLIER.
2. One month earlier. The room is full and warm; six coins stand in the
   glass. A knock. Baby Boss at the door, doll-small, hand out. "My
   landlord is a baby. Today he came to collect the rent himself." The
   baby climbs the door frame and points at the coin glass. Elias pays
   one coin — and the baby grows, one whole size, knee-high. Elias looks
   at him, then at us, and pictures the wish: this same room, rent paid,
   himself asleep in his bed, the baby tiny and asleep in a cup beside
   the night-light.
3. A week later (a calendar page tears itself off). The baby, knee-high,
   knocks and holds out his hand. "He only grows when I pay him. So I
   tried not paying." The baby shrinks one size in front of him — then
   takes hold of Elias's pillow, drags it to the door, and sits on it
   outside. Unpaid, he does not leave empty-handed.
4. A second knock. A second baby at the door, with a rent book identical
   to the first — except the little hat in its drawing sits on the wrong
   side. The impostor holds out his hand. Elias almost pays — then shuts
   the door on him. The Collector slides a black rent slip under the door
   and is gone. Elias, quietly, only to us: "I keep one coin under the
   bath. He does not know."
5. Night. Baby Boss cannot sleep. His night-light is gone — fallen behind
   the shelf in the day's dragging and pulling. He sits small in the dark,
   crying without a sound. Elias finds the light, and gives it back. The
   baby hugs it — then opens the rent book to the circled date: the whole
   month, due at once. Elias looks at his bed. He pays with the bed. The
   baby, fed on the payment, grows one size dragging it out.
6. The destination, again — the same picture as the opening, now we know
   every object that is missing and why. Elias in the bath with the
   returned pillow. Baby Boss huge in the corner, asleep, hugging the
   night-light. On the shelf, the coin glass — with one coin in it, the
   coin from under the bath. Elias, to us: "One coin left. Tomorrow he
   comes for the bath." In his sleep, the baby's hand opens, waiting.

Legibility check: the want is printed in frame 1 (the emptied room, the
huge baby, the empty glass — the whole episode is the explanation of one
picture). The body is the ledger — the baby's size is the rent account,
readable in any frame; the room's objects are the balance. Every narration
sentence is a move and is answered by an act in the same shot (pay →
growth; refuse → shrink and a taking; shut door → the black slip). Proof
is a positive picture: growth and shrinkage happen on camera, the
impostor is exposed by a visible wrong detail, never a deduction. The
premise is re-spent in all six units.
