## THE SLATE (5 shows, each rips one proven schema from the 2026-11-25 study wave)

**FORMAT (declared 2026-11-27, writing loop fold — GAPS G10):** every
show is 6 units × 10 seconds, 16:9 landscape, one 60-second episode.
Exception: NO TALKING AT THE TABLE — its six units are one complete
gag beat each in anthology rhythm (each unit re-opens the table),
chained by the chair ledger, not by a symmetric skeleton. Every
banked device was proven in longer vertical forms (60s–924s); each
show's bible carries a COMPRESSION note naming the device that does
the long-form→60s work for that show.

### 1. THE BIG JAR  — schema: eatsimple_x contract-on-the-counter + Adi fill-level stakes
Sentence: "Two neighbours race to fill the big jar before the fair. The loser
carries the winner's jar home on his head."
- Frame 1: a giant empty glass jar between two houses, a painted line near the
  top reading FAIR DAY, both neighbours already dropping things in, mid-motion.
- Printed stake: the jar's fill level + the FAIR DAY line. Readable in any frame.
- Body is the ledger: whatever each puts in came off his own house/body —
  his roof tiles, his coat buttons, his dinner. Houses visibly empty as jar fills.
- Proof beat: a produced object, never a count — the jar tips / a rival's item
  is found inside the other's side, held up in an open hand.
- Loss: loser physically carries the full jar home on his head in the final unit.
- Serial engine: each episode a new thing both want in the jar first; fill level
  resets only when somebody pays for it on screen.

### 2. SHE TOOK MY FACE  — schema: thecontentlab01 fruit-soap body-ledger serial
Sentence: "She stole his smile for the wedding photo, she stole his voice for
the song, but she didn't expect him to come collect them in person."
- Frame 1: a fruit character with NO mouth smiling for a photo while another
  wears two mouths. Want, theft and body-ledger in one tableau.
- Printed stake: a photo wall — each stolen feature appears in HER photos.
- Body is the ledger: features (mouth, voice, colour, shine) move body to body;
  plot state is readable off faces in any single frame.
- Proof beat: the stolen feature misbehaves on the thief's body in public
  (his laugh comes out of her face at the wrong moment) — a positive picture.
- Loss: the thief ends an episode with a feature visibly gone from her own face.
- Serial engine: one feature per episode, re-spent in ≥4 of 6 units; every line
  is a demand/refusal answered by a feature moving or refusing to move.

### 3. NO TALKING AT THE TABLE  — schema: ijeonguu zero-word pantomime (300K-view proof)
Sentence: "At this table nobody may speak. The first one who talks loses his chair."
Sentence ships as caption; the episode itself is wordless.
- Frame 1: a long table, one chair at the head, a big crossed-out mouth sign
  everyone can see, two rivals already miming insults, mid-gesture.
- Printed stake: chairs — each round the loser’s chair is carried out of the room
  by the winner, on camera. Chair count is the score, readable in any frame.
- Body is the ledger: hunger, cheating and revenge are all mime + props
  (hidden food, swapped plates, a chair sawn shorter between courses).
- Proof beat: the cheat is produced — the sawn chair leg held up, the hidden
  drumstick pulled from a sleeve. Never a deduction.
- Loss: final unit = loser eating standing up, chair gone, face at poster size.
- Serial engine: same table, new guest/invader each episode tries to make the
  hosts break the rule; premise re-spent every unit because every act is at the table.

### 4. ONE MILLION PETALS  — schema: Adi_8xx arena banner + prize serial
Sentence: "She needs one million petals to buy back her garden. Every man in
town is selling petals, and every petal costs her something she is wearing."
- Frame 1: arena/street banner "1,000,000 PETALS = THE GARDEN", a near-empty
  sack in her hands, the garden gate chained behind her with the invader inside it.
- Printed stake: the sack's fill + a tally board whose number is painted bigger
  each unit (number as object, carried and repainted on screen, never a ledger read-out).
- Body is the ledger: she pays with visible things off her own life — hat, coat,
  cart, house keys — she visibly diminishes as the sack visibly grows.
- Proof beat: a seller's fake petals melt/wilt in her hand in public — produced,
  bodily, no deduction.
- Loss (per episode): she ends owning the sack's gain and having lost one named
  thing, shown in the winner's/seller's hands in the same frame as her face.
- Serial engine: garden stays chained across episodes; each episode one seller,
  one scam, one real cost. Gentrification shown: the invader redecorates the
  garden behind the chain while she pays.

### 5. MY LANDLORD IS A BABY  — schema: catyfavorite storybook track (the one sanctioned narration form)
Sentence (also the narration's first line): "My landlord is a baby. Today he
came to collect the rent himself."
- Narration law (from the wave): first person, one plain sentence per shot,
  every sentence exactly what the frame shows, followable with sound off.
- Frame 1: a baby in a landlord's hat at the door, hand out; the tenant's last
  coin visible in a glass on the shelf behind him.
- Printed stake: the coin glass — coins leave it one by one, on screen.
- Body is the ledger: the baby grows one visible size each time he is paid
  (paid = bigger); unpaid, he shrinks. Rent pressure readable off his body.
- Proof beat: what the rent buys appears in the room as an object (a leak fixed
  becomes a dry patch + a bucket retired; a lie becomes a second baby at the door).
- Loss: an episode ends with the tenant paying with the thing itself (his bed
  carried out) or the baby shrinking to pocket size and being carried home in a cup.
- Serial engine: one demand per episode, escalating objects; the tenant's room
  visibly empties/fills as the season's ledger.

## SHE TOOK MY FACE (fruit-soap body-ledger serial)

1. **Win by re-aiming the contest, not by winning it** (0xfrosteeze). The victim should not try to out-perform the thief at being her — she forces the thief into the one contest the stolen face cannot stop playing: a mirror, a live camera, the face's original owner watching — a recognition loop the thief must keep winning forever, frozen in public while life resumes around her.
2. **The challenge that audits the fraud** (bajablstdeeznut). Nobody accuses the thief of wearing a stolen face — the victim (or a rival) challenges her to the one thing only the real face's owner can do (the signature expression, the family photo pose, the mother's recognition test), in public. And the audit must also endanger the auditor (the bar is too heavy for the challenger as well), so the test is a contest both can lose, not a clean execution.
3. **The stolen feature CRACKS on the thief** (clipcraft7, from Sweet Revenge Parts 3–7). Each episode one new crack crosses the stolen smile, readable in any frame; a stolen feature decays on the wrong body at a fixed rate, one visible stage per episode. The victim doesn't need to accuse — the crack is the accusation, a positive picture. [frames+inference]
4. **The foundation fake** (clipcraft7, forehead-price story — ASR line: "A little foundation, a million on my forehead and I'm ready"). For the wedding photo, the thief paints a smile on the victim's mouthless face so the photo "works" — painted, not stolen. In the photo flash it holds; in the next unit it smears down her chin when she cries/laughs. A forged feature that fails in public is the cheapest possible proof shot. [ASR]
5. **The tally under the makeup** (clipcraft7). Each theft leaves a small number on the thief's skin — 1, 2, 3 — which she covers with foundation every morning (shown once, in a mirror, as routine). The number sweats through during the song she stole the voice for. The victim's collection line: she doesn't argue, she wipes the thief's forehead with one thumb. [frames]
6. **Proof by performance, staged as a tasting** (clipcraft7, the donut heir verdict). The collection scene: the victim makes the thief USE the stolen feature in front of the family — sing the song with the stolen voice. Mid-line, the voice comes out in the victim's child-register / the crack crosses the smile; the table sees it happen. No deduction, no testimony: the feature misbehaves on the wrong body, in public, on demand. [frames]
7. **The seam** (clipcraft7, Sweet Revenge's knife beats). Both women carry the same faint seam line where the smile was cut. The victim holds a small knife to her own seam in the mirror — not cutting, measuring. Final-episode collection: the thief, face fully cracked, peels the smile off herself and holds it out — the peel comes off in one piece, one gesture, no gore, the whole serial in a single picture. [frames+inference]
8. **The fraud's profit written on her own body** (diesel_lord, secondary rip — the gold-teeth close-up). The thief's stolen beauty should leave one part of her own body visibly wrong that no stolen face covers.
9. **Identity replacement taught as a training montage with a performed test line** (gavi6ista). The face-thief is shown being coached into the victim's face — practising her smile, her signature line ("read it 1000 times") — and then *examined* on the stolen identity, the exam line being the same line the episode opened on. Cold open: the thief already performing the victim's life perfectly; the season's question is "how did she get her face?"
10. **The asymmetric-fates engine** (hebertysales). After a theft, cut between the thief thriving (her photos, her glow) and the victim serving time in the diminished body — and let the victim's *preparation to collect* be bodily and visible (he practises smiling in a mirror with a stolen photograph; the face he is building toward is the episode's clock).
11. **The defect named in the first scene, with a built-in timer** (miee711). Give the face-theft a printed expiry in episode 1 (the stolen face "sets" permanently at a named date/event — a wedding, a photo day, a contract signing) so every episode is the victim racing a deadline the audience can see — "Only after they turn 18" turns a static injustice into a countdown every cruelty also ticks.
12. **The belly-clock, translated** (mviews3). The stolen feature *grows* on the thief across the episode (her borrowed smile gets wider, brighter, more photographed) while the victim's face visibly flattens — two body states moving in opposite directions, readable in any frame. Costume constant: the victim keeps ONE unstealable marker (her red scarf) in every frame, so the audience never loses her even faceless. [frames]
13. **The theft proven by playing the sale back on a screen** (shahzaibazhar08). The horn IS a stolen face feature: steal it, wear it, sell it, then have the theft proven by playing the sale back on a screen in the room — the wedding photographer's slideshow runs at the party and one frame shows the mouth mid-transfer. The victim's line is one demand — "Give me back my smile" — answered only by tears and the feature visibly moving.
14. **The sabotage escalation / the container rule** (thatchaosgirl9). The stolen feature is carried visibly ON the thief (she wears his mouth over hers). At the wedding she is fed — through his stolen mouth — the one dish he is famous for hating; *his* gag reflex fires in *her* face, in public, in front of the table. Container rule: what is put INTO a stolen feature damages the thief, not the owner — so the victim can fight back by feeding his own face.
15. **The lie-ledger** (zombieonearth_). Every lie the thief tells *wearing his stolen face* adds one more of HIS features to her face, permanently. Her face becomes the public count of her lies; the only way to stop growing his features is to confess in the stolen voice — which spends it.
