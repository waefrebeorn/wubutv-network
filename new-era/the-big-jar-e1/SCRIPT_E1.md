## U1 — THE LAW (day)

FRAME: the giant empty glass jar between the two houses, FAIR DAY line
painted near the top. MARO drops a chalk-white roof tile in; PELL drops a
button tied with red string. BRAN, on a stool, paints "1 — 1" on the glass.

- BRAN: "First marked thing to touch the line wins." [states the law]
- BRAN: "Loser carries the jar home. On his head." [states the price]
- MARO: "Then start painting, Widow. My whole roof is coming down."
  [brag — commits his house to the race]
- PELL: "Your roof leaks. My coat is full of buttons." [counter-brag —
  commits his coat]

ACTION: TILO sets a ladder against MARO's house (sides with MARO). SARA
calls the count to the street. UMBER lays his coin on MARO's side of the
stand. REACTION BEAT: MARO looks up at his own roof, measuring it like a
bank account.
END STATE: count 1—1 on the glass; ladder up at MARO's house; both men
already reaching for the next thing.

## U2 — THE FEED, AND THE NIGHT (day → night)

FRAME: MARO's roof half gone, tiles stacked by the jar. PELL's coat hangs
open, buttons gone, his shirt tied shut with red string. BRAN paints
"9 — 7".

- MARO: "Nine tiles. Your buttons are finished, little man."
  [taunt — declares PELL spent]
- PELL: "Buttons are finished. I still have a house." [refuses to
  concede — looks at his kettle, his chair, his door]

ACTION: TILO carries PELL's kettle toward the jar (changes sides — the
street's first switch). SARA gossips the count to a growing knot of
neighbours.
NIGHT BEAT (no lines): the street dark, one lamp. MARO creeps out with a
string, fishes his FIRST tile back out of the jar, and drops it in again.
Close on the tile against the glass: it now carries TWO white chalk lines.
He grins at the jar. Nobody in the town sees it. The audience does.
END STATE: painted count 9—7; inside the jar, one tile with two marks.

## U3 — THE TILE (day)

FRAME: PELL drops his kettle in (red string on the handle). Sinking, it
knocks the double-marked tile up against the glass. PELL stops dead. He
fishes the tile out with TILO's hook and holds it high: TWO chalk lines
on one tile.

- PELL: "One tile. Two lines. It went in twice." [accusation — the
  object speaks first]
- MARO: "Paint is paint. That jar is full of my roof." [denial]
- PELL: "Judge. Count his tiles. Then count his roof." [demands the
  test]

ACTION: BRAN counts the gaps in MARO's roof, then the tiles in the jar,
holding up fingers for the street — the numbers do not match. She wipes
MARO's painted count off the glass with her sleeve. The double-marked
tile goes on the stand, in front of everyone.
REACTION BEAT: MARO's face at poster size — the grin gone. UMBER picks
his coin up and sets it on PELL's side (changes sides).
END STATE: MARO's side of the glass wiped clean; the cheating tile on
display; the street seated on PELL's side.

## U4 — THE PAYMENT (day)

FRAME: BRAN paints three small pictures on the glass: a dinner pot, a
chair, a hat. She points at MARO, at his house, at the jar.

- BRAN: "A cheat pays in things. His dinner. His chair. His hat."
  [the sentence]
- MARO: "My dinner is tonight's dinner." [protest]
- BRAN: "Then tonight, you eat air." [refuses the protest — the price
  stands]

ACTION: MARO puts in his dinner — the pot, steam still rising — then his
chair, then his hat. Each sinks. The fill rises honestly, past PELL's
things. SARA carries the pot the last two steps for him: even the gossip
helps the sentence land. Every drop lands on MARO's face (reaction beats).
TEXTURE: behind him, MARO's emptied house stands with its door open; a
small fruit family carries a tiny table in. Nobody comments.
END STATE: the jar high and honest; MARO's house empty, door open, a
fruit family's table inside it.

## U5 — THE KEY (day, fair light)

FRAME: the fill one hand below the FAIR DAY line. PELL's house is bare.
In his palm: his door key, a fresh red string tied to it. He holds it up
for the street.

- PELL: "My last thing is my door." [stakes the house itself]
- MARO: "A key sinks nowhere. It is too small." [taunt — tries to
  shrink the stake]
- PELL: "Small things sink straight." [answers — and acts]

ACTION: PELL drops the key. It sinks straight down through the fill and
TOUCHES the FAIR DAY line — the first marked thing to touch it. BRAN
rings the fair bell, hard, and paints the final count with a star beside
the key.
REACTION BEATS: PELL's face — disbelief into joy. MARO's face — he looks
at the jar, then at his own empty house. TILO holds the bell rope,
ringing it for PELL (changes sides, final switch).
END STATE: bell ringing; the red-stringed key resting on the line inside
the glass; BRAN's painted star.

## U6 — THE CARRY, AND THE KOAN (evening)

FRAME: MARO lifts the full jar onto his head — knees bending, both hands
steadying it — and carries it down the street to PELL's house. The whole
street walks with him, seated-side settled: everyone walks on PELL's
side. MARO stops at PELL's door, jar on head, breathing hard.
PELL knocks on his own door. Nothing. He knocks again. The house is
locked, and he is outside it — his key is in the jar on MARO's head.

- MARO (jar on head): "What will you put in it first?"
  [the koan question — the loser's only line]

PELL's answer is a held face at poster size. No line. He looks at the
jar — at everything he owns inside it — at his locked door.
Then MARO tips the jar, slow, careful, and the red-stringed key slides
down the glass into PELL's open hand.
FINAL FRAME: PELL holding the key high; MARO standing under the jar;
BRAN painting one last small thing on the glass — a door.
END STATE: the winner with his key, the loser under the jar, the race
paid in full.
