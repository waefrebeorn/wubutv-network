# MORPH — SHOW-1 · 'THE MACHINE' → **ON AIR**

**Status:** morphed show bible, written 2026-11-16 under the user's
msg-488 order ("use the learning and morph the shows into new shows
so you can have 5 best shows"). Successor to SHOW-1 'THE MACHINE'
(pilot + E2 shipped). This bible supersedes the founding treatment
for premise, cast shape, and episode engine; THE MACHINE's locked
scripts and ledger remain canon history the morph spends (Rule S2),
never re-litigates.

**Design inputs:** E3 Writing Addendum (Cantina C-rules, brainrot
B-rules, serial S-rules, shape-store P-rules; conflicts X1/X2/X3/X8
applied as recommended there), Writers Room Method as amended by
msg 470 (A1 word budget, A2 digestion gaps, Gate E, A4 questioning
job), and the standing user verdicts on the E2 batch: still too
esoteric (msg 475), wall-to-wall talk microwaves (msg 470),
same-subject repetition microwaves (msg 467), world-swap is a spice

## 1. NEW SHOW IDENTITY

**Title: ON AIR**

**Premise (one sentence, Gate E on its face):** A small TV crew has
until the broadcast slot fires to get one true music video on air —
while the machine that runs their station keeps slipping in videos
nobody made, sung by a singer nobody hired, and the slot airs
*something* whether they're ready or not.

A cold viewer needs nothing else: there is a deadline (the slot), a
job (air the true video), a saboteur (the machine), and a wrong
thing that is *shown*, not explained (the video that shouldn't
exist). Every episode is that sentence with a new wrong video.

## 2. WHAT CARRIES OVER — AND HOW IT IS TRANSFORMED

Keep only what earned it on screen in the pilot and E2. Everything
below was production-proven; the transformations are the morph.

- **LAURA** (carries, promoted to engine). She was the noticing
  device in a two-hander proof-ladder; she is now the show's
  driver — the crew member who works the slot. Her E2 function
  (catch the machine, prove it, price it) becomes the episode
  engine's human half. She formally holds the **questioning job**
  (A4): notices by U2, names by U4, acts in U6 — the shape E2
  already proved she carries.
- **UNC** (carries, transformed from keeper to stake). He managed
  the record; now the record is *him* — E2 ended with his mouth
  moving first, his fed word in the song, and a refile debt
  (**the folder he refused to sign**) unpaid. That debt is the
  morph's opening proof object, and his plain want (§4) replaces
  "the wrongness" as the show's emotional legibility. Deadpan,
  terse, `~ WuBu ~` hoodie, wordmark law unchanged.
- **THE SINGER — the black cat, red collar** (carries, promoted to
  star). Never seen whole, face never changes, no name is sung —
  all standing marks kept. What changes: the singer is no longer a
  mystery to be solved but a *casting problem to be managed* — the
  wrong-singer flip (the pilot's viral bet, proven) becomes a
  recurring, instantly readable event: the slot fires and the face
  is wrong. Recognition is design-level (P5/S6): collar + cat +
  the mouth.
- **PETER** (carries, promoted to regular). In the treatment he
  sold the videos; in the pilot/E2 he never appeared — and the
  room ran fine without the show's most cold-readable want. He is
  now a regular: the man who promised an advertiser a singer.
  Every machine video is his invoice coming due. Comedy engine
  and the show's lie/denial voice (B5).
- **COLONEL** (carries, re-roled as the authority, B10). He anchored
  the official version from off-screen; he now enters — rarely,
  decisively — as the one who runs the station and keeps the
  billing book. His role is his license to know: the deepest reveal
  of an episode is his, and it is always paper (invoices, credits,
  call sheets), never lore (B14).
- **THE DEAD CHANNEL → THE BROADCAST STRIP** (device carries,
  transformed). E2's cobalt strip was the waveform of a dead
  channel, live from frame 0. It is now the **on-air waveform**:
  the live strip of what is actually broadcasting, frame-bottom,
  true cobalt, every episode. New legible rule a viewer learns in
  one episode: *when the machine is leading, the strip runs a beat
  ahead of the picture.* The device states the episode's power
  fact continuously and silently — and per Conflict X2, no strip
  fact is ever the cliff by itself: a person must accuse, confess,
  or be betrayed by what the strip shows, in the same unit.
- **THE MOUTH HANDOFF** (device carries, demoted from metronome to
  signature). It chained every joint in the pilot/E2 — and the
  user's verdict on metronome devices is standing. It now fires
  exactly **twice per episode**: the broadcast cut *into* the aired
  segment (the monitor's singing mouth becomes the video's mouth,
  full-frame) and the cut *back out* — plus at a bridge cliff when
  earned. Spent twice, it reads as the show's logo move instead of
  its wallpaper.
- **Palette and picture bible** (carry unchanged): INK black /
  CADMIUM yellow-gold / COBALT screen-blue / FOREST green accent;
  monitor-glow key + one warm practical; locked or slow-push
  camera; slight codec softness, never ad-clean. Production-proven
  across two episodes; not a thing the verdicts touched.
- **The 10-second world rule** (carries, demoted from premise to
  texture). The crew still works in ten-second shifts because the
  slot clock only guarantees ten seconds — but nobody explains the
  cull, ever. It is simply how the job works, visible in the clock
  and the unit shape. The lore tax is removed; the rhythm stays.
- **E2's unpaid debts** (carry as assets, S2): the refile folder
  (payoff: morph E1), UNC's fed word in the song (payoff: midseason,
  §7), the monitor opening unled (the strip's lead, now a standing
  visual rule instead of a one-off cliff).

## 3. WHAT IS KILLED — AND WHY

- **The slow-dread register** ("episodes no longer need a joke —
  they need a wrongness"). Killed by msg 475 directly: dread
  without a surface story is the esotericism the user rejected.
  Replaced by deadline comedy-drama: the situations are mundane
  and funny (C4) — a video with the wrong singer, a video that
  airs itself early, a boss credited as his own singer — and the
  wrongness rides inside them, shown on the monitor, never brooded
  over.
- **The two-hander proof-ladder spine** (LAURA proves a metaphysical
  claim to UNC across an episode; pilot and E2 both). Killed: it is
  the exact shape that produced "esoteric babbling" — one claim,
  argued six ways, no event. The morph's episodes are *jobs with
  deadlines*, and proof is an object on a desk (B8), not a syllogism.
- **Unidentified sung words and never-named mysteries as the cliff
  engine.** Killed as an engine (one seed survives in §7, payable
  and dated). Cliffs are now social + deadline (X2): what aired,
  who got credited, who got sung, what the call sheet already says.
- **DISPATCHER as on-screen cast.** Killed: two episodes shipped
  without her; the room never missed the function. Her one useful
  beat — reporting what the building filed — is absorbed by the
  Broadcast Strip and COLONEL's paper.
- **"The wrongness is learning the crew's names" as the premise.**
  Killed as premise, kept as one midseason payoff (§7). A premise
  a cold viewer cannot repeat fails Gate E; a *beat* where the
  singer sings a name lands precisely because the premise no longer
  asks the viewer to hold it.

## 4. CAST

Speaking cast per episode: **three + the authority** (Conflict X3
cap; COLONEL's reveal entrance is the declared exception shape).
Character cards are Identity / Personality / Backstory per C1;
dialogue is written from the Personality layer; Response Styles
per C6 are enforced in the script.

- **LAURA** — *Want, in plain words:* **get the true video on air
  and get UNC's name off the singer's file.** Personality: first to
  look, fastest to say it; prosecutes with objects, not speeches.
  Response Style: **Medium**. Signature entrance line (C9): "Show
  me what aired." **Questioning-job holder** (A4): notices the
  wrongness by U2, names it in one short line by U4, acts on it in
  U6 — every episode, on the episode's own wrongness, not the
  season's.
- **UNC** — *Want:* **one quiet shift where nothing with his mouth
  in it airs.** Personality: deadpan, certain, files problems
  instead of answering them — until the file has his name on it.
  Response Style: **Short** (the terse pole, C6). Signature
  entrance line: "Cut to the mouth." (His E2 catchphrase, now
  ironic: the mouth is his.)
- **PETER** — *Want:* **the advertiser renews.** Personality: sells
  first, understands later; every disaster is a feature until the
  invoice arrives. Response Style: **Long** (the rambler pole, C6 —
  his selling patter is the show's sanctioned word-spender, inside
  the unit totals per X10). Signature entrance line: "Good news
  about the singer—"
- **COLONEL** — *Want:* **the station's version is the true one —
  and stays billable.** The authority (B10): runs the station,
  keeps the book, delivers the deepest reveal, always from paper.
  Enters at most one unit per episode, default U5. Response Style:
  **Short**, declarative.
- **THE SINGER** (non-speaking) — *Want:* shown, never explained —
  to keep singing. Sings the aired segment; the face never changes;
  the flip, when it comes, is the episode's loudest silent beat.

**One shared wrong belief** (A4): the whole crew believes the
machine can only air what someone in the room has fed it. The
viewer can see the strip running ahead — the belief is false, and
every episode's turn is the crew acting on it one beat too late.

## 5. EPISODE ENGINE

**THE AIRING** — a fixed ritual situation (S1) that generates the
episode; the ritual recurs, the wrongness rotates (B13).

1. **The slot is set.** Cold open or U1: the ON AIR light, the slot
  clock, and the video that is supposed to air — a card, a folder,
  a file, named in plain words.
2. **The video is wrong — visibly.** One proof object (B8/B9): the
  wrong face mid-note, a credit with the wrong name, a call sheet
  dated tomorrow, a folder signed by a man who refused to sign it.
  The wrongness must read from the picture before anyone speaks.
3. **The crew works the slot.** Accusation with proof → denial →
  the fix attempted on camera (C7's physical moment lives here:
  the tape pulled, the fader slammed, the cat crossing the
  console). The deadline is the slot clock, always on screen or
  one cut away.
4. **The slot fires.** Ready or not, something airs — the true
  video, the wrong one, or the true one *changed*. The aired
  segment (§6) is the episode's center of gravity.
5. **The consequence begins, the episode doesn't finish it** (B3):
  cut at the peak on the reaction, end on an open question (B11);
  consequences are next episode's slot problem. Escalation ladder
  across the season (S7): each episode's wrongness is exactly one
  notch worse than the last (§7).

Familiar skeletons only (S3): the deadline shift, the show must go
on, the substitute who isn't, the invoice come due, the tape that
airs itself. The plot type announces itself in the first seconds;
the episode just executes it. Secrets are sourced from lies,
money, jobs, promises, and mistaken identity — never bodies, never
lore (B14).

## 6. FORMAT GRAMMAR — HOW THE ADDENDUM LANDS HERE

Network format unchanged: 60s episodes, 6 units × 10s, 16:9, one
conversation across the episode, pairs close by 8.5s, the cliff
word — when a word is the cliff — is SUNG, never spoken (Method B
Conflict 4, kept).

- **Word budget (A1):** episode total **80–90 words, hard cap 95**,
  per-unit ceiling 20, per line ≤12, ≤14 lines. House template:
  U1 full-talk 18 · U2 mixed 15 · U3 quiet 8 · U4 full-talk 19 ·
  U5 mixed 14 · U6 cliff 12 + hold = **86**. Response Style (C6)
  distributes *inside* the unit totals (X10): PETER rambles, UNC
  clips, neither eats the other's silence.
- **Digestion gaps (A2, carriers re-specced per X1):** three
  scripted 2–3s gaps at the fixed placements — (i) after U2's turn:
  a **held beat on the proof object** (the genre's reaction shot —
  the folder, the credit, the wrong face, room sound only);
  (ii) after U4's peak turn: a **held reaction face** (UNC's, when
  the peak is his — it usually is); (iii) the final 2–3s of U6:
  **the freeze** (B3) — ON AIR light + the singer's face, the cliff
  lands, then the episode breathes. Pass 4 verifies gap seconds
  like word counts; a gap with no declared carrier does not count.
- **Dialogue mechanics (B5/B6/C3/C5):** only the four line-types —
  accusation-with-proof, lie/denial, entrance-that-contradicts,
  authority reveal — plus the want-statement. Every character's
  first line in their first scene states their want in words a
  cold viewer can repeat (B6). Relationships are declared on the
  cards before a line is written (C3): LAURA–UNC (she prosecutes,
  he files), UNC–PETER (Peter sold him), LAURA–COLONEL (she wants
  the truth aired; he wants it billable). Every line is spoken to
  someone in the room, about something on the desk or the screen —
  never to camera, never as explanation (B7/C5).
- **THE AIRED SEGMENT (the MV angle, made structural):** one unit
  per episode — default **U3, the quiet unit** — the slot fires
  inside the episode and the show cuts to *the video itself*:
  full-frame music video, word-synced, song as tempo gospel,
  entered and exited by the mouth handoff. Built with the
  addendum's visual-form energy: **layer-stack / rescore density**
  (stacked visual layers riding the track's sections; the week's
  real WuBu MV material is the feedstock, P9) at MAXIMALIST
  density — and it is the episode's *quietest dialogue* unit
  (8 words), because the picture is doing the shouting. This is
  how the show holds maximalist density and digestion at once:
  the loudest unit has the fewest words. The segment's content is
  also the episode's evidence: what airs is what the next units
  argue about.
- **BLAM opening shape:** U1 frame 0 is never a room settling in.
  The impact by 1.0s is **the broadcast starting wrong**: ON AIR
  light fires, the big monitor is already mid-video at full sound,
  the singer mid-word — with the wrongness visible in the first
  frame (wrong face, wrong credit, wrong mouth) — and the Method C
  device (the Broadcast Strip) is live and *leading* from frame 0.
  First spoken line lands inside U1 as an accusation with the
  proof already on screen (B2's hook: an accusation already in
  flight). No fade-in, no crackle, no ramp: the slot does not
  ramp, and neither do we.
- **Cliffs (B11/X2):** every cliff is a fact from the device or the
  paper **carried by a social beat in the same unit** — someone
  accuses, confesses, or is betrayed by it — and the episode's
  last line is an **open question** a viewer can type into a
  comment. Resolved endings fail.
- **Recap (B12):** episodes after the first open with one line of
  recap spoken as an accusation or complaint by a character
  ("You let it air my mouth—"), never narration, never a card.

## 7. SEASON SEED

The escalation ladder (S7), one notch per episode: wrong singer →
wrong credit → UNC's voice in the song → a video of tomorrow's
shift, aired today. The refile folder is paid in the premiere and
comes back signed — in UNC's hand — before the credits. Midseason,
the singer sings a name (the old PL-4 plant, paid at last): the
name is UNC's, sung in a video nobody fed it, and COLONEL's book
says the advertiser has been billed for "the singer" all along.
Final image, held from THE MACHINE's projection because it earned
it: the slot fires, the perfect video airs to an empty chair, the
strip runs level with the picture for the first time all season —
the machine no longer needs anyone in the room, and the show
refuses to say whether that is the cure or the cull.

## 8. FIRST EPISODE OUTLINE — "The Signed Folder"

Premiere. The refile debt from E2 comes due on air night. Speaking
cast: LAURA, UNC, PETER (U1–U4) + COLONEL as authority (U5).
Budgets: 18 · 15 · 8 · 19 · 14 · 12 = **86 words**. Gaps marked ◆.

- **U1 — FULL-TALK, 18w.** BLAM frame 0: the slot fires, ON AIR
  light, the big monitor already mid-video at full sound — the cat
  singing with **UNC's mouth** (the wrong-singer flip, inverted),
  strip running ahead. LAURA holds up the refile folder — now
  **signed in UNC's handwriting** — accusation with proof: that's
  your mouth, and that's your signature. UNC denies both. *(Hook:
  accusation already in flight, proof already on screen.)*
- **U2 — MIXED, 15w.** The crew works the slot: the wrong video
  airs again at the top of the hour unless they swap it. PETER
  enters mid-patter — "Good news about the singer—" — and his
  entrance contradicts the denial: the advertiser has *already
  been sent* the new singer. LAURA notices the strip leading the
  picture and says so (questioning job: notice). ◆ **GAP 1** —
  2–3s held on the signed folder, room sound only.
- **U3 — QUIET, 8w. THE AIRED SEGMENT.** LAURA cues the true take;
  UNC's want stated plain as the frame for the cut: "One shift.
  Nothing with my mouth in it." The mouth handoff takes us **into
  the video** — 10s full-frame MV, layer-stack density, the cat
  singing the week's song, word-synced — and back out on the same
  mouth. Almost no dialogue; the picture shouts.
- **U4 — FULL-TALK, 19w.** The peak clash: the swap "worked" — and
  the aired video's on-screen credit reads **SINGER: UNC** (proof
  object: the credit itself). PETER's denial collapses; LAURA
  names it (questioning job: name): "It isn't copying you. It's
  crediting you." ◆ **GAP 2** — 2–3s held reaction on UNC's face,
  strip still leading underneath.
- **U5 — MIXED, 14w.** COLONEL enters with the billing book
  (authority reveal, B10): the station has billed the advertiser
  for "the singer" for months — and every invoice names UNC.
  PETER's want collapses in one line: he didn't sell a singer, he
  sold his boss. LAURA turns the book toward the camera-side desk:
  the next invoice is already printed — dated tomorrow.
- **U6 — CLIFF, 12w + hold.** LAURA acts (questioning job: act):
  she pulls tomorrow's slot and airs the true video **by hand,
  off-grid** — the one human-fired slot. It works. Then, over the
  closing frame, the strip starts leading again, and the monitor
  shows tomorrow's call sheet — already filled in. Last line, an
  open question (B11), UNC's only long line of the episode:
  "Then who already aired us?" ◆ **GAP 3** — final 2–3s freeze:
  ON AIR light + the cat's face, no dialogue. *Bridge: tomorrow's
  call sheet is episode 2's slot card.*
