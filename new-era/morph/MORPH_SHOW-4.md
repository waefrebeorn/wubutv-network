## 0. POST-GUTTING ENGINE (2026-11-24)

**The engine, in one breath:** *somebody claims a number; the office proves it
or breaks it with a physical test; physics delivers the verdict.*

This section governs. It implements the gutting map's transplant for this show
(parent rulings, 2026-11-24; the slate constraints live in SLATE.md). Where a
section below conflicts, the passage is marked SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24 and kept

**RIPPED IN** — Boss Bee's MATCHED VERDICT PAIR (BB-A2) + Stickocalypse's
concrete-numbers discipline (T13):

- **Every episode is a CLAIM SETTLED BY A TEST.** A customer claims a number —
  the load will fill the rack, float the boat, stack to the line — and the
  office doesn't argue; it TESTS, on camera, with the count as a scoreboard,
  not a puzzle.
- **The verdict is delivered by physics in a matched pair:** the honest load
  performs at 150%; the padded claim is held at zero — "does not move at all"
  is the funniest sentence in the corpus, and it is a prompt, not a line.
- **The board becomes a scoreboard** — it prices the claim and the test; it no
  longer carries a puzzle.

**GUTTED (named):**

- **The count-along promise** — the viewer will never count 31 umbrellas, and

- **The hidden-shelf season arc as a story carrier** — the plot stored in the
  frame's corner during the pauses is cut.
- **The guaranteed outcome** — "one short, every time, by definition" is cut
  as the episode's foregone conclusion.

**KEPT:** the two-person office; the Counter and the Clerk; the board (as

**The missing one survives as a one-beat SIGHT GAG**, not an arc: the viewer
SEES where it went — an invader walks out wearing it, deadpan, unremarked by
the cast. (Msg-583 shown in a single beat: the gentrification is petty theft
at the counting office; it costs the office its perfect record over a season

**Episode grammar under the new engine:**

1. **The claim is stated** — a number, in the customer's first line, with
   money, a job, or a promise riding on it.
2. **The office tests it on camera** — the physical test is the episode's

3. **The matched verdict pair lands** — honest load at 150%, padded claim held

## 1. NEW SHOW IDENTITY

**Title: ONE SHORT**

**Premise (one sentence, Gate E on its face):** A two-person
counting office counts whatever the town brings through its door —
wedding chairs, umbrellas, a man's whole stamp collection — and
every single count comes up exactly one short, and nobody behind
the counter will say where the one goes.

Cold-viewer test: who (the Clerk and the Counter), what they want
(count the load, close the book even), what blocks them (the count
is always one short), what changes (a customer pushes back, the
book opens, the shelf grows). No lore terms. No station, no cull,
no roster. A shop, a counter, a number that won't behave.

**Register:** deadpan bureau comedy played completely straight —
the brainrot lesson (§2 of the addendum): the situation is absurd,
the feelings are not. Escalation is dark, one notch per episode
(S7), and always about the missing one.

**Format:** network standard — 60s episodes, 6 units × 10s, 16:9,
one master. Numbered episodes, one fixed title pattern (S9):
**"ONE SHORT — Ep. N: [the load]"** ("Ep. 1: Thirty-One
Umbrellas"). A cold viewer can reconstruct the series from titles
alone.

## 2. WHAT CARRIES OVER FROM THE COUNT — AND HOW IT IS TRANSFORMED

Only what earned it carries. THE COUNT's proven assets were its
two people, its arithmetic discipline, and its device. Its lore
frame earned nothing and is gone (§3).

**THE TALLY CLERK → THE CLERK, keeper of the counting office.**
Earned: in E2 the combined two-character anchor held her identity
in all six units — the pilot's central production failure (the
Clerk re-invented, older and cardiganed, in every unanchored call)
was solved on her. She is also the show's natural authority figure
(addendum B10: the one who keeps the book delivers the deepest
reveal). Transformed: from audit shadow standing at a desk in a
hall to the proprietor behind a counter — the office is *hers*, the
book is *hers*, the price of reopening it (E2's F-016, unpaid) is
now simply how the office does business: the book opens when she
decides, and she charges for it in concessions. Look unchanged —
sharp black bob, round spectacles, bone-white blouse, ink-black
vest, cobalt-blue ink on her fingertips. Cobalt stays her accent.

**SUPER NUMBER HEAD → THE COUNTER.** Earned: the deadpan, the
gaunt look, the brass tally counter on a chain, and the one great
flaw — he stepped backward once (1003→1002, F-006) and ordered it
kept secret. Transformed: the mystic title is killed (§3); he is a
man with a job title a child understands. He works the floor side
of the counter and counts aloud, in batches, always audible — the
count is now of *things the viewer can see being counted*, never
of abstractions. His secret carries as his shame, not as lore: he
is the counter who once lost count, in an office where the count
is always lost by one.

**THE SPLIT TOTAL device → the split-flap board over the counter.**
Earned: it passed every graphics audit in E2 (arithmetic exact at
every spot check, legible at phone size) — it was the best-behaved
element of the show. Transformed: from a corner graphic into the
office's physical furniture — a two-window split-flap board,
**COUNTED | CLAIMED**, mounted over the counter where customers
can see it. The Method C graphic layer keeps its E2 discipline
(events keyed to measured beats, FOREST #1F6B3A accent, furniture
bottom-left, bug bottom-right), but the board is now *in the
world*: characters look up at it, customers point at it, and per
addendum conflict X2 the device only ever supplies the fact — a
character supplies the cliff in the same unit. The board's two
numbers disagreeing by one IS the show in one image.

**THE DOOR → the office door, with a bell.** Earned: the door was
E2's whole stage machinery (opened itself, sealed itself, carried
two joints). Transformed: from dread portal to shop door. It is
the premise-engine ritual (S1): **bell → door → customer → load →
claim.** The ritual recurs every episode; the customer and the
load rotate. And the door is the BLAM fix (§6): a shop door does
not creak open on dread — it *slams*, with a bell, under an armful
of merchandise.

**THE GAP (her +1) → the office's founding defect.** Earned: two
episodes of canon (F-001, F-013, PL-1) and the only mystery the
audience could actually state. Transformed: universalized and
made visible. It is no longer "her book vs his board"; it is
*every count this office has ever run*, and it has a physical
address — **the shelf** (see §5, the shared wrong belief). The
skipped number of the old season projection survives as the
season's endgame (§7), restated in plain words: one day they will
count the office itself. **SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24 (as story carrier):** the
shelf and the hidden-shortfall arc are gutted as plot machinery per §0;
the missing one survives only as the one-beat sight gag (§0).

**THE NUMBER HANDOFF → the subtotal chain.** Earned: the
spoken→shown increment discipline was the old show's signature
craft and its arithmetic never once cheated on screen.
Transformed: the chain now counts *the load*. Each unit's cliff is
the board's new counted subtotal, spoken aloud; the next unit
opens on that subtotal on the board and the next batch of the load
in frame. Same discipline, but the numbers are stapled to visible
objects — a cold viewer can count along and catch the missing one
themselves. (The old show's viral bet — "count with us" — is now
the show's literal mechanics.)

**SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24:** the count-along promise is gutted per §0 — the
viewer will never count 31 umbrellas, and the show stops selling that it
can. The subtotal chain survives as scoreboard discipline for the test
(§0), not as a count-along mechanic.

**Palette:** INK + BONE + CADMIUM + the Clerk's COBALT accent,
FOREST reserved for the Split Total — carried unchanged. It was
kit-locked, it reads at phone size, and the office (bone paper,
ink suits, cadmium board-glow) is the palette's natural home.

**Production law carried:** the combined two-character anchor
panel per unit + the Clerk's CHAR sheet on every call she appears
in. This is non-negotiable morph inheritance — it is the proven
fix for this show's specific identity failure, and the morph keeps
the two-hander shape that made it work.

## 3. WHAT IS KILLED, AND WHY

- **The station / ARC-3 lore frame** — the cull, the roster, "the
  count completes when the station has earned its viewer," the
  world rebuilding every ten seconds as a story fact. *Why:* it is
  the esoterica the user judged twice (msgs 470/475). A cold
  viewer cannot say what a cull is; a cold viewer knows what a
  shop counter is. The 10-second world-swap also dies as a story
  beat (msg 467: scene swap is a spice, not a metronome) — the
  office does not swap; it *accumulates* (§5), which is the Gossip
  Goblin compounding mechanic pointed the other way: sameness you
  can watch filling up.
- **"SUPER NUMBER HEAD" as a name.** *Why:* a title that needs a
  wiki. He is THE COUNTER. The look stays; the mythology goes.
- **The satellite cast (PETER, UNC, LAURA, DISPATCHER).** *Why:*
  in two episodes they never appeared — pointer cards only — and
  the addendum's cast discipline (X3) caps speaking cast at three
  per episode. The show is a two-hander plus one customer. UNC
  stays network property, not this show's.
- **The abstract count** (counting viewers, seconds, "the
  audience"). *Why:* you cannot catch a missing viewer. Every load
  in ONE SHORT is countable on screen by the viewer personally.
- **The locked-camera, no-move-in-the-first-12-frames rule.**
  *Why:* it is the structural cause of the E2 BLAM A1 failure —
  a show forbidden to move or speak early cannot put half its
  opening energy in the first half-second. Replaced by the
  door-slam opening shape (§6). Locked coverage remains the
  office's *default* grammar mid-episode (deadpan suits stillness
  once the count is running); it is a style now, not a cage.
- **Dread-first tone.** *Why:* dread was the old show's ceiling —
  it read as atmosphere where the genre reads as story. The
  missing one is played as comedy and grievance first; the dread
  is demoted to the season's last-beat undertow (§7), earned by
  accumulation instead of announced by lighting.

## 4. CAST

Three speaking chairs per episode, maximum (X3): two fixed, one
rotating.

**THE CLERK** — keeper of the counting office; the book is hers.
- *Want, in plain words:* to close the book even by closing time,
  and to never once be the one who was wrong.
- *Voice:* Response Style **Medium** (C6) — complete sentences,
  prices, verdicts. The office register is deadpan bureau-speak
  (S4); her carrier line, the show's audio logo: **"We count
  what's brought."** Catchphrase (S5): **"The book doesn't
  blink."**
- *Authority license (B10):* she keeps the book, so she delivers
  each episode's deepest reveal — what the book says about every
  count before this one.

**THE COUNTER** — counts the loads aloud, floor side.
- *Want, in plain words:* to finish one count — just one — that
  comes out even.
- *Voice:* Response Style **Short** (C6) — numbers, fragments,
  flat denials. Catchphrase: **"Again."** (his whole personality
  in one word: every recount, every episode.) Signature entrance
  line (C9): he doesn't enter; he is always already there,
  counting something small. His first line of any episode is a
  number.
- *Flaw (full trait set, anti-Flanderization):* he once lost
  count and hid it (the back-step). He is not "the counting
  man"; he is a man whose one professional shame is the office's
  one professional fact.
- **QUESTIONING-JOB HOLDER (A4):** the Counter. He is the one
  whose hands are on the load, so he is the one who notices the
  pattern — *notices* by U2 (the count runs one light and he
  feels it before the board shows it), *names* it by U4 (one
  short line, in words: "Every pile comes up one light. Every
  one."), *acts* in U6 (he aims the count somewhere new — the
  shelf, the office, himself). Per addendum X8, the questioning
  job rides the accusation chain: he is its accuser-of-the-office
  from inside it.

**THE CUSTOMER** — one per episode, rotating, never recurring
within a season except the Inspector (§7).
- A borrowed real-world archetype dropped in for free cold-viewer
  context (S8), played as deadpan parody of the archetype — one
  trait exaggerated, everything else flat (P4): a wedding planner
  on a deadline, a census taker who counts people for a living, a
  landlord with a ring of identical keys, a game-show host with a
  case of numbered balls, a bride.
- *Want, in plain words:* stated in their first line (B6) —
  always a number they need to be true ("Thirty-one umbrellas.
  Count them before noon."), because their money, job, or promise
  is riding on it (B14: secrets and lies are sourced from lies,
  money, jobs, promises, mistaken identity — never bodies).
- *Voice:* Response Style **Medium**, plain modern speech — the
  customer is the viewer's surrogate and the only person in the
  room who talks like the audience.

**The shared wrong belief (A4):** both the Clerk and the Counter
believe — and say — that *customers* always miscount by one. The
viewer can see they are wrong: **one item from every load ends up
on the shelf behind the Clerk**, in frame, unremarked, episode
after episode. The shelf is visible in the office's CCTV-corner
angle (the show's declared camera uniform, §6) during the
digestion gaps. The cast never looks at it. The audience counts
it. That is design-is-the-plot (S6) and the visible secret (B9)
in a single piece of furniture — and it is the asset mountain
(S2): the shelf fills across the season, and everything on it is
a previous episode the viewer can point to.

**SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24 (as story carrier):** the shelf-as-arc is gutted
per §0. The shelf may remain as office furniture; nothing is stored in it
as plot. The missing one is now the one-beat sight gag (§0).

## 5. EPISODE ENGINE

**The ritual (S1), fixed:** bell → door slams → customer enters
under a load → want + claimed number stated in the first line →
the office counts the load in batches across the units → the
count comes up one short → **one accusation chain** (B1), and only
one: the customer's proof (a receipt, a list, a photo — the proof
object shown before it is explained, B8) → the office's denial
(the board, the book) → the entrance/reveal that breaks the
denial (the Clerk opens the book: every count in office history,
one short) → the Split Total settles COUNTED ≠ CLAIMED → freeze
on the customer's unanswered question, on screen (B11).

**SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24:** this engine is replaced by §0's claim-and-test
grammar (claim stated → physical test on camera → matched verdict pair →
verdict ending). The ritual's opening beats (bell → door slam → claim in
the first line) are KEPT and stand.

**What rotates (B13):** the load, the archetype, the lie. The
grammar never rotates. Loads are chosen so their nature generates
the twist mechanically (S6): umbrellas (one is the planner's own,
from the door — mistaken identity), wedding chairs (one is the
venue's), keys (one opens the office's own back door), numbered
balls (one is last week's winning ball — a job lie), stamps (one
is a forgery the customer is desperate to have counted as real —
money). If two consecutive episodes use the same lie-type, the
second is rewritten (B13).

**What accumulates (S2):** the shelf (+1 item per episode,
always), the book (the Clerk writes each episode's shortfall in
it, on screen, during U5), and the Counter's noticing (his A4 arc
is serial: each episode he is one episode closer to saying it to
a customer's face).

**Recap (B12):** from Ep. 2, U1 carries the Clerk's one-line
complaint-recap, spoken to the Counter as the bell rings —
*"Yesterday a woman swore blind she'd brought thirty-one
umbrellas—"* — never narration, always grievance.

**Known plot skeletons (S3):** every episode is a skeleton the
viewer already owns — the short count, the audit, the debt come
due, the new hire (a customer who wants the Counter's job), the
inspection. The plot type announces itself in the first seconds;
the episode just executes it.

## 6. FORMAT GRAMMAR — how the addendum lands in this show

**The BLAM opening (the A1 fix, by design).** E2 failed A1
(t50 1.1s / t90 2.5s vs bars 0.5s / 1.5s) structurally: a quiet
door event, one flat line, a protected silence — the energy was
never in the first half-second and no trim could put it there.
ONE SHORT's U1 is built so the energy cannot be anywhere else:
- **Frame 0 is mid-slam.** The door is already swinging, the bell
  is ringing, and the load is already crashing onto the counter —
  three sound events (slam, bell, crash) stacked inside 0.5s,
  carrying the head energy A1 measures.
- **The want-line starts at ~0.5s**, the customer mid-sentence
  and mid-stride (B2 Hook: an accusation or demand already in
  flight; B6: the want is the first line). Speech onset is
  scripted into the impact, not after it.
- **The Split Total flips at ~0.3s** keyed to the slam (E1 named
  impact ≤1.0s; the Method C device fires inside the first
  second, as on the E2 build's other shows).
- No fade-in, no locked first 12 frames, no tableau. The camera
  pushes in *with* the load. A1 passes by construction or the
  take is not a take of this show.

**Word budget (A1 amendments + B4):** 80–90 words per episode,
hard cap 95, per-unit ceiling 20; lines 8–15 words, one speaker
per turn, at ~2.5 words/second. Only the four line-types plus the
want-statement are allowed (B5): accusation-with-proof,
lie/denial, entrance-that-contradicts, authority reveal. A line
doing none of those jobs is deleted, not rewritten.

**Digestion gaps (A2, re-specced per X1):** three scripted gaps,
carried by genre beats instead of pauses —
1. *After U2's turn:* a held reaction beat — the Counter's hand
   stops over the pile; he has felt the shortfall (his A4 notice)
   and says nothing. The board shows one behind the pile.
2. *After U4's peak:* the CCTV-corner angle holds on the shelf —
   one new item standing on it, unremarked. The viewer does the
   arithmetic the cast won't.
3. *U6's final 2–3s:* the freeze cliffhanger (B3) — the reveal
   has landed, the question is on screen, nobody moves.

**Dialogue mechanics (Cantina family):** character cards split
Identity / Personality / Backstory, dialogue written from the
Personality layer (C1); shared-context brief capped at ~1,000
characters — it is the office, the counter, the book, the shelf,
and who owes whom (C2); relationships assigned before lines (C3):
Clerk owns, Counter serves and doubts, Customer needs. One
physical moment per unit (C7) — the count itself supplies it:
a dropped batch, a chair that won't stack, a key tried in the
wrong lock. One interruption per episode, at a declared point
only (C8/X5) — a customer talking over the count is the natural
one. Banter is mined in a Pass 3 pre-step (C10/X4); the lock
stays verbatim.

**The cliff (X2 + B11):** the Split Total supplies the fact; a
character supplies the cliff. Every episode ends on the
customer's spoken open question with the question on screen —
*"Then where does the one GO?"* — over the freeze. A bare number
change with no human reaction is a failed cliff, full stop.

**SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24:** episodes now end on the physical verdict and
the loser's reaction per §0 (parent law exception, recorded in SLATE.md),
not on a freeze question. The board still supplies the fact; physics
supplies the ending.

**Camera/finish uniform (P6, declared per X7):** the office is
shot like the store it is — counter-wide as the master angle, a
CCTV corner angle that owns the shelf, one close angle for the
book. One grain/grade pass over everything (the old show's fine-
grain law carries). 16:9 throughout; the shape-store's 4:3 is
not imported (X7).

**Joint grammar:** units chain on the subtotal (§2): cliff speaks
the subtotal, next unit opens on it shown. The world does not
swap at joints — the office persists, the pile shrinks, the shelf
grows. Motion-cliff take gate and end-on-motion discipline from
the standing production brief apply unchanged.

## 7. SEASON SEED (Season 1, 8 episodes)

**SUPERSEDED 2026-11-24:** the season ladder below is built on the gutted
shelf arc and the guaranteed one-short outcome. Kept for history; the
season under §0 escalates by the size of the claim and the cost of the
verdict.

The loads escalate one notch per episode (S7) — umbrellas, chairs,
keys, balls, stamps — and so do the lies, from honest miscounts to
a customer who *needs* the office to be wrong. Midseason, a
customer counts the shelf while the Clerk's back is turned and
finds the shelf itself one short of the episodes aired: something
takes from the office too. The finale brings the one returning
customer, **the Inspector** (S8), who audits the office — and the
last count of the season is the office itself: two people, one
book, one board, one shelf. It comes up one short. The Counter
finally counts himself, out loud, and the season ends on the
question of who in the room was never counted.
