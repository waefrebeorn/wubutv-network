# MORPH — SHOW-2 · 'THE TENTH SECOND' → **HOLD ON**

**Status:** morphed show bible, written 2026-11-16 under the user's
msg-488 order ("use the learning and morph the shows into new shows
so you can have 5 best shows and morph shows") and the msg-490 north
star ("focus on making the best most engaging" — when design choices
compete, engagement wins). Successor to SHOW-2 'THE TENTH SECOND'
(pilot + E2 shipped). This bible supersedes the founding treatment
for premise, cast shape, and episode engine; THE TENTH SECOND's
locked scripts and ledger remain canon history the morph spends
(Rule S2), never re-litigates.

**Design inputs:** E3 Writing Addendum (Cantina C-rules, brainrot
B-rules, serial S-rules, shape-store P-rules; conflicts X1/X2/X3/X8
applied as recommended there), Writers Room Method as amended by
msg 470 (A1 word budget, A2 digestion gaps, Gate E, A4 questioning
job), and the standing user verdicts on this show: E2's pacing
praised but "they repeated the subject so much that it microwaves
the user watching" and scene swaps "should be used in tenser
moments" (msg 467); the E2 batch "still too esoteric" — plain
surface story, actual dialogue (msg 475); wall-to-wall talk
microwaves (msg 470); characters get smarter and question their
environment (msg 470); FIRST-SECOND + BLAM; MAXIMALIST density.

**Primary lens:** the BRAINROT SOAP format grammar — talking-people
melodrama played completely straight: stated wants, one accusation
chain, a visible proof object, a lie broken by an entrance, an
authority reveal, a freeze on an open question. THE TENTH SECOND's
world (the ten-second reset) is kept as the stage; its lore is
demoted from subject to rule-of-the-game, the way gravity is a rule
of football and never its plot.

## 1. NEW SHOW IDENTITY

**Title: HOLD ON**

**Premise (one sentence, Gate E on its face):** The world restarts
every ten seconds and changes everything — except whatever you're
holding, which stays exactly as it was — so in this town every
debt, theft, and love affair is fought at the chalk wall that keeps
the town's only record, over one question anyone can understand:
*what will you be caught holding when the bell rings?*

A cold viewer needs nothing else: there is a bell, a wall that
decides what's true, a thing in somebody's hand that proves or
ruins them, and a person who wants their version written down.
Every episode is that sentence with a new hand, a new object, and
a new lie.

**Title-as-contract (A3 companion rule):** episode titles are the
wall's docket entries — the want itself, in the accuser's words:
"Write That He Owes Me." "Take Her Name Off His." "Write That
We're Married." A viewer scrolling titles alone can reconstruct
the season (Rule S9).

## 2. WHAT CARRIES OVER — AND HOW IT IS TRANSFORMED

Only what earned it. THE TENTH SECOND was the network's BLAM
champion (the only pilot to pass; E2 passed all seven bars,
dialogue verbatim, pacing praised by the user) — the morph keeps
its engine room and replaces its story.

**THE CARRY → the law of evidence.** The pilot's transition device
(a held object survives the cut, same hand, new world) was the
most legible thing the show owned. It stops being a transition
gimmick and becomes the plot mechanism itself (Rule S6,
design-is-the-plot): what you're holding at the bell is the only
proof that crosses the reset, so every grab, palm, swap, and
refusal-to-open-a-hand is a story beat a cold viewer reads from
the picture alone (Rule B9). The joint grammar is unchanged —
carry present and correct on both sides of every cut — but now
the carry is also the evidence the episode is *about*.

**THE CHALK WALL → the town's public record.** In E2 the wall was
a memory machine wrapped in lore (it "finishes names," marks get
"culled"). Transformed: the wall is simply the town's court,
register, and debt book in one — the only writing that survives
the reset, so what the wall says is what the town treats as true,
and everyone in town knows a man holds the chalk. No metaphysics:
a wall, a record, a keeper, and a queue of people who need their
version on it. The wall's E2-completed fact spends forward per S2:
LAURA's name stands finished on it from E2's cliff — in the morph
that reads socially, not mystically: the town believes the wall
*chose* her, a status she never asked for and must defend.

**LAURA → the chalk-hand with eyes.** E2's new hire asked the
treatment's metaphysical question ("which version of us is
real?") and the show drowned in it. Transformed: Laura is the
Watcher's assistant — she physically writes the docket — and her
drama is concrete: she *sees what people are holding* while the
wall only hears what they say. She holds the questioning job
(§4). Her want, in plain words: **to write one true thing on the
wall.** Her signature line (Rule S5): "I saw what you were
holding."

**THE COURIER → the Holder for hire.** The parcel loop was the
repetition trap (one parcel, two episodes, six units of custody
variations — the exact subject the user said microwaved him).
Transformed: the Courier is the town's professional Holder —
people pay her to hold things across the bell (a ring so it still
exists tomorrow, a letter so it can't be denied, a key so a debt
stays real). Her record pride survives as a boast the town quotes
(Rule S5): "Four hundred bells. Never dropped one." She is
Laura's assigned rival (Rule C3): **objects vs. words** — the
Courier believes a held thing is the only truth; Laura writes
words for a living and is starting to agree with her, which is
the problem. A parcel may appear as *one* episode's proof object,
never twice (Rule B13); the delivery job is gone (§3).

**THE WATCHER → the authority of the wall.** E2's lore-keeper
becomes the brainrot authority figure (Rule B10): the man who
runs the place and keeps the book, whose role is his license to
know. He delivers each episode's deepest reveal — by reading a
line already on the wall that re-prices everything said so far.
His flaw is visible and social, not mystical: **the wall says
what the wall is paid to say**, and the town's shared wrong belief
(A4) is that it can't be wrong. Catchphrase (Rule S5): "The wall
says what the wall says."

**THE CULL CLOCK (cadmium Method C device) → the bell.** It
earned its place: it ignited at 0.21s in E2's BLAM pass and
snapped full on every joint frame. Transformed in meaning only:
it is the town's bell — the deadline every grab, lie, and reveal
is played against — furniture-corner, sound-off legible, never
competing with speech. Per Conflict X2, the clock supplies facts;
a *character* always supplies the cliff.

**THE PALETTE AND THE STREET.** INK / COBALT / BONE + CADMIUM
accent, the depot-corner wall, morning light from frame left —
the show's visual identity is untouched. Recognition is spent,
not re-minted (Rule S2).

## 3. WHAT IS KILLED AND WHY

- **The tally/count as story.** Cull numbers, mark arithmetic,
  the circled gap, "what culls a mark" — lore machinery that made
  E2 fail Gate E (no want statable without a lore term). The clock
  still ticks on screen; nobody ever again discusses the count.
  SUPER NUMBER HEAD is killed as a speaking character.
- **The metaphysical question.** "Which version of us is the
  real one?" is killed as the show's subject. The characters are
  smarter now (msg 470): they don't philosophize about the reset,
  they *game* it — palming, swapping, holding, refusing — openly,
  the way everyone in town would. The only questions left are
  social: who's lying, who paid, who grabbed, who won't open
  their hand.
- **The delivery-job spine.** One job, one parcel, one episode was
  the repetition engine the user named. The docket (§5) replaces
  it: the want walks in off the street, different every episode.
- **The world-swap metronome.** E2 cut to a brand-new street at
  every joint; the user ruled swaps are a spice for tenser
  moments. New law: **the reset is not a relocation.** The bell
  changes one detail (a sign, a coat, who's standing where) and
  the street stays the street; a full world-swap is reserved for
  the Twist and the Cliff — maximum two per episode, both earned
  (§6).
- **The one-line-per-unit STATION grammar.** Superseded by the
  amended budgets and the accusation chain (§6), as it already
  was in practice by E2's pair writing.
- **UNC and the DISPATCHER as regulars.** Benched to silent
  background or rare cameo. The speaking-cast cap (§6) is
  structural now; a six-name ensemble was THE ROSTER's failure
  mode, not this show's ambition.

## 4. CAST

Speaking cast per episode is capped at **three** (Conflict X3):
the accuser, one co-lead, and the authority. Laura and the
Courier alternate the co-lead slot by episode; the one not
speaking still appears — holding something, silently (entrances
and held objects are exempt and load-bearing, Rule B10).

- **LAURA** (co-lead, odd episodes) — the chalk-hand. *Want: to
  write one true thing on the wall.* Response Style: Short —
  questions, mostly; her lines accuse by noticing. **She holds
  the QUESTIONING JOB (A4):** she notices by U2 (what a hand is
  doing doesn't match what a mouth is saying), names it by U4 in
  one short line, and acts on it in U6 (she writes — or refuses
  to write — the true line herself). Per Conflict X8 she is the
  chain's second voice, so the job rides the chain instead of
  competing with it.
- **THE COURIER** (co-lead, even episodes) — the Holder for hire.
  *Want: to be believed without the wall — a held thing should
  speak for itself.* Response Style: Short, deadpan, addresses
  and prices. When she co-leads, the episode's proof object is in
  her custody and the chain accuses *her* hands.
- **THE WATCHER** (every episode) — keeper of the wall.
  *Want: the wall's word to stay final — his authority is the
  wall's authority.* Response Style: Long — the one rambler
  (Rule C6); his reveals unspool. He is the authority of Rule
  B10 and the holder of the show's wrong belief: the wall says
  what the wall is paid to say, and he is the one being paid.
- **THE ACCUSER** (rotating guest, one per episode) — a borrowed
  real-world archetype (Rule S8) who walks in with the want: a
  landlady, a bride, an inspector, a brother, a cook. *Want:
  stated in their first line, in words a cold viewer can repeat
  (Rule B6).* The guest is the novelty budget; the guest never
  returns within three episodes.

Character cards are written Identity / Personality / Backstory
per Rule C1 before any room convenes; dialogue is drafted from
the Personality layer only.

## 5. EPISODE ENGINE

**THE DOCKET.** One premise-engine ritual opens every episode
(Rule S1): somebody steps up to the wall, states their want as a
docket line — *"Write that he owes me"* — and Laura lifts the
chalk. The ritual never changes. Everything inside it rotates:

- the **guest** (archetype, face, grievance),
- the **want** — sourced only from Rule B14's safe ground: lies,
  money, jobs, promises, mistaken identity. Never bodies, never
  visible difference read as guilt,
- the **proof object** — one holdable thing (a key, a ring, a
  letter, a second phone, an empty tin) shown before it is ever
  explained (Rule B8),
- the **lie** — and whose hand breaks it.

The episode then runs the single accusation chain (Rule B1):
proof shown → lie told → an entrance that breaks the lie → the
Watcher's reveal off the wall → consequence begun, cut at the
peak (Rule B3). The next episode opens with the consequence as a
one-line recap spoken as an accusation or complaint (Rule B12).

**Why repetition is now structurally impossible, not just
discouraged** — the E2 failure was one subject (parcel + wall +
name) orbited for six units, and a season plan that orbited it
for six episodes. The docket makes that shape unwritable:
(1) the want, guest, and object are *inputs* that must differ
before a board can be approved — the anti-repetition pre-check
(Room Method §2, G2) verifies them against the docket archive
the way it already searches the board archive; (2) Rule B13's
secret-type rotation is a hard gate — two consecutive episodes
may not share a secret-type, and no proof object may repeat
within a season; (3) inside the episode, the five beats occupy
five different *actions* (accuse at the wall → show the proof in
the street → lie in the doorway → the entrance → the reveal back
at the wall), so no two consecutive units share a subject,
a location beat, or a motion motif; (4) the alternating co-lead
(§4) changes the chain's second voice every episode. Grammar
constant, betrayal rotating (Rule B13) — variety is the engine's
output, not the room's homework.

**Escalation (Rule S7):** the docket's stakes climb one notch per
episode — a debt, then a marriage, then a theft, then a name the
Watcher refuses to write — and each episode ends on the new worst
fact, so the audience reads the season forward.

## 6. FORMAT GRAMMAR

Network standard holds: 60s episodes, 6 units × 10s, 16:9, one
conversation. Inside it, the addendum lands as follows.

**The chain on the unit grid (Rule B2's five beats, six units):**
U1 Hook + Setup — the accusation already in flight, the want
stated out loud · U2 the proof object shown, the first lie told ·
U3 Twist — the situation flips (the accused won't, can't, or
already did) · U4 Clash — the lie is pressed; Laura names what
she noticed · U5 the entrance breaks the lie; the Watcher's
reveal · U6 consequence begun, cut at the peak on the freeze.

**Dialogue mechanics.** Only the four line-types plus the
want-statement (Rule B5): accusation-with-proof, lie/denial,
entrance-that-contradicts, authority reveal. Every character's
first line states their want (B6). Characters talk to each
other, never to camera (B7). Lines run **6–12 words** (B4's
mechanics inside A1's per-line cap), one speaker per turn,
~2.5 words/second. Relationships are assigned on the card
before a line is written (C3), the shared-context brief is
capped at a paragraph (C2), and every unit carries one physical
moment a viewer can describe without quoting the show (C7) —
in this show it is almost always a hand doing something: a
grab, a palm, a fist that won't open. One scripted interruption
or unprompted entrance per episode, at a declared point only
(C8, X5). Drafting order is banter-first (C10) inside the
room's serial lock discipline, unchanged.

**Word budget (A1, binding).** Episode total **80–90 words,
hard cap 95, per-unit ceiling 20, ≤14 lines.** Unit classes:
at most two FULL-TALK (17–19), never adjacent; MIXED (12–15)
is the default; at least one QUIET (5–8 or 0); U6 is the
SILENCE-BEAT unit (10–13) whose gap is counted inside its
budget. A1 sets the unit total; Response Style (C6/X10) sets
the distribution inside it — the Watcher's ramble may not eat
Laura's silence.

**Digestion gaps (A2, carriers re-specified per Conflict X1 —
the gaps are the talk's punctuation, not pauses in it).**
Three, at the fixed placements, each with a declared carrier:
(i) after U2's turn — a held reaction on the **proof object**:
the camera stays on the thing in the hand while the bell ticks;
(ii) after U4's peak turn — a held reaction **face**: the person
just named, saying nothing, hand visible in frame; (iii) the
final 2–3s of U6 — **the freeze itself** (below). Plus ~1s
reaction holds after any reveal line. Pass 4 verifies gap
seconds exactly as it verifies word counts.

**The cliff is a freeze, not a fade (B3/B11, X2).** The reveal
lands, we hold the reaction face — fist still closed, ring still
unclaimed — and the episode's last line or card is the open
question, on screen, in words a viewer can type into a comment.
The clock may supply the fact (the bell rings mid-reach); a
character always supplies the cliff. A bare number, tally, or
device state as an ending fails the cliff check.

**BLAM opening shape.** This show was the BLAM champion; the
morph keeps the crown by designing the grab. Frame 0 is
mid-motion: **a hand snatching the episode's proof object** (off
the wall's nail, out of a pocket, across a counter) as the Cull
Clock ignites inside 0–1.0s, with a named first-second sound
event (the object's clatter, the bell's snap, a gasp). The first
spoken line lands inside 3s and is the accusation already in
flight (B2's hook slot) — never a greeting, never a lore term
(A3's title-as-contract: U1's first line names a visible object
and an action). No fade-in, no crackle head, no ramp from rest;
U1's PEAK panel is the grab itself.

**World-swap discipline (msg 467).** The bell rings every unit;
the street changes one detail. Full world-swaps fire at most
twice — at the U3 twist and the U6 cliff — where the tension
earns them. All other joints are same-place CARRY matches: same
hand, same object, one detail different, and the audience hunts
the detail (the founding treatment's own viral bet, restored as
grammar instead of garnish).

**Vernacular (S4).** One register for the whole town: flat,
deadpan, matter-of-fact about the absurd — nobody marvels at the
bell; they check their hands. Carrier words: *holding* and
*the bell*. The register is the audio logo and does not drift.

## 7. SEASON SEED

The docket escalates: a debt (E1), a marriage (E2), a theft
(E3) — then someone accuses the Watcher himself, proof object
in hand, and the wall's keeper must let Laura write about *him*
(E4, the midseason turn). All season the town is learning what
the viewer already sees: Laura writes what she *saw held*, not
what she's told, and a chalk-hand who can't be bought is the
most dangerous person in a town run on a record. Final image:
dawn, the wall full of finished names, one space chalked blank
where the Watcher's name used to be — and Laura holding the
chalk out, not writing, while the bell rings.

## 8. FIRST EPISODE OUTLINE — "Write That He Owes Me"

One chain: ROSA, the diner landlady (guest archetype: the
landlady, Rule S8), accuses her lodger BRUNO of three months'
unpaid rent. Proof object: **his room key**, which Rosa holds —
a man who'd paid would have his key back. Speakers: Rosa
(accuser), Laura (co-lead + questioning job), the Watcher
(authority). Bruno never speaks; his entrance is the chain's
third beat. Budgets: 18 + 15 + 7 + 19 + 14 + 12 = **85 words**.

- **U1 — FULL-TALK · 18 words · Hook + Setup.** Frame 0: Rosa's
  hand snatches Bruno's key off the wall's nail as the clock
  ignites and the key clatters (BLAM). At the wall, Rosa states
  the want — "Write it down: Bruno owes me three months." Laura,
  chalk up: "He told the depot he paid you, Rosa." Rosa holds
  the key high: "Then why am I holding his key?"
- **U2 — MIXED · 15 words · the proof, the lie.** Rosa chalks
  nothing yet; the key turns in her fingers for the camera.
  "Three months. Not one coin crossed my counter." Laura —
  noticing, down the street, Bruno standing very still with one
  fist closed (questioning job: NOTICE) — "Then what's he
  holding, Rosa?" **GAP 1 (after the turn):** held close on
  Bruno's closed fist, bell ticking, no words, 2–3s.
- **U3 — QUIET · 7 words · Twist.** One-detail reset (a sign
  changed, nothing more). Bruno crosses the street in silence
  and stops at the wall, fist still closed, and does not offer
  it. Laura: "Open your hand, Bruno." Rosa: "He won't." The
  twist reads off the picture: the one act that could clear him
  is the act he refuses.
- **U4 — FULL-TALK · 19 words · Clash.** Rosa presses the
  accusation with the key; Laura names what she noticed (A4:
  NAME) — "He's not hiding money. Look — his hand is shaking.
  He's holding something he'd drop dead before he drops."
  **GAP 2 (after the peak turn):** held reaction on Bruno's
  face, fist in frame, no words, 2–3s.
- **U5 — MIXED · 14 words · the entrance breaks the lie; the
  reveal.** The Watcher arrives and goes straight to the wall:
  "Line nine, Rosa. Read what got struck through." Rosa reads
  the chalk: the debt, crossed out. Watcher: "Struck the day
  your ring went missing." (The entrance that contradicts: the
  record itself was the third party in the room all along.)
- **U6 — SILENCE-BEAT · 12 words · consequence begun, freeze.**
  Bruno opens his hand at last: Rosa's lost ring, held through
  four hundred bells to be returned in person — he had paid,
  in the only coin she counts, and waited to put it in her hand
  himself. Laura acts (A4: ACT): she chalks the true line
  herself, her first — "PAID. ASK HIS HAND." Rosa takes the
  ring; then, to Bruno, the episode's last line, the open
  question, on screen over his frozen face: **"Why didn't you
  just knock?"** **GAP 3:** the freeze holds the final 2–3s —
  his face, her hand, the ring between them, no words. Full
  world-swap fires on the cliff joint only in the season's
  tensest episodes; E1 ends on the held street, the detail
  changed: the key is back on its nail.

Cold-reader check on this outline (Gate E): a landlady wants a
lodger's debt written down; his key in her hand says he never
paid; his closed fist says otherwise; the record shows the debt
was cancelled the day her ring vanished; he was holding the
ring. Who wanted what, what blocked them, what changed — all
statable without the words *cull*, *tally*, or *mark*.
