# MORPH SHOW-3 — THE NIGHT LIST
### (successor to SHOW-3 'THE ROSTER' — morph bible, 2026-11-16)

> **RETIRED 2026-11-24 — the gutting's verdict: unsalvageable as a distinct show.**
> Its silhouette is HOLD ON's (petition the keeper to change the writing), its core
> device is unproducible as drama, and its stakes resolve off-screen.
> The slot, the production berth, and its one true asset — a physical shortage as
> stakes you can point at — pass to its successor, **THE SHOP** (MORPH_SHOW-6.md),
> where scarcity is priced on a visible list. This file is kept, not deleted:
> the record of the show stands.

**Morph order:** user Telegram msg 488 — *"use the learning and morph
the shows into new shows so you can have 5 best shows."* THE ROSTER
is not continued. This bible is its redesigned successor, built from
the E3 Writing Addendum (five studies) under the Writers Room Method
as amended (msg 470). Primary lens: **Cantina** — the character is
the persistent asset; dialogue is a character-sheet output; the
situations are aggressively mundane.

**Why this morph has the most to fix.** THE ROSTER's E2 drew the
user's hardest verdict of the batch: *"esoterically babbling about
nonsense and numbers"* — no discernible story. The diagnosis, from
the show's own locked script: the episode's plot *was* a lore
mechanic — six names read in order (WREN → VASQUEZ → OKONKWO → HALE
→ KEEPER → WREN) — and the dialogue was lore stated at other people
("Spoken names stay kept; erasure un-keeps, Keeper."). A cold viewer
could not say who wanted what, because nobody on screen wanted
anything a stranger would recognize: they wanted the ritual
completed. The morph keeps the room, the book, and the people — and
replaces the ritual with a job, a want, and a fight.

## 1. NEW SHOW IDENTITY

**Title: THE NIGHT LIST**

**Premise (one sentence, Gate E on its face):** *A night clerk keeps
the list of who gets a bed in his building — and every night
there is one bed too few, and somebody fights the list on his
desk for it.*

No lore terms. A cold viewer reads the whole show off the sentence:
a desk, a list, a man with the pen, and a person at the door who
wants the pen moved. Every episode is that sentence with a new name
in the doorway.

Format: network standard — 60s episodes, 6 units × 10s, 16:9,
1280×720, 24fps, house CRF 18, AAC. Palette stays INK + BONE +
CADMIUM lamp with the VERMILION device (§2). Register stays deadpan:
the wronger the night, the flatter the clerk.

## 2. WHAT CARRIES OVER — AND HOW IT IS TRANSFORMED

Kept only what earned it across the pilot and E2:

- **THE KEEPER → the night clerk.** The pilot's best asset: a
  returned, stubborn man afraid he never really left. Transformed
  from *reader of a sacred text* into a *working man with a desk, a
  pen, and a rule he hides behind*. His fear survives, translated:
  not "the roster kept me" but "this desk is the only bed I have" —
  his own name is on the list, because he lives where he works.
- **THE ROSTER BOOK → the list.** Same big handwritten book, same
  roster page prop, same wet-ink close-ups. Transformed from a
  metaphysical object that "keeps" people into a workplace tool
  with human stakes: a name on it sleeps inside tonight; a name
  struck off it sleeps outside. The book does nothing by itself,
  ever again. People do things with it, on screen, for reasons
  stated out loud.
- **THE NAME-HANDOFF JOINT (spoken → written).** The show's one
  genuinely strong device, proven across two episodes: a unit ends
  on a name spoken; the next opens with that name already written
  somewhere new. Kept as the joint law — but the name is no longer
  a step in a reading. It is a *decision landing*: written on =
  someone gets a bed; struck through = someone loses one. The
  device supplies the fact; a character supplies the cliff
  (Addendum X2).
- **THE VERMILION/INK DEVICE (Method C).** Kept and sharpened: the
  vermilion strike-through is the show's graphic signature. A name
  circled in vermilion = *this one is decided tonight* (the pilot's
  circled-name frame-0 hook, the show's viral bet, survives intact
  as the BLAM shape — §6). Ink crossing from a card to a palm, ink
  still wet — E2's best images — survive as *proof objects*
  (Addendum B8): evidence a person can point at, not omens.
- **UNC → the owner.** Kept: the man who refused a request once and
  paid for it. Transformed from fellow-keeper of the mystery into
  the boss who owns the building and the beds — his pressure is
  money, stated plainly. He is the show's rambler (C6): the only
  character licensed to lecture, and the lecture is always where
  he's wrong.
- **LAURA → the long-stay guest.** Kept: the woman who asks the
  question everyone avoids ("What did you leave here, Keeper?" —
  the pilot's best line, fired verbatim in E2). Transformed from
  question-voice at the frame edge into a resident with a want of
  her own (§4) and the episode's accuser. She is in the room
  because she lives there; she fights because the list decides
  whether her family does.
- **THE DISPATCHER → the day clerk.** Kept: the building's other
  voice, the log-keeper. Transformed from disembodied building
  speaker into a person with a book of her own — the entry log —
  and the show's authority figure (B10): she delivers the deepest
  reveal because *her role is her license to know* — she wrote down
  who actually came in, and when. (Production reason, from E2's
  GATE_LOG: a lone unfamiliar name from her disembodied voice
  failed 7 of 7 fires; in-scene, she lands. She is never a voice
  from a speaker again.)
- **WREN → the first line of the book.** The oldest name, demoted
  from unseen mystery entity ("THE FIRST NAME") to a plain,
  human fact the season spends: the first name in the book is in
  the Keeper's own handwriting, and it is his. No entity, no voice,
  no hand-that-writes-itself — a man, a pen, a night he doesn't
  talk about (§7).
- **THE ROSTER ROOM → the night desk.** Same gothic room, one lamp,
  one chair — redressed as a working lobby: desk, board, log,
  door. The door is new and load-bearing: every episode's problem
  walks through it, already talking.

## 3. WHAT IS KILLED — AND WHY

Named, so no room re-imports them by habit:

- **THE NAME-PASS AS PLOT.** Reading six names in order *was* the
  E2 episode. Killed outright. Names now appear only when someone
  is fighting over one; an episode has ONE contested name (B1: one
  accusation chain per episode), never a procession.
- **THE KEEPING RULE AS DIALOGUE.** "A name spoken is a name kept;
  a name erased is a shift unkept" — the world rule spoken aloud
  was the single most esoteric artifact in the show. Killed as
  speech. What replaces it needs no statement: striking a name is
  a physical act a viewer watches, and its cost is a person in a
  doorway with a bag. Shown, never explained — the standing rule,
  finally applied to the show that coined it.
- **THE CULL / ROSTER METAPHYSICS.** Whether the roster is outside
  the cull or *is* the cull: killed, entirely, no residue. The
  morph's world has weather, rent, and winter in it. Nothing else
  needs a cosmology.
- **THE SUPERNATURAL MACHINERY.** The chair occupied on the monitor
  and empty in the room; the roster circling itself; the pen
  nobody held. Killed as plot. One visual oddity per episode may
  survive *only* as a proof object with a human explanation pending
  (wet ink = somebody was here recently — a fact about a person,
  not a spirit).
- **LORE BOOKKEEPING AS CONTENT.** Reading positions, F-number
  debts, wrap re-reads, "re-priced" facts — the ledger stays where
  it belongs: backstage, in the Continuity Editor's file. Nothing
  is owed, fired, or reminded *on screen* ever again. On screen,
  people owe each other beds, money, and answers.
- **UNFAMILIAR NAMES AS CLIFFS.** E2's U2 cliff — a lone unfamiliar
  name from a disembodied voice — failed every fire. Killed as a
  construction: cliffs are social beats (an accusation, a dare, a
  refusal) carried by a person in the room; the device name rides
  inside the beat, never alone. Cast and guest names are short,
  familiar, and speakable on the first try.

## 4. CAST

Character cards per C1 — Identity / Personality / Backstory;
dialogue is written from the Personality layer only. Relationships
assigned before any line (C3). Response Styles per C6/X10.

- **THE KEEPER — the night clerk.** *Want, in plain words:* one
  quiet night — and under it, to hand the desk to someone else
  before the desk is the only home he has left. *Pair dynamics:*
  owes UNC his job; owes Laura the truth; is owed an answer by
  nobody — he is the one who answers. *Response Style: SHORT* —
  the show's terse pole; his longest line in any episode is the
  one that costs him. Signature entrance line (C9): "Name." (one
  word, pen up — what he says to every arrival).
- **LAURA — the long-stay guest.** *Want:* her brother's name on
  the list before winter; he arrives in the season's middle (§7).
  *Pair dynamics:* accuser of the Keeper; the one person UNC
  cannot throw out, because she pays on time. *Response Style:
  MEDIUM.* **LAURA HOLDS THE QUESTIONING JOB** (A4/X8): as the
  chain's accuser she notices what is wrong with the list by U2,
  names it in one short line by U4, and acts on it in U6 — the
  questioning rides the accusation chain instead of competing
  with it.
- **THE DISPATCHER — the day clerk.** *Want:* her log believed —
  the book she keeps is the truth of who came and went, and she
  will open it on the desk to prove it. *Pair dynamics:* the
  Keeper's only ally and his auditor; UNC's employee, not his
  friend. *Response Style: SHORT, flat* — dates, times, names;
  her reveals land because she never decorates them (B10
  authority).
- **UNC — the owner.** *Want:* every bed full, every name paid
  for, and the list never argued about in front of a guest.
  *Pair dynamics:* boss to both clerks; creditor to Peter.
  *Response Style: LONG* — the show's rambler; his speeches are
  where the money motive says itself out loud.
- **PETER — the broker (recurring guest).** *Want:* names he can
  sell — a struck name is a bed he can rent twice. The E2-era
  Peter sold "the roster's names to the advertiser," a lore
  transaction nobody could picture; the morph's Peter runs the
  oldest business there is: he pays cash for vacancies. Secrets
  sourced from his money are B14-legal (lies, money, promises).
  *Response Style: MEDIUM, smiling.*
- **THE ARRIVAL — rotating, one per episode.** A borrowed
  real-world archetype (S8): the bride, the inspector, the man
  from the rain, the nurse off a double shift. *Want:* stated in
  their first line (B6) — on the list, or off it. One new face at
  a time, through the door (S2).

Speaking cast per episode: **three, plus the authority** (X3 cap)
— Keeper + one fighter (Laura or the Arrival) + one pressure
(UNC or Peter), with the Dispatcher's reveal as the fourth voice
only when she enters with the log.

## 5. EPISODE ENGINE

**The ritual (S1):** every episode opens the same way — the Keeper
at the desk after midnight, the book open, the board behind him;
the door opens and tonight's problem walks in *already talking*.
The ritual recurs; the problem rotates.

**The machine:** one bed is contested; the name is how the bed
is won or lost. Somebody wants it on,
off, moved, or paid for — and somebody else in the room is the
reason it can't simply happen. The episode runs ONE accusation
chain (B1): **proof shown → lie told → entrance that breaks the
lie → the Dispatcher's log reveals → consequence begun.** The
consequence is never finished in the episode (B3): a name is
written or struck, and the episode cuts on what that *starts*.

**Story sources (all B14-legal, all familiar skeletons, S3):**
the debt come due (a name kept on for a man who stopped paying);
mistaken identity (two guests, one name, one bed); the promise
(a name held for someone who may never come); the buyout (Peter's
cash against a stranger's need); the return (a struck name at the
door, in the rain). No episode may source its secret from the
book's nature — only from what people did with it.

**Escalation (S7):** across a season the contested names climb
toward the desk: a stranger's name → a friend's → Laura's
brother's → Laura's → the Keeper's own. The audience can predict
the ladder and come back to check.

## 6. FORMAT GRAMMAR

How the addendum's rules land in this show, mechanically:

- **Dialogue (Cantina family).** Shared-context brief capped at
  ~1,000 characters (C2): *a night desk, a list that decides who
  sleeps inside, the clerk who holds the pen, the people who need
  it moved.* Every line verbatim with speaker + delivery note
  (C5); lines written from Personality cards (C1); every unit
  contains at least one line that only makes sense because of a
  declared relationship (C3). Banter draft before plot draft in
  Pass 3 (C10). One physical moment per unit (C7) — the pen
  capped and uncapped, the log slammed open, a bag set down in
  the doorway — the clippable beat is physical, never a reveal.
- **Line mechanics (brainrot family).** 8–15 words per line,
  ~2.5 words/second, one speaker per turn (B4). Only the four
  line-types plus the want-statement (B5). First lines state
  wants (B6). Nobody talks to camera (B7). The proof object is
  shown before it is explained (B8) — the wet strike, the log
  page, the empty bed with its tag still on.
- **Word budget (A1, as amended).** Episode total **80–90 words,
  hard cap 95**; per-unit ceiling 20; FULL-TALK (17–19) at most
  twice, never adjacent; at least one QUIET unit (5–8 or 0).
  Response Style distributes the total inside each unit (X10):
  the Keeper's terseness is spent, never filled.
- **Digestion gaps (A2, carriers per X1).** Three scripted gaps,
  verified like word counts: **after U2's turn** — a held reaction
  beat (the Keeper looks at the proof, says nothing, 2–3s);
  **after U4's peak turn** — a held reaction on the fighter's
  face (the genre's reaction shot, not a pause in the talk);
  **the final 2–3s of U6** — the freeze: the pen hovering over a
  name, the question on screen (B11), no consequence played.
- **BLAM opening shape (FIRST-SECOND doctrine + BLAM gate).**
  Frame 0 is the decision already in flight: a name on the board
  struck through in vermilion — the strike is the impact event
  (E1) and its chalk-snap is the first-second sound event (the
  door does not bang on this show; ONE SHORT owns the door-slam),
  and the Arrival's first line is an accusation already
  mid-flight (B2 Hook slot, 0–3s). U1's PEAK
  panel = the strike landing. No fade-in, no atmosphere beat, no
  quiet room before the problem — the problem is the opening.
- **Cliffs (X2 + B11).** Every unit cliff is a social beat — an
  accusation, a dare, a refusal, a name demanded — with the
  device name written or struck inside it. The episode cliff is
  an unanswered question, on screen, that a viewer can type into
  a comment. Next episode opens with the recap spoken as a
  complaint (B12): *"You put him back on, Keeper. Now there's
  eleven names and ten beds—"*
- **Serial grammar (S family).** Numbered episodes, one fixed
  title pattern — *THE NIGHT LIST — "The [Name] Night"* — so the
  story-so-far reads off the titles alone (S9). Grammar constant,
  betrayal rotates (B13): same desk, same chain shape, new name,
  new lie, new proof.

## 7. SEASON SEED
*(This section is also SHOW-3's owed season projection — the debt
the old show never paid.)*

The Keeper works the desk all season while the contested names
climb the ladder toward his own (§5). At midseason Laura's brother
arrives — the name she has begged for since the premiere — and the
Keeper discovers the log for the night the book began is missing
its page, in the Dispatcher's own hand. The season's final image:
the first page of the book, in lamplight — the first name ever
written on the list, in the Keeper's young hand, is his own — and
the Keeper picks up the pen, strikes it through himself, and hands
Laura the desk.

## 8. FIRST EPISODE OUTLINE — "The Sol Night"

*Premise in one breath: a man struck off the list last winter is
at the door in the rain, saying the Keeper struck him for money;
the log says nobody struck him at night at all.* One chain:
Keeper = accused · Laura = accuser/questioner · Dispatcher =
authority · UNC = the lie's owner. Speaking cast: 4 (cap met —
Dispatcher speaks only at her reveal and the cliff).

| Unit | Business + who speaks | Words |
|---|---|---|
| **U1** | Frame 0: SOL struck through in vermilion on the board, the chalk-snap of the strike as the first-second sound — Sol, wet coat, mid-accusation: "You struck my name, Keeper. I slept in the rain." The Keeper, pen up, states his want: "I want one quiet night, Sol. Sit down." Sol doesn't sit; his bag goes down by the empty bed, not at the desk. | 14 (MIXED) |

| **U3** | Proof object first: the Dispatcher's log, slammed open on the desk — the strike is logged at *noon*. Her entrance breaks the frame: "Nobody struck him at night. It was noon." The Keeper, quieter: "I'm never here at noon." | 13 (MIXED) |

Unit classes: FULL-TALK ×2 (U2, U4 — non-adjacent), MIXED ×2,

carried. Cliff = social beat + on-screen question; consequence
