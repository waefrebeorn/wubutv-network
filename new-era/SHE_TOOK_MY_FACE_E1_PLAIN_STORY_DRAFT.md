# SHE TOOK MY FACE — E1 plain story (draft for user verdict)

Written by the parent, 2026-11-27. Bible: new-era/shows/SHE_TOOK_MY_FACE.md
(rulings applied: E1 steals ONE feature — the SMILE; the voice is E2's theft
and is not in this episode; every episode ends in the photo studio).

Premise: A face thief steals a man's smile and sells it on her bottles.
He cannot take it back by force. He can only prove it is his — in a photograph.

Cast: NINO (the victim, home character, red scarf), VELLA (the thief, invader
fruit character), MADAME ORA (the face-keeper), TOMI (Nino's little brother).

1. A photo studio, wedding day. Nino stands for a photo with NO mouth at all —
   a smooth flat face. Beside him Vella smiles with TWO smiles, one above the
   other. On the studio shelf, her face-cream bottles all carry the same
   smiling face on their labels: Nino's smile, printed and for sale.
2. Vella alone in her shop, before her wall of photographs of herself as a
   girl — in every photo the girl's face is plain and unsmiling. Vella takes
   the stolen smile off her own face, holds it, tries it on again in the
   mirror. Alone, she practices smiling at nobody. It does not fit her right.
3. Tomi sees a bottle in the shop window and stops dead: the label is smiling
   his brother's smile. He drags Nino to the window and points — label, then
   Nino's flat face, then label again. Nino touches the smooth place where
   his smile was.
4. Nino demands his smile back. Vella refuses: today is the wedding photo,
   the bride buys only from a smiling seller, and a face is just a sticker —
   she peels, she sells. Nino turns to the camera, once: "Then watch me peel
   it back."
5. At the wedding shoot, Vella sells bottles to the guests, wearing the smile.
   The bride begins to cry with happiness — and Nino's smile on Vella's face
   bursts out laughing at the wrong moment, loud, in the quiet room. Every
   guest stares. The smile laughs again. Vella clamps both hands over it.
6. The photographer lines everyone up for the wedding photo. Nino steps in
   beside Vella and holds a bottle up next to her face — the label's smile
   and the smile she is wearing, side by side, identical. The camera flashes.
   The printed photo shows two matching smiles and Nino's flat face between
   them. Nino takes the photo home and pins it on his empty wall — the first
   picture in his collection. In her shop, Vella touches the stolen smile,
   and a small crack opens at its corner.

Legibility check: the want is printed in the world in frame 1 (two smiles,
one flat face, the smile for sale on bottles). The body is the ledger — the
smile moves, is worn, misbehaves and cracks; every state reads off faces.
Every line is a move and is answered (demand → refusal → public exposure).
Proof is a positive picture: the printed wedding photo, two identical smiles
beside one flat face. The premise is re-spent in all six units.
