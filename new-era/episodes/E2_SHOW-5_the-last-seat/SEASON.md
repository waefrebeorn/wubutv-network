# SHOW-5 THE LAST SEAT — SEASON PROJECTION (Season 1)

Founded at the E2 lock, 2026-11-15 — the show had no season

## The season's question

ARC-1, "The One Viewer," carried by this show: the station
earned exactly one viewer, and the viewer has exactly one
seat. The season asks what the seat IS to the performers —
a vault that keeps what it likes, or a witness that watches
what it is given — and what it costs a room full of
performers to find out. E2 moved the question from keeping
to watching (T-ARC1-1, DEVELOP). The season ends when
someone finally treats the seat as a seat.

## Character arcs — start → end of season

- **THE DANCER** — starts: a performer who outsources her
  memory to the seat (it keeps; she forgets on purpose).
  Ends: a performer who dances to be watched in the moment
  and accepts the cull as the price of being present —
  the archive no longer employs her. (E2 is the hinge:
  her want changed on record.)
- **THE USHER** — starts: the keeper who has never asked
  the seat for anything; his whole faith is tending without
  spending. Ends: the man who finally asks — once, for
  something that matters, knowing it makes him a wanter
  like everyone he keeps the room for. (E2-P1 is the
  season's longest fuse: planted E2, payoff Ep 6.)
- **THE SEAT** — starts: a keeper of acts, answering after
  the fact. Ends: a presence that answers DURING — and, in
  the final image, is finally sat in. What sits, and
  whether anything answers then, is the season's last
  held card; lore is shown, never explained, to the end.
- **COLONEL** (enters Ep 4) — the official version: the
  seat is occupied, the house is full. His arc is the
  version meeting the evidence.
- **THE SINGER** (enters Ep 5) — cat or girl, the seat
  hears a mouth; the season keeps the question open and
  lets the seat's answers vote.
- **LAURA** (placed late season) — the one who asks what
  if the seat is empty; her episode is the season's
  cold-water test of everything the room believes.

## Midseason turn (Ep 4)

The Colonel declares a full house over a warm, empty
chair — the official version stops being a comfort and
becomes a claim the seat itself can contradict. From Ep 4
on, the room's question is no longer what the seat keeps
but whose account of the seat is true.

## The final image (Ep 8)

The last act of the season ends. The room empties. The
Usher — alone, his rounds done — crosses to the seat he
has tended all season and, for the first time, SITS in
it. From the stage end, empty now, the stage light warms:
the seat's oldest sign, given to the one person who never
asked for anything. Hold. Nothing is explained. (This
image is also E2-P1's payoff paid in picture, one episode
after its spoken payoff in Ep 6 — the ask, then the
sitting.)
