## §7 Pass 6 — critic (fresh context; cold read first)

The Critic received the locked breakdown, the dialogue
draft, and the checklists — not the drafting history.
**Cold read:** followed every beat; told the speakers
apart in every unit (cover-the-names corroborated from
the cold side); stumbled at exactly one place — U1 turn 3
(see C-1).
**Located objections:**
- **C-1 — U1, turn 3 · RETURNED.** The draft line "You
  said the seat kept it." quotes the pilot's shipped U3
  line near-verbatim inside the EPISODE-OPENING pair.
  Gate cited: Method B §9, Conflict 2 — callbacks are
  banned from opening pairs; a verbatim quote functions
  as a callback whatever the pattern label (P6) claims,
  and a cold viewer cannot hear it as the pressure it is
  meant to be. **Ruling: UPHELD.** Pass 3b (surgical, one
  line): "You told this room it kept it." — the claim is
  still his, still on record, still pressure; the
  quotation is gone. Words 6 → 7; unit total 24 → 25;
  Pass 4b re-run ✓ (§5).
- **C-2 — U2, the wordless turn 2 · ANSWERED, no change.**
  Objection: a refused second part played as flat light
  may read to a cold viewer as "nothing happens."
  Answer, located: U1 has just named the price on screen
  (asking = looking); U2's picture shows the price visibly
  refused (her back), and the breakdown's mirror tail beat
  gives the non-answer a witness inside the frame (her
  eyes find the seat's reflection — the seat is present
  and NOT answering). The nothing is legible because the
  episode spent U1 pricing it. Recorded; no rewrite.
- **C-3 — U6, cliff fragment · ANSWERED, no change.**
  Objection: "It's answering." risks the narrator test —
  a report a narrator could say. Answer: it is a speech
  act TO the Dancer from the keeper, in the moment, and
  it does disambiguating work no picture can: the pilot
  taught the audience that shifting light can mean the
  CULL; the keeper — the show's authority on signs —
  reads this change, on record, as an ANSWER. Cut the
  fragment and the cliff's sign class is genuinely
  ambiguous in the wrong direction. Kept.
- **Legibility note (not an objection):** the Usher's U1
  unsaid sentence ("I have never asked it for anything")
  is not recoverable from U1's DIALOGUE alone. It is,
  however, carried by U1's scripted delay beat — he looks
  at the seat like a man who has never addressed it so —
  and A2 counts the dispreferred delay as part of the
  unit's text; it is then stated outright as his account
  in U3, inside the same episode. The Critic records the
  note; the Room-runner accepts it as the episode's one
  known subtext lag, priced and deliberate.
**Round 2:** the Critic re-read U1 in its 3b form cold:
the pressure lands without the quotation; no new
objections. **PASS.** Two rounds used of a maximum of
two; no third was needed or permitted.
