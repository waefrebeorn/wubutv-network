# E2 — SHOW-2 THE TENTH SECOND — DIALOGUE DRAFT (Pass 3, Dialogue Writer)

Drafted serially, unit 1 → 6, under the carry-forward rule: each
unit's final line and exit state were pasted into the next unit's
context before drafting (the carry chain is the physical form of
the carry-forward here — chalk custody, then parcel custody).
Episode: "The Chalk Wall" — one conversation, Courier / Watcher /
Laura, at the wall and the depot, the morning after the pilot.

Budgets (hard): U1–U5 FULL-TALK 22–25 words · U6 silence-beat
unit 15–18 words · no turn over 7 words · hard ceiling 28.
COUNTING METHOD: every SPOKEN quoted word is counted, including
cliff-device words; per-turn counts are printed in brackets and
summed per unit. Unsaid sentences and unpicked punch-up
alternatives are not spoken and are not charged. Hyphenated
compounds ("twenty-one") count as one spoken word.
Draft counts (recount mechanically at Pass 4): U1 23 / U2 24 /
U3 23 / U4 22 / U5 22 / U6 17.

## Pass 3c — Critic round 1 answer (Dialogue Writer)

One line changed; all counts unchanged (verified by recount,
DECISION_LOG §5/§7). U4 T4 (the repair, turn line): "One mark
was culled from it." → **"The cull took that mark, Watcher."**
[6] — the original's "from it" named neither agent nor object
to a cold ear; the replacement names both (the cull, the mark)
at the same word count, and it sharpens the episode's dread:
the cull takes from the wall too. All other critic objections
were answered without text change (DECISION_LOG §7).

Anti-front-load construction (Method B A3) in every unit: turns
are separate quoted blocks with the picture turn written between
them; the turn line is parked at the late band on paper; every
unit's tail carries a speech-independent event (see BREAKDOWN).
