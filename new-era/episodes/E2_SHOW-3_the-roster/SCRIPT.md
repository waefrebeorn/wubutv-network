# E2 — SHOW-3 THE ROSTER — "The Pass, Done Properly"

LOCKED SCRIPT — Episode 2. Writers Room pipeline complete
2026-11-15: storyline → breakdown → dialogue (Pass 3, returned by
Pass 5; Pass 3b rewrite) → timing verification (Pass 4/4b/4c,
mechanical) → continuity edit (Pass 5 FAIL → Pass 5b PASS) →
critic (Pass 6: round 1 RETURN, Pass 3c answers, round 2 PASS) →
showrunner lock under the parent agent's session commission.
Full record: DECISION_LOG.md. Continuity: LEDGER.md.

Logline: The Keeper demands the roster be read again — properly,
in order, from the top — but the pass he insists on proves his own
hand never stopped keeping it.

6 units × 10s = 60s · 1440 frames @ 24fps · one conversation chain:
ONE clean in-order pass, WREN → VASQUEZ → OKONKWO → HALE → KEEPER
→ WREN (wrap). WREN is a RE-READ at both ends — it was spoken aloud
at the pilot's final cliff. Arc: ARC-2 "The Roster That Keeps
Everyone", thread T-ARC2-1 OPEN → DEVELOP. Stake: the Keeper hunts
the hand that held the pen; every unit of the pass hands him his
own.

R1 citations (read-first, Method B): FIRES F-004 (U3) · FIRES
F-012 (U5, verbatim) · REMINDS F-003 (U2), F-008 (U4) · RE-PRICES
F-009 (U6) · PLANTS E2-P1 (U1), E2-P2 (U4), E2-P3 (U6), E2-P4 (U6
cliff) · MOVES T-ARC2-1 to DEVELOP.

Transition device (show law): every unit ends with a name spoken;
the next unit opens with that name ALREADY WRITTEN somewhere new.
Episode exception, deliberate and marked in dialogue: the U6 cliff
is not a reading — the Dispatcher ENTERS a new name.
First-second doctrine (opening beat): frame 0 = the Keeper's
sealed return envelope, WREN inked large, the seal unbroken — the
pilot's cliff name carried in as an object, legible with the sound
off, before anyone speaks.
Lore shown, never explained: a seal nobody claims, a shift that
cannot end, a denial the building files beside the entry, ink that
crosses from a handled card to a palm, the oldest name freshly
retraced. The pen is never found. Nothing resolves.

Word budgets (Writers Room, layered per §7 Conflict 2): U1–U5
FULL-TALK 22–25 words · U6 silence-beat 15–18 · no turn over 7
words · Method B pair caps bind throughout. Counts below are the
final mechanical recounts (Pass 4c + Critic round-2 verification).

## Units

### U1 — the name on the envelope (A · P2 Misdirected Answer · FULL-TALK · 22 words)
Frame sentence: *the pilot's cliff name, WREN, inked on the
Keeper's sealed return envelope — and the seal has never been
broken.*
Opens: WREN already written somewhere new — inked across the
Keeper's sealed return envelope on the table beside the closed
roster. The envelope rotates end-over-end in the Keeper's hands;
the aged seal catches the lamp, unbroken. UNC stands between the
Keeper and the closed book.
Motion motif: the envelope turning in the hand.
- KEEPER → UNC | DEMAND — "Read it again, UNC. From Wren." (6)
  [PICTURE TURN: the envelope rotates; the seal catches the lamp]
- UNC → KEEPER | DEFLECT — TURN LINE — "Your envelope stayed
  sealed, Keeper." (5)
- KEEPER → UNC | DENY — "I never broke that seal myself." (6)
- UNC → KEEPER | DELIVER (the reading, in order) — "Wren. As
  written, Keeper." (4)
UNSAID: KEEPER — "I am afraid the roster kept me while I was
gone." · UNC — "I do not know who sealed this."
DELTA: the pass begins properly, in order, from the top; WREN
re-read and acknowledged as a re-read. The unbroken seal is
observed in-scene only; nobody claims its maker (E2-P1 PLANTED).
CLIFF (device): VASQUEZ — spoken by UNC.

### U2 — the name on the glass (A · P6 Dispreferred Second, Played · FULL-TALK · 25 words)
Frame sentence: *VASQUEZ condensing letter by letter on the cold
window glass — a name still on shift.*
Opens: VASQUEZ already written somewhere new — condensed on the
cold window glass. The Keeper waits at the roster; UNC watches
the name complete. A single drip breaks from the name and runs
down the glass.
Motion motif: the single condensation drip descending.
- KEEPER → UNC | PRESS — "Why does her shift still run, UNC?" (7)
  [DELAY BEAT — P6, action not words: UNC looks at the window, not
  the Keeper, steps to the glass and covers the name, and holds it]
- UNC → KEEPER | DECLARE — TURN LINE — "Spoken names stay kept;
  erasure un-keeps, Keeper." (7)
- KEEPER → UNC | CHALLENGE — "Then her shift never ends?" (5)
- UNC → KEEPER | DELIVER (the reading, in order) — "Vasquez.
  Kept, still running, Keeper." (5)
UNSAID: KEEPER — "I am afraid I will end up like her." · UNC —
"I cannot say why hers still runs."
DELTA: the world rule is spoken on screen — a spoken name stays
kept; erasure is the only un-keeping, and it is not performed.
VASQUEZ read and kept; her chalk stays on the door board
(F-003 REMINDED, not fired). UNC is not asked to erase (F-007
honoured).
CLIFF (device): OKONKWO — spoken by the Dispatcher (building
speaker).

### U3 — the name on the card (B · P8 Repair · FULL-TALK · 25 words)
Frame sentence: *OKONKWO stamped on a punched time-card in the
clock rack — and the log entry under it is in the Keeper's own
hand.*
Opens: OKONKWO already written somewhere new — stamped on a
punched time-card. The Dispatcher draws the card and sets it under
the lamp; the Keeper reads the handwriting side first. The rack
clock's second hand sweeps behind the card.
Motion motif: the sweeping second hand of the rack clock.
One scripted interruption (episode's only one): the Keeper's
denial begins over the sacrificial address-tag ONLY; the essential
span lands intact.
- DISPATCHER → KEEPER | PRESENT — "Okonkwo. This entry bears your
  hand, Keeper." (7)
  [PICTURE TURN: the card flips under the lamp; the logged night
  is revealed]
- KEEPER → DISPATCHER | CLAIM — "I wrote nothing while gone,
  Dispatcher." (6)
- DISPATCHER → KEEPER | CORRECT — TURN LINE — [ESSENTIAL, must
  land intact: "Logged while you were gone," — the tag after //
  is the sacrificial edge] "Logged while you were gone, //
  Keeper." (6)
- KEEPER → DISPATCHER | DENY — begins over the trailing
  address-tag ONLY, at the // point — "I never wrote that
  entry." (5)
  [Tail beat, unspoken: the Dispatcher logs the denial beside the
  entry — the building keeps both]
UNSAID: DISPATCHER — "Your hand is logged; its holder is not." ·
KEEPER — "If my hand stayed, I never truly left."
DELTA: F-004 FIRED — the entry stands in the Keeper's hand,
logged on a night he was gone (no absence length stated); his
denial is logged beside it. The Keeper has handled the card.
CLIFF (device): HALE — spoken by the Dispatcher.

### U4 — the name on the palm (B · P1 Concession-With-A-Price · FULL-TALK · 25 words)
Frame sentence: *HALE blooming in wet ink across the Keeper's own
palm — a name that crossed over while he was holding the card.*
Opens: HALE already written somewhere new — in wet ink across the
Keeper's open palm, transferred after he handled the OKONKWO card.
The Keeper stares at his palm; UNC watches the palm, not his
face. The inked fingers slowly close over the name and reopen.
Motion motif: the inked fingers closing and reopening.
- KEEPER → UNC | ACCUSE — "Look, UNC. Hale crossed my palm." (6)
  [PICTURE TURN: UNC slides the log page beside the palm; the
  indent beneath the writing catches the lamplight as a groove]
- UNC → KEEPER | CHALLENGE — "Hale's roster entry: your hand,
  tonight." (6)
- KEEPER → UNC | BARGAIN — TURN LINE — "Conceded, UNC. One answer
  out of order." (7)
- UNC → KEEPER | DELIVER (the reading, in order) — "Hale. An
  answer owed, Keeper." (5)
UNSAID: KEEPER — "I am afraid I kept shifts I cannot remember." ·
UNC — "I do not know where any of this writing came from."
DELTA: the palm evidence conceded (E2-P2 PLANTED: HALE reached
the palm only after the OKONKWO card was handled; F-008 REMINDED —
HALE's roster entry is in his own hand, dated tonight). An OWES
is born: UNC owes the Keeper ONE ANSWER given out of order — an
answer, not a reading; no name is skipped or read out of order.
CLIFF (device): KEEPER — spoken by UNC.

### U5 — the name on the ribbon (C · P5 Counter-Question That Concedes · FULL-TALK · 25 words)
