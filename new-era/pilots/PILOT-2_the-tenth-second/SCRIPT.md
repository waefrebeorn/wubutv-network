# PILOT-2 — THE TENTH SECOND, "The Parcel" (Chain T1-1, extended to 6 units)

Format: 6 units × 10s = 60s / 1440f @ 24fps, 1280×720, one 16:9 master.
Source chain: SHOW-2 treatment, Chain T1-1 "The Parcel" (OPEN). Unit 1 line is the
FIRST-SECOND DOCTRINE §4 SHOW-2 opening line. Chain lines adapted to the ≤9-word
unit rule (show grammar: exactly one spoken line per unit, at 7–9s).

Stake: the courier's record — 400 culls, zero lost parcels. This parcel is
addressed to someone who no longer exists in this version of the street.
Cast: THE COURIER, THE WATCHER. Handoff device: THE CARRY — the parcel is
visibly in a named hand in the first 1.5s of every unit and changes hands or
state in-frame at the joints. Characters know the cull rule and work it;
awareness is a working fact, never explained.

## Cast LOOK blocks (invariant traits, restated in every vehicle)

- COURIER: adult woman, 34; sharp black bob haircut with a straight fringe;
  calm deadpan face; long cobalt-blue courier coat to the knee; black
  cross-body strap; canvas satchel at her hip; dark trousers; scuffed boots.
- WATCHER: adult man, 68; short grey stubble beard; deep-set eyes; ink-black
  cardigan over a bone-white shirt; sleeves pushed up; white chalk dust on
  his fingers; holds a stub of white chalk.
- PARCEL: one mid-size parcel wrapped in brown paper, cross-tied with
  string, one gold CADMIUM stamp on top, no readable words anywhere on it.
- WORLD: cobbled depot street — wide wooden depot door, brick wall beside
  it covered in white chalk tally marks, one brass lamp, morning light
  from frame left. Palette: INK / COBALT / BONE + CADMIUM accent.

## Units

### U1 — "The door is gone" (carry IN: parcel, courier's hand)
Frame sentence: *the depot doorway, and it is a blank brick wall with a
parcel offered to it.*
- 0–1.5 HOOK: frame 0 mid-stride — COURIER's arm extended, parcel offered
  to a doorway that is now blank brick; WATCHER at the wall beside it,
  chalk tally visible, one mark short. Already in motion.
- 1.5–4 STATE: she holds the offer, professional, unbothered; he counts
  the tally marks with the chalk stub, stops one short.
- 4–7 TURN: the last tally mark is missing — the count does not close.
- 7–9 LINE — COURIER to WATCHER (command): "Hold this. The street is
  leaving." (6 words)
- 9–10 CLIFF: her arm still extended, parcel in hand, light shifting —
  the cull arrives mid-offer.
- Motif: REACH / OFFER. Unspent fact: the tally is one mark short.

### U2 — "The street that left" (carry: parcel, watcher's hands)
Frame sentence: *the same held parcel, and the whole street behind it is
a different street.*
- 0–1.5 HOOK: WATCHER now holds the parcel (took it at the joint);
  COURIER's hand still extended, empty. Behind them the depot is gone —
  a narrower street, laundry lines, afternoon light.
- 1.5–4 STATE: he weighs the parcel, turns it to the gold stamp.
- 4–7 TURN: she looks from her empty hand to the street that replaced
  hers; a door appears in a house wall where none was.
- 7–9 LINE — WATCHER to COURIER (rule): "The street moves every ten
  seconds. The parcel doesn't." (9 words)
- 9–10 CLIFF: he extends the parcel back toward her; her hand rising.
- Motif: WORLD-SWAP around a held pose. Unspent fact: the new door.

### U3 — "The address" (carry: parcel, courier's hand again)
Frame sentence: *a street of identical doors, and one opens onto
another street.*
- 0–1.5 HOOK: her hand closing on the parcel in close frame; pull wide:
  the street is now a row of identical doorways.
- 1.5–4 STATE: she tries the parcel at the nearest doorway; it opens —
  onto another street, not a room.
- 4–7 TURN: she shuts it; every door along the row creaks at once.
- 7–9 LINE — COURIER to WATCHER (report/accusation): "This address
  doesn't exist anymore. The street moved." (8 words)
- 9–10 CLIFF: she turns the parcel over — a second gold stamp on the
  underside she has never seen.
- Motif: DOOR-TESTING. Unspent fact: the second stamp.

### U4 — "The name" (carry: parcel, courier's hands, close)
Frame sentence: *the chalk wall of tallies, and one tally is a circled
drawing of this parcel.*
- 0–1.5 HOOK: parcel close in her hands, second stamp up; behind it the
  chalk wall, tallies — and among them a chalk drawing of a parcel,
  circled.
- 1.5–4 STATE: she studies the stamp; he watches her, not the wall.
- 4–7 TURN: her eyes go from the parcel to the circled drawing of it —
  the wall already knew.
- 7–9 LINE — COURIER to WATCHER (counter-question): "Who do I give it
  to? The name is mine." (9 words)
- 9–10 CLIFF: he takes the parcel from her hands, gently, both hands.
- Motif: INSPECTION / slow turn. Unspent fact: whose chalk drew it.

### U5 — "Sign for it" (carry: parcel + chalk, watcher's offer)
Frame sentence: *the depot door at dusk, and a fresh chalk mark on the
wall nobody wrote.*
- 0–1.5 HOOK: depot door restored, street at dusk, lamps lit; WATCHER
  holds the parcel out to her, a stub of chalk resting on top of it.
- 1.5–4 STATE: she stares at the chalk, not the parcel.
- 4–7 TURN: at the wall, in the flicker of the cull light, a fresh chalk
  mark stands among the tallies that was not there before (shown, never
  explained).
- 7–9 LINE — WATCHER to COURIER (verdict/command): "You carried it to
  yourself. Sign for it." (8 words)
- 9–10 CLIFF: she takes the parcel in one hand, the chalk in the other.
- Motif: HANDOVER of a pair. Unspent fact: the unwritten mark.

### U6 — "The next me" (carry: parcel + chalk, courier's hands)
Frame sentence: *the home street at dawn, and its far end is already
swapping away.*
- 0–1.5 HOOK: anchor street at dawn, bookend framing; she holds parcel
  and chalk, poised over the parcel's paper. The depot door stands open
  on darkness.
- 1.5–4 STATE: she raises the chalk an inch above the parcel. He steps
  back, giving her the moment.
- 4–7 TURN: the cull light rises EARLY — shadows slide, and at the far
  end of the street the buildings begin swapping mid-frame.
- 7–9 LINE — COURIER to WATCHER (refusal): "Then the next me signs it.
  Not me." (8 words)
- 9–10 CLIFF: chalk hovering an inch above the parcel, unsigned, cull
  light rising — cut on the onset. Nothing resolves.
- Motif: SUSPENSION. Bridge cliff: the unsigned parcel is the next
  chain's first problem.

Escalation: blank-wall door → whole street replaced → doors everywhere,
one opening onto a street → the wall already knew → a mark nobody wrote →
the cull comes early and visibly eats the street. Each peak stranger than
the last. Motifs: reach / world-swap / door-testing / inspection /
handover / suspension — no motif repeats.
