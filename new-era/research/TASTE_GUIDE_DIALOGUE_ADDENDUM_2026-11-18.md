## The new gate — THE STRANGER TEST

A dialogue-specific cold test, run twice: at script stage before
lock, and on the finished master's transcript beside Gate (f).
Input: the dialogue only, annotations stripped, read aloud once by
a reader who did not write it. The reader answers: **Q1** — what
does each person want, from what they *do* with words (a want that
is only announced = fail)? **Q2** — what did each second line do to
the first (a two-line exchange of filed statements = fail)?
**Q3** — mark STAGE any line no person in that situation would say
to that person (≥2 = fail). **Q4** — retell the scene as gossip in
two sentences (contradiction with the locked story, or ≥2 facts
needed that the dialogue never carries = fail). Gate (f) keeps
comprehension; the Stranger Test owns plausibility. Full protocol:
DIALOGUE_METHOD.md §3; template fields: template v3 §2 Pass 3.7
and §5.4.

## Conflicts with standing law — named explicitly

This addendum **supersedes** the following dialogue-construction
provisions. Everything not listed here stands unchanged.

1. **F-3 (want spoken by the wanter, plainly, by U2) — SUPERSEDED
   as a construction driver.** The legibility goal is retained;
   the mechanism is replaced. A want now enters dialogue only as a
   move (offer / demand / refusal / complaint / bargain); the
   grammatical form "I want X" is banned except one logged plea
   exception per episode. Compliance is tested by Stranger Test Q1
   (want recoverable from the character's first two moves), not by
   locating a want-sentence. *Why:* F-3 complied with literally
   produced want-declarations addressed to the gate — ONE SHORT
   E3's Clerk: "I want the book closed even by noon.", annotated
   in-script "(her want, plain, spoken by her in U1)".
2. **E2 Speech & Story Addendum §3, TW-1 (borrowed-words /
   echo-twinning as rooms apply it) — SUPERSEDED as a construction
   target.** Echo survives only as a genuine answer shape
   (completion / negation of the line just spoken). Matching-form
   exchanges — want for want, claim for claim — are the named
   failure class SYMMETRIC FILING. (F-17 had already struck verbal
   echo-chaining as a device; this closes the loophole by which it
   survived as *symmetry*.)
3. **E2 Addendum §3, TW-11 (bookend restatement device) —
   SUPERSEDED.** A closing line that restates an opening line with
   the outcome swapped in is the named failure class BOOKEND
   RESTATEMENT. Facts are spent twice by the Fact Spend rule
   (event, then verdict restatement in different words by a
   different voice) — never by verbatim return.
4. **F-26 (hear-before-it-moves) stumble definition — AMENDED.** A
   stumble is a *meaning* failure (the listener cannot say what was
   meant or who it was meant for). Parse texture is not a stumble;
   contractions are the default surface and may never be repaired
   into expanded forms. FORMAL REPAIR is a named failure class.
   *Why:* the old gradient's emblematic repair was ONE SHORT E3's
   "Thirty's the count" → "Thirty is the count" — the gate edited
   spokenness out and logged it as an improvement.
5. **F-5 / F-17 "connective lines" quota — SUPERSEDED as a quota.**
   Connection is a property of exchanges, tested by Stranger Test
   Q2, not a countable line property. A script may no longer record
   "connective lines: 5 (floor 4)" as evidence of contact.

**Unchanged, deliberately:** F-25 spoken-load caps and the
Container Law (arithmetic, not style); F-13 ear test and F-9
(delivery and story gates — they keep their jobs; they were never
the dialogue gate); Gate (f) cold run (comprehension); F-14 /

delivery notes — generation route unchanged); F-16 prompt hygiene
and the colon form; the F-22 end-state pin (the verdict *line*
that closes an episode is now written under the Fact Spend rule,
not as a bookend).
