**Provenance:** the four studies the user ordered on 2026-11-16 —

opera, "never doubt a king" / pauper, and basic story packs / nursery
rhymes / Brothers Grimm), executed same-day, plus the verdict that

**The verdict this addendum answers (msg 513, front and center):**
the best E1 was rated **ONE STAR out of five**. The user's words:
linked scenes and a working conversation are "the bare basics of
creating something" — the story still did not make sense, the
characters had no meaning and did not do much, and nothing good or
decisive happened. Cohesion is the floor, not the goal. Every rule
below is measured against the four charges:

1. **The story must MAKE SENSE** — one argument, stated early,
   settled on screen.
2. **Characters must MEAN something** — each one is a function in
   that argument, with a want and a stake.
3. **Characters must DO things** — by U6 every principal has taken
   an action, not just said lines, that changed the situation.
4. **Something must HAPPEN** — one value change per episode,
   visible, with a consequence left open.

And from msg 508, the speech charge: dialogue must be **twined** —
lines answering, escalating, and quoting each other — not muttered
phrases in alternation. Babble survives only as booked lore-bait
(SR-3).

source or verbatim specimen). [L] = LEAD-LEVEL (inference, synthesis,
or single-source) — never upgraded here beyond what the source
report claimed. [I] = this addendum's construction (e.g. the 6×10s
mappings). Full honesty notes are in §7; read them before quoting

## §1 — MERGED STORY AND SERIAL RULES (the transfer set, deduplicated)

Four studies, four vocabularies, one engine. Where two studies
stated the same rule, it appears once, with both citations.

### Story rules — bind Pass 1 (storyline) and Pass 2 (breakdown)

- **ST-1 — ONE EPISODE, ONE VALUE CHANGE.** Before any dialogue is
  written, the board states the episode as a before/after pair in
  one sentence ("in control → exposed"; "intruder → heir"). If the
  end state equals the start state, the episode does not exist yet.
  This is Gate B raised from the unit to the episode. [E — F §1,
  Microdrama Academy: "if nothing changed, the episode can be
  deleted with nothing lost"]
- **ST-2 — ONE ROPE.** Every line and every beat is a move in the
  episode's single visible argument — a misjudgment, a debt, a
  claim, a count. A cold reader must be able to state the argument
  in one sentence; a line that isn't pulling on the rope is cut or
  booked under SR-3. [E — K §6.1: in the king/pauper engine "every
  line is a move in one visible argument"]
- **ST-3 — THE FIVE-BEAT SPINE.** Every episode maps onto Hook
  (mid-conflict) → Denial → Evidence → Turn → Hanger. Beats may
  share units, but the Denial and the Hanger may never be dropped,
  and the Evidence must be a thing *shown* — a prop, a photo, a
  witness, an entrance — never a thing argued. §2's skeletons are
  this spine wearing different clothes. [E — F §1, §9 rule 3;
  Poptoy's five-beat sheet]
- **ST-4 — THE TEST IS STATED BEFORE IT IS TESTED.** Rules,
  passwords, prices, and conditions are spoken aloud *before* they
  are first applied — the mother goat names the rough voice and the
  black feet before the wolf knocks. Suspense is not "what will
  happen?" but "will he pass the test we were told?" [E — G §1.2,
  T3; K §6: the duel's rules are stated by everyone]
- **ST-5 — THE AUDIENCE HOLDS ONE SECRET BY THE END OF U1.** Give
  the viewer one visible fact the characters lack — a face, an
  object, a number, never a lore fact. Retention is watching the
  gap close, not withholding what the scene is about. The
  characters must not state the secret until the reveal unit.
  [E — K §7: dramatic irony is the engine's retention device]
- **ST-6 — ESCALATION IS ITEMIZED.** Offences and steps escalate
  in nameable stages — a word, a prop struck, an authority
  summoned — one per unit, listed in order on the board, so the
  payoff can settle the account item by item. Vague hostility
  doesn't twine; a list does. [E — K §6.6]
- **ST-7 — THE TURN RE-RANKS A RELATIONSHIP.** The reveal's
  sharpest edge is an inversion — *X was Y's driver / debtor /
  keeper all along* — not a spectacle. The breakdown names the
  inversion in words before any spectacle is designed around it.
  [E — K §3.2, §9 rule 6]
- **ST-8 — FACTS ARE ANNOUNCED WHEN THEY BITE.** Identities,
  relationships, and secrets are spoken aloud at the beat where
  they change the power in the room — by the person they damage or
  the authority who enforces them. No front-loaded exposition; no
  key fact carried by visuals alone. In talking scenes the
  sequence is: announce yourself on entry, declare the plan before
  doing it, report the feeling at the turn. [E — F §5.2; D §6,
  GameToons triad]
- **ST-9 — THE CORE FACT ENTERS THE RECORD, TWICE MORE.** The
  episode's core fact is stated (a) as event, (b) as verdict —
  restated by a second voice, which is what makes it *record* —
  and (c) carried out as the written end-question or the next
  episode's recap line. Separately, after *every* turn, one
  in-character recap line says what just happened and what it
  means. Redundancy of information, never of wording — rephrase
  each time, with one licensed exception: a single key word
  repeated louder as the quotable beat. [E across specimens;
  the three-placement taxonomy is F §4.2's synthesis — L as a
  genre-wide claim; G T5 for the per-turn recap]
- **ST-10 — FRAME ONE IS THE CONTRACT.** The episode's working
  title states the setup and the promised turn (if the title can't
  carry the premise, the premise isn't surface enough yet), and
  the opening frame is a *contrast* — the wrong label on the
  person, the wrong number on the board — with the conflict
  already in motion, never the setup being assembled. (This is
  the story-side satisfaction of BLAM E1.) [E — D §3.5, §3.7;
  K §7]
- **ST-11 — THE MORAL, IF ANY, IS ONE PLAIN SENTENCE.** Spoken
  once, at the end, by the figure who earned it, in words with no
  lore terms — then nothing after it except the hanger. No
  epilogue chatter. [E — K §6.4: "no branch trusts the viewer to
  extract it"]

### Serial rules — bind the lock and the next episode's Pass 1

- **SR-1 — END MID-CONSEQUENCE; OPEN BY PAYING.** The episode
  ends on a *visible new fact* (an arrival, a changed board, a
  person in the wrong seat), not a mystery box and not a
  resolution. The next episode's first beat — inside its first
  five seconds — answers the previous hanger at least partly,
  then opens a larger debt. Full payment ends series; partial
  payment compounds them. [E — D §3.4; F §6.2; Microdrama
  Academy's Pickup beat]
- **SR-2 — THE HANGER LEDGER.** The continuity ledger gains a
  booked-promise column: what the last hanger promised, and the
  episode/beat where it is due. A hanger with no ledger entry may
  not be locked; a due hanger unpaid is a continuity defect of
  the same class as a word-count breach. A hanger may be rolled
  over only by paying interest on it in the same episode.
  [E — F §6.2, §6.3: Strawberrina's unpaid ending is the negative
  case]
- **SR-3 — BABBLE NEEDS A BOOKING.** Deliberate confusion — the
  lore-building babble msg 508 explicitly keeps available — is
  written as a *plant*: short, repeated identically where it
  recurs, framed in the same unit by another character's twined
  line that marks it as confusion ("He's lying. His voice is
  wrong."), and logged with its payoff episode named at lock
  time. Booked confusion is lore; unbooked confusion is
  muttering, and the Critic strikes it. [E — F §9 rule 12;
  K §9 rule 10; G §3 standing cautions]
- **SR-4 — PERSIST THE SITUATION; MOVE ONLY THE POWER. ONE NEW
  ELEMENT PER EPISODE.** The situation (the list, the count, the
  seat, the cell) survives the cut; episodes continue it with the
  power reversed or tightened — the prisoner becomes the jailer.
  Each episode adds exactly one new element to the known system,
  never a second system. [E — D §3.1, §3.2, §7 rules 4 and 8]
- **SR-5 — CONTINUITY IS WORN, NOT RECAPPED.** Each episode
  scripts one visible state-carrier — a scar, a held object, a
  number changed on the board — that tells a returning viewer
  where things stand and orients a cold viewer in the same image.
  The carrier is named at breakdown and written into the
  production brief (this is the writing-side half of the A6
  strong-reference rule). [E — D §3.3: "the character's face *is*
  the recap"]
- **SR-6 — SOUND CARRIES EMOTION; ONE SOUND-LOGO MARKS THE
  TURN.** Screams, laughter, crying, and score carry feeling at
  zero comprehension cost; the word budget is not spent narrating
  emotion. The turn gets one repeatable audio mark — a catchphrase,
  a bell, a stamp — at the exact frame the gap closes, reusable
  across episodes as lore that costs a new viewer nothing.
  [E — D §4, §7 rule 12; K §6.5]
- **SR-7 — NUMBERS ARE STATUS, WHERE NUMBERS EXIST.** [L — the
  identification of Dookie Bowl as the user's "champions"
  referent is lead-level; the mechanics are evidenced from its
  summaries] In a show with a count, a board, or a tally: the
  number is worn where the camera can see it, and characters
  state and challenge each other's numbers — arithmetic hierarchy
  needs no biography and gives dialogue a built-in
  call-and-response. At least one number-challenge exchange per
  episode in such shows. [D §5; native fit: ONE SHORT's board]

## §2 — THE STORY-SKELETON MENU (Pass 1: pick one, by name)

The Storyliner names the episode's skeleton in the board header.
Every skeleton below is the ST-3 spine in a folk-proven shape; all
run on one want, ≤3 speaking characters, one location or one road,
and the A1 word budget. The folk shapes are centuries public-domain;
the tellings — cast, world, lines — are ours (this is also the
msg-486 cosplay/parody route's story side: borrow the *shape*,
never the telling). [Skeleton shapes E from the primary texts in
G §2; the 6×10s mappings are I — this network's construction]

**Rule-of-three placement (all skeletons):** attempts land in
U2/U3 (compressed pair) and U5 (the resolving attempt) when U4
carries a protocol change or midpoint reversal, or in U2/U3/U4
when U5 is reserved for consequences. Never spend U1 on an
attempt — U1 is for the want and the rule.

### S0 — THE FIVE-BEAT SPINE (the soap default; any skeleton's fallback)

The genre's own sheet, mapped to our units. If a board fits no
other skeleton, it runs S0 or it doesn't run.

- **U1 — HOOK.** One line states the conflict, mid-flight; the
  want is spoken (ST-8, TW-3).
- **U2 — DENIAL.** The lie or refusal — slightly too specific
  (TW-4).
- **U3 — EVIDENCE.** A prop, photo, witness, or entrance: shown,
  not argued.
- **U4 — TURN.** The line or reveal that changes who holds power.
- **U5 — VERDICT.** A second voice restates the core fact as
  record (ST-9); the consequence begins.
- **U6 — HANGER.** The last line opens the next episode: a spoken
  question or an entrance mid-sentence (SR-1, TW-11).

### S1 — THE DOOR TEST
*Sources: The Wolf and the Seven Young Kids (Grimm 005); The Three
Little Pigs.* A guarded threshold; the keeper states the test
aloud; the claimant fails on the stated evidence, adapts exactly
one thing, tries again; the third attempt resolves at the door.

- **U1** Keeper states the want (keep them safe / keep them out)
  AND the test, in so many words. Rule spoken before tested.
- **U2** Attempt 1: right words, fails test A. Keeper's side cites
  the evidence back in the claim's own terms.
- **U3** Claimant adapts A off-screen. Attempt 2: passes A, fails
  test B. Evidence cited again, same frame.
- **U4** Claimant adapts B. Keeper's side, suspicious, *upgrades
  the protocol aloud* — the audience learns the new rule before
  the attempt.
- **U5** Attempt 3: both tests pass. The door opens; the reveal
  lands in the same unit — who actually came in.
- **U6** Consequence, played fast — or, in the pass version, the
  keeper's closing line hands the test to the newcomer. End on
  the refrain, ownership transferred.

### S2 — THE BARGAIN WITH A PRICE
*Sources: Rumpelstiltskin; The Frog Prince.* A desperate want with
a deadline; a helper names a small price; the fix works and creates
the need for the next fix; the final price is the one thing the
hero cannot pay; the escape is a test the helper himself proposes,
won with information the helper leaked.

- **U1** Want + deadline stated by the hero. Helper enters and
  states the deal's grammar: "What will you give me if I do it?"
- **U2** Bargain 1: small price. Fix performed on screen. It
  works. A bigger demand arrives in the same unit.
- **U3** Bargain 2: bigger price, same exchange, one variable
  changed. It works. The impossible demand arrives — nothing left
  to pay with.
- **U4** Bargain 3: the unpayable price named. Hero accepts in
  despair. Time jump: the price comes due.
- **U5** The escape clause, proposed by the helper himself (guess
  my name / keep your promise). Two rounds burn fast; a messenger
  or overheard refrain leaks the answer.
- **U6** Final round as call-and-response: two wrong answers, then
  the true one. Last line re-states the price, now void.

### S3 — THE WISH LADDER
*Source: The Fisherman and His Wife (Grimm 019).* A granter bound
by a rule, summoned by an identical refrain. Each cycle the want
grows, the messenger carries it verbatim, the granter's line gets
flatter; a visible meter degrades one step per cycle. The last
wish overreaches the rule itself — snap back to the start.

- **U1** The accident that creates the granter. The hero asks for
  nothing; the want is installed in the partner instead ("Did you
  not wish for anything first?").
- **U2** Wish 1 (small). Refrain summons, verbatim. Messenger
  restates the want in plain words. Granted. Meter slightly wrong.
- **U3** Wish 2 (bigger). Same refrain, same restatement shape.
  Meter worse. Granter's line shorter.
- **U4** Wish 3 (status). The partner now argues the ladder aloud
  — the want has a logic the audience can follow and dread.
- **U5** Wishes 4–5 compressed: the refrain is pure ritual, the
  meter is a storm, the granter barely speaks. Speed is the
  meaning: the ladder is out of control.
- **U6** The final wish breaks the rule itself. The granter's one
  line restores the opening frame. Final shot = U1's shot.

### S4 — THE JOINING ROAD
*Source: The Bremen Town Musicians (Grimm 027).* An exile with a
stated plight meets the next exile; the joining exchange is always
the same two moves — plight stated, invitation offered. Each member
adds exactly one skill, named at joining. The assembled group
performs one combined stunt; a scout's wrong report seals the win.

- **U1** Exile 1 states plight + plan in one breath. Meets exile
  2; plight 2 in parallel form; invitation; they walk.
- **U2** Exiles 3 and 4 join, same exchange, compressed — the
  audience now completes the pattern themselves. Each skill named.
- **U3** The group finds the prize occupied. Scout reports what he
  sees — recap #1, the accurate version.
- **U4** The plan stated aloud as a stack: who stands on whom, who
  sounds when. The stunt fires; the occupiers flee; the group
  takes the house.
- **U5** The enemy sends one scout back. He meets each member in
  the dark, in reverse order; each skill fires once, silently
  misread.
- **U6** The scout's report: the whole climax re-told in wrong
  words — and it is the *report*, not the fight, that ends the
  story. Final frame: the group at the table, not travelling.

### S5 — THE CUMULATIVE CHAIN
*Sources: The House That Jack Built; There Was an Old Lady Who
Swallowed a Fly.* One new link per unit; every unit re-recites the
whole chain back to the root. The chain *is* the exposition — a
cold viewer joining at any unit is fully caught up within ten
seconds. This skeleton passes the cold-join test (§5) by
construction.

- **U1** Root + link 1, stated twice (once new, once as the first
  full recitation).
- **U2** Link 2. Full recitation: new link first, then the chain
  unwinding to the root — delivered as a character *reporting* the
  chain to a newcomer who asked.
- **U3** Link 3. The recitation grows; the listener starts
  interrupting with the root line — the audience now owns the
  refrain.
- **U4** Link 4. Mid-chain crisis: someone challenges the chain,
  and the answer is the chain itself, recited as evidence.
- **U5** Link 5. Recitation at maximum length, played as a feat —
  breath, speed, the listener's face. The episode's set piece.
- **U6** Final link. The chain snaps shut with a verdict line, or
  the root itself answers back. End on the root image, alone,
  quiet — U1's first line, now carrying everything.

### S6 — THE SWAP-DOWN ROAD
*Source: Hans in Luck (Grimm 083).* The hero carries one valuable
object down a road and trades it at every stop. Each trader pitches
in the same grammar ("your X is a burden; my Y is freedom"); the
hero accepts with the same satisfaction line, re-worded one degree
each time. The audience watches the value fall and the confidence
rise — the gap is the comedy and the meaning.

- **U1** The prize, earned and stated. Hero states his want: get
  home. First trader appears.
- **U2** Swap 1: the pitch names the burden; satisfaction line v1.
  The new object misbehaves once, on screen, in the same unit.
- **U3** Swaps 2–3 compressed: same pitch grammar, faster;
  satisfaction line v2, one word more certain. Each object
  misbehaves once.
- **U4** Swap 4, with the scare beat — the only moment the hero
  almost doubts. The pitch talks him out of it. Satisfaction v3.
- **U5** Swap 5: the heaviest "freedom" yet, plus the seller's
  add-on. Hero carries both to the well, satisfaction at maximum.
- **U6** The load slips into the well. Beat of silence — then the
  verdict: the satisfaction line completed at last, the loss
  re-framed as the win. Final frame rhymes with U1: same road,
  empty hands.
- *Trickster variant:* the swap is a theft — U5 becomes the
  discovery test spoken aloud to the object ("Little table, set
  thyself!" — nothing happens), U6 the cudgel's verdict. The Door
  Test wearing a different coat.

### S7 — THE DEMONSTRATION TRAP
*Source: Hansel and Gretel (Grimm 015), the oven scene.* A captive
is ordered to operate the dangerous thing. The captive's weapon is
a plain statement of incompetence. The captor, contemptuous,
demonstrates. The captive applies the demonstrated action to the
captor. The climax is three lines and one shove.

- **U1** Captivity and apparatus, both stated: the captor's want
  spoken aloud, the danger heating, the small deception the
  audience is let in on (ST-5).
- **U2** The captive asks questions whose answers are the layout
  ("How does your oven open?"). The captor answers freely — she
  is explaining her own trap to its victim.
- **U3** The order. The captive's refusal is not defiance but a
  request, stated plainly: "I do not know how. Show me."
- **U4** The demonstration. The captor performs, narrating her own
  competence. Longest held beat of the episode.
- **U5** The reversal. One line only, the captive's — a restatement
  of the captor's own words from U3/U4 ("There — that is how.").
- **U6** Release and inventory: the freed prisoner, the hoard
  discovered and named item by item (the Cumulative Chain in
  miniature), the road home. End on a door that opens the right
  way, answering U1's cage door.

### S8 — THE THREE VERDICTS
*Sources: Goldilocks and the Three Bears; the three-brothers quest
frame (The Golden Bird).* One tester meets the same test three
times in three sizes; each meeting ends with a verdict line built
on one frame — too big / too small / just right — with exactly one
word changing. The third verdict is the trigger. The smallest
complete skeleton in the pack, and the easiest to write.

- **U1** The tester arrives at the empty place and states her want
  (food, rest, a seat — all three, in order). First object class
  announced: "Three bowls."
- **U2** Verdicts on class 1: too hot — too cold — just right
  (consumed). Each verdict one short line, same grammar. Pattern
  installed.
- **U3** Class 2: same verdicts, one variable changed — this time
  "just right" breaks under her. The third verdict now has a cost.
- **U4** Class 3: verdicts at double speed — the audience supplies
  the grammar before she speaks it. Just right = sleep.
- **U5** The owners return. Each re-runs the verdicts as
  accusations, in the same grammar, escalating by size — the whole
  episode re-told in three lines (ST-9's recap, as a cascade).
- **U6** Confrontation. The tester wakes, sees the three owners in
  size order — the rule of three made visible in one frame — and
  runs. Final line belongs to the smallest owner: the verdict
  frame, applied to her.

### S9 — THE DOUBTED KING
*Source: the king/pauper engine (K §8) — Twain's root, the Sheru
cartoon cluster, the vertical-drama apps.* One figure of true high
rank wears low rank. The world prices him by his clothes and says
so to his face, in escalating, itemized insults. One person treats
the pauper as a person. When the insult crosses a line, the
disguise ends in the most spectacular reveal the budget allows —
and its sharpest edge is a relationship inversion.

- **U1** The contrast in frame one: rags against the palace. The
  audience is told the secret (ST-5); the mockery is already in
  progress — never open on the disguise being put on.
- **U2** Doubt, itemized insult 1: the visible prop named ("that
  broken bicycle"). The pauper's claim stated once, plainly, and
  disbelieved.
- **U3** Insult 2, one step up (ST-6). The kind one is marked:
  one character treats the pauper as a person (CH-6).
- **U4** The line is crossed — a physical or institutional insult.
  The call is made / the arrival begins.
- **U5** The reveal: spectacle maximal, and inside it the
  inversion — the proud one's true rank exposed (ST-7). The
  sound-logo fires (SR-6).
- **U6** The re-rank made visible: who stands where, who holds
  what, whose name is on the board. The moral in one plain
  sentence (ST-11). The gift/reform begun. Final frame = U1's
  contrast, reversed — and the A6 strong-reference joint into the
  next episode.

## §3 — SPEECH-TWINING RULES (bind Pass 3; tested at Passes 4–6)

**Definition.** A line is *twined* when it cannot be moved,
reordered, or given to another character without breaking —
because it borrows its words, its grammar, or its facts from the
line it answers. **The diagnostic of muttering is the shuffle
test:** twined dialogue breaks audibly when its lines are
shuffled; our E1 lines survived shuffling intact, which is the
precise proof they were never attached to each other. [F §3.1,
§7] Every rule below exists to make a scene fail shuffling.

- **TW-1 — BORROWED WORDS.** Every reply reuses a word, name, or
  fact from the line it answers — or explicitly negates it.
  Agreement in form, detonation in content: "I was. *With
  Cherry.*" [E — F §3.1, Clippie specimen; G T9]
- **TW-2 — ANSWER THE LINE YOU WERE GIVEN.** Every line rules on
  the previous line: answer its question, accept or refuse its
  offer, cite evidence against its claim. The kids at the door
  say nothing except rulings on the wolf's claim. A line that
  rules on nothing is an orphan — cut or rewrite. [E — G T1]
- **TW-3 — THE QUESTION–ANSWER LOCK.** Each unit contains at
  least one question or claim engineered so the next line is
  mandatory — ideally a question whose *any* truthful answer
  damages the answerer ("What flavour?" / "…Strawberry."). The
  breakdown names, per unit, what makes the next line mandatory.
  [E — F §3.2]
- **TW-4 — THE TOO-SPECIFIC DENIAL.** Every lie or refusal
  contains one detail more than the truth would need — a date, a
  time, a category ("She's… she's my *accountant*"). The audience
  hears the lie before anyone proves it, and the Evidence beat
  gets a specific claim to detonate against. A vague denial
  ("nobody!") fails this rule. [E — F §3.3; Poptoy: "slightly too
  specific"]
- **TW-5 — VERBATIM REPEAT, ONE VARIABLE.** When an exchange
  repeats, its wording stays identical and exactly one element
  moves — the voice, the paw, the wish, the price. If two things
  change, the audience can't tell which one mattered and the
  scene reads as new babble instead of a developing pattern.
  [E — G T4; the fisherman's summons is word-identical five times]
- **TW-6 — ONE REFRAIN, ONE MOUTH.** Each episode has one refrain
  — a summons, a greeting, a satisfaction line, a door formula —
  word-identical every time, always from the same character,
  until a scripted transfer or break in U6. Licensed variant: the
  **escalating one-word ladder** ("Mama." / "Help." / "Somebody."
  / "Anybody.") — repetition with widening scope reads as
  escalation, not babble; babble is repetition without a ladder.
  [E — G T6; D §4]
- **TW-7 — TWO TO A SCENE.** Two speakers per exchange, maximum.
  A third character enters by starting a *new* exchange, never by
  joining the current one. (This governs exchanges, not the
  episode cast: the standing cap of ≤3 speaking voices + authority
  still binds the episode.) Three-way talk is where
  return-conversation babble breeds — nobody knows whose line is
  being answered. [E — G T7, Olrik's law of two to a scene]
- **TW-8 — NOUNS, NOT WEATHER.** Every line contains at least one
  concrete thing the camera can show — a coin, a paw, a station,
  a name, a number. Lines whose content is a mood, an energy, a
  destiny are banned. Test: if a line could be spoken unchanged
  in any other episode, it belongs to no episode. [E — G T8]
- **TW-9 — NAMES AND ROLES ARE SPENDS.** In two-handers, names
  are cut from routine lines; a name or role-address is spent
  only where it does work — an **entrance**, an **accusation**, a
  **reveal** — and every principal is role-addressed at least
  once per episode ("your driver," "my clerk," "the man who owes
  me"), so a cold viewer never has to infer the relationship
  chart. The two source rules pull opposite ways (fruit soap cuts
  names because identity is visual; the king engine announces
  relationships because the chart is the plot); the spend rule is
  the reconciliation: scarce, but never absent. [E — F §5.3;
  K §9 rule 11]
- **TW-10 — LINE MECHANICS.** One idea per line (a line carrying
  two ideas can be neither answered nor echoed cleanly). The
  payload word goes last in the line, where the next line can
  grab it. Silence is written in as a scripted beat, not left as
  dead air. Per-line ≤12 words and per-unit ceilings stay A1.
  [E — F §3.6]
- **TW-11 — THE LAST LINE DOES ONE OF TWO DECLARED JOBS.** Either
  it **restates a U1 line with the outcome swapped in** ("Go
  home. She is already in the pig-stye again."), or it **poses
  the booked hanger question**. The breakdown declares which;
  a last line doing neither fails. The two source rules (folk
  grammatical closure vs the soap's cut-on-the-question) are both
  satisfied across a season by declaring per episode, never by
  drifting into a third shape. [E — G T10; F §6.1]
- **TW-12 — FACT FIRST, FEELING SECOND.** [L — sequencing pattern
  across F's specimens, consistent with the soap scholarship; not
  measured] No unit opens with an emotion whose causing fact has
  not yet been stated. Emotion-first writing is precisely what
  reads as muttering: affect with no attached fact for the next
  line to grab. The feeling rides on the fact's repetition ("He
  said strawberry was his favourite. His *FAVOURITE.*").

## §4 — CHARACTER MEANING AND AGENCY (bind the Pass 2 cards; audited at Pass 5)

Msg 513's charges, verbatim targets: characters "didn't even have
meaning and they didn't do much." Meaning = a function in the
episode's one argument. Agency = an action that changes the
situation. Both are card-level properties, fixed before lines
exist — a character problem discovered at Pass 3 is a Pass 2
failure returned, not a line problem patched.

- **CH-1 — THE CARD IS COMPLETE BEFORE PASS 3.** Every speaking
  character's card carries: **one want** (never changes across the
  series; only means and allies do), **one stake** (what it costs
  them if they fail — want + deadline + cost, stated), **one
  wound or wrong belief** (the A4 shared wrong belief lives here),
  **one rule they will not break**, **one tell** (visible or
  audible, CH-5), **one voice rule** (CH-2), and **one signature
  line nobody else would say**. A missing field blocks the
  breakdown. [E — F §3.6; Dawnwood character card via F §3.6;
  A4]
- **CH-2 — A VOICE RULE, NOT A VOICE VIBE.** Each card states the
  character's speech rule in one sentence: *speaks in questions
  when hurt; over-explains with dates when lying; one-line
  sarcasm, always takes the last word; formal and slow, holds the
  secrets.* The rule is twining infrastructure — because the
  liar's lying is audibly marked, his denial lines twine with
  every earlier scene where he told the truth plainly. A line any
  character could say fails the card and is rewritten. [E — F
  §3.6, Poptoy's four worked rules]
- **CH-3 — AGENCY: ONE SITUATION-CHANGING ACTION BY U6.** Every
  principal performs at least one action — not a speech act alone
  — that changes the situation: opens the door, hands over the
  note, crosses out the name, drags the attacker into the cell.
  The board card for that unit names a verb done to a person or
  an object. A principal whose episode is all lines and no deed
  fails this check at breakdown, where the fix is cheap. Intent
  is shown as action in the same beat it forms; speech is spent
  only where action can't carry it — a farewell, a plea, a
  threat, an invitation, a verdict. [E — D §4, §7 rule 1: nine
  parts on ~five lines; K §6.3]
- **CH-4 — THE FUNCTION PHRASE.** Each principal's function in the
  episode's one argument (ST-2) is stateable in one phrase on the
  board: *the keeper, the liar, the doubter, the kind one, the
  authority who knows.* If two principals share a function phrase,
  merge them or differentiate them before the board is approved —
  two characters with one function is why E1 casts felt
  interchangeable. [I — synthesis of K §6.3 and F §7's voice-rule
  diagnosis]
- **CH-5 — THE TELL FIRES BEFORE THE TURN.** The character's tell
  (from CH-1) appears at least one unit before their decisive
  line or deed, so the turn is earned and the audience reads it
  coming: the liar over-explains in U2 so the U4 collapse needs
  no announcement. A turn with no prior tell is a deus ex
  machina at character scale. [E — F §3.6, §8 Sample A mechanics]
- **CH-6 — THE KIND ONE (misjudgment skeletons).** In any
  misjudgment story (S9 above all), one character treats the
  mispriced person as a person *before* the reveal. Their reward
  at the re-rank is the emotional receipt that proves the moral
  without stating it twice. [E — K §5, §9 rule 7]
- **CH-7 — INTELLIGENCE BEATS (A4, restated as a check).** The
  questioning character notices by U2, names by U4, acts in U6 —
  and "acts" means CH-3's deed, not a concluding speech. A4
  already binds; this addendum's only change is that the U6 act
  is audited against CH-3's verb-on-the-board standard. [A4;
  E — F §8 samples]

## §5 — VERIFICATION: every rule has an owner, a pass, and a test

A rule without a check becomes a wish. The three master
instruments, then the per-rule matrix.

**The three master tests (Critic, Pass 6, on the locked draft):**

1. **THE SHUFFLE TEST.** Shuffle one scene's lines. If the
   exchange survives reordering unbroken, it is not twined —
   return to Pass 3. [F §3.1]
2. **THE COLD-JOIN TEST.** Cover all units except one random
   middle unit. From that unit's dialogue alone, the reader must
   recover (i) the want, (ii) the rule or obstacle, (iii) what
   has already been tried. S5 passes by construction; every other
   skeleton passes if ST-4 and ST-9 are obeyed. [G §3]
3. **THE DELETION TEST.** Strike every line that could be deleted
   without changing the story. If more than three lines qualify,
   rewrite the scene. [E — Microdrama Academy's exercise, via
   F §4.3]

Gate E (the cold-reader lock gate) is unchanged and remains the
final word: who wanted what / what blocked / what changed by U6 /
why it matters, no lore terms.

## §6 — CONFLICTS WITH THE STANDING METHOD (recommendations — parent decides)

**Y1 — Dookie's near-wordlessness vs the A1 episode budget.**
Life of Dookie runs nine parts on ~five lines; A1 floors an
episode at 80–90 words. Both cannot be episode templates.
**RECOMMENDATION:** Dookie governs the *beat-level default* —
action and face first, speech only where action can't carry it
(CH-3) — not episode totals. The slate's dialogue episodes keep
A1. A genuinely near-wordless episode would need a declared
exception at the Showrunner gate, not a quiet drift under budget.

**Y2 — Names: cut vs announce.** Resolved inside TW-9 (names and
roles as scarce spends) rather than left as a conflict — flagged
here so the parent can overrule the reconciliation if either
source rule should win outright for a given show.

**Y3 — The last line: restatement vs question.** Resolved inside
TW-11 (declared per episode at breakdown). Flagged for the same
reason as Y2.

**Y4 — Two to a scene vs the cast cap.** TW-7 governs exchanges;
the standing cap (≤3 speaking + authority) governs episodes. No
change recommended; stated because a room reading only one
document could misread the other as repealed.

**Y5 — The moral sentence vs cut-at-the-peak.** ST-11's moral and
the hanger compete for the episode's last breath.
**RECOMMENDATION:** fixed order — verdict (ST-9b) → moral, if any
(ST-11) → hanger (SR-1). The moral is a content line, never a
scene; nothing is played after it except the question or the
arrival.

**Y6 — The audience secret vs Gate E's no-lore law.** ST-5 could
be misread as licensing withheld lore. It cannot: the secret must
be a *visible* fact picturable in U1 (a face, an object, a
number). A secret that needs explaining fails ST-5's own check
and Gate E simultaneously.
