blocks, all PASTE-READY, all gated: prompt_audit caught nine problems in my first draft - six instances of 'the eyes do not widen or narrow' in
the codec load-bearing blocks, and three 'without changing it' lines in the Archive - and all are now positive statements. That leaves zero
channels in the grid without a written set.
THE CODEX IS ONE SKELETON WITH MOVING CONTENT, AND I HAVE DELIBERATELY LEFT THE SIX GENERATION PROMPTS BYTE-IDENTICAL APART FROM THE WORDING YOU
SUPPLY AT THE VOICE STAGE. Same locked-off medium shot, same 0-3-11-18-28 timing, same cut to full-frame static at 28s, same plain black hoodie.
That is not laziness, it is the channel: a continuous broadcast is one set seen repeatedly, and the moment the set drifts between codec segments
the network stops reading as a network. Please 6-tile all six against each other and if the hoodie or the studio drifts on one, re-fire that one
against the C-1 seed rather than accepting the outlier.
THE CODEC STRUCTURE IS OURS, NOT A GUESS. Urgent open, news item, personal aside, non-sequitur, abrupt cut, static - and the personal aside is
always a small real thing. I found a penny and the ground still gives things back. Four pennies today, I have stopped thanking the ground and
started counting. A bent nail that is still a nail and still worth the jar. That aside is the one place in the network where something genuine
happens, and it is the only reason the non-sequitur lands.
THE CODEX CROSS-PROMOTES THE OTHER SEVEN CHANNELS WITHOUT EVER NAMING THE NETWORK. C-1 is the corridor. C-2 is the room that was found. C-3 is a
scoreboard that never leaves zero and a game show where a man counts past a number and the room stays empty. C-4 is a puppet film that has
started describing real events accurately. C-5 is a school teaching people to notice when a picture has been arranged. C-6 is the standing four-
hour-and-fifty-nine-minute transmission, and it ends by telling the viewer not to adjust their set and that there is a channel which does nothing
but stand on. A viewer meets all seven other channels through six pieces of deadpan military code, and no character ever says the word - that
stays a canon bug.
THE ARCHIVE IS THE OPPOSITE OF EVERY OTHER CHANNEL AND THAT IS THE POINT. Unedited found footage, diegetic only, NO VOICE AT ALL - the ~ WuBu ~
burn does the work a voiceover would do everywhere else. Six clips in which nothing happens, and the load-bearing detail in every one is an
ordinary, correctly-rendered, entirely static piece of the world. The gag is the length and the commitment, so a clip that tries to be eerie
fails. The QC here is stability, not drama: a corridor, a shut door, a bare room, a vending machine, a painted number on a wall, and the same
vending machine again from closer.
AND A-6 IS A-4 REPEATED FROM A CLOSER FRAMING, ON PURPOSE. The archive does not editorially curate, it just keeps the tape. But that will not
survive a fresh seed, so please fire A-6 against the A-4 seed with a changed framing instruction rather than cold - and if Muse refuses the
reuse, tell me, because a different machine is a different prop and the episode stops being an archive and becomes a scene.

## VOICE STAGE — four spoken beats per episode (Colonel's register: grave, formal, bureaucratic irony; native audio only)

C-1 (the corridor):
- URGENT OPEN (0-3s): "We interrupt this broadcast. The corridor is still there."
- NEWS ITEM (3-11s): "The corridor behind Studio B has been confirmed present for the ninth consecutive day. It leads nowhere new. This is the news."
- PERSONAL ASIDE (11-18s): "I found a penny by the studio door this morning. The ground continues to provide. I have stopped thanking it and started keeping count."
- NON-SEQUITUR (18-28s): "The vending machine takes exact change. So does the hallway."

C-2 (the room that was found):
- URGENT OPEN (0-3s): "We interrupt this broadcast. A room has been found."
- NEWS ITEM (3-11s): "The room was found exactly where it was left. It contains a chair, a door, and no explanation. We are investigating all three."
- PERSONAL ASIDE (11-18s): "My dress shoes have developed a squeak on the left foot only. I have decided to consider this a feature."
- NON-SEQUITUR (18-28s): "The weather will continue until it stops."

C-3 (the scoreboard that never leaves zero; the game show that counts past the number):
- URGENT OPEN (0-3s): "We interrupt this broadcast. Nothing has scored."
- NEWS ITEM (3-11s): "The scoreboard remains at zero to zero. A man on the game show counted past the number anyway. The room stayed empty, as scheduled."
- PERSONAL ASIDE (11-18s): "I ironed my uniform twice this morning. The second ironing was for morale."
- NON-SEQUITUR (18-28s): "Zero is a number. We checked twice."

C-4 (a puppet film describing real events accurately):
- URGENT OPEN (0-3s): "We interrupt this broadcast. The puppets are correct."
- NEWS ITEM (3-11s): "A puppet film has begun describing real events with unsettling accuracy. The puppets have been asked to stop. They have declined to comment."
- PERSONAL ASIDE (11-18s): "I keep a spare button in my left pocket. It has never been needed. That is the point of a spare button."
- NON-SEQUITUR (18-28s): "Felt is a fabric. This has been verified."

C-5 (a school teaching people to notice arranged pictures):
- URGENT OPEN (0-3s): "We interrupt this broadcast. Look at the picture."
- NEWS ITEM (3-11s): "A local school now teaches students to notice when a picture has been arranged. Enrollment is mandatory. The pictures have been notified."
- PERSONAL ASIDE (11-18s): "My coffee this morning was exactly the right temperature for exactly four minutes. I drank it in three, out of respect."
- NON-SEQUITUR (18-28s): "The frame is part of the picture. So is the wall."

C-6 (the four-hour-and-fifty-nine-minute transmission; do not adjust your set):
- URGENT OPEN (0-3s): "We interrupt this broadcast. It has been standing for four hours and fifty-nine minutes."
- NEWS ITEM (3-11s): "The transmission continues to stand on, doing nothing, and doing it well. Engineers confirm the nothing is stable."
- PERSONAL ASIDE (11-18s): "I have stood at this desk for six broadcasts. My posture has never been better. The desk agrees."
- NON-SEQUITUR (18-28s): "Do not adjust your set. There is a channel which does nothing but stand on. This was almost it."
