SUBJECT (quality bar): the stake is what the dark counts — if the
dark counts itself, the desk sells a count with no buyer, and if
nobody buys it, the count leaves Peter's hands and the sale lands
on the unlit board; the station-specific detail is the archive
room, the machine humming low with its counter window LIT holding
fifty-seven, the ground's ONE LIGHT held beneath Peter standing in
it at the threshold, the tower wall holding FIFTY-SEVEN ticks in
lights with the first, second, and fifth brightened, the slate's sum
changed a fifty-seventh time, the chosen floorboard's far edge
settled flat, and the next board EMPTY with its far edge settled
flat and dark.
WRITING (quality bar): every line is an action with a named
addressee and want — a demand to count the dark board, a
counter-question that throws the price back, a refusal to sell the
board dark, a payoff dare that lets the dark count itself; the
status shift is Peter's: from standing in the light he sells to
standing out of the light it counts — the seller loses the light
to the count itself.
LORE (quality bar): the count-as-currency rule changes a choice in
the scene — Peter must not sell the dark board himself to close
the sale, because selling it would sell the count's dark, so he
demands a counting instead of a sale, and the counter ticking to
fifty-eight with no hand on the machine is the count answering
without a seller.
Conversation question: if the dark counts itself, what does it
count toward?
Poached move: NAME THE UNAFFORDABLE SENTENCE (CRAFT-820) — the
sentence nobody can afford to say is "the dark counts itself";
every line argues about sales and boards while that sentence does
the work. CONTROL-VOICE FRAMING (CRAFT-835) — the frame is the
machine, which states the count's control by ticking with no hand
on it and returns control when Peter dares the desk to count the
dark. STATUS BY SYLLABLE COUNT (CRAFT-836) — Peter holds status by
spending the fewest words: his demand (4 words) costs less than
Dispatcher's counter-question (4 words) only by the refusal it
forces, and UNC's refusal (4 words) outranks them both by refusing
outright, until Peter's payoff (5 words) spends the most to dare.
A REVERSAL MUST REDIRECT (CRAFT-837) — the light leaving Peter for
the next board redirects who the count counts: from the seller
standing in it to the board lying dark, and every next move must
reckon with a count that needs no buyer. COUNTER-PUNCH, DON'T Q&A
(CRAFT-832) — P2's counter-question is the counter-punch:
Dispatcher never answers Peter straight, and the dodge raises the
price of the next question. THE UNTOUCHED OBJECT (CRAFT-833) — P3's
refusal names its object: the next board, which UNC holds his hand
above and never sells, because selling it would sell the count's
dark. 2+2 (CRAFT-834) — the audience is given two pieces (a counter
ticking to fifty-eight with no hand on the machine, a light leaving
a seller's footprints for a dark board) and assembles the one piece
no line states: the count counts without a buyer. THE REFUSED TOOL
— the refused action is selling the dark board to close the sale;
its price is selling the count's dark. ONE MIRACLE, THEN KICKBACK
(CRAFT-830) — the single miracle is the counter ticking to
fifty-eight untouched; the kickback is that the light leaves Peter
for the dark board, and no sale can buy it back. GIVE THE AUDIENCE
THE SECRET FIRST (CRAFT-826) — the audience knows from the P1 hook
(a lit threshold, a held fifty-seven, a dark empty board) that the
count already counts its dark before any character counts it;
Dispatcher fears pricing it, Peter fears selling it, UNC fears
counting it. AN OPEN LOOP MUST CLOSE ON AN OBJECT (CRAFT-838) —
the loop "what does the dark count?" closes on the flat dark board
with the light held beneath Peter again, an object state the eye
can verify, not a name. A DODGE MUST COST THE DODGER (CRAFT-839) —
Dispatcher's P2 dodge costs him the price: he must now count a
board nobody bought. WARMTH IS A CHARACTER WHO LEFT (CRAFT-840) —
the absence is the buyer; the object holding its trace is the dark
board, lifted with no buyer beside it.
Pool move: MEGA — scale as status: Peter argues the count's size
is the sale, the board is the excuse, the credit is the fight; the
counter's fifty-eight outranks any price he can name.
