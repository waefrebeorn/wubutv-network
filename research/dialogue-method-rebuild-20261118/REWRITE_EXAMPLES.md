# REWRITE EXAMPLES — SAME STORY, DIFFERENT TALK

Two scenes rewritten under DIALOGUE_METHOD.md. Story facts, payoff,
and unit structure are preserved exactly; only the talk changes.
Word counts respect the template's spoken-load caps (2 speakers in
one unit ≤18 words; 1 speaker ≤20). Annotations name the Phase-1
failure class removed and the Phase-2 mechanic used.

---

## SCENE 1 — ONE SHORT E3, U1–U2 (the opening exchange)

**Facts that must survive:** the Planner claims 31 umbrellas and
demands a count before noon · the Clerk's want is the book closed
even by noon · the Counter counts batch one to twenty · the
Counter's want is one count that comes out even · the remaining
pile is running light (GAP 1's stopped hand follows U2).

### Original (verbatim, ONE SHORT E3 SCRIPT.md)

> U1 — PLANNER: Thirty-one umbrellas. Count them before noon.
> U1 — CLERK: I want the book closed even by noon.
> U2 — COUNTER: Eighteen, nineteen, twenty — batch one stands now.
> U2 — COUNTER: One count even — that's all I want today.

### Rewrite

> U1 — PLANNER: Thirty-one! Count them — I need them by noon. *(8w)*
> U1 — CLERK: Then pray it's thirty-one — or the book shuts short. *(9w)*
> U2 — COUNTER: Eighteen… nineteen… twenty. Batch one. *(5w)*
> U2 — COUNTER: Twenty. Crate felt heavier than that. *(6w)*

### Line by line

- **Planner L1 — kept in substance, given a pulse.** "Count them
  before noon" was a scheduling note; "I need them by noon" is a
  demand with a person behind it. The claim and the deadline are
  unchanged. (Class removed: none — this was the original script's
  best line; the method keeps what already sounds said.)
- **Clerk L2 — WANT ANNOUNCEMENT → the want used as a weapon.** The
  original files her want ("I want the book closed even by noon")
  in matching form — SYMMETRIC FILING: a demand answered by a
  declaration. The rewrite makes her want a *stake she puts on the
  table*: if the count fails, she closes the book *short* — so the
  "even" of her want is now the thing at risk in the scene, and the
  Planner's deadline (already in L1) is the clock it runs against.
  Her want (book closed even by noon) is fully recoverable — a
  Stranger Test Q1 reader answers it from what she *does* with
  words: she names the price of the count being wrong. It also
  starts the conflict the original only schedules: she has already
  implied the claim may be short.
  (Mechanics: McKee's exposition wielded as ammunition; the
  too-specific pressure of the genre's denial family, inverted —
  a too-specific *condition*.)
- **Counter L3 — count kept, bookkeeping trimmed.** "Batch one
  stands now" was ledger phrasing ("stands" is record language);
  "Batch one." is a man putting umbrellas down. The count itself —
  the show's music — is untouched.
- **Counter L4 — WANT ANNOUNCEMENT → reaction that plants the
  doubt.** "One count even — that's all I want today" announced his
  want because F-3 required it spoken by U2. The rewrite spends the
  line on the *first move of that want*: he notices the crate felt
  heavier than twenty umbrellas. His want (a count that comes out
  even) is now carried by his attention — he is a man bothered by a
  number that doesn't feel right — and the short-pile fact, which
  the original carries only in GAP 1's picture and the script's
  annotations, enters the dialogue as a reaction, setting up his
  stopped hand. (Mechanics: fact first, feeling riding on it;
  reaction before record.)

**Stranger Test, applied retrospectively:** original U1 scores
FILED ITS OWN STATEMENT on the exchange (demand → want-declaration)
= FAIL condition 2. Rewrite U1 scores ANSWERED/TOPPED. Original U2
L4 is a Q1 ANNOUNCED = FAIL condition 3; rewrite Q1 is recoverable
from behaviour + line ("he wants the count to come out right —
look at him weighing the crate").

## SCENE 2 — HOLD ON E3, U1 (the accusation opens)

**Facts that must survive:** Ansel's claim — the Courier held his
tin, and his coins are gone · Laura's want — one true line on the
wall today, or none · the Courier is present with her hands open
(picture) · Laura is the unit's only speaker (Camp B).

### Original (verbatim, HOLD ON E3 SCRIPT.md, locked v5)

> LAURA: Ansel says the Courier held his tin. His coins are gone.
> LAURA: I write one true line today, or none.

### Rewrite

> LAURA: Your tin, Ansel. She held it. And the coins? *(9w)*
> LAURA: Gone, he says. Hands, Courier — one true line today, or none. *(11w)*

### Line by line

- **L1 — LEDGER LINE → interrogation.** The original reports the
  claim in the third person to a room containing both parties —
  a docket entry read aloud (Phase 1, D-1). The rewrite puts the
  claim *in play*: Laura addresses Ansel directly, states the part
  nobody disputes ("She held it") as common ground, and turns the
  disputed part into a question — "And the coins?" The episode now
  opens with a character *asking* something (the original episode
  contains no question between characters at all). The claim is
  still fully stated in line 1; it is stated as a move in an
  argument instead of as minutes of a meeting. (Mechanics:
  question–answer lock; echo-completion — her second sentence
  completes the claim's own grammar.)
- **L2 — report + VOW → Laura doing her job, want as her working
  standard.** "Gone, he says" keeps the claim *attributed as a
  claim* — Laura is testing it, not filing it; the audience hears
  her skepticism, which is the episode's engine. "Hands, Courier"
  converts U1's picture (the Courier's open hands, previously
  something Laura *describes* in U3's law line) into something
  Laura *causes* — she is working the case in her first unit. The
  want line survives but changes jobs: "one true line today, or
  none" is no longer a vow spoken to the wall; it is the standard
  she announces *while acting on it*, pressure on both parties —
  Ansel hears that his line must earn its place, the Courier hears
  what the hands are for. (Classes removed: LEDGER LINE, WANT
  ANNOUNCEMENT-as-vow. Mechanics: the want used, not declared —
  §1.3 of the method; fact first, stance riding on it.)

**Stranger Test, applied retrospectively:** original L1 draws the
Q3 STAGE reason "she's telling them their own news"; L2 draws
"vow — who is she telling?" Rewrite: Q1 for Laura is recoverable
from action ("she wants the truth on her wall — watch her work the
claim"); Q3 clean; the gossip retell ("A cook says the courier who
carried his coin tin lost his coins; the wall-writer makes her
show her hands and says only the truth gets written today") needs
no fact from outside the dialogue.

## What these rewrites deliberately do NOT do

- They do not change any plot fact, plant, payoff, or hanger. The
  umbrella is still by the door; the tin still came back empty.
  The plots were never the problem the user named.
- They do not add words. Scene 1: 29 → 28 words across the two
  units (U1 14→17 — within the two-speaker hard cap of 18 total
  and ≤9 words per turn; U2 15→11). Scene 2: 19 → 20 (at the
  one-speaker hard cap). Contact is not bought with budget; it is
  bought by spending lines on reaction and pressure instead of
  on record.
- They do not make the lines "grittier" or more naturalistic in
  surface. Every rewritten line is still plain, short, and
  performable at the measured rate. What changed is the *address*:
  each line is now aimed at a person who can answer it.

*End of examples.*
