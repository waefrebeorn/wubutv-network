**The one-sentence version:** dialogue is written as people trying
to get something from each other, in lines that lean on the line
before them — and no gate may ever again be passed by a line whose
only job is to be found by the gate.

## 1. The pass order (replaces Pass 3 dialogue construction)

**Story inputs (Passes 1–2 — storyline, breakdown).** Before
breakdown, the episode's story is stated as **THE ONE-SENTENCE
PETTY WANT**: one plain sentence naming a person who wants
something *petty* from another person — money, a name on a
board, revenge for a slight, a seat, a meal, to be believed,
to not be laughed at. Procedures — claims, counts, dockets,
tests — are the world's filing system and may carry the plot's
mechanics; they may never *be* the want: if the one sentence
names a process instead of a desire, the episode is not ready
to break down. The value-change pair must be a **STATUS
CHANGE** — a change in *standing between people*, from the
field's table: Humiliated → Striking back · Suspected →
Trusted · Trusting → Betrayed · Winning → Losing on a new
condition · Unaware → Holding a secret. A record correction
(claim denied → claim granted) is not a status change. Someone
must be able to **lose face** in every episode; name who, at
storyline stage. **THE COLLISION TEST (fold-in — the
thecontentlab01 study, Telegram msg 706, 2026-11-19):** beside
the petty want, the one-sentence premise must read as a
*collision* a stranger can picture — character + situation +
twist ("Pepe the frog builds something inside Tung Tung
sahur's body") — never a plot summary. The study's captions
are premises and ours are loglines; the difference is the
collision. *(Template: `PETTY_WANT` / `STATUS_CHANGE` /
`FACE_AT_RISK` in §2 storyline declarations.)*

**Pass 3.0 — VOICE RULES BEFORE LINES: THE RIZZ CARD.** Before
any dialogue is drafted, each speaking character's cast card
carries a **RIZZ CARD** — six fields stating how the character
*sounds*. This replaces the SPEECH_RULE-as-prose requirement
(retired 2026-11-19 — THE_NEW_STANDARD §4; TASTE_DIAGNOSIS
T-3: tactics are not sound; the v6 cards carried no carrier
word, no catchphrase, no specimens). A rule a reader cannot
*hear* is not a voice rule. The six fields:

- `CARRIER_WORDS` — one to three words or phrases only this
  character says: an address habit, a verbal tic, a pet word.
  A carrier word must appear in the character's lines at least
  once per episode they speak in — it is how the audience's
  ear files them.
- `CATCHPHRASE` — one repeatable line or line-shape that is
  theirs, designed to be quoted: a verdict formula, a sworn
  stamp, a counting ritual, a greeting. It is declared on the
  card *before* the episode, and the episode must give it one
  clean instance. A catchphrase arriving after the event that
  earns it is a signature, not a recitation (the signature
  exemption, §3).
- `BRAG` — what this character thinks is cool about
  themselves, statable in one line in their own voice.
  Charisma is a character's pride made audible; a character
  with no brag has no rizz to write.
- `AUDIBLE_TELL` — how they sound when they lie, boast,
  panic, or lose: a behaviour of the *voice*, not of the plot
  ("over-explains when lying — volunteers witnesses nobody
  asked for"; "goes quiet and counts when she's sure"). The
  tell must be audible in a transcript with the names removed.
- `SPECIMEN_LINES` — three lines in the character's voice,
  written at card stage, **mandatory and grep-checked**: a
  card without them is a defect that blocks the room (B-5's
  specimens existed and were left empty on the v6 cards).
  Specimens are the harvest's reference grain: a harvested
  line that matches neither the tell nor the specimens is
  re-bantered in the speaker's mouth, not polished in place.
- `REGISTER_DEVIATION` — the show declares a vernacular (the
  REGISTER, THE_NEW_STANDARD §3); the card states how this
  character leans on it or against it (the invader glossier
  than the street; the clerk drier than everyone). Two
  designed speakers must differ in at least three of the six
  fields, audibly.

The card also keeps the habitual **length trait** (A-9) — how
long this character's lines run ("answers in one-word bursts
when cornered"; "runs long and volunteers dates when lying") —
because line length is itself a distinguishing character
trait, and the two designed speakers' length traits must
differ. A character without a RIZZ CARD does not speak in the
episode.

**THE SHOW-OFF.** Every episode cast designates one
**SHOW-OFF** — the character licensed to play to the room:
brag, strut, address the audience's delight. Default: the
invader (they arrived to be looked at; that is the occupation
in miniature). The show-off's lines may carry the target class
DELIGHT (Pass 3.2) without rationing; every other character
gets the one licensed grievance beat the old method allowed.
The show-off's license is a *mode*, not a quota.
*(Template: the RIZZ CARD field block on the §1 cast card —
`CARRIER_WORDS` / `CATCHPHRASE` / `BRAG` / `AUDIBLE_TELL` /
`SPECIMEN_LINES` / `REGISTER_DEVIATION`, `length_trait` kept —
plus the episode-level `SHOW_OFF` field in §1.)*

**Pass 3.1 — BANTER FIRST (F-27's mine-the-banter, made a step, not
a slogan).** The scene's exchange is drafted loose, as talk:
fragments, interruptions, wrong answers, overlaps, and reactions
are not merely allowed, they are the draft's job. The banter draft
is **one continuous two-voice transcript** of the scene played in
order — interruptions, wrong answers, and overlaps in place — not
a pool of candidate lines from which the script is assembled;
contact cannot be retrofitted onto pooled lines, so a line pool is
the board by another name. The banter draft
is kept in the episode's work dir. Lines are then *harvested* into
the locked script; a locked line that exists in no banter draft
and was written directly to the board is presumed a ledger line
until defended. The harvest itself is governed by Pass 3.5 (**THE KEEP-LIST
HARVEST**): the draft's flavor carriers are marked on a KEEP
LIST before any compression, keep-list lines are harvested
first and may not be cut for length, and compression falls on
connective and procedural lines — never on the tell. (The B-8
ratio that stood here — draft double, cut half — is **retired**
as the harvest law, 2026-11-19: the ratio was executed against
the flavor carriers, which are surplus by definition —
TASTE_DIAGNOSIS T-1.) The move ledger (Pass 3.2) still records
the drafted count against the kept count, and the harvest
record names what compression removed (`KEEP_LIST` /
`cut_record`, template §4.4); a harvest that cut nothing still
says why in the GATE_LOG (a two-line unit is a defence; silence
is not). *(Template: `BANTER_DRAFT`
field, §2 sign-off.)*

**Play the show-off hot.** In the banter draft, the episode's
SHOW-OFF (Pass 3.0) is played at full strut — brags, name-spam,
witness-invoking, playing to the room. The draft is the one
place excess is free; a banter draft with no line the room
would be embarrassed to lock is under-played, and says why in
the GATE_LOG. **The liar's tell is played, not implied.**
Whoever lies in the scene lies *in their tell* during the
draft (over-explanation, too-specific detail, volunteered
witnesses — the `AUDIBLE_TELL` on their RIZZ CARD), so the
harvest has the tell on the page to protect (Pass 3.5).

**Banter input — the proof object.** Before the scene is played,
each unit's driving claim is assigned a named physical proof
object (the tin, the letter, the count board). In the banter, an
accusation must touch its object: conflict is shown, then named —
never reported secondhand. A claim that exists only as one
character reporting another's off-screen act is a ledger-line
risk and is re-bantered around the object. (Pass 3.4 carries the
other half of this rule: the first spend of a driving fact is the
object beat.) *(Template: per-unit `PROOF_OBJECT` field, §4.)*

**Scene devices beside the proof object (Z-14, from the
China tactics study, 2026-11-18).** Two named constructions
a room may reach for at banter input, as drafting devices,
not gates: the EXTREME DEMO — a claim stress-tested on
screen, with a visible score (the bell test is this
episode-form's native example); and COMPARISON — the same
scene played twice with two behaviours, where the contrast
is the argument (a natural staging for home-vs-invader in
the occupation lore). A device is chosen at input, like the
proof object; it is never retrofitted at gate stage.

**Banter input — the withheld line.** Banter input for a unit is
the proof object, the want, and the **withheld line**: the
sentence each character will not say in this unit, which their
lines are written around. Naturalness is briefed as intention
plus constraint, never requested as a quality — information
without intention is robotic dialogue's root cause. A unit where
nobody is avoiding anything is re-bantered: flat exchanges are
diagnosed at input, not at the Stranger Test. *(Template:
per-unit `WITHHELD` field, §4.3.)*

**Pass 3.2 — THE MOVE LEDGER.** Every harvested line is labelled
with (a) its MOVE — accuse / deny / dodge / plead / offer / refuse /
demand / expose / decide / react — and (b) its TARGET — the person
in the scene it is aimed at. A line may also carry the target class **DELIGHT** — a line
aimed at the audience's delight (brag, strut, catchphrase
instance, steal-the-insult). DELIGHT lines are legal for the
episode's SHOW-OFF (Pass 3.0) without ration, and for any
other character as the episode's one licensed
grievance/confession beat (F-21-adjacent; logged). The old
rule that stood here — *a line whose target is "the room",
"the record", or the audience is cut* — is **retired**
(2026-11-19; TASTE_DIAGNOSIS T-4: it banned charisma by
construction). Playing to the room is not the LEDGER LINE
defect (§2): a ledger line's addressee is the *gate*; a
DELIGHT line's addressee is the audience, inside the scene.
Two consecutive lines by the same speaker carrying the same move
and no new fact are a repeated beat: cut or escalate (McKee).
*(Template: `line_move` + `line_target` per SPEECH BLOCK entry.)*

**Genre move (the cross-check).** Each harvested line also
carries a GENRE MOVE tag — which of the field's four line-types
it performs for the audience: ACCUSE-WITH-PROOF / DENY /
ENTER-CONTRADICT / REVEAL-BY-AUTHORITY — or NONE. The ten moves
above describe a line's intent; the four describe the genre
function the audience receives; the two vocabularies are a
cross-check, not rival taxonomies. Each harvested line also carries a **FLAVOR TAG**
(`line_flavor_tag`) — CATCHPHRASE / TELL / BRAG / LADDER /
INSULT / CARRIER / NONE — naming the flavor carrier the line
performs, if any. A line is presumed an orphan only if its
genre move AND its flavor tag are both NONE, undefended (the
A-1 cross-check, widened 2026-11-19: the quotable beat is
almost always a repetition, a one-word answer, or a verdict
line — shapes the four genre moves do not name). An episode
whose harvested lines carry no CATCHPHRASE, TELL, or BRAG tag
at all is flavor-empty by construction and returns to banter
before any gate runs. The tags stay out
of the test's own input (annotations remain stripped); they are
checked in the GATE_LOG afterward, against its Q2 scores. The
episode's one licensed grievance/confession beat is the standing
defence. *(Template: `line_genre_move` + `line_flavor_tag` per SPEECH
BLOCK entry, beside `line_move` / `line_target`.)*

**Reactions are lines too.** A scripted silence — gasp, pause,
silent close-up — is written into the locked script as a REACTION
entry with a named carrier (face / object / room), carrying zero
words and the `react` move. A reaction is exempt from the
repeated-beat rule above when its carrier changes. The episode's
three standing digestion gaps are written as REACTION entries, so
the gap exists in the script the gates read, not only in the
assembly plan. *(Template: `REACTION` entry type in the §4.4
SPEECH BLOCK.)*

**Pass 3.3 — WANT CARRIAGE (replaces want announcement).** A want
enters dialogue only as a move: an offer, a demand, a refusal, a
complaint, a bargain — something the other character can answer,
refuse, or use. The grammatical form "I want X" / "that's all I
want" is **banned** from dialogue, with one named exception: a plea
where the nakedness is itself the move (a character begging,
stripped of tactics) — logged as an exception in the brief, at most
one per episode. Legibility of wants is achieved the genre's way:
one want per lead that does not change; the obstacle changing
shape in front of it; and the verdict restatement (§1.4) entering
it into the record *after* it has bitten. The gate question is no
longer "is the want stated by U2?" but "can a stranger recover the
want from the character's first two moves?" — tested by the
Stranger Test (§3), Q1.

**Pass 3.4 — FACT SPEND.** Each core fact of the episode is spent
exactly twice in dialogue: once as a move inside the action
(accusation, offer, entrance), and once as a **verdict
restatement** — a different voice, different words, after the fact
has landed ("Valentina is your sister" shape). Wording is never
repeated verbatim between the two spends, with one licensed
exception: a single key word may repeat, escalated ("His
FAVOURITE."). Recap at an episode head is a complaint or an
accusation, never narration. Bookend restatement of a U1 line at
U6 is not a spend — it is banned (§2).

Where a fact drives a unit, its first spend is the **object
beat**: the unit's proof object (Pass 3.1) shown in the scene and
named in the dialogue. A driving fact is never first spent as a
report of something off-screen.

The episode's **audience secret** — the one secret the audience
holds that the characters don't (or the reverse), planted no
later than U1's close — is a fact whose first spend is to the
audience: shown, not said. *(Template: `AUDIENCE_SECRET` field,
§2 storyline declarations.)*

Fact spend is checked against the series **RUNNING_BRIEF** — the
living per-series file of who knows which secret, who is lying to
whom, what the last hanger promised, and each character's
current stake number — so a fact cannot be spent a third time by
accident. The continuity editor owns the brief and updates it at
every script lock; a room session that opens without reading it
is out of order.

**The verdict line must pass the one-sentence moral test.** The
verdict restatement must be speakable by a character in the scene
as the episode's moral in one plain sentence — what changed, said
as a judgment on the scene's people — without naming the
episode's premise or restating U1. A verdict that merely swaps
U1's outcome into U1's words fails by construction (a bookend
restatement wearing a verdict's clothes); a verdict that needs
the episode around it to parse is rewritten as a judgment in the
verdict-speaker's voice. The test surfaces at Stranger Test Q4: a
gossip retell that cannot produce the moral has found a failed
verdict.

**HANGERS.** A hanger may roll over only by paying interest on
it in the same episode, and the next episode's first beat pays
the previous hanger at least partly within its first ~5 seconds,
before opening a larger debt. At lock, the showrunner reads the
previous episode's hanger from the running brief and writes the
payment beat before U1's dialogue is drafted; an episode whose
U1 contains no payment beat and no interest payment does not
lock. The hanger must also pass the **swap test** at lock: if it
could be pasted onto another episode without rewriting this
episode's middle, it is decoration and fails — the last beat is
rewritten before U6's dialogue is drafted, not after the master
is gated; the GATE_LOG's hanger entry records the test and the
middle beat a passing hanger hangs from. *(Template:
`HANGER_PAID` sign-off, §2.)*

**The QUOTABLE is declared at fact-spend time.** The episode's
**QUOTABLE** — the line designed to be repeated (usually the
catchphrase instance, the verdict with its twist word, or a
ladder rung) — is declared here, at fact spend, beside the
audience secret. And the hanger should, where the story
allows, hang from a spoken line a character has already made
repeatable — the proof rewrite's "ask the street" pattern
(`research_notes/writing-rework-20261119/PROOF_REWRITE.md`):
the Peach's volunteered witness returns at the close as the
court that decides her name, so her own flavor becomes her
consequence. *(Template: `QUOTABLE` + `STANDALONE_BEAT`
declarations in §2, beside `AUDIENCE_SECRET`.)*

**Pass 3.5 — THE KEEP-LIST HARVEST (replaces the plainness
pass as the harvest law, 2026-11-19).** "Draft double, cut
half" is **retired** as a global ratio (TASTE_DIAGNOSIS T-1:
the ratio was executed against the flavor). The harvest runs:

1. **THE KEEP LIST.** Before any compression, the room marks
   the banter draft's flavor carriers: catchphrase candidates,
   the tell in action, the brag, the insult, carrier-word
   lines, the ladder's rungs. Keep-list lines are harvested
   FIRST and may not be cut for length.
2. **Compression targets plot, not voice.** If the unit is
   over budget, compression falls on connective and procedural
   lines. A liar's over-explanation may be trimmed only to the
   shortest form in which the tell is still audible — the
   **tell-protection floor**: surplus witnesses ≥1, surplus
   detail ≥1. A claim stripped to its fact has been harvested
   wrong.
3. **If it still doesn't fit, re-play the bit shorter**
   (Pass 3.7) — the tell is never what gets trimmed.

Payload-last, names rationed, one idea per line (Pass 3.6)
stand — they shape lines; they do not select them. The
plainness discipline survives as the floor check's repair
rule (§3): meaning failures only, contractions never
"repaired" into expanded forms, and the whole repair capped
at one round. *(Template: `KEEP_LIST` + `cut_record` harvest
record fields, §4.4.)*

**Pass 3.6 — LINE SHAPE (drafting rules, explicitly not a
gate).** Two rules apply at harvest, once, while lines are
lifted from the banter transcript: (a) **payload word last** — a
line ends on its hard word, where the next line can grab it;
(b) **names rationed** — in two-character scenes a character's
name is spent only for emphasis, accusation, or entrance. Line
length follows the same harvest discipline: one idea per line,
usually ≤12 words, payload last, with short and one-word lines
normal and licensed. The F-26 soap band of 8–14 words per line
applies as a **ceiling range, not a target band** (A-13, landed
as a flagged application change to F-26 — see the TASTE dialogue
addendum): a soap scene whose lines all sit at 10–14 words is
over-full and returns to banter for compression toward
move-sized lines. The F-25 unit caps are untouched by this rule.
These are drafting rules only — the plainness pass does **not**
repair toward them (that would be FORMAL REPAIR, §2); a line is
shaped at harvest or not at all.

**Pass 3.7 — THE BIT + THE QUOTABLE.** Each unit declares its
**BIT**: the repeatable comic/dramatic mechanism the unit
runs, with a setup and a payoff *inside the scene*. The
standing menu (rooms may add, with specimens): **THE COUNT** —
accusation or verdict delivered as arithmetic the victim must
watch being done (counting the tin, the coins, the bells) ·
**THE SWORN STACK** — a liar escalates a claim in sworn layers
(claim → detail → volunteered witnesses), each layer more
convictable than the last · **THE TOO-SPECIFIC DENIAL** — the
denial with more detail than the truth would need; the
audience hears the lie first · **THE ENTRANCE RE-PRICE** — a
third party enters with a line that re-prices everything just
said · **THE LADDER** — a repeated call or word escalating in
scope ("Mama." "Help." "Somebody." "Anybody."), each rung a
beat · **THE KEY WORD LOUDER** — the licensed repetition: the
core word said again, escalated, never rephrased ("His
FAVOURITE.") · **STEAL THE INSULT** — the strongest attack on
the character or the show, placed in a mouth inside the scene,
as a joke · **THE CONFESSION** — the grievance delivered to
camera, in full sentences, restating the core fact as a
complaint · **THE VERSUS** — two claims stated as a
call-and-response the audience can score (numbers, counts,
ranks). A unit with no bit is a procedure — and procedures
are pictured, not spoken: if a unit's work is purely
procedural, its dialogue budget moves to the units that carry
bits. Per-episode, the **QUOTABLE** (declared at Pass 3.4)
and the **STANDALONE BEAT** — the frame + line that could be
posted alone as a reaction (the torn-line test) — are
declared in the brief. *(Template: per-unit `BIT` field, §4.2
beside `PROOF_OBJECT`; `QUOTABLE` + `STANDALONE_BEAT`, §2.)*

## 2. Banned constructions (failure classes — gate logs cite these names)

| Class | Definition | E3 specimen it names |
|---|---|---|
| LEDGER LINE | A line whose addressee is the record/gate, not a person in the scene | "Ansel says the Courier held his tin. His coins are gone." |
| WANT ANNOUNCEMENT | A want declared in "I want X" form instead of used as a move (plea exception excepted) | "I want the book closed even by noon." |
| LAW RECITATION | A world-rule lectured before the event that teaches it; includes describing, in dialogue, a picture the viewer is already seeing | "At the bell, what's held stays held. What's loose changes. The Courier's hands are open." |
| BOOKEND RESTATEMENT | A closing line that restates an opening line with the outcome swapped in, existing to close a pair | "I wrote one true line today…" (for U1's "I write one true line today, or none.") |
| APHORISM PAIR | Two self-contained proverbs in sequence, answering no question | "We count what's brought." / "Receipts say what was paid, not what came." |
| ORPHAN LINE | A line that rules on nothing before it — answers, accepts, refuses, or contradicts no prior line | any line that survives the shuffle test |
| SYMMETRIC FILING | An exchange of matching-form statements (want for want, claim for claim) with no contact between them | ONE SHORT U1's demand/want-statement pair |
| FORMAL REPAIR | A gate repair that expands a contraction, names a subject the scene already supplies, or otherwise trades spokenness for parseability | "Thirty's the count" → "Thirty is the count" |

**Taste failure classes (2026-11-19 — gate logs cite these
names beside the classes above):** HARVEST CUT — a flavor
carrier (a keep-list line, the tell, a catchphrase instance,
a brag) removed in compression · SIGNATURE EXECUTION — a
declared catchphrase or maxim removed, or flagged as stage,
despite arriving after its earning event · VOICEPRINT-FREE
LINE — a line any cast member could say (it fails the Poptoy
swap: change the speaker and nothing breaks) · PROCEDURE AS
STORY — the episode's want is a process, not a petty desire ·
UNREPEATABLE EPISODE — no quotable, no ladder, no repetition
device anywhere in the episode (the episode-level class).
Definitions and evidence: `TASTE_DIAGNOSIS.md` (T-0…T-7).

The **shuffle test** remains the standing diagnostic (addendum §3):
if a unit's lines can be reordered without loss, the unit contains
orphans and fails — but it is now a *diagnostic inside the Stranger
Test record*, not a construction target; lines are not written
*to* the shuffle test any more than to any other gate. They are
written to the banter, and the tests merely listen.

## 3. THE FLOOR CHECK (demoted 2026-11-19) — THE STRANGER TEST

A dialogue-specific cold test. It exists because every current gate
asks comprehension questions (Gate E), parse questions
(hear-before-it-moves), or delivery questions (ear test, Gate (f)) —
and none asks the only question the user asks: *would a person say
that line, to that person, there?*

**Demoted 2026-11-19 (msg 704).** This test is a *plausibility
floor*: it certifies that lines are sayable by a person, to a
person, there — never that they are good. Its Q3 mechanic
cannot distinguish a dead maxim from a live signature and
executed both (TASTE_DIAGNOSIS T-2), and three repair rounds
under it produced a monotone flavor-loss curve (T-0). It may
no longer be the gate a script is said to have "passed." The
headline gate is **THE QUOTE-BACK TEST** (§3A). The protocol
below stands as the floor check, with two amendments: **the
signature exemption** and **the repair cap**.

**Placement.** Twice: (a) at script stage, after Pass 3.5, **before
script lock** — a script may not lock without a PASS record;
(b) at master stage, on the finished master's own transcript,
beside Gate (f) — Gate (f) keeps comprehension; the Stranger Test
owns plausibility. A master-stage FAIL returns the episode to the
room as a story-gate failure (repaired, never disclosure-shipped).

**Inputs.** The dialogue ONLY — colon form, in order, with speaker
names. **No** picture summaries, premise, annotations, bible, or
gate paperwork. Annotations are stripped before the test: the E3
scripts' annotations would have told the reader what each line was
*for*, and that priming is part of the old blindness. The reader
did not write the dialogue and has not read the episode's brief.

**Procedure.** The reader reads the dialogue aloud once, in order,
flat. Then answers, in writing:

- **Q1 (per designed speaker):** "What does this person want?
  Answer from what they *do* with words. If the only evidence is a
  line where they announce it, write ANNOUNCED." (Quote the line
  relied on.)
- **Q2 (per exchange):** "What did the second line do to the
  first?" — one of: ANSWERED / REFUSED / DODGED / TOPPED /
  MISHEARD / **FILED ITS OWN STATEMENT**.
- **Q3 (per line — the would-a-person test):** mark any line
  **STAGE** if no person in that situation would say that sentence
  to that person. Each STAGE mark carries a reason of ≤6 words
  ("she's telling him his own news"; "nobody recites the rulebook").
- **Q4 (the gossip retell):** "Retell this scene the way you'd tell
  a friend, in two sentences." The retell is checked against the
  locked story: any fact the retell *needs* that is not in the
  dialogue must be listed (undramatized exposition); any fact the
  retell gets *wrong* is a legibility failure in the dialogue, not
  in the reader.

**FAIL conditions (any one fails the gate):**
1. ≥2 STAGE lines in the episode.
2. Any exchange scored FILED ITS OWN STATEMENT on both lines.
3. Q1 answered ANNOUNCED for any designed speaker (outside a
   logged plea exception).
4. The Q4 retell contradicts the locked story on any fact, or
   needs ≥2 facts that exist nowhere in the dialogue.

**Q2 under Camp B — the unit of analysis (clarification,
2026-11-18, parent ruling on the HOLD ON E3 v6 readings).**
Q2 was written against two-voice banter transcripts. Under
Camp B (one speaker per unit) and in proceeding-shaped
scenes, exchanges also live at three other joints, and the
reader scores all of them: (i) ACROSS UNIT JOINTS — the
next unit's first line against the previous unit's last
line; (ii) WITHIN A SPEAKER'S RUN — a run is one move
advancing (bid -> claim, finding -> attribution -> cost);
a line that starts an unrelated topic mid-run is FILED;
(iii) CHAIR-TURNS AND INTERRUPTIONS — a turn to a new
addressee that the previous line's result causally calls
for is the answer's consequence, not a filing; an
interruption that seizes the floor is a move (contact),
not a filing. A line that answers a PICTURE beat (a reach,
a spill, a gesture) is answered-in-picture: the script-
stage test cannot see it, it is noted as such, and the
master-stage test — where pictures exist — is its final
judge. FAIL condition 2 is unchanged in substance: a line
with no verbal caller, no picture caller and no causal
call is doubly orphaned and fails. The clarification
names where exchanges live under standing form law; it
does not lower the bar — the HOLD ON v6 repairs passed
under it because the repairs added the causal calls the
early drafts lacked. Q3's "in that situation" likewise
includes the speaker's trade and the act in front of them:
a rule's substance spoken as a taunt at the act it names
is in use, not recitation; the maxim form (the rule stated
as a rule) remains a STAGE line.

**On FAIL:** the record names the failure class(es) from §2, and
the repair is at Pass 3.1 (re-banter the exchange) — never
line-polish toward the test. The reader's Q3 reasons are quoted in
the GATE_LOG verbatim.

**THE SIGNATURE EXEMPTION (2026-11-19).** A line that is a
character's declared CATCHPHRASE or carrier construction,
arriving after the event that earns it, is marked
**SIGNATURE**, not STAGE — the reader notes it and moves on.
A maxim that states a world-rule *before* the event that
teaches it remains STAGE (LAW RECITATION, §2, stands).

**THE REPAIR CAP (2026-11-19).** The floor check allows **one
repair round** at script stage. A repair may not remove a
keep-list line, a catchphrase instance, or a tell without a
written showrunner ruling in the GATE_LOG naming what the
removal buys. Flags beyond the one round are adjudicated by
the showrunner in writing (the parent's R3 rulings on HOLD ON
E3 v6 are the model), not by another re-banter: iterated
cold-reader repair is the one-way gradient itself (T-0), and
each round must justify what it *keeps*, not only what it
removes.

**The floor check's repair rule (the plainness discipline,
moved from Pass 3.5, 2026-11-19).** Repairs under this check
mark **meaning failures only**: a line where the listener
cannot say what was meant or who it was meant for. Parse
texture is not a failure: contractions are the default
surface and may never be "repaired" into expanded forms;
fragments are licensed wherever they complete, answer, or
react; a line's grammar may lean on the previous line (that
dependence is the twine, and it is the point). A meaning
failure returns the *exchange* to Pass 3.1 (banter), not the
line to polish. Hear-before-it-moves (F-26) survives with
this definition of "stumble" substituted; its old repair
gradient is revoked — and its repairs now run under the cap
above.

## 3A. THE HEADLINE GATE — THE QUOTE-BACK TEST (2026-11-19)

Run twice: on the locked script before production, and on the
finished master's transcript beside Gate (f). The reader did
not write the dialogue, has not read the brief, and gets the
dialogue only, in order, read aloud once. Then, **without
looking back**, the reader must produce three things:

1. **QUOTE BACK.** One line, verbatim or near-verbatim, from
   memory. *If no line can be quoted back, the episode FAILS*
   — nothing in it can travel, be repeated, or be memed;
   there is nothing for a viewer to carry out of the room.
2. **NAME THE SHOW-OFF.** Which character would you follow
   into another episode — and what is their verbal signature
   (catchphrase, carrier word, or tell, named in the reader's
   own words)? *If the reader cannot name a character and a
   signature, the episode FAILS* — it has no charisma carrier.
3. **GOSSIP WITH AN ATTITUDE.** Retell the scene as gossip in
   two sentences, the way you'd tell a friend — including the
   judgment you'd actually pass on the people in it (petty,
   shameless, obsessed, due). *If the material gives the
   retell no attitude to take — if it comes out as a neutral
   summary — the episode FAILS* — the story was a procedure,
   not a piece of gossip, and gossip is the genre's native
   transmission form.

**PASS requires all three.** A FAIL is repaired at the level
that failed — quote-back: the episode's QUOTABLE is (re)built
(Pass 3.7 bits; a catchphrase instance or ladder that lands);
show-off: the cast's RIZZ CARDs and the show-off's license,
not the lines; attitude: the story input (the petty want, the
status change, someone who can lose face). A taste FAIL is
never repaired by polishing the lines that were read; flavor
is a construction property, and construction is where it is
fixed. *(Template: `TASTE_TEST` block in §2 at script stage;
`TASTE TEST (MASTER)` block in §5.4.)*

## 4. What does NOT change

The items listed here are **FLOOR CHECKS** as of 2026-11-19:
they certify correctness — **no floor check may be cited as
evidence of quality.**

F-25 spoken-load caps and the Container Law (they are arithmetic,
not style); F-13 ear test and F-9 (delivery and story gates — they
keep their jobs; they were never the dialogue gate); Gate E's
transcript+pictures discipline for *story* sense; F-14 two-speaker
design (Camp B generation stands — but exchanges are now *written*

**Vehicle-layer notes (online-research wave, 2026-11-18 — these
govern the prompt, never the script):**

**Delivery tags (B-1).** The colon form governs the *script*; at
vehicle build, each spoken line carries exactly one short
delivery tag (1–5 words) attached in the model's own syntax —
Kling's tone parenthesis, Seedance's delivery phrase before the
braces, a bracket tag where the route reads one — copied from the
script's delivery intent: the line's DELIVERY_NOTE, or the
character's SPEECH_RULE default where the script carries none,
with the absence noted in the GATE_LOG. Tags are never invented
at prompt time and never free-styled per take. *(Template:
`line_delivery_tag` per line, §4.4.)*

**The listener's mouth (B-2).** Camp B generates one speaker per
clip; this governs the *other* face in that clip. Every vehicle
framing two characters names the non-speaker's state explicitly —
"listens silently, mouth closed", plus one micro-action (blink,
exhale, eyes tighten) — for every beat in which they do not
speak; an unnamed listener mouths the speaker's line. *(Template:
`LISTENER_STATE` per unit, §4.2 — the Sequence Gate checks it the
way it checks END STATE.)*

**Name hygiene (B-4).** A character's name appears inside a
generation prompt only inside the spoken line's wrapper or as a
bound reference label (@Image / @Name element) — never as free
prose, where models render names as on-screen text or speak them.
A prompt lint joins the vehicle checks: grep the vehicle for cast
names outside the wrapper and the reference labels; a hit is
fixed before firing — the same class as the prompt-leak scrub.
*(Template: §0 MODEL_SYNTAX notes.)*

**One-take performance on the TTS route (B-6).** Camp B governs
video clips; this governs audio takes. When VOICE_ROUTE is
TTS-first, the unit's (or scene's) lines are performed as one
continuous multi-voice take and cut afterward — never generated
line-by-line and concatenated: line-by-line reads leave every
reply landing with the same gap, a shout answered calmly, nobody
in the scene listening to anybody. The ear-test record notes
whether the exchange was performed whole or assembled; an
assembled exchange that fails the ear-test is re-performed whole
before any line is rewritten. *(Template: §0 VOICE_ROUTE
performance-granularity clause.)*

**Ceiling vs budget (B-12 — FLAGGED, ratified as a budgeting
rule; the F-25 caps above are unchanged).** The F-25 caps are
ceilings, not budgets. A unit carrying a REACTION entry or a
second voice is *budgeted* at ~13–15 words, because tested field
budgets for generated speech sit at 60–75% of the ceiling
whenever the clip must also carry reaction and ambience; the
ceiling is headroom for the plainness pass only, and a loaded
unit written to the ceiling must be defended in the brief like a
NONE genre move. Ratified on single-tester-plus-guide evidence
(Ambience AI's Seedance tests; Dreamdrive's Veo budgets) — an
application distinction, not a cap change. *(Template: §4.1
ceiling-vs-budget note.)*
