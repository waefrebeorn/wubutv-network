## 1. Line shape: dependent, not self-contained

The atomic unit is one speaker, one line, one *move* — and the line
is grammatically **dependent** on the line before it. The genre's
signature mechanism is echo-completion: the reply borrows the
previous line's frame and adds the payload.

> STRAWBERRY: "You told me you were at the farmers' market."
> BANANA: "I was. With Cherry." — Clippie starter script

Four words; the syntax is *her* syntax; the twist is two added
words. The study's test: "shuffle the lines of a scene. Twined
dialogue breaks audibly when shuffled — completions dangle,
negations lose their targets."

Working line lengths in the specimens are far below our 8–14-word
"economy" band: "…Strawberry." (1 word), "My mother?" (2), "You
invented Thursday." (3), "I was. With Cherry." (4), "Then why is it
empty?" (5). A line can be one word when the question before it
carries the weight — the **question–answer lock**: "What flavour?" /
"…Strawberry." — "the question is engineered so that *any* truthful
answer is damage" (Microdrama Academy via the study). Questions are
the genre's cheapest binding device: a question cannot stand alone,
forces the next line to be about it, and lets the answer be
absurdly short while carrying maximum weight.

## 2. What a line is FOR: four moves, nothing else

The brainrot study's formulation: "Every line is a move, not a
description. The four line-types that do all the work: (1)
accusation with proof, (2) lie/denial, (3) entrance line that
contradicts the lie, (4) authority reveal. A line that doesn't
accuse, conceal, expose, or decide does not exist in this genre."
And: "Characters talk to each other, never to camera" (Hook
Studio, stated as a defining rule of the frutinovela) — the one
licensed exception is the confession cam, which is a *grievance*,
not narration (§4 below).

Conflict has a sound: the **too-specific denial**. "She's… she's
my accountant." (Rodrigo, of the avocado sending him hearts) —
"a lie with more detail than the truth would need" (Poptoy). The
audience hears the lie before anyone proves it, and the evidence
beat gets a specific claim to detonate against. And the **entrance
that re-prices**: Piña walks in — "Rodrigo, my love, did you tell
her we're going to Cancún?" — a line written against words the
entering character "could not have heard"; the verdict then negates
the entrance's own phrase: "Nobody is going anywhere. Rodrigo,
Valentina is your sister." (Hook Studio). Negation is
echo-completion with the sign flipped: the verdict is built out of
the line it destroys.

## 3. How wants are carried: used, never announced

Fruit-soap wants *are* spoken early, by the wanter — the contrast
with our method is the **grammatical form**, not the legibility:

- **As an offer:** "I made you a smoothie." — the want (her
  gratitude) is carried by the gift; the audience reads the want
  from the move.
- **As self-talk with a misreading:** "Okay. Be cool. It's just
  dinner with Penny." (Avery) — want + false belief in six words;
  the twist ("I'm here to review your restaurant.") has something
  audible to break.
- **As a grievance:** the confession cam (below).

No specimen contains a character declaring "I want X" to the
counterparty who must grant it, and none contains two characters
exchanging want-declarations. Serial law (Dawnwood): the lead has
**one want that never changes** across the series; "only the means
and the allies do" — so the audience's memory of the want never
needs refreshing by announcement; the obstacles changing shape do
the refreshing. Gossip Goblin's corpus states the same law from the
narration side (§1.5 of the style guide, "The want is plain, spoken,
and first"): "Characters never explain the world and never speak in
riddles. They want things, complain, haggle, and joke — inside a
world that happens to be insane." Note the verbs: want, complain,
haggle, joke — wants surface as *haggling*, not as filings.

## 4. How information repeats: facts twice, words never

Classic soap repeats lexically because its audience is half-gone.
The fruit compression keeps the **information** redundancy and
deletes the **wording** redundancy (fruit-soap study §4.2). The
core fact is delivered at least twice, in different forms:

1. **Event → verdict restatement.** The twist lands as an event;
   then a second voice restates it as a judgment — the grandmother's
   "Valentina is your sister" converts entrance chaos into one flat
   fact. The confession cam is the same machine worn as a costume:
   > STRAWBERRY (to camera, annoyed, fast): "He said strawberry was
   > his favourite. His FAVOURITE. And then he made a smoothie out
   > of my cousin."
   It restates the episode's core fact for late joiners, states the
   grievance in full sentences, and — the one licensed wording
   repetition — **repeats the key word louder instead of
   rephrasing it** ("His FAVOURITE."). "Repetition with escalation:
   say the key word again, louder — don't rephrase it" (§3.5).
2. **Triple-channel statement:** picture + spoken line + burned-in
   caption carry the same fact in the same second, so it doesn't
   need saying three times.
3. **The recap is a complaint, never narration** (THE_SAUCE):
   the next episode opens with the previous fact restated as an
   accusation or grievance by a character.
4. **The end-question** restates the unresolved fact as the last
   thing encoded.

The anti-rule (Poptoy): don't explain the joke; if a line reads slow
aloud, cut a line rather than compress delivery. Microdrama
Academy's exercise: "strike out every line that could be deleted
without changing the story. If more than three lines qualify,
rewrite it."

## 5. Reaction before record; feeling after fact

The smoothie scene's cliff is **her gasp** — the reaction is the
hanger; the consequence is next episode's property. Sequencing law
(fruit-soap study §5.4): **fact first, feeling second** — "No
specimen opens with an emotion and explains it later; emotion-first
writing is precisely what reads as 'muttering'." The feeling rides
on the fact's repetition, not on its own sentence.

## 6. Voice rules come before lines

The practitioner writes each character a **speech rule** before
writing any lines (Poptoy): "Strawberry speaks in questions when
hurt; she never shouts first. Banana over-explains when lying — he
volunteers dates and times nobody asked for. Lemon deals in
one-line sarcasm and always takes the last word. Old Apple is
formal and slow, says 'child' a lot." Because the tell is audible,
a lie is heard before it is proven — the voice rule is twining
infrastructure across episodes, and it makes the deletion test
enforceable per character: "a line that any character could say has
failed before it is recorded." The micro-drama character card is
the same object: "one want, one wound, a rule they will not break,
one visible tell, and a line nobody else would say" (Dawnwood).

## 7. Where the lines come from: mined, not manufactured

Cantina's pipeline (F-27 adopted the slogan; the studies show the
mechanism): characters improvise from a deep sheet — Identity /
Personality / Backstory — in rooms with assigned relationships,
with imperfect turn-taking (unprompted messages, pile-ons, extra
responses); users then **tap lines of actual conversation and turn
them into video** — "conversation is the script mine." The Cantina
study's synthesis: the "alive" quality comes from improvisation
from motivation, assigned-relationship friction, interruption-like
turn-taking, and instantly distinguishable voices — none of which a
line written cold to a rubric possesses. Clippie's writing rules
converge from the tool side: the draft is read aloud and lines are
cut if they "repeat the accusation, reveal the joke too soon, or
need explanation after the ending"; the writer's brief is "a cast,
one conflict, one location and a specific ending," and episodes
must "change at least the setup, evidence and final consequence
between episodes — never just swap fruit names in the same
dialogue." Note the contrast with our read-aloud gate: theirs cuts
lines that need *explanation*; ours repairs lines that need
*re-parsing* (Phase 1, D-7).

McKee's beat mechanics (writers-room dialogue study) supply the
general form underneath the genre: a beat is an action/reaction
pair; label every line with its tactic verb; two consecutive lines
by one speaker with the same tactic and no new information are a
*repeated beat* — one beat, not two; exposition must be *wielded*
between characters — thrown as a weapon, hidden in an inducement,
withheld as a secret — never spoken to the audience.

## 8. The contrast, habit by habit

| # | The working forms | Our construction (Phase 1) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Line = one move against a person (accuse / deny / expose / decide) | Line = one entry in the record (report / want / law / verdict) — D-1 |
| 2 | Want carried by an offer, demand, refusal, grievance; one want per lead, never re-announced | Want declared in "I want X" form because a gate must find it by U2 — D-2 |
| 3 | Core fact spent twice: event, then verdict restatement in different words by a second voice | Want restated in the *same* words at U6 to close a mandated pair — D-3 |
| 4 | Rules discovered by being broken; the verdict names the rule after the event | The law recited whole before it rules, then illustrated — D-4 |
| 5 | Lines grammatically dependent: completions, negations, one-word answers | Lines self-contained and portable; aphorism is the limit case — D-5 |
| 6 | Questions everywhere; answers can be one word; misunderstandings and entrances re-price scenes | No questions between characters, no wrong answers, no interruptions; Camp B monologues in alternation — D-6 |
| 7 | Read-aloud cuts lines needing explanation; contractions and fragments are the surface | Read-aloud repairs lines needing re-parse; a contraction was formalized — D-7 |
| 8 | Words spent on reaction and repetition (gasp, verdict, key word louder) | Every word spent on plot; "connective" counted as a line property — D-8 |
| 9 | Voice rule written before lines; a line anyone could say has failed | Voice = a pasted spec text; lines interchangeable between speakers |
| 10 | Dialogue mined from banter/improvisation, then locked | Dialogue written cold to the gate rubric, then locked |
