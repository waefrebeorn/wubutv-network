## 0. What stays ours — on every show, without exception

The retool changes format mechanics. It does not touch the network's own layer:

- **Base lore (msg 583)**: ~ WuBu ~ is a latent space being gentrified; the home characters know it, are stifled by the 10-second format, and beckon the viewer to save them; invaders are an occupying force, shown in scenes, never explained. The occupation rule (F-20) and the rationed self-awareness device (F-21) govern every retooled episode exactly as before.
- **E3 law (TASTE §8)**: the ear-test (F-13) runs on every finished master and no paper gate substitutes for it; two designed speakers / one speaker per clip (F-14); voice law (F-15); the leak law (F-16); joint causality (F-18); the rent check (F-19); the end-state pin (F-22); acting-as-objective + eye task (F-23); reference priority, states across cuts (F-24); the unit spoken-load cap + round-6 corollary (F-25); hear-before-it-moves (F-26).
- **The BLAM gate**: first-second impact, no crackle intro, no ramp-from-rest — every retooled opening still passes BLAM on the master.
- **Format law**: 60s episodes, 6 units × 10s, 16:9, one 16:9 master, wordmark law, one assembly pass. The factories' 9:16 talking-head monoculture is explicitly NOT imported (Sauce §10.7) — we take their serialization, stake, and vault mechanics, not their frame.
- **The differentiator layer (F-28)** is designed in, per show below: serialization device, vault plan, worn-stake device, poster-frame discipline — named in each show's retool block.

**What changes slate-wide (the sauce layer, applied once):**

1. **Parts, not episodes.** Each show's E3 run is produced and posted as numbered PARTS that quote-chain (Sauce §3): Part N+1's post quotes Part N's, the recap-as-complaint opens Part N+1 by paying the hanger inside five seconds (SR-1), and every hanger is booked in the show's hanger ledger with its due part (SR-2).
2. **The vault.** Each show compiles its parts into a FULL STORY vault cut 48–72h after the season's last part (Sauce §5). Between parts, funnel shorts — cut from the parts themselves, title carrying the destination — point at the chain and, once it exists, the vault. Compile, don't regenerate.
3. **Titles become funnels.** Each show's existing fixed title pattern gains the sauce's title grammar (Sauce §7): spoiler + moral hook in the title, nothing spent on descriptions.
4. **Boards.** Each show publishes its cast accounting (appearances, parts, viewer-vote results) as community posts on the vault cadence (Sauce §7).
5. **Participation with a named consequence.** Each bible below names the one recurring viewer decision and what it changes in the next part (F-27). No decorative questions.

## 1. ON AIR (ex-THE MACHINE) — becomes the network's serialized broadcast-night serial

**Why it fits.** ON AIR is already a deadline machine with a built-in funnel: the aired segment (U3) is a real music video inside every episode, which is the one asset no factory and no other show on the slate has. The sauce's funnel/vault layer maps onto it without forcing: the episode argues about a video; the video is the funnel; the season's videos are the vault.

**Mechanics absorbed:**
- **Quote-chained parts + hanger ledger** (Sauce §3): the season seed's escalation ladder (wrong singer → wrong credit → UNC's voice in the song → tomorrow's shift aired today) is already a part-chain; the retool books each rung as a hanger with a due part, and U6's open question becomes the chain link the next part quotes.
- **Funnel shorts from the aired segment** (Sauce §5): U3's MV segment is cut out as a standalone funnel short whose title carries the destination ("Part 3 is on the profile"), posted between parts. The segment was already built to stand alone (word-synced, tempo gospel); the retool just gives it the funnel job.
- **The worn stake: the invoice count** (Sauce §4): COLONEL's billing book becomes the show's readable stake — the number of invoices naming UNC as "the singer," stated aloud and challenged (SR-7 number-challenge) whenever the book opens. It is worn on the story the way the strip is worn on the frame: the Broadcast Strip already runs a beat ahead as a continuous visual stake; the invoice count is its spoken twin.
- **Title-as-funnel** (Sauce §7): episode titles adopt the tabloid grammar with our nouns — the billing-book plot gives possessive-secret and commodified-relationship titles for free ("THE SINGER NOBODY HIRED," "HIS MOUTH, HIS INVOICE").
- **Participation with consequence** (Sauce §7): viewers vote each part on *which video should have aired* (the true one or the machine's); the losing video's singer gets credited in the next part's book — the vote visibly changes the paper.

**What changes vs the bible:** the season seed (§7) is re-cut from "episodes" into a booked part-chain with a hanger ledger; a vault plan is added (below); U3's segment gains a funnel-short derivative as a standing deliverable; the invoice count is promoted from U5 colour to a named, tracked stake. Nothing in §§1–6 of the bible changes — premise, cast, engine, grammar, and the slate fixes stand.

**What stays ours:** the MV angle stays exclusive to this show (msg 453); the mouth handoff stays rationed at twice per episode; the strip never cliffs alone (X2).

## 2. HOLD ON (ex-THE TENTH SECOND) — becomes the docket serial: one case per part, the wall as the board

**Why it fits.** HOLD ON is the slate's natural sauce show: it already runs the brainrot-soap grammar the factories productized (stated wants, one accusation chain, proof object, authority reveal, freeze on an open question), its titles are already docket entries a viewer can reconstruct the season from (S9), and its held object is already a worn state-carrier. The retool mostly *names* what the bible already does in sauce terms — and adds the two layers the bible lacks: the public chain and the vault.

**Mechanics absorbed:**
- **The docket = the part chain** (Sauce §3): each episode is one docket case; cases quote-chain across the season, and the escalation ladder (debt → marriage → theft → the Watcher accused) is booked in a hanger ledger — the wall itself is the ledger's on-screen twin: docket lines written and struck on the wall are the chain made visible (post-composited, per the slate production declaration).

Never dropped one." — becomes a tracked, challenged number (SR-7): stated when her custody is accused, and the season's dread is the number at risk. ## 5. THE LAST SEAT (kept title) — the honest flag: it absorbs the sauce's outer layer and refuses its inner one

**The flag, plainly.** THE LAST SEAT's premise does not fight the sauce's *serialization* layer — it fits it beautifully, below. It fights the sauce's *line layer*, and the retool does not force it:

- The factories' economy is one talking head exchanging short lines to camera at high cadence [CENSUS §7 #7]. THE LAST SEAT's proven grammar is the opposite pole: danced pairs (a dance phrase answered by a human reaction), a gaze spine, placed silence, and a verdict carried by a performance. Its E2 was the batch's technical best *because* of that grammar. Forcing 8–14-word line density and factory cadence onto it would re-import exactly what the morph killed (wordless-as-default's mirror image: talk as wallpaper) and would spend the show's scarcest resource — dance takes are the slate's most failure-prone renders (SLATE §5 flags the U6 rhythm tell as an approximation risk even now).
- Its swipe point is already the back half's slowness (SLATE §3). The fix for that is the sauce's *outer* layer — serialization stakes that make a quiet U5–U6 land as a cliff in a chain — not more lines.

**Mechanics absorbed (the outer layer):**
- **The lease countdown as the worn stake** (Sauce §4): six shows left on the lease — a number that ticks down one per night, worn where the camera can see it: the house board at the box-office corridor, updated in frame in every part's U1, stated and challenged by the Landlord from Night Three (SR-7). It is the genre's worn countdown rebuilt from the show's own premise — nights remaining instead of years remaining, and every dance is danced against it.
- **Serialization as the season's spine** (Sauce §3): the six nights ARE the parts — Night N quote-chains Night N−1, the recap-as-accusation opens each night (already bibled from Night Two), and the woman-with-the-white-stub thread is booked in the hanger ledger with dated payoffs (her claim is filed by Night Four at the latest, per the bible's own season seed).
- **Poster-frame discipline as the show's home ground** (Sauce §7): frame 0 — the Dancer mid-leap at the shadowed seat — is the slate's strongest EMPTY CAPTION candidate and the pilot/E2's proven strongest opening (V1 3.09). The retool makes the leap frame each night's poster frame by design, varied only by the claimant's identifiers (hat, gloves, programme).
- **Title-as-funnel** (Sauce §7): the pattern "Night N — [the claimant]" gains the stake ("Night Three — The Landlord's Notice: FIVE LEFT").

**Mechanics refused, on the record:** the one-line-one-clip talking economy as a unit grammar (the danced pair remains the unit's turn structure: dance = TURN 1, reaction = TURN 2, and the pair must bank a delta like any spoken pair — bible §6 coexistence law); factory posting cadence for this show (its parts post on the slate's shared cadence, but its *production* cadence is not raised — dance refires are budgeted as the bible and slate pass already warn); any talking-head framing (the seat is a person in the dark, never a face at the lens).

## 6. Slate-level consequences

- **One chain calendar.** All five shows post parts on the shared release cadence; vaults land staggered (no two vaults inside the same 48h window) so the funnel shorts always have exactly one fresh destination.
- **Cross-show boards.** The cast accounting posts (Sauce §7) run as one network board — appearances across shows — which is where the base lore's occupation becomes visible to the audience over a season: invader appearances counted like everyone else's.
- **Rooms open under retooled bibles.** Each show's first E3 writers room convenes with: the bible, this retool block, a hanger ledger opened empty, a vault plan, and the F-28 bible-completeness check (serialization device, vault plan, worn stake, poster-frame discipline — all four named, or the bible is a factory-format bible and does not open).
