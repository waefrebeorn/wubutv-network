## 0. The sauce in one paragraph

Cast an ensemble once and never re-invent it. Write the episode as single-speaker lines, one line per scene, conflict in the first line, a reveal at two-thirds, and a final line that lands and opens the next episode. Review every scene as a still plus a performed voice line before spending anything on motion, and spend motion only on the reveal and the final beat. Chain the parts in public (each new part visibly attached to the previous one), number them, and compile a week's parts into one long-form cut on a fixed lag. Put one readable stake on a character's body — a number, a count, a state — that everyone in the story can fear and bargain against. Make the poster frame pitch the episode with zero caption. That is the whole recipe; sixteen commercial products and the genre's biggest independent creator all run on it [CENSUS §6; CC7 §5].

## 1. Episode structure — one rope, five beats, one value change

- **The five-beat spine.** Hook (mid-conflict) → Denial → Evidence → Turn → Hanger. Beats may share units; the Denial and the Hanger may never be dropped, and the Evidence must be a thing *shown* — a prop, a photo, a witness, an entrance — never a thing argued [ADD ST-3]. The factories' version of the same spine: line 1 is already the conflict → escalation → a reveal at roughly two-thirds → the final line lands and doubles as the cliffhanger [CENSUS §6 #3; CLIP §4].
- **One value change per episode.** Before any dialogue, the episode is stated as a before/after pair ("in control → exposed"). If the end state equals the start state, the episode does not exist yet [ADD ST-1].
- **One rope.** Every line and beat is a move in a single visible argument — a misjudgment, a debt, a claim, a count. A line not pulling on the rope is cut [ADD ST-2].
- **Frame one is the contract.** The opening frame is a *contrast* — the wrong label on the person, the wrong number on the board — with the conflict already in motion, never the setup being assembled [ADD ST-10]. The factories state it as pacing doctrine: open mid-discovery or mid-slap, never on the setup [CENSUS §2.5, Revid].
- **The audience holds one secret by the end of the first unit** — one visible fact the characters lack (a face, an object, a number). Retention is watching the gap close, not withholding what the scene is about [ADD ST-5].
- **Facts are announced when they bite.** Identities, relationships, and secrets are spoken aloud at the beat where they change the power in the room — never front-loaded as exposition, never carried by visuals alone [ADD ST-8]. The core fact enters the record three times: as event, as verdict restated by a second voice, and carried out as the end-question or the next episode's recap line [ADD ST-9].
- **Escalation is itemized.** Offences escalate in nameable stages, one per unit, listed on the board in advance, so the payoff can settle the account item by item [ADD ST-6]. The turn's sharpest edge is a *relationship inversion* — X was Y's keeper/debtor/driver all along — named in words before any spectacle is designed around it [ADD ST-7].
- **Familiar skeletons only.** The plot type announces itself in the first seconds (the deadline shift, the door test, the bargain with a price, the wish ladder); the episode just executes it. Secrets are sourced from lies, money, jobs, promises, and mistaken identity — never bodies, never lore [ADD §2; bibles' B14].

## 2. Line economy — one line = one scene = one clip

- **The atomic unit is one speaker saying one line.** Each line becomes exactly one scene/clip of ~4–7 seconds. Short lines, one speaker, no multi-speaker pile-ups inside a unit — the form itself enforces the economy [CENSUS §6 #2; CLIP §1, §4].
- **8–14 words per line** in the soap register [CLIP §4; ratified as F-26, TASTE §8]. Lines run ~2.5 words/second, one speaker per turn [ADD B4 family, via bibles].
- **The per-unit ceiling governs.** Our measured law sits on top of the genre's per-line discipline: a 10-second unit carries at most ~30 spoken words and at most 2 speakers per single generation, because generated speech runs ~0.3s/word and does not compress on direction — and a composite of two *spoken* halves is arithmetically unassemblable (each half's speech expands toward the full take length) [TASTE F-25 + round-6 corollary].
- **Dialogue is written in the model's native speech format** — speaker + voice + colon, **no quotation marks**, because quotes leak on-screen text [CENSUS §6 #6, §3 DIY layer]. Acting directions live *inside* the script text as bracketed tags that are performed, not read aloud [CLIP §6.5].
- **Only four line-types plus the want-statement**: accusation-with-proof, lie/denial, entrance-that-contradicts, authority reveal — and every character's first line states their want in words a cold viewer can repeat [bibles' B5/B6, from ADD §1 and the msg-475/508 studies].
- **Read the draft aloud; cut what repeats the accusation, reveals the joke too soon, or needs explanation after the ending** [CLIP §4]. Ratified for us as the script-stage gate HEAR-BEFORE-IT-MOVES: the locked script's lines are read aloud cold, in order, by a voice that did not write them, before any vehicle is written [TASTE F-26].

## 3. Serialization — chain in public, end mid-consequence, pay on schedule

- **Quote-chaining.** When a story runs in parts on a threaded platform, Part N+1 quote-posts Part N. The chain *is* the navigation; no playlist dependency [CC7 §2b, §4.2]. Measured on the genre's hit creator: vertical parts of 79–257s at near-daily cadence, chained in-platform [CC7 §2b].
- **End mid-consequence; open by paying.** The episode ends on a *visible new fact* (an arrival, a changed board, a person in the wrong seat), not a mystery box and not a resolution. The next episode's first beat — inside its first five seconds — answers the previous hanger at least partly, then opens a larger debt. Full payment ends series; partial payment compounds them [ADD SR-1].
- **The hanger ledger.** Every hanger is booked with its due episode at lock time. A hanger with no ledger entry may not be locked; a due hanger unpaid is a continuity defect of the same class as a word-count breach; a hanger may roll over only by paying interest in the same episode [ADD SR-2].
- **Numbered parts, fixed title pattern.** Episode titles follow one rigid pattern so the story-so-far reads off the titles alone [bibles' S9; CC7 §2a title grammar]. The title carries spoiler + moral hook and does *all* the metadata work — the genre winner's archive descriptions are literally empty [CC7 §2a].
- **Persist the situation; move only the power.** The standing situation survives the cut; episodes continue it with the power reversed or tightened, and each episode adds exactly one new element to the known system — never a second system [ADD SR-4].
- **The repeatable situation + rotated mechanism.** A standing premise explains why the same cast keeps meeting; episodes rotate the *mechanism* (misunderstanding / hidden object / audience-knows-first / betrayal / reveal) — never just swap names in the same dialogue, which both the tools' guides and platform inauthentic-content policies punish [CENSUS §6 #9; CLIP §4.9].
- **Recap is a complaint, never narration.** The next episode opens with one line of recap spoken as an accusation or grievance by a character [bibles' B12].

## 4. The worn stake — a readable state on the body that the plot bargains against

- **The signature device of the genre's winner**: a lifespan countdown worn on the body as an LED headband ("5 YEARS / 3 YEARS / 6 MONTHS"); eating shortens the number — *the clock is the plot* [CC7 §2c]. The liftable mechanic, restated as ours: **a character's remaining stake rendered as a number on their body that other characters can read, fear, and bargain against** [CC7 §4.4; banked as THE RIPENING CLOCK].
- **Numbers are status where numbers exist.** The number is worn where the camera can see it; characters state and challenge each other's numbers aloud — arithmetic hierarchy needs no biography and gives dialogue a built-in call-and-response [ADD SR-7, the number-on-head mechanic].
- **Continuity is worn, not recapped.** Each episode scripts one visible state-carrier — a scar, a held object, a number changed on the board — that tells a returning viewer where things stand and orients a cold viewer in the same image [ADD SR-5].
- **Zero product support exists for this device anywhere in the commercial field** — it appears in the census only as raw writing material ("expiry date" as a conflict engine), never as a mechanic. It is unclaimed territory [CENSUS §7 #2; CLIP §3]. Ratified as a standing network advantage to be designed into bibles, not discovered after [TASTE F-28].

## 5. Funnel / vault architecture — monetize the same scenes three times

- **The three-use conveyor** [CC7 §3.4, §4.7]: (1) serialized parts premiere on the fast platform; (2) short funnels whose *title* literally carries the destination ("Full Story link…", "Go to profile for the Full Story") convert scrollers instead of entertaining them; (3) exactly 48–72 hours after the last part, the parts are compiled into one chaptered **FULL STORY vault cut of 30–45 minutes** on the archive platform. The compilation is an assembly job, not a new generation job — *compile, don't regenerate*.
- **Measured shape**: parts 1.5–4.5 min → vault 30–45 min; a FULL STORY lands every 2–3 days [CC7 §2a].
- **No tool in the census does any of this.** Every factory stops at the single episode — no chaining, no funnels, no vault. The entire above-the-episode layer is the field's open gap [CENSUS §7 #1]. Ratified as ours: the FULL STORY vault is named in the show bible at bible stage [TASTE F-28].

## 6. Cadence — the calendar is part of the format

- **Near-daily parts; a vault every 2–3 days.** The genre winner runs ≈4.6 media posts/day on its stage platform and a compilation every 2–3 days, from a premise/title rotation rather than per-story invention [CC7 §1, §3.5].
- **Plan ahead in threes (or a season).** The factories' series methods converge on pre-planning: three episodes ahead, each with a different mechanism [CLIP §3]; the census's only pre-planned season arc pre-writes 3–10 episodes with titles and cliffhangers before the first one is made [CENSUS §2.13, Zyvo]. StoryShort's scheduled auto-posting is the census's only nod to the factory rhythm — nobody else sells the calendar [CENSUS §7 #9]. We already run one (release wheel + nags); the retool wires the shows' parts into it (see RETOOL_PLAN).
- **Short early episodes.** 45–90 seconds recommended for early episodes — "short early episodes help new viewers commit" — with longer forms as a later stage [CLIP §3].

## 7. Engagement hooks — the frame pitches, the viewer acts

- **The EMPTY CAPTION test.** The genre winner's two biggest posts (6.5M+ views) carry *empty* captions — the poster frame is the entire pitch [CC7 §2b]. As a gate: if the frame can't pitch the episode alone, with zero copy, the episode isn't ready [CC7 §4.6; ratified as the poster-frame discipline, TASTE F-28].
- **Title-as-funnel grammar.** One maximally legible title carrying spoiler + moral hook (possessive secret / commodified relationship / transformation boast — as *grammatical slots*, filled with our own lore nouns), and nothing else; no description labour [CC7 §2a, §4.1].
- **The episode's last line is an open question a viewer can type into a comment** [bibles' B11]. Resolved endings fail.
- **Viewer participation with real consequences.** The genre's originator let viewers vote on contestant choices via forms [CANT §5.7]; Cantina's rooms let audiences talk *with* the cast. The mechanic: a recurring viewer decision that visibly changes the next episode — announced in the episode, honored in the next. Participation that changes nothing trains viewers to stop participating [CANT §5.7; ratified F-27].
- **Boards make characters into sport.** Public rankings (interactions, followers, creations, star ratings) convert accumulation into discovery and give the audience a *character's season* to follow, not just a show's [CANT §2.6, §5.6]. Our zero-cost version: publish the same accounting for our own cast — appearance counts, episode counts, viewer-vote results — in captions and community posts.
- **Sound carries emotion at zero comprehension cost**; one repeatable sound-logo (a catchphrase, a bell, a stamp) marks the turn at the exact frame the gap closes, reusable across episodes as lore that costs a new viewer nothing [ADD SR-6].

## 8. The character-consistency stack — the character is the asset

Three independent ecosystems converged on the same stack; it has three legs, and every unit must draw from all three [CANT §5.2; ratified F-27]:

1. **Locked look.** A short fixed look description per character — permanent traits only in the character note; temporary state goes in the scene note ("a red scarf belongs in the character description; a wet scarf belongs in a scene note") [CLIP §2]. Where references aren't locked by the tool, the same descriptive paragraph is repeated verbatim in every prompt, and characters are addressed **by appearance, never by name**, in motion prompts — "the video model doesn't know who 'Strawberry' is" [CENSUS §6 #6, §3].
2. **Locked voice.** One voice per character, kept across every line and every episode; a familiar silhouette with a different voice reads as a new character — voice is treated as identity [CLIP §3]. Cantina's voice spec is a structured text recipe (age/gender, accent, pitch, texture, pace, behavior cue) [CANT §2.2]; ours is the verbatim-pasted structured spec of F-15.
3. **Written persona, rewritten per scene.** Identity / Personality / Backstory as a versioned cast card; dialogue is written from the Personality layer. The persona leg is *rewritten per scene, never pasted* — a pasted persona is a spec the scene has already stopped obeying [CANT §5.1–5.2; TASTE F-27]. When a performance fails, debug the card first: several of our E2 ear failures were card-level problems (undefined voices, undeclared relationships) chased as performance fixes [CANT §5.1].
- **The drift taxonomy** (QC checklist): *identity drift* (check cast/name/reference), *wardrobe drift* (shorten the appearance note to defining colours and shapes), *prop drift* (state the owner and position), *motion drift* (simplify the action; check start and end frames — "motion does not repair an inconsistent starting image") [CLIP §2, §6.4].
- **Base performances as reference.** Prior finished performances of the character are themselves a consistency leg — Cantina uses a character's base videos as the reference substrate for every new video [CANT §2.3]. Ours: last-frame chaining + the anchor stack (Method A6 / F-22 end-state pin).
- **Off-screen flag.** Characters who are discussed but never shown are explicitly flagged as such in the cast data [CLIP §6.4].

## 9. Production discipline — hear/see before it moves; spend motion last

- **HEAR / SEE BEFORE IT MOVES.** Stills + performed voice lines for every scene are generated and *reviewed first*; fixes happen at the cheap layer — swap a speaker, fix a line, redraw a picture — before any animation spend [CENSUS §6 #4; CLIP §2 step 4]. Cantina's version is the same gate from the other side: the anchor still is the scene's *contract* — wardrobe, setting, camera, lighting, color, finish are decided in the still, and the video prompt's remaining job is only action + dialogue. Gate the still, not the render [CANT §5.3; TASTE F-27].
- **Selective animation.** Animation is the expensive layer and is spent only where it pays: the reveal and the final beat fully animated; the rest carried by stills with slow zoom/pan at a fraction of the price (⅓ price, 1/10th the credits, or free, depending on the tool) [CENSUS §6 #5; CLIP §2 step 5].
- **Cost shown before the burn.** Every generation displays its price before it runs; failed generations refund. Metering per second of animation is *why* the two rules above exist [CENSUS §6 #10].
- **One fixed look.** A single house style held constant across the whole series; style is a series-level constant, not a per-episode choice [CENSUS §6 #8].
- **Captions are part of the render.** Styled, word-timed, burned in — generated from the synced speech and **checked against the words actually spoken**, names and puns first. Sound-off viewing is assumed [CENSUS §6 #7; CLIP §6.7].
- **Mine the banter, then script.** Run loose multi-character banter first, harvest the exchanges that actually land, promote them into locked units — the lines that survive being *said* are the ones worth animating [CANT §5.4; TASTE F-27].
- **Caps are a format designer.** Rationing (unlimited cheap lane / hard cap on hero renders) silently shapes the content ecology whether designed or not — so design it: unlimited stills/banter/quick line-deliveries, a hard cap on hero renders, written into the production plan [CANT §5.10].

## 10. What the sauce deliberately does NOT contain (the gap list = our layer)

The census's strategic gap list is the map of what the entire commercial field does *not* do [CENSUS §7] — and it is why a SLERM of the sauce is not a clone of the field:

1. Nothing above the episode (chaining, funnels, vault) — except the one creator who does it by hand [§5 above].
2. No worn-stake device support anywhere [§4 above].
3. No long-form melodrama — factory ceilings run 60s–5min; the audience's proven product is bigger.
4. **No ear-test.** Every tool checks pictures; none checks the finished episode's actual audio against sense. Our ear-test gate (F-13) is a QC layer the entire commercial space lacks.
5. No cross-episode story memory worth the name — the census's best is a 5-episode summary buffer; nobody keeps a structured continuity ledger (who holds what, who knows what, what was promised). Our hanger ledger [ADD SR-2] is ahead of the market.
6. No poster-frame discipline in any tool [§7].
7. Format monoculture: everything in the field is 9:16 talking heads exchanging lines to camera. Our 16:9, montage-first, dialogue-in-the-world canon is structurally differentiated from the entire clone output class [CENSUS §7 #7].

These seven are ratified as standing network advantages, named in each E3 bible at bible stage [TASTE F-28] — and they are the layer the retool keeps while absorbing §§1–9 (see RETOOL_PLAN.md).
