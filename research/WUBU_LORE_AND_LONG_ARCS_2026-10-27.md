of the ~ WuBu ~."

CANON RULE: what actually shipped is canon. Everything below marked
ESTABLISHED comes from shipped pieces and the standing engine. Everything
marked OPEN is a question the long arcs will answer on screen, in scenes —

## PART 1 — THE LORE OF ~ WuBu ~ (as established)

### The station
ESTABLISHED: a failing AI television station. It earns one viewer and one
advertiser. Its engine: a promise is made, the station cannot keep it, and
the failure is funnier than the promise. The station does not close. It
keeps broadcasting. That refusal is the whole show.
OPEN: why does it keep going? Who keeps the lights on when the promise
fails? What does the station want — a viewer, or proof it exists?

### The wordmark — ~ WuBu ~
ESTABLISHED: UNC's hoodie reads exactly `~ WuBu ~` in CADMIUM (yellow/gold).
The tildes are part of the wordmark: `~ WuBu ~` is `~ WuBu ~`. It appears
on every UNC frame, exact, no border, no box, no emblem around it. The
wordmark is the station's signature and its promise: this is WuBu, and
WuBu is still here.
OPEN: who made the wordmark? Why tildes — what do they hold open, what do
they keep out? A wordmark with open ends is a door that never closes.

### UNC — the manager
ESTABLISHED: UNC manages the station. INK + CADMIUM. Short, certain,
managerial sentences. Deadpan. UNC never reacts the way a manager should —
and that is why the station survives him. He keeps the roster, he keeps
the promise, he keeps the show on the air.
OPEN: what did UNC promise, and to whom, before the station started
failing? What is on the roster that he will not erase?

### LAURA — the new hire
ESTABLISHED: LAURA is new. FOREST only. She asks the question everyone
avoids. She is the audience's way in: she notices what the old hands have
stopped seeing.
OPEN: why did she come to a failing station? What did she leave behind
that the roster already knows?

### COLONEL — the anchor
ESTABLISHED: COLONEL anchors. COBALT. He states the official version. The
official version is never the whole story, and he knows it — that is why
he states it so well.
OPEN: what did the Colonel announce that never happened? What broadcast
does he refuse to repeat?

### PETER — sales
ESTABLISHED: PETER sells. VERMILION. He sells, deflects, or counts. He
promises the advertiser, the viewer, the numbers. His promises are the
station's promises, one desk removed.
OPEN: who is the one advertiser, really? What did Peter sell that the
station cannot deliver — and what happens when the buyer comes to collect?

### DISPATCHER — what the building knows
ESTABLISHED: DISPATCHER reports what the building knows. INK + BONE. The
building knows things the staff does not: which rooms are empty, which
machines answer, which names stay.
OPEN: how much does the building know? Is the station itself the one
viewer — watching its own broadcast, counting its own staff?

### SUPER NUMBER HEAD — the count
ESTABLISHED: a numbers-obsessed counting figure. The count is always
underway and never finished. Numbers are the station's other language.
OPEN: what is the count counting toward? What happens when it completes —
or when it refuses to?

### MEGA — the oversized scale
ESTABLISHED: MEGA is the station's oversized transmission/set scale — the
station is bigger than its audience, bigger than its staff, bigger than
its promises. The scale is the joke and the threat at once.
OPEN: what is the station transmitting to, if only one viewer watches?

### The roster
ESTABLISHED (from shipped pieces): the roster keeps names. Keepers return.
Shifts keep their keepers. The night had two keepers. Names stay on the
roster after the people leave — "The Keeper Who Returned Twice," "The
Shift That Kept Its Keeper," "The Night With Two Keepers."
OPEN: is the roster a memory, a promise, or a trap? Whose name is circled,
and why does nobody erase it?

### The one viewer and the one advertiser
ESTABLISHED: the engine's stakes. One viewer. One advertiser. The station
earns them, keeps them, or loses them — that is every story's pressure.
OPEN (the longest thread of all): WHO is the viewer? WHO is the
advertiser? These two questions are the spine of the long arcs. They are
answered on screen, in scenes, across many chains — never in one reveal,
never in narration.

## PART 2 — THREE LONG ARCS

Arcs interleave: the wheel drafts chains in rotation (one chain per arc in
turn), so the audience follows three threads at once. Each chain in an arc
carries an arc tag and a thread state: OPEN (the thread starts), DEVELOP
(the thread deepens or twists), PAYOFF (the thread lands — and opens the
next one). Chains in an arc link by bridge cliff: each chain's final cliff
frame is the next chain-in-arc's first frame. A payoff never closes the
world; it opens the next question.

### ARC 1 — "THE ONE VIEWER" (the spine)
The station's oldest question, answered across 6 chains.
- Chain 1 (OPEN): a monitor in the lobby plays the station to an empty
  room — and the room is not empty on the monitor. Lore shown: the lobby
  monitor, the empty chair. Question: who is watching?
- Chain 2 (DEVELOP): the count does not match the roster. Super Number
  Head counts one more viewer than the station has. Lore shown: the count
  that refuses to add up.
- Chain 3 (DEVELOP): the tower answers a broadcast nobody sent. Lore
  shown: the transmitter, the reply signal.
- Chain 4 (DEVELOP): the advertiser asks who the viewer is, and Peter
  cannot answer without lying. Lore shown: the contract with the empty
  signature line.
- Chain 5 (DEVELOP): Laura finds a name on the roster that matches no
  employee — and the Dispatcher confirms the building knows that name.
  Lore shown: the roster as memory.
- Chain 6 (PAYOFF): the viewer is shown, not told — in a scene where the
  station broadcasts to the one seat that is always occupied, and the
  occupant is the station itself, watching through its own machines. The
  payoff opens the next question: if the station is its own viewer, what
  is it trying to prove — and to whom?

### ARC 2 — "THE ROSTER THAT KEEPS EVERYONE"
The roster refuses to forget. 5 chains.
- Chain 1 (OPEN): a keeper returns after leaving. The roster never erased
  him. Lore shown: the circled name.
- Chain 2 (DEVELOP): two keepers listed for one night. Both showed up.
  Lore shown: the night with two keepers.
- Chain 3 (DEVELOP): a shift keeps its keeper — the keeper who left is
  still on the schedule, and the schedule is still kept. Lore shown: the
  kept shift.
- Chain 4 (DEVELOP): UNC is asked to erase a name. He refuses — and the
  refusal costs the station its advertiser for one night. Lore shown: the
  roster as promise.
- Chain 5 (PAYOFF): the roster is read aloud, full, in one scene — every
  kept name, including the ones who left — and the station keeps
  broadcasting because the roster says it must. The payoff opens the next
  question: who wrote the first name on the roster?

### ARC 3 — "THE COUNT"
Super Number Head counts toward something. 5 chains.
- Chain 1 (OPEN): the count passes a round number and nothing happens —
  except the Dispatcher logs it. Lore shown: the count itself.
- Chain 2 (DEVELOP): the count skips a number. Super Number Head refuses
  to say which one. Lore shown: the missing number.
- Chain 3 (DEVELOP): the thirteenth count arrives twice. Lore shown: the
  seat that counted thirteen.
- Chain 4 (DEVELOP): Peter tries to sell the count to the advertiser as
  an audience. The advertiser asks what is being counted. Peter cannot
  answer. Lore shown: the count as currency.
- Chain 5 (PAYOFF): the count completes — and what it was counting is
  shown: every broadcast the station ever made, kept, in order, by a
  machine that never stopped listening. The payoff opens the next
  question: who built the machine that counts?

## PART 3 — ARC RULES FOR Q0 AND Q1

- Q0 banks arcs, not just chains: every banked arc names its thread states
  (OPEN → DEVELOP → PAYOFF), its lore beats, and what each chain shows.
- Q1 drafts chains with an arc tag (ARC-1 / ARC-2 / ARC-3) and a thread
  state in the chain header. Chains in an arc link by bridge cliff.
- Every chain shows at least one lore beat from Part 1 — a rule, a room,
  a name, a machine — inside the scene, never as explanation.
- Arcs interleave in rotation. No arc runs two chains in a row.
- A payoff never closes the world. Every PAYOFF chain ends on the next
  question, named in its cliff.
- Canon discipline: shipped pieces are canon. New lore must cohere with
  them. Contradictions are repaired in the open, not papered over.
