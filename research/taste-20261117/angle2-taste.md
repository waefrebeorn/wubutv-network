# ANGLE 2 — TASTE IN AI VIDEO: what separates loved AI shorts from slop

Research date: 2026-11-17 · Commissioned after a WuBuTV episode was rejected by the showrunner as "MISHMASH" (incoherent jumping scenes; characters who looked good but said and did nothing).
Method: web search + full-page reads, 2026-11-17. Claims are tagged **EVIDENCED** (directly supported by a cited source, quoted or closely paraphrased) or **LEAD** (single weak/marketing source, or my inference — treat as hypothesis, not law).

---

## TL;DR — the line, in one paragraph

Across festival juries, production studios and editing craft literature, the same line keeps appearing: **slop is what nobody decided; taste is a small number of strong decisions, visibly enforced.** The work that wins is not the most generated or the most realistic — it is the most *selected*: one premise you can say in one sentence, one look the surface can hold, characters locked as reusable elements rather than re-described, dialogue that is exact and sparse, and cuts chosen for emotion and causality rather than clip order. Everything else — maximalism included — is seasoning on that skeleton, never a substitute for it.

## 1. What gets PRAISED as taste (observable properties)

### 1.1 A premise you can say out loud in one sentence
- **EVIDENCED** — The Higgsfield Global Film Festival (5,000 finished films, $1M pool) put *film people* on the jury — Ed Catmull (Pixar co-founder), cinematographer Phedon Papamichael, Paul W. S. Anderson, Alex Proyas — judging on four criteria: "Directing & Storytelling, Visual Craft, Sound & Editing, Originality & Concept. Fidelity is not one of the four." The four winning films: a gaucho who can't get rid of a black sheep stuck on his roof; a plant engineer certain the plant will hold "right up until it doesn't"; a drive-in superfan handed his own mission; rabbits raiding a tomb for a golden tortoise. The analyst's summary: "Four winning films, four premises you can say out loud in one sentence. None of them is a demonstration of what a model can render." (Phosphene, "The Winning AI Films Did Not Try to Look Real," 2026-11-05 — https://phosphene.cc/blog/higgsfield-festival-winners-style-over-realism)
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's production rubric runs the same test from the script side: the **one-sentence test** — "Say what the video should make a viewer feel or realise, in one sentence. If you can only name its topic, it has no point… 'You can't line-edit a point into existence.'" And the **swap test**: replace the name and the one specific detail with placeholders; "If the video could now belong to any competitor, it's generic, however good it looks." Their rule: "One video should carry one idea. If two compete, make two videos." (Popcorn, "What makes AI video look cheap" — https://www.popcorn.co/guides/what-makes-ai-video-look-cheap)

### 1.2 Decisions made BEFORE generation — script, shot list, someone accountable
- **EVIDENCED** — Talentless AI (an AI film studio) defines it flatly: "Slop happens when somebody hits generate without deciding what they're making, then ships whatever comes out… It's fluent and empty, it uses the same six compositions with a new subject pasted in, and nobody threw anything away." Their five-question test starts with: "What was decided before they started? Ask for the script and the shot list. If the generating came first and the thinking came after, it's slop." And: "A hundred clips nobody directed is slop. The same hundred clips shot as coverage against a script, then cut by an editor with taste, is a film." (Talentless AI, "What Is AI Slop?" — https://www.talentless.ai/slop/)
- **EVIDENCED** — Easyviral's 2026 guide reaches the same diagnosis from audience data: "What makes a video feel like slop is the absence of a human making decisions, having opinions, and caring whether the final product is actually good… The most telling characteristic of AI slop isn't any single technical flaw. It's the total absence of a point of view." (https://easyviral.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-videos-that-dont-feel-like-slop-2026)

### 1.3 Ruthless selection — taste is mostly throwing away
- **EVIDENCED** — The Higgsfield winner *Listen to MEH* (Purga, Argentina) logged **1,215 assets and 1,158 generations** for a ~5-minute film of roughly 40–60 shots. "The honest unit of AI film production is not seconds of video. It is attempts per accepted shot." And the closing verdict: "The winners were not the people with the best model. They were the people who spent five weeks on one film instead of five weekends on twenty ideas… The work that won was directed, not generated. Somebody decided what the sheep means, what the film looks like, and which forty shots out of eleven hundred generations were the film." (Phosphene, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — Independent workflow guides converge on the same ratio discipline: "generate about three times the footage you keep" as a rule of thumb, and "Over-generate and select ruthlessly: a disciplined take-logging system saves more time than any single tool." (UPSC Limitless, "From Idea to AI Film" — https://www.upsclimitless.com/post/ai-film-making; Skillveris, "Assembling Generated Clips Into a Finished AI Video Edit," 2026-11-12 — https://www.skillveris.com/blog/ai-video-editing-pipeline)

### 1.4 A stylized world the model can actually hold — not photorealism
- **EVIDENCED** — Purga "did not aim for photorealism," building *Listen to MEH* as 3D animation "between realism and caricature": "A photoreal AI film invites the viewer to compare it with a camera. Every soft jawline, every light that changes between cuts… gets read as a defect… A stylized world does not offer that comparison. The same small physics error reads as a choice." Second reason: "it lets you keep the art direction consistent when the model cannot. Purga's film has one look." Prescription: "Pick a world you can hold for the whole runtime, and prefer one whose style absorbs small errors over one that invites comparison with a camera." (Phosphene, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — AIMA London 2026 coverage reads the same pattern in *A Face Only A Mother Could Love* (Grand Prix at Runway AIF, 1st at Reply Venice, Best Short at AIMA): "juries rewarding character-led storytelling over showcases of technique." (aipopdb, 2026-11-13 — https://aipopdb.com/news/aima-london-winners)

### 1.5 Specificity from the maker's own life, not the model's average
- **EVIDENCED** — *Ancestra* (Eliza McNitt, Tribeca 2025 premiere, exec. prod. Darren Aronofsky, Google DeepMind × Primordial Soup) is built from the director's own birth story — her mother, an emergency C-section, a baby born with a hole in her heart. McNitt trained the model on her own family archives: "she trained the Veo model on personal archives, including baby photos and home videos shot by her late father." McNitt: "It's a film about my mother." Coverage stresses the hybrid craft: live-action performances blended with AI visuals, a 200-person crew, "storyboards and concept art to keep the AI focused on amplifying the story's heart." (Sundance Collab — https://collab.sundance.org/catalog/The-Poetry-of-the-Prompt-AI-Storytelling-and-the-Making-of-ANCESTRA-Video; LBBOnline, 2025-07-11 — https://lbbonline.com/news/Chromistas-Eliza-McNitt-Blends-AI-with-Heartfelt-Human-Touch-for-Ancestra; IBM Think — https://www.ibm.com/think/news/how-filmmakers-are-reimagining-cinema-with-ai)
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's cliché rule is the negative form of the same point: "The usual clichés: a lone figure in the rain, neon cyberpunk, a fork in the road — the first idea is everyone's first idea. Use specific behaviour and props from your own world." (Popcorn, as above)

### 1.6 Sound treated as an authored layer, not per-clip residue
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's sound rules, learned from failures: pin **one narrator voice per series** ("If nothing fixes the voice once for the whole video, separately generated scenes can each pick a different one"); with two speakers "give each a distinct voice and say so explicitly in every scene"; **level all dialogue to one target** (their measurements: dialogue "swung by up to about 3 loudness units either way between takes, enough to hear as a dip at a cut"); "One score written for the whole piece holds it together; a stock bed under each scene doesn't." (Popcorn, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — Skillveris: "Sound is the strongest continuity tool in the pipeline. A continuous ambience bed or music cue across several shots makes them feel like one space and one moment even when lighting varies slightly." (Skillveris, as above)

### 1.7 Performance and motivated camera
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn on camera: "Camera swoops and a glossy, oversaturated look — models default to spectacle. Move the camera only when something motivates it." And on staging: models can't hold named finger gestures across any model in their stack ("The fix is to rewrite the action, not the prompt") — "Stage common, whole-body actions, not delicate gestures." (Popcorn, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — *Ancestra*'s praised structure anchors AI imagery to **real human performances** (live-action scenes shot first, AI woven around them) — performance is the load-bearing element, generation is the amplifier. (Sundance Collab / Google blog, as above)

## 2. The failure smells — what gets NAMED as slop

### 2.1 The competent, correct, dead script
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's script-review rubric: AI slop "is not a script with errors — it's a script that is competent, correct, continuity-clean, and dead. A flawless script about nothing in particular is the most common cheap video there is." Five named tells to count in any script: the **"not X, but Y" line**; the **fake-wisdom ending** ("And that's what real connection looks like" → instead "End on a concrete image or a line only this video could say"); **buzzwords as proof**; **the reassurance** ("And that's okay" → "Commit to a position the viewer could disagree with"); the **sweeping opener** ("In a world where everything is changing…" → "Open on the most specific thing in the video"). (Popcorn, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — Writing-craft critics name the dialogue version of this **local coherence, global nonsense**: "Markov chains and GPT-2 can produce text that's locally coherent and globally nonsensical. Modern AIs have dramatically reduced, but not eliminated, this failure mode… things that seem reasonable on a quick scan but actually make no sense." (Nick Heiner, "Slop is Deeper Than Cringe Word Choice" — https://www.nickheiner.com/p/slop-is-deeper-than-cringe-word-choice) This is the exact mechanism of word-salad dialogue: every line scans, the exchange means nothing.

### 2.2 The random montage / collage of cool shots
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's picture tells include: "A collage of 'cool shots' — shots generated one by one, with no thread between them. Make each shot exist because of the one before it." And: "The same presenter, in the same frame, for a whole minute — the easiest thing to prompt, repeated." (Popcorn, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — Arcloop's slop checklist: "The visuals feel like a random montage: The video is just a collection of cool looking clips strung together. Because there is no story connecting them, the audience stops caring after the first few seconds"; "Characters look different in every shot… the viewer gets confused because they can no longer recognize who the main character is"; "The audio and video don't match… They don't feel like they belong to the same world." Their definition: "Slop isn't about using AI tools, it's about the absence of intent." (Arcloop, "How to Make AI Videos That Don't Look Like AI Slop," 2026-10-27 — https://arcloop.ai/handbook/en-US/how-to-avoid-ai-slop-in-ai-videos)

### 2.3 Characters speaking prompt-details — the dialogue failure specific to generated video
- **EVIDENCED** — Independent Veo 3 testing (aivideos.eu review): when a prompt *describes* a person speaking ("a woman explains something animatedly to camera"), the model generates voice with lip sync but "the dialogue content itself is inferred rather than specified; you cannot currently script specific dialogue text in the standard prompt interface… unscripted dialogue is a liability, not a feature." In other words: describe speech instead of scripting it, and the character will improvise plausible-sounding filler — the on-screen equivalent of reading the prompt aloud. (https://aivideos.eu/blog/google-veo-3-review-native-audio-video-generation)
- **EVIDENCED** — The working fix from production prompting guides: quote the **exact line**, keep it to one short line per clip (~6–12 words per 8s), one speaker, with a delivery note — ""A man says something about being late" produces improvised, mumbled speech" vs. the exact quoted line, which performs. (DreamDrive, "Veo 3 Prompting: Dialogue, Sound Effects and Ambience" — https://www.dreamdrive.ai/learn/google-labs/veo-3-prompting-dialogue-and-sound/)
- **EVIDENCED** — Models also *pad*: Veo "tends to pad silence with sound effects or filler dialogue to make the clip feel 'complete'," and negative prompts backfire ("no talking" introduces the concept of speech — "You have to treat your prompt only in the positive sense"). Unattended, a quiet reaction shot will invent a voice. (apipass.dev, "Veo 3.1 Keeps Adding Dialogue? 9 Ways to Fix It Fast," compiling r/VEO3 creator reports — https://apipass.dev/blogs/how-to-solve-veo-3-keep-generating-unwanted-dialogue)

### 2.4 Template fatigue and the default look
- **EVIDENCED** — "When thousands of creators use the same tool with the same default settings, the output converges. Audiences develop pattern recognition for AI-default aesthetics — particular voice cadences, caption fonts, background styles, avatar behaviors." (ViralCompany, "Avoiding the AI Slop Problem" — https://www.viralcompany.com/guides/viralcompany-avoiding-the-ai-slop-problem-how-to-make-your-shorts-look-intentional)
- **EVIDENCED** — NielsenIQ (Dec 2024, 2,000+ viewers, ~150 with brain measurement): viewers picked out most AI ads themselves and rated them more "annoying," "boring" and "confusing" than traditional ads; even AI ads rated *high quality* produced **weaker memory activation**. Popcorn's gloss: "Prettier footage alone doesn't close the gap. What viewers read as 'cheap' is mostly everything around the footage: what the video says, how its shots hold together, how it sounds and how it's cut." (NielsenIQ via Popcorn, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — Mirador Studios names the "slop tax": "AI didn't just lower the cost of making video — it raised the cost of making *forgettable* video… Most of the disappointing AI video out there comes from treating generation as a button: prompt in, 'content' out… No script worth the name. No point of view. No sound that grounds it. No editorial hand deciding what actually belongs on screen." (https://miradorstudios.com/blog/ai-video-not-a-button/)

### 2.5 Edit-level smells
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's edit tells: "**Transitions everywhere** — crossfades and wipes chosen by default look like a template" (their editor now defaults to hard cuts only); "**A metronome rhythm** — if every shot runs five seconds, the video ticks. Vary shot lengths with what each moment needs"; "An ending that stops on the last word — let the final image breathe." (Popcorn, as above)
- **EVIDENCED** — Zgion's AI-editing guide: "AI clips often arrive at their maximum generated duration, not their ideal editorial duration. Cut based on information and emotion, not the fact that a clip exists… short generated clips can feel repetitive when each is shown from beginning to end." (https://zgian.com/ai-film-editing-ai-generated-video/)

## 3. The maximalism line — where density works and where it curdles

**The line the evidence draws: maximalism works when the density is *ornament on one simple, legible emotional story* — every spectacle beat is simultaneously a story beat, and the emotional stakes never slip. It curdles when novelty itself is the content — when each new element arrives because the model could render it, not because the story needed it.**

- **EVIDENCED (works)** — *Everything Everywhere All at Once* is the canonical proof. Sight and Sound (BFI): "for all the goofily surprising, high-octane action, this is at heart a character piece; call it **poignant maximalism**… Fantasy writes large feelings that come with the territory." The Letterboxd critical consensus echoes: "even when there are dildos being used as weapons or rocks having a subtitled conversation on a cliffside, **the emotional stakes never slip**"; the film is "both a maximalist assault on the senses and a deeply intimate apology between a mother and a daughter." Note the structure underneath the chaos: one family, one want (a mother trying to reach her daughter), one antagonist who is that same daughter. The multiverse is a *metaphor for one relationship*, so every new world is also a new angle on the same single idea — density with a spine. (BFI Sight and Sound — https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/everything-everywhere-all-once-shows-life-fathomless-multiverse; Letterboxd reviews, 2022–2026)
- **EVIDENCED (curdles)** — The AI-demo failure mode is the mirror image: spectacle with no hierarchy. Popcorn: models "default to spectacle" — unmotivated camera swoops, glossy oversaturation, a new marvel per shot — and each unmotivated element is read by viewers as cheapness, not richness (see §2.5, §1.7). The Higgsfield jury made the same cut from the other side: 5,000 films, and the winners were "not a demonstration of what a model can render." (Phosphene; Popcorn, as above)
- **LEAD** — Cognitive-load numbers circulate in marketing content (e.g., claims that top-decile watch-time videos average ~1.2 effects/minute vs ~4.7 for the bottom decile; "each unnecessary effect costs ~0.8s of refocus") — directionally consistent with the craft sources but published by a tool vendor with unverifiable study attributions (Digen resource page). Treat as a hypothesis to test against our own retention data, not a fact. (https://resource.digen.ai/overused-effects-ai-video-editing-fix/)

**Operational form of the line:** before adding any element, ask "what story beat does this also carry?" If the answer is "it's cool / the model nailed it," it goes in the slop pile. EEAAO's rocks-with-subtitles scene is two characters *failing to communicate* — the gag IS the theme. Density that doesn't carry story is just more chances to drift, confuse, and break the world.

## 4. Editing & coherence — what good AI editors do AT THE CUT

### 4.1 Judge every cut by Murch's hierarchy — emotion first, spatial continuity last
- **EVIDENCED** — Walter Murch's Rule of Six (*In the Blink of an Eye*) ranks the criteria for an ideal cut: **Emotion 51%, Story 23%, Rhythm 10%, Eye-trace 7%, 2D plane 5%, 3D spatial continuity 4%**. His tradeoff rule: "If you have to give up something, don't ever give up emotion before story. Don't give up story before rhythm, don't give up rhythm before eye-trace…" — sacrifice from the bottom up. "A cut that breaks continuity but serves the emotion will work. A cut that preserves continuity but breaks the emotion never will." For AI footage this is liberating and strict at once: small face/light drift (a 4% problem) is forgivable; a cut that lands on the wrong feeling or stalls the story (74% of the value) is not. (StudioBinder — https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/walter-murch-rule-of-six/; Video&Filmmaker — https://videoandfilmmaker.com/wp/tutorials/film-editing-walter-murchs-rule-6/)

### 4.2 Give the sequence a story spine BEFORE generation; QA the joints for causality
- **EVIDENCED** — A production pipeline post-mortem (Humza Tareen, video-generation platform) describes the exact "MISHMASH" failure: "Each scene was generated independently from a single script line. Scene three had no idea what happened in scene two. The result felt like a montage of unrelated clips stitched together… Our pipeline was generating twelve unrelated micro-stories and calling it a video." Three QA symptoms: **no causal connection between scenes**, **visual discontinuity** (lighting/wardrobe/time-of-day drift), **staccato voiceover** (lines force-fit to cuts, "sentences broke mid-thought"). The fixes: (1) a **story spine** in the planner — "every scene must advance the protagonist, and scene N must set up scene N+1"; (2) **cross-scene narrative QA** with a named check, `missing_causality` — "scene N does not logically follow from scene N-1"; (3) **continuous voiceover** across cuts using L-cut/J-cut structure instead of per-clip narration fragments. (https://humzakt.github.io/blog/narrative-continuity-story-spine-video-gen.html — full-page fetch failed once during this research; claims are from the page's indexed text, which was extensive and internally consistent)

### 4.3 The concrete cut toolkit for generated shots
- **EVIDENCED — Cut on action / match on action.** "Cutting from one shot to another in the middle of a continuous physical movement, matching the subject's position, direction and speed across the cut… the edit itself disappears and the scene feels like one unbroken piece of time." (Imaginode editing guide — https://imaginode.ai/en/cinematic-techniques/editing; InVideo — https://invideo.io/blog/video-editing-types-of-cuts/)
- **EVIDENCED — J-cuts, L-cuts, sound bridges.** Let the next scene's audio arrive before its picture (J) or the previous scene's audio linger over the new picture (L); a sound bridge "can connect two disjointed shots with ease" — e.g., one door slam shared by two locations. These are the cheapest coherence devices available because generated *audio* can be continuous even when generated *pictures* can't. (Freelance Video Collective — https://www.freelancevideocollective.com/match-cuts-in-film-creative-editing-techniques/; Skillveris on ambience beds, §1.6)
- **EVIDENCED — Frame chaining.** "Using the final frame of one shot as the starting reference for the next is one current AI-specific way to improve continuity across a cut." Higgsfield workflow notes agree: animate approved keyframes, "sometimes with the first and last frames pinned so the shots connect where they are supposed to." (Zgian, as above; Phosphene, as above)
- **EVIDENCED — Change framing at every cut.** "Two separate generations never match at a cut. If one shot ends on a medium shot of the host and the next begins on almost the same medium shot, the small differences read as a glitch. Change the framing at every cut, from wide to close or from one angle to another, and the difference reads as editing." (Popcorn, as above) — the counterintuitive rule: near-matches expose drift; decisive reframing hides it.
- **EVIDENCED — Trim the drift zones.** "Trim the first and last moments of generated clips, where artefacts and drift are most common"; "Trim to rhythm. Cut on action and before the AI motion starts to drift." (Skillveris; UPSC Limitless, as above)
- **EVIDENCED — Select for motion, not beauty.** "Select takes by judging motion and continuity first and image beauty second, because a gorgeous frame with unnatural movement will stand out more in sequence than a plainer shot that moves convincingly." (Skillveris, as above)
- **EVIDENCED — Unify in post.** Conform all selects to one resolution/frame rate before editing; colour-match shot-by-shot to a reference, then apply one unified grade (and light grain) over the sequence: "Colour matching makes shots agree with each other; grading makes them look like your film." (Skillveris; UPSC Limitless, as above)
- **EVIDENCED — Lock elements, don't re-describe.** "The failure mode is not a bad prompt. It is re-describing a character instead of reusing them" — store each character/set/prop once as a reference element and recall it; a written description guarantees nothing about the next generation. Same for prompt text: "Repeat your descriptors — copy the same wording for clothing, lighting and style in each prompt," and repeat the shared style/lighting block at the head of every shot prompt. (Phosphene; UPSC Limitless; OnPattison, "AI Video Generator Mistakes" — https://onpattison.com/news-from-our-partners/ai-video-generator-mistakes-that-make-footage-look-off/)
- **EVIDENCED — Coverage grammar.** Keep the cast small (one or two per scene), one subject doing one main action with one camera move per shot, and use close-ups / over-shoulders / inserts / cutaways "so small differences do not show." (UPSC Limitless, as above)

### 4.4 The publish ritual (a 60-second self-test battery)
- **EVIDENCED** — Popcorn's pre-publish ritual, built for exactly this failure class: (1) **Watch it muted** — "With every spoken line gone, does the story still read?" (2) **Pause on the first frame** — would you stop scrolling? (3) **Look at the last image** — does it pay off what the opening promised? (4) Say the idea in one sentence. (5) Run the swap test. (6) "Say honestly whether you would watch it to the end. A reviewer who flatters is worse than none; a false pass costs a whole production." They also separate writer from judge institutionally: "one agent writes, and a separate reviewer agent judges, with a standing bias against safe, bland drafts… 'a timid, safe-in-every-direction draft must score worse than a bold draft with a rough edge.'" (Popcorn, as above)

## 5. Character agency — what makes a character in 60 seconds feel like they DID something

### 5.1 McKee: character is revealed only by choices under pressure
- **EVIDENCED** — Robert McKee, *Story*: "**True character is revealed in the choices a human being makes under pressure — the greater the pressure, the deeper the revelation, the truer the choice to the character's essential nature.**" The seminar unpacking (Writer Unboxed's notes on McKee's Story seminar) sharpens it for construction: **characterization** (all visible traits — costume, look, quirk) is not **character**; character "is what emerges when protagonists are forced to make choices under pressure as they pursue their Object of Desire." Design the choice as a dilemma: "Make your characters pick between two equal, but mutually exclusive goods, or between the lesser of two evils," with "elements of sacrifice." A character who is never forced to choose has shown the audience only their wardrobe. (McKee via Steven Mintz — https://www.stevenmintzethics.com/single-post/2016/03/29/character-and-trust-is-key-to-ethical-decision-making; Writer Unboxed, "All Hail Dilemmas" — http://writerunboxed.com/2014/05/19/mckee-morsels-part-ii/)
- This is the precise diagnosis of "looked good but did nothing": all characterization, no choice. A designed face + costume + catchphrase is characterization; agency appears only at the moment the character must give something up.

### 5.2 Parker & Stone: beats must connect by BUT or THEREFORE, never AND THEN
- **EVIDENCED** — Trey Parker and Matt Stone's writers'-room rule (NYU Tisch lecture): write the beats in a row; between every pair you must be able to write **"therefore"** (consequence) or **"but"** (conflict/complication). "We can take these beats of your outline, and if the words 'and then' belong between those beats, then you're fucked." They literally write the connectives between scenes on the whiteboard. A beat connected by "and then" is decoration — it can be deleted and nothing changes. Applied to a 60-second piece with ~6 beats: five joints, each must be a *but* or a *therefore*, and most of those joints should be a character's decision causing the next beat. (LADBible, 2025-11-30 — https://www.ladbible.com/entertainment/tv/south-park-strict-writing-rule-revealed-612058-20251130; Storytelling Edge — https://storytellingedge.substack.com/p/the-golden-rule-of-storytelling-according; Selfstorming — https://www.selfstorming.com/tools/libraries/storytelling-techniques/but-therefore)

### 5.3 Short-form craft: one character, one want, one turning point, one irreversible change
- **EVIDENCED** — Short-film writing consensus: "build the story around one character, one want and one turning point… In a short you usually get one situation, one central relationship and one change." Beats: "opening image, setup of the want, complication, escalation, turn, final image." And the ending rule: "A turn should change the meaning of what came before, not erase it." (Lowerated, "How to Write a Short Film Script," updated 2026-10-14 — https://www.lowerated.com/post/how-to-write-a-short-film)
- **EVIDENCED** — "Character efficiency" for micro-shorts: state the desire immediately and specifically; "show their strengths and weaknesses through action rather than dialogue"; the short-film arc is not a personality transplant but "a moment of irreversible change or a new understanding" — e.g., the stubborn character is "forced to listen just once, and seeing the immediate impact of that choice. It's about that one specific decision." Also: hook in the first 3–5 seconds or the swipe is gone. (Befreed, "Short Film Storytelling," summarizing BAFTA Student Awards judge Sarah Juma's observations — https://www.befreed.ai/podcast/short-film-storytelling-that-hooks-your-audience)
- **EVIDENCED** — The situation-vs-story test (Janaki Cedanna's screenwriting packet, FMP): beginners write "a situation, instead of a story," where "a final twist provides the resolution of his troubles — often through no action of his own" and "the character is often unchanged by his experiences." A story requires: the hero "must want something more than anything in the world," meet obstacles/conflict, act to resolve it, succeed or fail, and be "forever changed." Her compression: "**Character is ACTION. An action is what the character DOES in order to get what he WANTS.**" (https://omarhhashem.com/ar/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/Screenwriting-Packet.pdf)
- **EVIDENCED** — Scene-level version (Brett Lamb's short-film guide): "Scenes should always cause change in the life of a character — if a scene doesn't [cause change], cut it," and "something must happen in the first thirty seconds of your film." (The Free Library / Creative Conventions — https://www.thefreelibrary.com/Creative+Conventions%3A+HOW+TO+SCRIPT+A+GREAT+SHORT+FILM%3A+Nearly+all...-a0535883702)

### 5.4 The 60-second agency checklist (synthesis of the above — EVIDENCED components, LEAD as a packaged formula)
1. By second 5, the protagonist's want is visible in an action, not a line of exposition. (Befreed/Juma; Lamb)
2. The want meets a *but* — an obstacle embodied by another character's opposing want. (Parker/Stone; Cedanna)
3. Mid-piece, the protagonist makes ONE decision under pressure that costs something — two goods or two evils, with sacrifice. (McKee)
4. The ending is *therefore*: the final image exists because of that decision, and something is irreversibly different. (Lowerated; Befreed/Juma)
5. Every other character exists to force, block, or price that decision — anyone who does none of those is decoration; cut or merge them. (Cedanna; Parker/Stone deletion test)
