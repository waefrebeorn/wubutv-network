## Working title options

1. **THE MACHINE** — plain, and the show's own subject. The machine makes
   the videos; the machine is the set.
2. **THE CUTTING ROOM** — where the wrongness first shows: a cut that
   nobody made, a take that sings a line nobody wrote.
3. **TAKE TWELVE** — the reroll that never lands. Every episode is another
   take of the same song, and the song is starting to notice.

Recommendation: THE MACHINE. It names the premise in two words and leaves
the wrongness unnamed, which is where the slow build lives.

## Premise (one paragraph)

Inside the failing station, a small crew makes music videos for a singer
who is not a person. The machine writes the song, times every word, fires
the takes, and cuts the master — and the crew's job is to keep the mouths on
the words. It works, most days. But the machine has started mishearing its
own song: a hook nobody wrote, a word the transcription drops every time, a
take that sings the right line at the wrong speed. The crew fixes it, the
machine thanks them, and the fix does not hold. Something is wrong, and the
wrongness is learning the crew's names.

## Cast (established canon only — no new faces until the user says so)

- **LAURA** — notices first. Lore: she asks the question everyone avoids;
  she notices what the old hands have stopped seeing. She is the audience's
  way in, and the machine's first problem: it cannot route around her.
- **UNC** — manages the machine. Deadpan, short certain sentences, keeps the
  roster and the promise. He treats the wrongness as a scheduling problem
  until it stops being one.
- **COLONEL** — anchors the official version. He states that the machine is
  fine, and states it so well that for a while it is.
- **PETER** — sells the videos. He promised the advertiser a singer; the
  machine delivered a voice with no one behind it. His promises are the
  station's promises, one desk removed.
- **THE SINGER** — never seen whole. A black cat with a red collar (MV-5's
  singer, established on screen) or a voice with no face — the show keeps
  which one it is as an open question. The singer sings the lines; the
  machine decides who sings them.

## The slow build (three beats, across the first arc)

1. **A dropped word.** The machine's transcription hears the hook zero
   times in 206 seconds — the hook has to be carried by picture, not audio.
   The crew calls it a measurement problem. (Real, from MV-5: Whisper heard
   "easy" zero times; "Easy now" came back as "is it" 28 times.)
2. **A wrong singer.** A line the cat should sing arrives in the girl's
   mouth, on time, in tune. Both Gemini runs agree on the flip; the pitch
   data argues against it. Nobody can prove who sang it. (Real, from MV-5:
   the L12/L15 singer splits that only the user's ear could settle.)
3. **A take nobody fired.** A finished take appears in the ledger with no
   volley behind it. It sings a line that is not in the song. UNC logs it
   as a clerical error. LAURA keeps the file.

Rule for the whole show, from the lore doc: lore is SHOWN, never explained.
No character ever says "the machine is becoming self-aware." The machine
just keeps answering questions nobody asked, correctly.

## Pilot chain (dialogue-first, one conversation, four lines)

Setting: the cutting room, after the dropped-word night. LAURA, UNC.

- LAURA: "The machine heard 'is it' twenty-eight times. The song says
  'easy now.' Which one do we cut to?"
- UNC: "We cut to the mouth. The mouth is the record."
- LAURA: "The mouth sang a line that isn't in the song. Whose record is
  that?"
- UNC: "File it under the singer. The singer is always the record."

Narrator test: no line could be spoken over the frame by a narrator — each
is a speech act to a named addressee (accuse / rule / counter / file away).
The hook shows the fact (the dropped word); no line restates it. The final
line lands one true message: the crew has agreed to pretend the machine is
fine, and the pretending is now procedure.
