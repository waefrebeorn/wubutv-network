# 03 — ALGO MAX: the 10-second story part, with the retention numbers

Owner order: *"we are now in the algo maxxing — each 10 seconds being story
parts."* This file is the evidence base. Every number below is from a page saved
in `web/` or the DA-25 evidence set, dated.

## The retention cliff is at 1.5 seconds, not 3

- **CreatorHouse, 1,000 Reels sampled March 2026**: the median retention curve
  **drops 47% in the first 1.5 seconds**, then another 8% between 1.5 and 3.0 s;
  healthy Reels have lost ~45% by 3 s. The gap between passed and failed Reels is
  **23 percentage points at 1.5 s** and 26 points at 3.0 s.
- **Meta internal data (via Percee Digital)**: **65% of people who watch the first
  3 seconds** will watch for at least 10 seconds.
- **Socialinsider 2024 benchmarks (via Percee)**: TikTok videos **under 15 s hit a
  92% completion rate**; completion falls off for 16–30 s.

**Consequence for a 10-second part: the hook must land inside 1.5 seconds or the
part is already dead.** That is 45 frames. Not a title card, not a fade, not a
wide establishing shot.

## The watch-time gates (YouTube Shorts, 2026)

- Every Short is **cold-seeded to 50–500 viewers, ~70% non-subscribers**.
- To be pushed wider: **average view duration ≈65% of length for sub-30 s
  Shorts**, ≈50% for 30–60 s (Socialync, 2026).
- **Sub-15 s Shorts collapsed in reach in 2026** — at 15 s you need 100% retention
  to clear the gate; at 35 s, 65% clears it. **30–45 s is the sweet spot.**
- Instagram: median Reel **watch-time retention 31.7%**; Reels **under 15 s retain
  66.9%** of their length; engagement **5.8% for 15–30 s** vs **3.2% over 90 s**
  (Klap, 2026).

**Consequence: a 10-second part is the atom, but the SHIPPING unit is a 30–45 s
run of three to four parts in one piece.** Ten seconds alone is too short to clear
any watch-time gate.

## Microdrama pacing — the only published second-level grammar I found

From AutoWeeb's 60-second microdrama structure (the most concrete pacing document
in the corpus):

- **One new cut or movement every 1.5 seconds.**
- **One minor plot point every 10 seconds.**
- **One to two major plot points per minute.**
- At 60 s, the **first major beat anchors near 0:27 (~45% through)**; the
  **cliffhanger lands ~0:49**.

From drama.so's short-drama beat sheet:

| Beat | Window | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0:00–0:05 | detonate one question the viewer needs answered |
| Setup | 0:05–0:20 | the stakes, in one location |
| Mini-cliff | 0:15–0:20 | beat the first drop-off with a small jolt |
| Escalation | 0:20–0:50 | three rising beats |
| Cliffhanger | 0:50–1:00 | withhold exactly one fact |

Repeated across creator playbooks: **70–80% retention through the first 30 s** is
the working target (charisma.cloud); episodes **60–120 s**, sweet spot **90–100 s**
(invideo); split into Part 2 **only** when there are two genuine arcs, never to
stretch one (getreeltok).

## The 10-second story part, derived

Ten seconds = **one minor plot point**, delivered as:

| Window | Beat | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0–1.5 s | **HOOK FRAME** | the single most legible image of the part; no title, no fade, no establishing wide |
| 1.5–4.0 s | **STATE** | who wants what, where — in one action, not dialogue |
| 4.0–7.0 s | **TURN** | the thing changes: an object moves, a person enters, a count is wrong |
| 7.0–9.0 s | **LINE or SOUND** | one utterance (or one hard sound) that reframes the turn |
| 9.0–10.0 s | **CLIFF FRAME** | the withheld fact, held; **this frame is the next part's first frame** |

Rules that follow from the numbers, not from taste:

1. **A 1.5-second beat grid.** Some visible change every 1.5 s or the retention
   curve eats the part.
2. **The cliff frame is the next part's hook frame.** First-frame chaining is both
   a continuity technique (PixVerse/Seedance guidance) and an algorithm technique:
   the viewer's eye is already in position.
3. **Three parts = one shipping piece (30 s)**, four = 40 s (the Shorts sweet spot).
   A serial is then a chain of 3-part pieces, not a chain of atoms.
4. **One withheld fact per part**, spent by the next. That is the microdrama
   grammar and it is also the only reason a viewer tolerates a 10-second window.
5. **No part may require the previous part to be understood.** Cold-seed viewers
   arrive mid-chain; the hook frame must work alone.
