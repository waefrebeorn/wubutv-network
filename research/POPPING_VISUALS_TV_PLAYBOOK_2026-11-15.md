# POPPING VISUALS — THE STATION PLAYBOOK (TV shows / WuBuTV)

**Date:** 2026-11-15. **Order:** Telegram msg 433 — "self improve another
research volley into learning how to make the best music video and tv show
popping visuals." This is the STATION half (a sibling playbook covers MVs).
**Companion, not replacement:** the FIRST-SECOND DOCTRINE
(`wubutv-network/research/FIRST_SECOND_DOCTRINE_2026-11-15.md`) owns the
*writing* — opening lines, soap grammar, self-aware grammar. This playbook
owns what the camera shows while those lines land. Panel mechanics live in
the panels spec (`research_notes/panels-reference-workflows-20261115/`,
§4.4 show kits); this doc covers station-specific uses only.

**Evidence base (all on disk, all measured or sourced — no vibes):**
- Network performance, 28 days to 2026-11-12: the 10-second bumper is the
  ONLY format that ever performed. Top 3 IG posts are all bumpers
  (1,494 / 1,395 / 1,135 views); best 1-minute episode: 419. Audience is
  ~98% non-followers reached through the feed — every frame is a first
  impression for a stranger. Same-format, same-day pieces split 32.5% vs
  87.5% on Reels skip rate: **the opening seconds are the whole
  distribution decision.** Nothing converts views to followers — so the
  frame's job is the watch, not the follow.
- Attention science (deep-research report §4, sourced): disgust/texture
  wrongness grabs attention at **100–200 ms** (van Hooff 2013); a
  scene-inconsistent object is fixated earlier, longer, and remembered
  better — **prediction error** (Allegretti & Coco 2023); morbid curiosity
  is reward-linked (Oosterwijk 2020) but enjoyment is an inverted-U —
  keep the grotesque obviously artificial (Andersen 2020, benign
  masochism); an unresolved gap sustains curiosity (Loewenstein 1994) —
  a morph that never completes, a count that never finishes.
- Banked forms from the lilypad studies: THE LOOPHOLE (portal-chained
  cuts), THE KEYHOLE (bumper as glimpse of an unseen larger film), THE
  FOG SEAM (loop closed by one ambient mover), THE LIQUID WALL (one
  boundary in a solid frame reads liquid — the peak frame), THE
  VERIFICATION FREEZE (hold the wrong frame a beat past the rhythm),
  STILL-FIRST SLOT, ONE COHERENT ELSEWHERE.

## §1 — OPENING FRAME DOCTRINE (per format)

The universal law, translated from the science: **frame 1 is a state, not
a setup.** The viewer decides in ~200 ms whether the image is
categorisable; a categorisable frame (an empty room, a desk, a street
"about to" have something happen) is scrolled past before the event
arrives. Every format below is that law at a different length.

### 1a. The 10-second bumper (the proven format — protect it)

- **Frame 0 = the peak image, already wrong.** One scene-inconsistent
  thing in an instantly legible place: the office reads in one glance,
  the flesh-fused worker at the desk is already there (Kurupuri's
  verified house style). No "before" shot, no entrance, no wind-up.
- **One event only, unresolved.** The bumper's single event is a
  transformation / invasion / scale violation that does NOT complete.
  Completion is a resolution; resolution is a scroll cue. End mid-morph,
  mid-fall, mid-count.
- **The joint is designed, not matched.** Open with an arrival (something
  falls/drops/enters from above — THE LOOPHOLE head) and close with an
  exit through a visible portal (hole, door, screen, mouth, drain — the
  LOOPHOLE tail), so the bumper loops back into its own opening and the
  replay (333 replays on the top bumper) feels like continuity.
- **Ambient loop insurance:** where the event is slow, close the loop
  with one cyclical ambient mover (fog, rain, candle, a fly — THE FOG
  SEAM) so the seam is never a freeze.
- **Texture is the finish, not an accident.** A deliberate grain / VHS /
  flicker pass (Dor Brothers' documented practice) reads as signature
  and hides model error; a clean corporate render reads as an ad and is
  skipped as one.
- **No text in frame 0.** No titles, no logos, no captions — LoopHole
  bans them outright for the same reason: the image must do all the work
  in the first seconds. The `~ WuBu ~` wordmark lives on UNC's hoodie
  (diegetic), never as an overlay opener.

### 1b. E1M / E2M episodes (1–2 minutes — the format that has NOT performed)

Episodes don't get 200 ms of patience *once*; they get it **every 10
seconds**, because the episode is a chain of 10-second units and the
viewer can leave at any joint. The shows' transition devices ARE the
retention devices — shoot them that way:

- **Unit 1 opens on the episode's peak image, not its premise.** The
  first unit is cut like a bumper (§1a) that happens to continue: peak
  frame first, context after. E1M-003 ("The Short Count", the best
  episode at 419 views / 36.2% skip) is the shape to beat, not the
  average.
- **Every unit re-earns the next.** Each show's handoff device is shot
  as a *visual event in frame*, not an editing convenience:
  - THE MACHINE — the mouth handoff: unit ends on a mouth opening on a
    word; next unit opens on the same mouth mid-word, new angle/singer.
    The mouth must be BIG in frame at the joint — a wide shot's mouth
    carries nobody across.
  - THE TENTH SECOND — the carry: the carried object is named visually
    in the first 1.5 s (hand + object in close frame). If the audience
    can't identify the carried thing in both units, the thread is lost
    and the episode reads as random clips — the old era's core failure.
  - THE ROSTER / THE COUNT — name/number handoffs: the spoken name or
    number must be *already visible* in the opening frame of the next
    unit (page, door, board, chalk). Legibility at feed size is the
    gate: if it can't be read on a phone at arm's length, the handoff
    didn't happen.
  - THE LAST SEAT — the gaze handoff: unit ends with the turn to the
    seat landing ON the beat; next unit opens from the seat's POV on a
    NEW world, never the same room reversed.
- **Escalation across units, not repetition.** ~1 second per world is
  the dreamcore extreme (teber: 14 scenes / 14 s); our unit is 10 s, so
  the episode-scale version is: each unit's peak image is stranger than
  the last unit's, and the final unit holds the episode's biggest
  wrongness. A flat middle unit is where the 87.5%-skip pieces died.
- **The last frame points forward.** Bridge-cliff grammar (lore bible):
  a chain's final frame is the next chain's first frame. Shoot the
  cliff as an image, not a line — the unanswered visual (the occupied
  seat, the circled name, the skipped number) is what a stranger
  screenshots.

### 1c. Shot terms — the first second, concretely

| Need (science) | Shot term |
|---|---|
| Legible place, instant read | Locked wide or medium-wide, familiar set, motivated light, no camera move in the first 12 frames |
| The wrong thing (prediction error) | ONE inconsistency only, large in frame, foreground or centre — Muroi's discipline: exactly one boundary fails per frame (THE LIQUID WALL) |
| Grab at 100–200 ms | Texture/face wrongness at frame 0, not frame 24 — if the wrongness enters, it enters already mid-state |
| Hold past the grab | THE VERIFICATION FREEZE: hold the wrong frame 6–12 f past the cut rhythm at the END of a moving take (never a dead-still opening — motion law) |
| No resolution | Cut on the onset of the next event (mouth opening, number landing, portal opening), never after it completes |

## §2 — PER-CHANNEL VISUAL IDENTITY

Rule: **a muted, logo-less frame must name its channel.** Palette is the
station's six-colour system (INK, CADMIUM, FOREST, COBALT, VERMILION,
BONE) plus each channel's texture finish and camera grammar. (Channel
set: CHANNEL_GRID.md canonical 10 — TALK survives inside PUPPET
THEATER, which superseded it.)

| Channel | One-line visual signature |
|---|---|
| **NEWS** | Cobalt desk-glass and codec static: locked-off broadcast framing, the Colonel centre-frame, [STATIC] tears as the only camera "move" — a news frame is a desk, a stare, and a signal failing. |
| **ORIGINALS** | 80s found-footage camp: warm BONE light, VHS softness and tracking error, UNC deadpan in impossible MEGA spaces — every frame looks recovered, not rendered. |
| **SATIRE** | Clipart-flat collage on paper-white: cut-out figures, visible edges, scoreboards and tallies as set dressing — the frame reads as an encyclopedia page that misbehaved. |
| **LABS** | Clean INK workshop: macro lenses on machines and hands, diagram light, one experiment per frame — the only channel allowed to look precise. |
| **ARENA** | Height and sweat: low angles looking UP at climbers and stacks, CADMIUM floodlight glare, broadcast lower-third energy without the text — scale is the joke in every frame. |
| **MEDIA SCHOOL** | The decoy exposed: glossy ad-perfect surfaces with one seam showing — a frame that looks like the thing it's dissecting, lit a half-stop too well. |
| **PUBLIC ACCESS** | Green-couch domestic chaos: warm living-room tungsten, too many people in too small a frame, cable-access zoom — UNC ducking into shot is the channel's establishing move. |
| **PUPPET THEATER** (ex-TALK) | Stop-motion tactility: clipart and puppet cast on miniature sets, VHS grain, narrator's-world stillness between jerky moves — nothing in frame pretends to be alive smoothly. |
| **NETWORK** | The 24-hour loop: NEWS's desk stretched to liminal — empty studios, corridor cams, breaking-nothing events, codec roll as wallpaper; the channel is the station watching itself. |
| **ARCHIVE** | No hand at all: unedited found footage, diegetic only, whatever light the room had — the frame's authority is that nobody composed it. ("The room was found.") |

Cross-channel laws: (1) a channel's palette may borrow ONE foreign
colour per piece, as the scene-inconsistent element — the wrong colour
is the prediction error; (2) texture finishes never cross channels
(VHS belongs to ORIGINALS/PUPPET, codec static to NEWS/NETWORK, clean
render to LABS only); (3) UNC's hoodie `~ WuBu ~` in CADMIUM is the
only element allowed in every channel unchanged — it is the station's
through-line, and its exactness is a hard QC gate.

## §3 — SOAP + SELF-AWARE VISUALS (the visual half of the writing doctrine)

The doctrine doc owns soap *writing* (serialized stakes, triangles,
cliffhangers, dialogue as speech acts). Visually, a soap is a
**memory machine**: the audience must recognise the room, the pairing,
and the unresolved thing before a word lands.

- **Recurring sets are anchors, shot identically.** The roster room,
  the lobby with the monitor, the seat room, the depot: each gets ONE
  canonical establishing framing (locked in the show kit, §4) and every
  episode returns to it at the same angle. Recognition is free
  attention — a returning viewer reads the set in 200 ms and spends
  their attention on what CHANGED in it. What changed is the scene's
  prediction error, so stage the change inside the known frame: the
  chair warm, the name circled, the chair count off by one.
- **Reaction-shot grammar.** Soaps run on faces receiving news, and our
  cast is built for it: deadpan is the house style, so the reaction
  shot is a HOLD, not an expression — camera stays, the face doesn't
  move, the room tone does the work. Cut pattern: line lands → hold on
  the listener 12–18 f longer than comfort → the cull/handoff. A
  reaction cut away too early wastes the freeze (THE VERIFICATION
  FREEZE, applied to faces).
- **Two-shots for relationships, singles for secrets.** Triangles are
  composed, not described: the two-shot pairing the audience knows,
  with the third party at the frame edge or in the doorway — visible
  before they're acknowledged. A character alone in frame is a
  character keeping something; the audience learns the grammar once and
  reads it forever.
- **The world-cull, visualised.** In self-aware shows the cull is not
  explained, it is SEEN, the same way every unit: one held element
  (the carry, the chalk, the seat, the count) survives while the world
  behind it visibly swaps — new light, new extras, new set dressing,
  same held thing in the same screen position. The swap must be
  *detectable in a single glance at the joint*: if the audience has to
  rewind to see the world changed, the cull didn't happen on screen.
  Chalk survives, paper doesn't (SHOW-2 canon) — the survival rules
  are visual rules, and they never change without being shown breaking.
- **Self-awareness is played to the seat, not the camera.** Characters
  who know they're generated show it by glancing at the seat / the
  lens-side dark (THE LAST SEAT's gaze grammar generalised), never by
  winking at the viewer. The fourth wall in this station is a chair.
- **The lore is furniture.** Roster, count board, lobby monitor, the
  seat: lore objects sit in frame as set dressing in scenes that
  aren't "about" them (lore bible: shown, never explained). A lore
  object that only appears in its own exposition scene is a lecture;
  the same object in the background of six scenes is a world.

## §4 — PANELS FOR THE STATION (station-specific uses only)

Mechanics, panel types, naming, and QC live in the panels spec §4.1–4.6
— not repeated here. What the STATION adds:

- **Show kits, frozen at ship.** Per §4.4 of the spec: each show locks
  a kit (CHAR panels for the full cast + WORLD plates for its recurring
  sets + bible lines) at the show root; the kit freezes when an episode
  ships and is inherited, never silently edited. For the five shows
  this means five kits, and the shared cast (UNC, LAURA, COLONEL,
  PETER, DISPATCHER, SUPER NUMBER HEAD) uses ONE master CHAR set
  inherited by every kit — the same face sells the whole station.
- **Set sheets before cast sheets for new shows.** A soap's memory is
  its rooms (§3): when a new show is stood up, WORLD plates for its
  2–3 anchor sets lock FIRST, at their canonical establishing angles,
  because every episode's recognition grammar depends on them. Cast
  CHAR panels second, PEAK panels per episode after.
- **Cull-survivor PROP panels.** Each show's held element — the parcel,
  the chalk, the roster page, the count board, the seat — gets a PROP
  panel (single object, clean background, per spec §4.1). The survivor
  must be pixel-recognisable across the cull joint; its panel is the
  reference the joint is QC'd against.
- **The seat gets a panel, the viewer doesn't.** THE SEAT (chair,
  light, warmth) is a WORLD/PROP subject with locked panels from
  several angles; whoever sits in it is never panelled, never
  generated full-frame — the mystery is a production rule, not just a
  writing one.
- **Drift bridge test on generator change** (spec §4.4): re-fire three
  archived anchor takes per show kit and compare before any episode is
  committed on a new model version. Station continuity is the product;
  a silent model swap that re-faces UNC is a canon break, and the
  bridge test is the only thing standing in front of it.

## §5 — THE ANTI-FLAT LIST (old-era visual failure modes — banned)

The user's verdict on the old shows: "made no sense and made no moves."
In visual terms, these are the specific failures — each is a QC reject,
not a taste note:

1. **The establishing-shot opener.** Opening on an empty room / street /
   desk and letting the subject arrive. The stranger is gone before
   the subject is. (Fix: §1 — start already in the state.)
2. **The halted-photo start.** A take that begins frozen and then
   starts moving — reads as a slideshow, breaks the motion law, and
   the first second (the only second that matters) is a still.
3. **The illustrated script.** Frames that merely depict the line being
   spoken — one image per sentence, no event in the frame the words
   don't already carry. If muting it loses nothing, it was radio.
4. **The wandering camera with no event.** Slow pushes/pans across a
   set where nothing changes: motion without a move. Camera movement
   is not an event; a transformation, a handoff, a count landing is.
5. **The same-world cut.** A cut to the same room, same angle, same
   light — the cull that didn't cull. Every joint must show the world
   changed or the held thing carried; a cut that changes nothing
   teaches the viewer the joints don't matter, and they stop watching
   them.
6. **Exposition framing.** Characters arranged to explain — two people
   at a desk facing camera, talking about the lore. Lore is furniture
   (§3); a scene staged to deliver information has no prediction error
   and no grab.
7. **Clean-render house style.** Untextured, evenly lit, ad-clean
   frames across every channel — no signature, no wrongness, mistaken
   for a commercial and skipped as one. Texture finish is channel
   identity (§2).
8. **Text doing the image's job.** Title cards, caption hooks, and
   overlays opening a piece. Banned in frame 0 (§1a) and suspect
   everywhere: if the hook needs words, the frame failed.
9. **The unrepeated set.** A "recurring" location that looks different
   every visit — new angle, new dressing, new light — so the audience
   never builds the recognition a soap runs on (§3). Recurring means
   identical until the one staged change.
10. **The resolved ending.** A final unit that completes its morph,
    lands its count, answers its question. Resolution is a scroll cue
    even at the end — it trains the feed that our pieces stop. End on
    the onset of the next thing (bridge cliff, §1b).

## TOP 5 RULES (the short version)

1. **Frame 0 is the peak, already wrong** — one scene-inconsistent
   thing, large, in a one-glance-legible place; no befores, no
   entrances, no text.
2. **Every 10-second unit re-earns the next** — the handoff (mouth,
   carry, name, number, gaze) is a visible event in frame, legible at
   phone size, or the episode is a clip pile.
3. **A muted frame names its channel** — palette + texture + camera
   grammar per §2; finishes never cross channels; the `~ WuBu ~`
   hoodie is the only universal.
4. **Soaps run on recognition** — identical anchor sets, reaction
   holds, the cull SEEN at every joint (one held thing, world visibly
   swapped); lore is furniture, never a lecture.
5. **Nothing resolves** — morphs stay mid-morph, counts stay
   unfinished, endings open on the next onset; resolution is a scroll
   cue at any timestamp.
