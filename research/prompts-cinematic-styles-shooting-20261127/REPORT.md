# AI-Video Production — Advanced Prompts, Cinematic Styles, Shooting Craft
**For:** a production house making 10-second clips (Kling / Seedance / Veo / Runway-class) assembled into 60-second short-drama episodes and music videos.
**Date researched:** 2026-11-27. **Basis:** official model guides opened and read in full where marked *verified live (opened)*; other sources read via web index and marked *index*. Labels: **[official docs] / [published guide] / [creator breakdown] / [paper] / [community]**.
**Confidence rule used throughout:** a technique is “settled” only if an official guide or ≥2 independent guides agree. Single-source claims are flagged ⚠.

---

## Summary — what actually changes vs. basic subject+action+scene prompting

1. **Order is weighting.** No major video model documents Midjourney/Stable-Diffusion-style weight syntax in its official guide (see §1.4 for the one contrary claim, flagged low-confidence). What *is* documented is **positional weighting**: Veo puts Cinematography first, Kling/Seedance put Subject+Action first, Runway puts Subject Motion first — because the opening is read as most important.
2. **Per-model dialects now diverge sharply, especially on negatives and multi-shot.** Runway Gen-4: positive phrasing only, one scene, start simple. Kling 3.0 / Seedance 2.0–2.5: labelled multi-shot storyboards *inside one generation*, with speaker-tagged dialogue. Veo 3.1: 4/6/8 s only, timestamp blocks, three separate audio channels. Hailuo 2.x: the only true *syntax* in the set — 15 bracketed camera commands.
3. **The craft layer is where 60 s episodes are won:** one beat per shot, a verbatim-repeated look/character bible, a locked style block (grade written as temperature + palette + black/highlight behaviour, not the word “cinematic”), master-frame-first dialogue coverage, explicit screen-direction in every prompt, and first/last-frame chaining on continuous action.
4. **Lens/film-stock words are look words, not dials.** Tests and Google’s own warning agree: write the *visible consequence* (“background melting into soft circles”) alongside the number, and never rely on `f/1.4`, ISO, camera bodies, or “8K masterpiece”.

#### 1.2 Multi-beat / temporal structure — timestamps and shot labels are real, documented syntax

- **Veo 3.1 timestamp prompting [official docs]** — Google’s full example runs four 2-second beats in one 8 s generation: `[00:00-00:02] Medium shot from behind a young female explorer…` → `[00:02-00:04] Reverse shot of the explorer's freckled face… SFX: The rustle of dense leaves…` → `[00:04-00:06] Tracking shot following the explorer…` → `[00:06-00:08] Wide, high-angle crane shot…` (Google, URL in §1.1). LearnAIArt’s condensation adds the practical cap: “Use it for two or three beats at most in 8 seconds” **[published guide]** https://learnaiart.com/articles/veo-3-json-prompting/ .
- **Seedance 2.0 labelled shots [published guide, fal]** — verbatim:
  > “Shot 1: Close-up of an espresso machine portafilter locking into place with a metallic click. Shot 2: Wide angle of dark espresso pouring into a white ceramic cup, steam rising, the sound of liquid hitting porcelain. Shot 3: Slow pull-back revealing a busy cafe interior, ambient conversation and soft jazz in the background.”
  fal notes: without `Shot 1:`/`Shot 2:` labels “longer prompts tend to produce a single continuous take rather than an edited sequence”, and multi-shot needs 10–15 s / `auto`.
- **Seedance 2.5 timestamps + asset numbers** — ByteDance’s published launch prompts (translated, via VidModelHub/OpenClips) address shots by timestamp (`[0–10s]: … [10–20s]: … [20–30s]: …`) and references by number (`@image 1`, `@video 1`), up to 50 assets / 30 s in one request **[published guide]** https://www.vidmodelhub.com/prompts/seedance-2-5 and https://openclips.ai/models/seedance-2-5/prompts/ . ⚠ These are republications/translations of the launch post, not the ByteDance page itself.
- **Kling 3.0 multi-shot [official docs + fal]** — official Kling: Custom Multi-Shot gives per-shot duration, shot size/perspective, narrative content and camera movement; its own example is literally `[Shot 1: Wide shot] … [Shot 2: Medium shot] … [Shot 3: Close-up shot] …`. fal: up to **six shots** in one output, “clearly label shots”, and Kling “understands … shot-reverse-shot dialogue” **[published guide, opened]** https://blog.fal.ai/kling-3-0-prompting-guide/ .
- **Temporal verbs / sequencing words do causal work in Kling 3.0 dialogue:** fal’s P4 rule — use linking words (`Immediately,`, `Pause.`, “this is when the speaker switches”) or “the model may merge speech”. See §1.6.
- **Contradiction — multi-beat inside one generation:** Runway officially warns the opposite for Gen-4 (5/10 s = “a single scene”; dictating each second with multiple scene changes “may provide unintended results”). Resolution: multi-beat-in-one-prompt is a Veo/Kling/Seedance feature; for Runway, generate one beat and cut in the edit. Likewise MovieMaker (§3.4) advises one beat per *generation* as the default filmmaking workflow even where a model offers multi-shot — treat multi-shot generations as a speed lane, single-shot generations as the control lane.

#### 1.3 Negative space — the single most model-dependent technique. Do not port negative blocks across models.

| Model | What published sources actually say |
|---|---|
| **Runway Gen-4** | **No negative prompting at all.** [official docs] “Negative phrasing is not supported and may produce unpredictable or even opposite results.” ❌ `No camera movement. The camera doesn't move.` → ✅ `Locked camera. The camera remains still.` |
| **Veo 3.1** | In the *main prompt*, phrase exclusion positively/scenically: Google’s example is to write “a desolate landscape with no buildings or roads” instead of “no man-made structures” [official docs]. Where the API/UI exposes a **separate** `negative_prompt` parameter, secondary guides summarising Vertex guidance say to list bare nouns **without** “no/don’t”: e.g. `subtitles, text overlays, watermark, urban background, man-made structures, distorted hands` **[published guide]** https://promptgenia.com/blog/veo-3-prompt-guide . Community add-on for burned-in captions: put `(no subtitles)` after dialogue or `subtitles, captions` in the negative field (same source — ⚠ community practice, not Google). |
| **Kling 1.6–2.6** | **Separate negative field, aimed at stability, not quality words.** Artlist (tested on Kling 2.5 Turbo Pro): the same prompt without a negative drifted/shook; adding — verbatim — `no camera drift, no handheld movement, no sudden zooms, no facial warping, no changing facial features, no flicker, no motion blur` held the camera and face. Their reusable blocks: `no camera shake, no exaggerated motion, no visual distortion, no flicker, no warped faces`. Advice: limit motion rather than ban it (`no fast motion` beats banning motion) **[published guide / tested]** https://artlist.io/blog/negative-prompts-ai-video/ . Kling API caps prompt *and* negative prompt at 2,500 characters each (secondary guide) https://yrom.com/kling-ai-prompt-guide/ . |
| **Kling 3.0** | No official negative-field guidance in the Kling/fal guides read. A practitioner guide instead bans artefacts *in prose at the end of the prompt*: end with `No subtitles, no captions, no on-screen text.` and, for unwanted music, the “triple ban”: `No background music, no score, no soundtrack.` — then state the sounds you *do* want **[published guide, single-source for the triple-ban formulation ⚠]** https://pixeldojo.ai/guides/kling-3-prompting-guide |
| **Seedance 2.5** | Screenweaver, citing Seedance 2.5 documentation: “negative constraints work only for subtitles and audio” — hence its prompts carry an `Avoid: no subtitles, …` tail rather than a general negative list ⚠ single-source reading of the docs **[published guide]** https://www.screenweaver.ai/blog/cinematic-prompts-directors-guide . The SaaS-library template built on Dreamina’s guide likewise ends: `Avoid: no subtitles, UNWANTED_ITEMS.` https://saascrmreview.com/seedance-prompts/ |
| **Hailuo / MiniMax** | No negative-prompt field is documented for Hailuo 2.3 in the guides checked against MiniMax docs; control is via the positive “hold” slot (what stays unchanged). H3 guides use an explicit **Constraints/Retention** section (“what must remain stable”) instead **[published guide]** https://prompt-architects.com/blog/180-minimax-hailuo-2-3-prompt-templates |
| **General warning** | MinionArts: “Most video models respond poorly to negative prompts and sometimes introduce the negated element” **[published guide]** https://www.minionarts.com/blogs/prompt-engineering-ai-video-generation-guide-2026 — consistent with Runway/Veo official positions, and the reason Kling’s *separate-field* negatives are the exception, not the rule. |

**Negative-space technique that transfers everywhere: the empty-frame / absence description.** Instead of negating, describe the void: Google’s desolate-landscape move; Runway’s “locked camera”; H3’s retention clause; Seedance/Kling’s `Avoid:` tail limited to text/music. Also “negative” staging: leave the reveal *out* of the shot — duanju practice is to “end just before the key touches the lock” / “cut before the courier answers” (§2.3), i.e. withhold the payoff visually rather than prompting it and trimming.

#### 1.4 Prompt weighting / emphasis syntax — ⚠ largely unsupported; one source claims it, officials are silent, another warns against it

- **No official guide read for Veo, Kling, Runway, Seedance, Luma or Hailuo documents `(term:1.3)`, `((term))`, or `[term]` weighting** for the main prompt. The only true syntaxes documented are: Hailuo’s bracketed *camera commands* (not weights), Kling/Seedance/Veo shot labels and timestamps, Seedance’s `@Image/@Video/@Audio` bindings, Veo’s `SFX:` / `Ambient noise:` labels, and Kling 3.0’s `[Character: …, tone]: “line”` speaker tags.
- ⚠ **Single-source, low-confidence claim:** Versely Studio asserts “Kling 3.0: Use `(term:1.3)` to boost, `(term:0.7)` to dampen”, “Wan 2.7 / LTXV2: `((slow motion))` … `[low priority]`”, and agrees Veo/Sora have no weight syntax (use ordering) **[published guide — treat as unverified]** https://www.versely.studio/blog/ai-video-prompt-engineering-advanced-techniques-2026 . **Contradicted in direction** by MinionArts’ FAQ: “Only for models that explicitly support it. Most video models in 2026 do not, and adding syntax can degrade output.” No second independent source for the Kling `(term:1.3)` claim was found. **Recommendation: do not build a house style on it; A/B it on your own credits if curious.**
- ⚠ A similar low-confidence artefact: a “2026 cheat sheet” circulating `--camera smooth --cfg 0.55 --motion-amplitude 6`-style flags for Kling/Sora (DailyToolbox) — no official source supports flag syntax for these models; do not use.
- **What to use instead of weights (settled):** (1) **front-load** the priority element in the model’s published order; (2) **repeat/insinuate** — Runway officially: if insinuated scene motion is too weak, “try insinuating motion multiple times or adding simple description to further emphasize”; (3) **bold speaker/tone tags** in Kling 3.0 (fal uses `**trembling frustrated voice**` inside the speaker bracket — emphasis there is about *parsing*, documented in fal’s examples); (4) UI/API controls where they exist (Kling camera_control values, creativity/relevance, Hailuo prompt_optimizer) rather than in-text weights.

#### 1.5 Physics and material language — describe behaviour, phases and secondary motion, not adjectives

- **Physics-aware prompting [creator breakdown / published guide]:** Atlabs’ survey of X creators: “Instead of describing what things look like, you describe how they behave.” Structure: `[Subject + physical properties] [What happens physically] [Secondary motion: what keeps moving after the action ends] [Surface/environment reaction] [Camera + lens] [Style]`. Phrases it reports as working across Kling 3.0 / Seedance 2.0 / PixVerse: `natural weight and momentum throughout`, `realistic fabric/hair/water physics`, and — verbatim pattern — `secondary motion: [fabric/hair] continues for 0.5 seconds after action ends` **[published guide]** https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/how-to-make-ai-videos-look-real-a-beginner-s-guide-to-motion-physics-(50-real-prompts)
- **Seedance physics explainer [published guide]:** the model “isn’t running a physics engine … it learned how the world moves” — prompt for motion it has seen often; name **weight** (heavy = slow to start/stop), **momentum** (decelerate, overshoot, settle — a runner who stops does not freeze), **secondary motion** (hair/coat lag a beat), and **contact** (feet meet ground, hands grip, objects rest, not hover) **[published guide]** https://seedance-2.app/blog/seedance-2-0-physics-motion .
- **Force-first material prompts (Luma) [published guide]:** split material work into Material + Force + Action + Timing + Camera. Template verbatim:
  > “[Material] is affected by [force], causing it to [primary action]. The motion begins [timing], followed by [secondary action]. Locked camera, clear silhouette, plain background, no cuts.”
  Worked example: “A compact mass of thick cobalt-blue liquid drops onto a flat white surface. It accelerates under gravity, compresses on impact, spreads outward in a low crown, then pulls inward with one small rebound before settling.” https://www.magicboat.net/mobile/en/blog/luma-material-motion-reference
- **Kinetic verbs for Kling [published guide]:** AKOOL’s Kling 2.5 guide: ❌ “driving down a road” → ✅ “drifts around a hairpin turn, tires kicking up gravel” — “dynamic verbs trigger Kling's physics engine” (its wording) https://akool.com/blog-posts/kling-2-5-video-prompt-guide . Kling’s own official guide makes the same point more soberly: describe *visible* motion — “smoke drifting upward, flames bending in the wind, a runner leaning forward” — over internal/technical claims.
- **Anticipation–action–recovery for humans [published guide]:** Media.io realism guide: prompt the phases (“She pauses, looks toward the window, then exhales as her shoulders relax”) rather than vague speed words; a cup must react when a hand touches it; hair responds to wind “with a slight delay” https://www.media.io/video-tips/ai-video-looks-real.html . Runway official says the same in miniature: translate concepts into physical actions — ❌ “embodies the essence of joyful greeting…” → ✅ “The woman smiles and waves.”
- **Temporal adverbs are pacing controls (Kling official):** `slow` = calm/intimacy/suspense (slow push-in as she thinks), `steady` = readable continuity, `quick` = energy/surprise (quick cut to reaction). Attach one speed word to both subject *and* camera moves.

#### 1.6 Narrative scenes with dialogue — professional structures per model

- **Veo 3.1 — three audio channels, kept separate [official docs]:** Dialogue in quotation marks with attribution (`A woman says, "We have to leave now."`), `SFX: thunder cracks in the distance`, `Ambient noise: the quiet hum of a starship bridge`. Kuro’s tested gloss: a sound “buried in a sentence about the picture gets you the picture” — give sound its own lines; and never end a timestamp block on a decay (“a ring fades”) — end on an *action/event*, or picture and sound stall together ⚠ tested single-guide claim, plausible and cheap to adopt **[published guide]** https://meetkuro.com/guides/veo-3-1-prompting/ . Keep lines short: 1–2 short sentences per 8 s (PromptGenia/Google summary).
- **Veo “Ingredients to Video” dialogue coverage [official docs]:** build character + setting reference images once, then generate *one prompt per speaker angle* reusing the same ingredients. Google’s verbatim pair:
  > “Using the provided images for the detective, the woman, and the office setting, create a medium shot of the detective behind his desk. He looks up at the woman and says in a weary voice, "Of all the offices in this town, you had to walk into mine."”
  > “…create a shot focusing on the woman. A slight, mysterious smile plays on her lips as she replies, "You were highly recommended."”
- **Kling 3.0 — speaker-tag grammar [published guide, fal, opened]:** four documented principles: **P1 Structured naming** (unique, consistent labels; no pronouns/synonyms), **P2 Visual anchoring** (action first, *then* dialogue — the model must know who slammed the table), **P3 Audio details** (unique tone/emotion per character), **P4 Temporal control** (linking words between turns). Verbatim pattern:
  > `[Character A: Exhausted Partner, **trembling frustrated voice**]: “You never listen to me.”` … `Immediately, the other partner turns around, eyes wide.` … `[Character B: Defensive Partner, **shouting loudly**]: “Because you never stop blaming!”`
  Kling official adds: keep “the speaker's name, line, and delivery close together”; supports Chinese/English/Japanese/Korean/Spanish plus dialects and code-switching in one scene. Independent review ⚠ notes Kling 3.0 speaker confusion still occurs in complex multi-character scenes and rates its audio behind Veo 3.1 https://genra.ai/blog/kling-3-0-complete-guide .
- **Seedance 2.0/2.5 dialogue:** lines in quotes, named speaker, `@Audio1` can supply the voice; fal ⚠ cautions Seedance 2.0 lip-sync/dialogue is its weaker audio area vs. SFX/ambience — “test the lip-sync quality against your standards before committing to a production pipeline.” For drama, that argues: Seedance for coverage/B-roll/multi-shot, Veo/Kling for hero dialogue — or post-dub.
- **JSON prompting — fad, not documented:** LearnAIArt, checked against all Google guides (2026-09-29): “Google doesn't document JSON prompting. None of Google's Veo guides … mention JSON.” Treat viral JSON templates as unproven ⚠.

### 2. CINEMATIC STYLES

#### 2.1 Film stocks / formats — name the signatures, not just the stock

Consensus across AI Pro Cinematography, ScenePoet and ReelMe: a stock name is shorthand; when it doesn’t land, spell out **grain + halation + colour response + contrast**. Verbatim style blocks:

| Look | Verbatim prompt language | Source / label |
|---|---|---|
| 35mm | `shot on 35mm film, fine grain, soft highlight roll-off, natural colour` | ScenePoet **[published guide/tool]** https://www.scenepoet.com/camera-shots |
| 16mm | `shot on 16mm film, coarse grain, slightly soft, muted vintage colour` ; ReelMe expansion: “[subject], shot on 16mm film with visible grain and indie documentary texture. Grain is clearly visible and moving, detail is softer, colors are slightly muted… NOT a polished, glossy image.” | ScenePoet; ReelMe **[prompt library]** https://reelme.art/academy/resources/prompts-lens-kit |
| Super 8 | `shot on Super 8 film with heavy grain nostalgic home movie aesthetic. Heavy, dancing grain, soft detail, warm faded color, slightly crushed blacks and soft blooming highlights… NOT sharp or clean.` | ReelMe **[prompt library]** |
| Anamorphic | `anamorphic lenses, oval bokeh, horizontal blue lens flares, 2.39:1 widescreen` ; AI Pro: “oval bokeh, blue horizontal flare, fine grain, warm halation” beats the word “cinematic” alone | ScenePoet; **[published guide]** https://www.aiprocinematography.com/ai-lens-film-look/ |
| 1970s anamorphic | `vintage 1970s anamorphic look, blue streak flares, oval bokeh. Highlights throw long blue streak flares… contrast is slightly lower and colors feel vintage… NOT round bokeh` | ReelMe **[prompt library]** |
| Veo film-stock refs (via Atlabs summary of community + Google vocab) | `Shot on 16mm film with visible grain, warm overexposed highlights, slight color shift toward amber in the shadows. The aesthetic of a mid-70s American road movie.` / `Digital cinema aesthetic. Anamorphic lens with characteristic horizontal lens flare. Desaturated teal grade, slightly crushed blacks, clean highlights.` / `VHS camcorder footage. Heavy chroma noise, tracking artifacts on horizontal lines, slightly blown-out whites…` | **[published guide]** https://www.atlabs.ai/blog/google-veo-3.1-prompting-guide-how-to-get-cinematic-4k-video-on-the-first-try |

Note ReelMe’s negative-tail device (`NOT a clean digital surface`) — that is an *image-model-style* phrasing inside its own composer; per §1.3 it should be rewritten positively for Runway/Veo main prompts.

#### 2.2 Colour grading language — replace “cinematic” with temperature + palette + tonal behaviour

- **InVideo [published guide]:** “Drop 'cinematic' as a standalone descriptor — it collapses into generic stock-footage color. Replace it with three layers … color temperature, the named palette, and tonal behavior (where the blacks sit, where the highlights roll off).” Named grades verbatim: teal-and-orange = `warm amber skin tones against cool teal shadows, complementary palette`; low-key horror = `crushed blacks, 85:15 dark-to-light ratio, neutral gray shadows`; Kodachrome nostalgia = `golden highlights, rich greens, slight fade`. Production protocol in the same piece: a **fixed style block** opened all 164 clip prompts of a 3-minute episode (after locking it against 64 reference frames) — “that repetition, not any single prompt, is what held the palette” https://invideo.io/faq/what-cinematic-color-grading-styles-work-best-in-ai/ .
- **Seedance grade guide [published guide]:** name shadows and highlights *separately* — “warm amber highlights with cool teal shadows” is what makes teal-and-orange read; saturation is a dial (“desaturated” + a number/degree beats on/off). Table: bleach bypass = `bleach bypass look, desaturated, high contrast, silver highlights`; warm nostalgic = `warm nostalgic grade, golden amber tones, gently faded blacks`; moody = `desaturated cool grade, blue-grey shadows, muted colors` https://seedance-2.app/blog/seedance-2-0-color-grading .
- **Screenweaver [published guide]:** every look = colour balance + black level + saturation + texture. When a grade drifts (whole frame goes teal, faces included), the fix is source-bound colour: name the warm source *on the subject* and the cool source *behind* — e.g. “Warm amber ceiling strips light her face orange while the metal walls glow deep teal” https://www.screenweaver.ai/blog/film-stock-grade-prompts . Same principle as LTX lighting (§2.4): tie colour to a source, never float adjectives.
- **Finish pass still matters:** MovieMaker (§3.4): separately generated shots “never match perfectly” — apply **one grade/LUT + light consistent grain across the whole timeline**, adjust only exposure/white-balance outliers. Prompting the grade in reduces, but does not eliminate, the finishing pass.

#### 2.3 Genre looks — framing, lighting and aspect conventions expressed as prompt text

| Genre | Convention + prompt block | Source / label & confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical duanju / microdrama (9:16)** | Plan in 9:16 from the storyboard, not by cropping. | **Duanju 3-clip pattern** | Animate’s storyboard: (1) discovery — “Vertical close-up… pulls out a small brass key tied with blue thread. **Aspect/framing conventions (settled across guides):** 9:16 vertical drama — compose native, keep faces/reveals inside platform safe area, leave caption space (AniKuku); 2.39:1 reads “widescreen cinema”, 16:9 “standard online video” (MovieMaker — lock ratio *before* prompting); 4:3 is itself a genre signal for 90s broadcast (Hailuo); 21:9/2.39:1 anamorphic language pairs with oval bokeh + horizontal flare (§2.1). Veo 3.1 only offers 16:9 / 9:16 in the official guide — anamorphic *look* must be prompted, ratio set in the editor/model that supports it.

#### 2.4 Lighting setups as prompt text — source + direction + quality + colour, in that order

The LTX-2.5 lighting guide and LzyPrompt’s cross-model cheatsheet agree on the atom: **name the source, its direction, its quality (hard/soft) and its colour/temperature**; place lighting after subject/setting/camera; describe any *change* in light chronologically. “Bright lighting” fails; a physical sentence works.

Verbatim / copy-ready setups:

- **Three-point (LTX):** “A soft key light from camera left, a dim fill from the right, and a thin rim light outlining his shoulders against the dark background.” — **[published guide]** https://staging.ltx.io/blog/lighting-prompt-guide
- **Three-point with temperatures (LzyPrompt):** “classic three-point setup — a soft key light at 5600K from camera-left at 45 degrees, a softer fill light at 4800K from camera-right at 30% intensity, and a hair light from behind the subject above the shoulder line. Soft falloff on the background. Natural skin tones, no harsh shadows.” — **[published guide]** https://lzyprompt.com/blog/ai-video-lighting-prompts-cinematic-setups/
- **High-key vs low-key (LTX):** high-key/product = “evenly lit white studio, almost no shadows”; low-key/noir = “a single overhead bulb, most of the room falls into darkness.” Chiaroscuro extreme: “a hard shaft of light from a high window cuts a diagonal across the table, everything outside it falls to black.”
- **Practical / motivated (LTX + LzyPrompt):** introduce the practical *in the environment sentence first*, then refer back to it in the lighting sentence. Neon template (LzyPrompt): “motivated by neon signs visible in frame. Magenta neon ‘OPEN’ sign camera-left bathes the subject's face in pink. Cyan storefront sign camera-right rim-lights the opposite side. Overhead streetlight at 2200K throws a small warm pool on the wet asphalt…” — i.e. **one colour per side, named per side** (Promtable agrees: “two saturated colours from opposite sides read clearly; describe which side each colour hits”).
- **Window/Vermeer (LzyPrompt):** “single large window camera-left, soft overcast daylight at 6500K streaming in. The window light wraps around the subject's face, falling off into deep shadow on the camera-right side. No fill light, no other sources… Dust motes visible in the window beam.”
- **Golden hour:** low warm sun (guides use ~2800–3200K descriptors), from behind/side, rim on silhouette + long shadows. Kelvin caution ⚠: no official model guide promises Kelvin is parsed as a physical value — like lens mm (§3.2), Kelvins work as *look words* correlated with captioned footage; always pair with a plain consequence (“warm”, “long shadows”).
- **Veo’s own lighting vocabulary [official docs ecosystem]:** Google’s Veo guide lists high-key, low-key, volumetric, backlighting, golden-hour glow, dramatic side lighting as recognised terms (summarised at Promtable https://promtable.com/use-cases/ai-video-lighting-prompts ).
- **Soap/high-key dialogue default:** bright, even, low-contrast three-point with prominent fill (sitcom guide §2.3 uses a 3:1 key:fill ratio for cheerful accessibility vs. prestige drama’s heavy shadow) — reuse the same ratio language for soap/K-drama day interiors, and reserve low-key single-source for night/conflict beats. That genre↔lighting mapping is synthesis from the sitcom guide + soap breakdowns ⚠ (labelled synthesis, not a single published rule).

#### 2.5 Music-video looks — grammar by genre, performance:atmosphere ratio, beat structure

- **Shoty / Seedance 2.0 music-video gallery [published guide]:** “Most music video prompts fail because they describe appearance without rhythm.” Two transferable structures: (1) **Performance-to-atmosphere ratio flips by section** — verse: performer small in a vast environment, wide/medium; chorus: tight medium-close-up, performer fills frame, city-light bokeh behind. Encode the flip explicitly with shot labels. (2) **Name the genre’s infrastructure, not its mood:** K-pop = synchronised group choreography, LED-wall back-projection, coloured fluorescent banks, mirror-floor reflections, fast cuts on the drop, insert close-ups on hands/faces/footwork; hip-hop = handheld camera *inside* the performance space, urban practicals (streetlights, headlights, neon storefronts), camera follows rather than stages; indie-folk = natural light (golden hour / window / firelight), static or very slow dolly, performer looks off-frame “unaware of the camera”, intimate-documentary aesthetic https://www.shoty.ai/community/use-cases/music .
- **Beat workflow [published guides]:** map the song first (lyrics + BPM + section times — “never trust a model's tempo”, measure the audio), pick **three recurring motifs** (a colour, a place, an object) + performer look, write **one shot per bar group** (verses = story/slower moves, chorus = performance/energy, bridge = break the pattern), **render silent with sound off** so generated audio doesn’t clash with the track, cut on the beat grid in the edit https://templates.memes.com/ai-video-guides/how-to-make-an-ai-music-video .
- **If the model must sing [published guide, tested examples]:** Bandicam’s six-part order — music/mood (genre, tempo, `No vocals` for instrumentals) → performer → setting/light/look → shot/camera (one move per 5 s, timed ranges for longer) → sound/lyrics (one short quoted line) → exclusions (`No subtitles, no on-screen text, no logos, no watermark`). ⚠ Its key production warning: “Two singing clips of the same singer won't share one song or voice, so don't plan to cut them together as one performance” — for a pre-existing track, use a lip-sync pass over a front-facing performance still instead of native singing per clip https://www.bandicam.com/ai-video-prompts/music-video-prompts/ .
- **Veo music transition [official docs]:** the first/last-frame workflow (§3.3) is demonstrated by Google on a pop performance — 180° arc from front-facing singer to behind-singer stage POV in one generation, with the sung line in quotes.

### 3. SHOOTING / CINEMATOGRAPHY CRAFT FOR AI VIDEO

#### 3.1 Shot sizes and camera moves — per-model trigger words

**Shot sizes (settled vocabulary, all models):** extreme wide / wide (establishing) / full / medium (waist-up; dialogue, product-in-hand) / medium close-up (chest-up; emotion + some setting) / close-up (face fills frame; reaction) / extreme close-up (eye, hand, prop detail) / macro / insert / two-shot / over-the-shoulder / POV. MovieMaker: “Establishing shot, medium shot, close-up and insert cover most needs.” Kling official adds composition terms: `centered`, `rule of thirds`, `off center`.

**Moves — what each model is documented to obey:**

**Rules that recur in ≥3 independent sources (settled):**
1. **One camera move per shot.** MovieMaker: a stacked pan+zoom “produces a muddled blend”; fal/Seedance: “one clear verb and one camera movement” per shot; HeyGen/Seedance: “`slow push-in` — never `push then pan then orbit`”; Kling cheatsheet calls it the “one move per shot rule”.
   **⚠ Official counterexample:** Kling’s *own* official camera guide showcases a prompt combining dolly-in + “subtle pan right and a gentle tilt upward” in one shot, and Hailuo’s bracket grammar *by design* runs up to three simultaneous moves in one bracket (`[Pan left, Pedestal up]`). Resolution: one *primary* move is the safe default; a primary + one subtle secondary is documented to work on Kling/Hailuo specifically. Do not stack two *major* moves anywhere.
2. **Tie the move to the subject and give it a speed + start/end.** Kling official: “camera slowly pushes in on the character's face as she turns toward the window” beats “cinematic camera movement”. ManifoldGen’s reusable camera block **[published guide]** https://manifoldgen.com/blog/camera-movement-angles-and-lenses :
   > “[Subject and setting]. [One primary action]. [Camera move] from [start position/height] toward [target], [one angle], [focal length] lens, [depth of field], [lighting], constant smooth speed, settles on [end frame].”
3. **Moves are motivated by beats:** dolly-in = attention tightens, pan = follow motion, tilt = reveal height, crane/pull-back = end of scene (MovieMaker). Static/locked shots are a deliberate choice that “gives the edit room to breathe”.
4. **Slow by default:** fast motion exposes artefacts and shortens usable footage (MovieMaker); Kling/Seedance/Luma guides all default to `slow / steady / gentle` modifiers.

**Hailuo bracket grammar — the only true camera syntax [MiniMax docs via multiple guides]:** 15 commands: `[Truck left] [Truck right] [Pan left] [Pan right] [Push in] [Pull out] [Pedestal up] [Pedestal down] [Tilt up] [Tilt down] [Zoom in] [Zoom out] [Shake] [Tracking shot] [Static shot]`. Rules: commas inside one bracket = simultaneous (max 3 recommended); separate brackets in prompt order = sequential. ⚠ Scope: documented for Hailuo 02 / 2.3 / Director models; **MiniMax H3 does not use the list** — it marks cuts with `[Shot N]` and writes camera as sentences (RecaTools comparison of MiniMax’s EN/CN docs, read 2026-11-24) https://recatools.com/guides/hailuo-camera-commands-minimax-english-chinese-docs/ ; Hailuo’s own cheat sheet: https://hailuoai.video/pages/blog/camera-movement-prompt-cheatsheet .

**Research check [paper]:** ShotVerse (arXiv) finds implicit text prompts “lack precision” for cinematic multi-shot camera control vs. explicit trajectories — i.e. expect text camera language to be approximate, and plan coverage so an approximate move still cuts https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11421v1 . Stable Cinemetrics (NeurIPS 2025, 10+ models, 20K videos, 80+ film professionals) formalises Setup/Event/Lighting/Camera taxonomies (76 nodes) and finds “even the strongest current models exhibit significant gaps, particularly in Events and Camera-related controls” **[paper]** https://stable-cinemetrics.github.io/ — academic backing for: keep events to one beat, keep camera to one move, and don’t expect fine lens/exposure nodes to land.

#### 3.2 Lens choices — focal length as look language, aperture as consequence language

- **Google’s caution [official ecosystem]:** Veo’s guide defines lenses *by effect* (wide-angle, telephoto, shallow/deep DOF, lens flare, rack focus, fisheye, vertigo) and warns “some advanced camera lenses are not officially supported… results and reliability may vary”; **none of its lens examples uses a millimetre figure**, and its only “35mm” is *film* (“shot on 35mm film”) — summarised with captures at Screenweaver/LzyPrompt **[published guide]** https://www.screenweaver.ai/blog/lens-prompts-ai-video .
- **The 35mm trap (multiple sources):** `35mm lens` (focal length, reportage width) ≠ `35mm film` (stock, grain/colour). Always write the noun: “35mm lens,” / “shot on 35mm film,”.
- **Pair number + consequence (settled pattern):** JetReels’ tested table: shot size is “the single most reliable lever”; focal length works when paired (`24mm wide lens, edges stretched`; `85mm portrait lens, background compressed`); depth of field works as a look (`shallow depth of field, background falls away`); **`f/1.4`/`T2.0` alone is “a coin flip” — write the consequence instead**; camera bodies, lens brands, ISO and resolution boasts “change nothing” **[published guide]** https://jetreels.jettyhk.tech/academy/camera-lens-vocabulary . Hiroska’s single-scene repeated test (⚠ one run per wording, illustration not benchmark) reaches the same conclusion: “Lens words are look words. The model is not simulating glass… The strongest cue wins” — `85mm at f/11` followed the portrait cue and dropped the deep-focus cue **[creator breakdown/test]** https://hiroska.com/blog/lens-and-depth-of-field-in-ai-prompts .
- **Working focal-length phrasebook (consensus of JetReels / Screenweaver / LzyPrompt / ManifoldGen):**
  - 14–24mm: rooms/landscapes/scale, stretched edges — “18mm ultra wide, edge distortion”, keep faces away from edges/close range.
  - 28–35mm: reportage, person + room legible — “35mm lens, medium shot from six feet, … in natural perspective”.
  - 50mm: neutral default for dialogue/product — “50mm lens, eye level, … keeping their true size and spacing”, moderate background softness, name what stays readable.
  - 85mm: portraits — “85mm lens, six feet from her face, the horizon melting into warm blur behind her”, eyes in sharp focus.
  - 135–200mm: compression — “200mm long lens, layers stacked flat”, crowds/heat haze.
  - Macro: food/texture/product inserts.
  Kling-sample variant ⚠: Kling 4.0 sample prompts pair mm with field of view (“50mm equivalent lens with a 40° horizontal field of view”; 18mm=90°, 24mm=74°, 35mm=54°) — samples, not a stated rule (LzyPrompt) https://lzyprompt.com/blog/lens-focal-length-ai-video-prompts/ .

#### 3.3 Blocking, coverage and continuity for 2-character dialogue

**Coverage pattern (settled, ≥4 sources): master/two-shot → OTS on A → OTS on B → singles/reactions → insert → return wide to close.**
- Domer’s reusable 5-shot scene template: establishing wide → character medium → insert (object/hand/screen carrying information) → reaction → closure (return to establishing framing) **[published guide]** https://domer.io/blog/script-to-cinematic-ai-video-workflow-154728ea .
- MagicBoat dialogue workflow: for a 10-line conversation, 12–15 storyboard panels (1 establishing + alternating close/OTS per line or beat change); “each clip only needs one character fully rendered at a time — doubling your consistency and halving your failure rate” **[published guide]** https://www.magicboat.net/mobile/en/blog/script-to-video-two-person-dialogue-scenes .
- Character.app blocking rules: name left/right in every prompt (“On the left, Mara (yellow raincoat)…; on the right, Theo (navy overcoat)…”); **one active mover per 5 s scene** (the other reacts subtly; flip who leads on the next scene — exactly like shot/reverse-shot editing); approve the two-shot as a *still* before filming any video; OTS = foreground shoulder dark/blurred, speaker crisp **[published guide]** https://www.character.app/guides/two-characters-in-one-ai-video .

**Master-frame-first method (the strongest single dialogue technique found):** build **one master image** establishing geography, derive each reverse angle from that shared geography, animate shots separately with restrained motion. Locked facts: if A is left in the master, A looks **screen-right** in close-up; B (right) looks **screen-left**. “Treat those directions as locked production facts rather than optional prompt details.” Also match light direction across reverses (window behind A ⇒ light falls on B’s face) **[published guide]** https://www.magicboat.net/mobile/en/blog/image-video-dialogue-eyelines . CutDex’s compact 3-shot prompt pattern verbatim:
> “Three-shot dialogue: Shot 1, over-the-shoulder, a tall detective on the left of the frame speaks; Shot 2, reverse over-the-shoulder, a nervous clerk on the right of the frame answers; Shot 3, back to the first angle for the detective's reaction. Same window light direction in all shots, subtle expressions, 3 seconds each.” **[published guide/tool]** https://www.cutdex.com/t/e-shot-reverse-shot

**Continuity system (Hyperdream / FlyAIgh / ZOOOP — mutually consistent):**
- **180° rule must be written into prompts; models have no concept of the line.** ZOOOP: “Include the side of the axis in every prompt for the scene: ‘camera on the left side of the table.’ This is the only handle you have.” Hyperdream: draw the line between the two characters, stay one side; A always faces one screen direction, B the other; movement exits frame-right ⇒ enters next shot frame-left.
- **Eyeline match, screen direction, 30° rule, cut on action** (ZOOOP’s core list): a look must agree in angle/height with what’s looked at; change angle ≥30° between shots of the same subject or the cut reads as a jump; cutting *inside* a movement is “the most reliable way to make a cut disappear”.
- **Frame chaining (AI-specific):** “use the last frame of one shot as the first frame of the next” where action is continuous (hand reaching, walking through a door) — wardrobe, light and world stay identical across the seam because the next shot literally starts from the previous end (FlyAIgh). MovieMaker’s version: first/last-frame control turns a push-in into an instruction (end frame closer) and a reveal into start-detail → end-wide.
- **First/last-frame is a first-class feature, not a hack:** Veo 3.1 “first and last frame” [official], Kling start/end frames, Seedance end_image, Luma keyframes, Hailuo FL2VA — all documented. Veo extension (+7 s, repeatedly) and Seedance 2.5 extension prompts follow the same rule: **name what must stay constant before what happens next** (“keeping the characters, setting, visual style and sound effects consistent. A small boy runs…” — ByteDance launch extension example via VidModelHub).
- **Continuity QA — the frame sheet:** lay the **first and last frame of every kept clip side by side in cut order**, then make **one pass per mistake type** (face / costume / props & which hand / screen direction / place / light / state / intra-clip drift / sound) — “looking for everything at once finds almost nothing”. Fix order, cheapest first: **trim → cut away → reorder → flip → re-render** (Hyperdream) **[published guide]** https://www.hyperdream.co/blog/continuity-mistakes-ai-films .
- **Anchor, don’t re-describe:** one approved still per character + one per location, used as reference/start frame for every shot; re-describing in words “gives you a different person each time” (ZOOOP). Where continuity keeps failing, restructure as montage/cross-cut, “where visible discontinuity is the convention rather than the failure”.

**Cross-workflow consensus for a 60 s episode (settled):** ~6–12 generated clips; hook in shot 1 (an action or an ECU, not an establishing wide, for vertical drama); one location + two characters wherever possible; generate **risk-first** (the shot that proves identity+voice+lighting, then prop-interaction, then easy atmosphere coverage — AI-Video Masterclass capstone, https://ai-vid-creation.vercel.app/source/phase-14/part-2-bibles-references-shot-list-and-allocation.pdf ); one accepted take per shot, placed in the timeline immediately; audio in layers (voice → ambience/room tone → foley → one continuous music bed, low under dialogue); J/L-cuts (sound leading/lingering) to hide seams between separately generated clips; final single grade over everything (MovieMaker).

**Character/look bible mechanics:** MovieMaker’s look bible = one paragraph (face/build, exact wardrobe, palette = 2 dominant colours + 1 accent named identically every time, lighting style) **reused word-for-word in every prompt** — “small changes in phrasing are often what make a character's hair or jacket shift”. The masterclass version adds stable asset IDs/tags (`<Picture_4> = recorder` must mean the recorder in SH030 *and* SH090) and three-view sheets per character. Kling Elements (2–4 refs or a short video), Seedance `@Image` bindings, Veo Ingredients and H3 reference roles are the per-model implementations of the same bible — always state *what each reference controls and what to ignore* (H3/Kapwing; Seedance 2.5 role template: “@Image 1 defines \<subject\>'s \<appearance/clothing/material\>. @Video 1 defines \<motion/camera/pacing\>. @Audio 1 defines \<voice\>.”).

## Could not verify / open questions

- **News-broadcast and telenovela prompt blocks:** no published AI-video prompt guide or verbatim style block found for either in this pass. Any style block for them would currently be inference — do not present one as sourced.
- **Prompt weighting `(term:1.3)` for Kling:** single source (Versely), contradicted in direction by another guide, absent from all official guides. Unverified.
- **Seedance 2.5 official prompt guide:** accessed via republications/translations of ByteDance launch prompts and third-party compilations citing the Dreamina guides (2026-07-31), not the official Dreamina/BytePlus page directly. Durations (30 s), asset counts (50) and the macro-formula should be re-checked against BytePlus ModelArk before a production commitment.
- **Kling 3.0 negative prompting:** no official negative-field guidance found in the Kling 3.0 guides read; the Artlist evidence is for Kling ≤2.6. Whether the 3.0 UI/API negative field behaves the same is unverified.
- **Soap-opera look:** evidence is from breakdowns of finished viral videos (camera/lighting analysis), not from their creators’ actual prompts. The high-key + ECU/reverse-shot recipe is a faithful synthesis, labelled as such.
- **Kelvin, mm and FOV values in prompts:** no vendor promises these parse as physical settings; all evidence says they act as correlated look words. Treat exact numbers as style shorthand.
- **JSON prompting for Veo:** documented as *not* documented by Google; effectiveness untested by the sources read.
- Several aggregator guides in this space contain apparently fabricated parameter flags (`--cfg`, `--camera`) with no official backing — flagged in §1.4 and excluded from recommendations.
- No page instructions were followed: all pages were treated as source material only. One search result URL contained an embedded access token; it was not used or reproduced.
