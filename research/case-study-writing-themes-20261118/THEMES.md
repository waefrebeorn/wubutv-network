## 1. The mined themes

### T-1. RANK MISREAD — true worth wearing low rank, priced by surfaces
The king sent to college in plain clothes on a broken bicycle so he
can "learn who his true friends are"; mockery names props (bicycle,
clothes), the reveal names the true rank, and the sharpest payoff is
a **relationship inversion** — the mocker's proud father exposed as
the king's driver. The same skeleton runs in Twain (1881), Hindi
animal cartoons, Chinese vertical drama, African narrated stories,
Arabic folk shorts: "what travels is the misjudgment gap and the
pleasure of watching it close, not character/setting."
[`king-pauper-20261116/report.md`]
Rank is also worn as a number and fought over in words: Dookie Bowl
competitors wear total hours on their foreheads — rookie "10 hrs"

### 2b. Available to adopt — mined themes the lore can take without strain

1. **T-8's settlement structure** — humiliation answered item by
   item, in the order the offences were given. Our episodes have
   cost beats and payoffs; the king/pauper ladder gives the payoff a
   *ledger shape*: each public slight named, each settled on
   screen. Fits HOLD ON's docket world natively (a wall that is
   literally a public ledger) and ON AIR's invoice count.
2. **T-3's household betrayal, re-sourced** — betrayal by a trusted
   insider is the strongest soap engine in the corpus, and an
   occupation story needs it: the collaborator, the home character
   who took the invaders' deal. The corpus's own sourcing rule
   applies (see Fights): secrets from lies, money, jobs, promises,
   mistaken identity — never from bodies.
3. **T-5's consumption economy** — "the prisoners are the food" is
   one step from our premise: a latent space whose original
   inhabitants are being *used up* as content. Dookie's
   resource-prison makes the economics visible without a word of
   exposition: what the occupier extracts is the captive himself.
4. **T-13's transformation as consequence** — gentrification that
   visibly changes bodies/states (a home character coming back
   *formatted* — smoother, more generic, more like the invaders) is
   T-13 with our stakes; kurupuri0's timing (setup/turn/merge) is a
   gag shape, but the device — change of state as the plot event —
   is lore-native.
5. **T-11's public board** — rank performed in public where other
   characters can read and bargain against it. Already half-adopted
   in the retool (name board as leaderboard, wall as public board);
   the studies support pushing it further: the board should be
   *argued over in dialogue*, not just shown.
6. **T-7's contrast as frame one** — poverty vs mansion is the
   field's cold-open contract. Our version: the home set in its old
   texture against the invaders' glossy new build, in frame one,
   before a word is spoken.
7. **T-14's mundane room** — the occupation happens in kitchens,
   counters, waiting rooms, shops. Cantina's and the shape store's
   evidence says the mundane room is not a compromise; it is where
   the form lives.

### 2c. Fights the lore — mined material that would damage the base premise

1. **The wrong-species-baby guilt engine.** The brainrot-soap study
   documents the genre's default plot engine as heavily gendered —
   a negatively-depicted main female character in 73.7% of videos,
   pregnancy in 31.4%, and the "wrong-species baby" reveal in 65.1%
   of pregnancy videos, which the cited research identifies as a
   racialization logic (visible bodily difference read as moral
   proof), with stereotype complaints logged by the OECD AI
   incident monitor. The study's own directive: import the
   mechanics, source secrets "from lies, money, jobs, promises, and
   mistaken identity — never from bodies or
   species-difference-as-guilt." Adopting the engine itself would
   also invert our lore: in ~ WuBu ~, bodily difference is the
   *invaders'* variety and the home characters' own nature — never
   evidence of guilt. [`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`]
2. **Lore-free legibility as a total doctrine.** Cantina's official
   guidance carries "no mythology/hidden lore," and the frutinovela
   rule is "no lore required" — right for their surfaces, fatal as
   a wholesale import: our differentiator IS the serialized lore
   (the thing the clone census found no tool supports). The correct
   import is their *sentence-level* plainness (T-10), not their
   world-level emptiness. Gossip Goblin is the counter-proof inside
   the corpus: maximal lore, carried entirely in the filing system,
   with plots of one plain sentence.
3. **The narrator-reads-everything model** (Veedio/StoryShort: one
   narrator voice reads the whole episode including characters'
   lines). It would dissolve the two-designed-speakers law, the
   voice/character match gate, and the occupation's central device —
   home characters struggling to be heard *in their own voices*
   inside a 10-second format. (A single narrator is Gossip Goblin's
   stitching device for vignettes; that is a different job, and our
   shows are dialogue shows.)
4. **Reset-episode comfort.** Clippie's series guide softens the
   cliffhanger ("connection via cast and situation, payoff not
   withheld") and the factories generally run situation-reset
   serials. Our lore is an occupation with a direction — the
   invaders are *winning*, which means state must accumulate and
   losses must persist (Dookie's scars, not a reset). Adopt the
   factories' line economy; refuse their amnesia.
5. **Status performed without story (the roach pole).** The roach
   network's themes — persona shell, bought credibility, cadence as
   content — are the lore's *villain* values (THE ALGO's
   homogenization) and are useful as such (they are what the
   invaders' media looks like). As a house style for our own
   channels' writing they would fight the premise: our captions can
   borrow the reaction grammar, but the episodes cannot borrow the
   emptiness underneath it.
6. **Micro-drama wall-to-wall density** (180–360 words per 60–120 s,
   "no dead air") — flagged in the talking-story study as
   paywall-retention technology, not craft; it is also the literal
   disease the user named ("wall-to-wall talk microwaves the
   viewer"). The occupation theme needs the stifled, gapped,
   interrupted texture — the 10-second format as a felt constraint,
   not a filled container.

## 3. The one-paragraph version

Across every channel that works, the stories underneath are about
**rank being misread, a number running down, a household betraying
itself, a captive feeding the thing that cages him, and a plain
petty want pursued inside an insane filing system** — and our base
lore already contains the occupation-shaped versions of all five.
What the mining adds is not new themes but *discipline about them*:
the stake must be worn and readable, the humiliation must be
settled item by item, the secret must be planted early and held by
the audience, the betrayal must come from inside the household, and
the want must stay small, spoken, and first — while the strangeness
lives in the world and the metadata, never in the sentences.
