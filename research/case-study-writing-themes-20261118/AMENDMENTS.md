## A-1. Every harvested line also carries a GENRE MOVE tag

**(i)** Pass 3.2's move ledger gains a second, cross-checking label:
each line is tagged with which of the field's four line-types it
performs — ACCUSE-WITH-PROOF / DENY / ENTER-CONTRADICT /
REVEAL-BY-AUTHORITY — or NONE, and a NONE line is presumed an orphan
until defended at the Stranger Test.
**(ii)** "Only four line-types do all the work… A line that doesn't
accuse, conceal, expose, or decide does not exist in this genre"
[`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`]; the same four in the
five-beat sheet (hook/denial/evidence/turn) [`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`]
and THE_SAUCE §2. THE_STANDARD.md §2 already states the four moves;
DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.2's ten-move vocabulary (accuse / deny /
dodge / plead / offer / refuse / demand / expose / decide / react)
never reconciles with them — this amendment is the reconciliation,
not a second taxonomy: the ten moves describe intent, the four
describe the genre function the audience receives.
**(iii)** DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.2 (one added paragraph);
template §4.4 per-line field `line_genre_move` beside `line_move` /
`line_target`, added to the §6 grep contract.
**(iv)** At harvest, the dialogue writer tags both labels; at the
move-ledger review, any line tagged NONE is either re-bantered into
one of the four or explicitly defended in the brief (e.g. the one
licensed grievance/confession beat). The Stranger Test reader does
not see the tags (annotations stay stripped); the tags are checked
in the GATE_LOG after the test, against its Q2 scores.

## A-2. The proof-object rule: conflict is shown, then named — never reported

**(i)** Every accusation or claim that drives a unit must be carried
by a named physical proof object that is shown in the scene and
named in the dialogue; a conflict that exists only as one character
reporting another's off-screen act is a ledger-line risk and must
be re-bantered around an object.
**(ii)** "A proof object carries the conflict — phone with hearts,
pregnancy test, suitcase — shown, then named in dialogue. Conflict
is never reported secondhand" [`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`];
the five-beat sheet's Evidence beat is "a thing *shown* — never a
thing argued" [`slerm-their-shows-20261118/THE_SAUCE.md` §1;
`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`]. This is the direct
counter-mechanism to DIAGNOSIS D-1: a claim addressed to the record
cannot survive being forced onto an object in the room.
**(iii)** DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.1 (banter inputs) and Pass 3.4
(fact spend: the first spend of a driving fact is the object beat);
template: per-unit field `PROOF_OBJECT` in §4, added to the §6 grep
contract.
**(iv)** At storyline pass, each unit's driving claim gets its
object named in the brief before dialogue is drafted ("the tin",
"the letter", "the count board"). In the banter draft, the
accusation line must touch the object; if a draft's conflict can be
fully understood with the object removed, the unit goes back.

## A-3. Line shape at harvest: payload word last, names rationed

**(i)** Two drafting rules join the harvest step: a line ends on its
hard word (the payload word sits last, where the next line can grab
it), and in two-character scenes a character's name is spent only
for emphasis, accusation, or entrance.
**(ii)** Both are Poptoy's published line rules
[`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`]; the specimens obey them —
"…Strawberry." lands the payload as the final word of the exchange;
the Hook Studio episode spends names exactly at accusation and
entrance [`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`]. Payload-last is
also the mechanical precondition for echo-completion (A-1's twining
needs a grabbable final word).
**(iii)** DIALOGUE_METHOD.md: new short subsection **Pass 3.6 —
LINE SHAPE** at the end of §1 (drafting rules, explicitly *not* a
gate: the plainness pass does not repair toward them, per the
FORMAL REPAIR ban).
**(iv)** The dialogue writer applies both while harvesting from the
banter draft — the banter itself stays loose; shaping happens at
harvest, once, and is never a repair gradient afterward.

## A-4. Silence is a scripted beat: the REACTION entry

**(i)** The locked script may contain explicit REACTION beats —
gasp, pause, silent close-up — written as script lines with a named
carrier (face / object / room), carrying zero words against F-25
and counting as beats in the move ledger.
**(ii)** "Silence is written in as a line (gasp/pause is a script
beat)" [`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`]; the frutinovela
cliffhanger is "a freeze, not a fade: silent close-up reaction shot"
[`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`]; THE_STANDARD.md §5:
"Reaction (gasp) is the hanger." The talking-story study's measured
basis: a 2–3-s silence reads as meaning, and its digestion-gap
amendments already require gaps to declare a carrier
[`talking-story-creators-20261115/report.md` + `amendments.md` A2].
**(iii)** Template §4.4 SPEECH BLOCK: a `REACTION` entry type beside
dialogue entries (fields: carrier, duration, follows-line); noted
in DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.2 (a reaction is a `react` move with
no words, exempt from the repeated-beat rule when its carrier
changes).
**(iv)** When the banter draft produces a gasp/pause moment, the
writer scripts it instead of leaving it to the edit; the three
standing digestion gaps are written as REACTION entries with
carriers, so the gap exists in the locked script the gates read —
not only in the assembly plan.

## A-5. The audience secret is planted by end of U1

**(i)** Every episode names, at storyline stage, the one secret the
audience holds that the characters don't (or the reverse), and the
unit in which it is planted — no later than U1's close.
**(ii)** King/pauper's cold open: "The audience is told the secret
characters don't know — dramatic irony as retention"
[`king-pauper-20261116/report.md`]; THE_SAUCE §1: "audience holds
one secret by end of unit 1" [`slerm-their-shows-20261118/THE_SAUCE.md`];
Neural Viz's "smart = dramatic irony: the viewer knows more than
the expert" [`talking-story-creators-20261115/report.md`]. In lore
terms this is free: the audience already knows the invaders are
winning; the amendment makes each episode *plant its local version*
of that knowledge on screen.
**(iii)** Template §2 storyline block: field `AUDIENCE_SECRET`
(secret, holder, planted-in-unit); DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.4
note (the secret is a fact whose first spend is to the audience —
shown, not said).
**(iv)** The storyliner writes the secret into the storyline before
breakdown; Gate E's cold reader is asked, in addition to the
standing questions, "what do you know that they don't?" — a blank
answer means the plant failed, and the fix is a shown beat in U1,
never an explanatory line.

## A-6. A running brief per series

**(i)** Each show keeps a living RUNNING_BRIEF file — who knows
which secret, who is lying to whom, what the last hanger promised,
each character's current stake number — updated at every script
lock and read at every storyline pass.
**(ii)** Fruit-soap practice: "a running brief kept per series —
'who knows which secret, who is lying to whom, and what the last
hanger promised'" [`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`]. The
clone census's negative finding is the supporting evidence from the
other side: no factory tool maintains a structured cross-episode
continuity ledger — it is precisely the layer the field lacks
[`fruit-gen-clones-20261118/report.md` §7].
**(iii)** Series-level artifact beside the show bible (not the
episode template); DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.4 gains one sentence:
fact spend is checked against the running brief, so a fact cannot
be spent a third time by accident.
**(iv)** The continuity editor owns the file; at lock, they update
it in the same pass that writes the hanger ledger; a room session
that opens without reading it is out of order.

## A-7. Hanger interest, codified in the method

**(i)** Two serialization rules move from the sauce doc into the
method: a hanger may roll over only by paying interest in the same
episode, and the next episode's first beat pays the previous hanger
at least partly within its first ~5 seconds before opening a larger
debt.
**(ii)** "A hanger may be rolled over only by paying interest on it
in the same episode" — the lesson of the founding saga's unpaid
lemon-lawyer hanger [`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`];
Pickup 0–5 s exists solely to close the previous hook
[`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`, Microdrama Academy];
hanger-ledger law as stated in THE_SAUCE §3
[`slerm-their-shows-20261118/THE_SAUCE.md`]. DIALOGUE_METHOD.md
currently governs episodes one at a time; the hanger is where
episodes touch, so the rule belongs in the method, not only in
format docs.
**(iii)** DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.4 (new closing paragraph,
"HANGERS"); template §2 sign-off gains `HANGER_PAID` (previous
hanger, how this episode's beat one pays it) beside the existing
hanger-ledger practice from the retool plan.
**(iv)** At lock, the showrunner reads the previous episode's
hanger from the running brief (A-6) and writes the payment beat
before U1's dialogue is drafted; an episode whose U1 contains no
payment beat and no interest payment does not lock.

## A-8. The verdict line must pass the one-sentence moral test

**(i)** Pass 3.4's verdict restatement gains a checkable shape: the
verdict line must be speakable by a character in the scene as the
episode's moral in one plain sentence — what changed, said as a
judgment — without naming the episode's premise or restating U1.
**(ii)** King/pauper: "Moral spoken out loud at the end, in one
sentence; no branch trusts the viewer to extract it" — verbatim
specimen: "A person is not defined by their clothes and money, but
by their values and behavior" [`king-pauper-20261116/report.md`];
the verdict-restatement mechanism itself is already ours
(DIALOGUE_METHOD Pass 3.4, from [`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`]).
This amendment only gives the verdict a *testable form* so it
cannot decay into a bookend restatement (banned, §2 of the method)
or an aphorism pair (banned): a verdict that merely swaps U1's
outcome into U1's words fails this test by construction.
**(iii)** DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.4 (one added sentence + the
test); no new template field — the existing verdict line in the
SPEECH BLOCK is the object checked, and the Stranger Test's Q4
retell is where a failed verdict surfaces (the retell cannot
produce the moral).
**(iv)** At dialogue pass, the writer reads the verdict line alone
and asks: could this be the one sentence a viewer repeats? If it
needs the episode around it to parse, it is rewritten as a judgment
on the scene's people, in the verdict-speaker's voice.

## A-9. SPEECH_RULE gains a length trait

**(i)** Each character's SPEECH_RULE line gains a habitual length
trait (e.g. "answers in one-word bursts when cornered", "runs long
and volunteers dates when lying"), so line length itself is a
distinguishing, gate-visible character trait.
**(ii)** Cantina's editor makes Response Style (Short / Medium /
Long) a per-character setting, and its study's distilled rules
name "length is a character trait (Short/Medium/Long collision)" as
a distinguishability device [`cantina-study-20261115/report.md` §6;
`cantina-20261118/product_report.md`]. Poptoy's rules already imply
it (Lemon's one-line sarcasm vs Banana's over-explaining)
[`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`] — the amendment makes the
implication a field.
**(iii)** Template §1 cast card: `SPEECH_RULE` field extended with
a `length_trait` clause; DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.0 (one added
sentence).
**(iv)** At cast-card stage, the two designed speakers' length
traits must differ; at the plainness pass, a line wildly off its
speaker's trait is a flag for the reader's notes (not a repair
instruction — the exchange returns to banter only on a meaning
failure, per standing law).

## A-10. The banter draft is a transcript, not a list

**(i)** The BANTER_DRAFT artifact must be one continuous two-voice
transcript of the scene played in order — interruptions, wrong
answers, and overlaps in place — not a pool of candidate lines from
which the script is assembled.
**(ii)** Cantina's production shape: conversation comes first and
"conversation is the script mine" — users tap actual lines out of
real exchanges into video [`cantina-study-20261115/report.md`;
`cantina-20261118/product_report.md`]; THE_STANDARD.md §7's rule
"mined, not manufactured" [`dialogue-method-rebuild-20261118/THE_STANDARD.md`].
Pass 3.1 already presumes a ledger line is one "written directly to
the board"; a line pool is the board by another name — a transcript
is the only draft form in which a line's dependence on the previous
line can exist at all (DIAGNOSIS D-6: zero contact is the disease;
contact cannot be retrofitted onto pooled lines).
**(iii)** DIALOGUE_METHOD.md Pass 3.1 (the BANTER_DRAFT definition,
one tightened sentence); template §2 `BANTER_DRAFT` field
description.
**(iv)** The room produces the transcript by playing the scene —
two writers (or writer + reader) in role, from the cast cards and
speech rules only, no plot beats in hand. Harvest (Pass 3.2) then
selects; a harvested line's transcript line number is recorded in
the move ledger, making the "presumed ledger line" rule auditable.

## A-11. Caption & title grammar is declared per show, and the poster frame is a gate

**(i)** Each show declares a CAPTION_MODE in its bible — SERIAL
(factory pole: burned-in word-timed captions, series tag in the
first second, numbered parts under one rigid title pattern) or
POSTER (winner pole: near-empty captions, ALL-CAPS spoiler+moral
titles, poster frame carries the pitch) — and every episode passes
the EMPTY CAPTION test at Gate (f): with captions removed, frame
one must pitch the episode alone.
**(ii)** The two poles are both evidenced and unreconciled in the
studies (WRITINGS §20): factories burn captions in, always
[`fruit-gen-clones-20261118/report.md`; `brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`];
the biggest measured hit in the corpus runs empty captions and
empty descriptions, "the title is the entire funnel," poster frame
as the entire pitch [`lilypads/clipcraft7-20261118/report.md`];
overcookmyself proves the rigid-pattern pole: "a cold viewer can
reconstruct the whole series order from captions alone"
[`lilypads/overcookmyself-20261115/report.md`]; THE_SAUCE §7 already
names the EMPTY CAPTION test [`slerm-their-shows-20261118/THE_SAUCE.md`].
The amendment makes the choice *declared and per-show* instead of
accidental and per-episode.
**(iii)** Show bible (CAPTION_MODE declaration + the show's rigid
title pattern); template §5.4 beside Gate (f): `POSTER_FRAME` check
(frame-one still, pitch read by the cold reviewer, PASS/FAIL).
DIALOGUE_METHOD.md is untouched — this is packaging, and it is
filed here because msg 677 asks for the channels' *writings*, of
which titles/captions are the layer our episodes actually publish.
**(iv)** At bible stage the showrunner picks the mode and writes
the title pattern (e.g. `SHOW NAME — PART N: <spoiler + moral
hook>`); at production, titles are written from the pattern before
the master is gated, and the Gate (f) reviewer sees frame one with
no caption before seeing the episode.

## A-12. Social copy reacts; it never describes

**(i)** One-line platform captions on our own posts follow the
reaction grammar: react (laugh/gasp/point), quote a torn mid-scene
fragment, or bait a cheap self-revealing reply — never describe,
summarize, or hashtag-explain the episode.
**(ii)** The roach network's COMMON RECIPE: "Own posts never
describe; they react… The payload pitches alone"
[`lilypads/roach-network-20261118/NETWORK.md` §5]; the reaction
caption as signature device, zero description, zero hashtags
[`lilypads/imgoodvibez-20261118/report.md`]; the torn-line caption —
"the caption is a mid-scene fragment, never a title"
[`lilypads/roach-network-20261118/NETWORK.md`, node 4]; the open
question engineered for cheap self-revealing replies, 29% of the
aphorism account's sample [`lilypads/bossbee-20261118/report.md`].
The episode's last line is already required to be an open question
a viewer can type into a comment (THE_SAUCE §7) — the caption's job
is to point at it, not to repeat it.
**(iii)** Not DIALOGUE_METHOD.md: lands in the release/caption step
of the production template (delivery section) as `CAPTION_RULE`,
and in the release plan's caption guidance.
**(iv)** Whoever writes the post copy picks one of the three forms
(reaction / torn fragment / cheap question) and writes one line;
a caption that contains a plot summary or the words "in this
episode" is rewritten before posting.

## A-13. ⚠ FLAGGED — argues a change to ratified law's application (F-26 line economy)

**(i)** F-26's soap-register band of "8–14 words per line" should
be applied as a **ceiling range, not a target band**: the field's
working lines run far below it (specimen exchanges at 1–5 words per
line, one-word answers carrying maximum weight), so the room's
default expectation becomes *one idea per line, usually ≤12 words,
payload last, with short and one-word lines normal and licensed* —
while the F-25 unit caps (template v3: ≤20 words one speaker,
≤18 two speakers per 10-s unit) are untouched.
**(ii)** The specimens: the KreatorFlow smoothie scene runs "four
beats, three lines, eleven words of dialogue" *total*; the
question–answer lock exists precisely to let an answer be "absurdly
short while carrying maximum weight"; "…Strawberry." is a one-word
line that re-prices an entire scene [`fruit-soap-study2-20261116/report.md`].
THE_STANDARD.md §1 already records this ("working lengths far
below 8–14w: 1–5w specimens cited")
[`dialogue-method-rebuild-20261118/THE_STANDARD.md`], and the
brainrot study's 8–15-word figure is a *clip* target "so the
character has time to react" [`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`]
— i.e. the band describes the container, not the line.
**(iii)** Lands, if the parent ratifies, as a clarifying note in
the TASTE dialogue addendum against F-26, and as wording in
DIALOGUE_METHOD.md's new Pass 3.6 (A-3). **This file does not land
it** — F-26 is ratified E3 law; the argument and evidence are
presented for the parent's ratification decision, exactly as the
brief requires flagged changes to be.
**(iv)** If ratified, rooms apply it at harvest: a soap scene whose
lines all sit at 10–14 words is read as *over-full* and returns to
banter for compression toward move-sized lines; nothing about unit
totals changes.

## Checked against the evidence and HELD (no amendment)

- **The Stranger Test — held, and the evidence supports it.** Its
  Q4 gossip-retell is the field's own shape: Gossip Goblin's plots
  survive retelling because they are "one plain sentence each" with
  petty spoken wants [`lilypads/gossip-goblin-full-20261116/ESOTERIC_STYLE_GUIDE.md`];
  king/pauper's scenes survive translation because "the next line's
  function is predictable" from one visible argument
  [`king-pauper-20261116/report.md`]. No case study contains a
  stronger plausibility gate; several contain none at all.
- **Camp B — held.** The field generates one speaker per clip too
  (clone census Common Sauce #2; brainrot study's one-speaker
  default, two as ceiling). DIAGNOSIS D-6's "no contact" is a
  *writing* failure, and the field's fix is the same as ours:
  exchanges written as exchanges (A-10), assembled in the edit.
  Cantina's multi-voice Rooms are an improvisation surface, not a
  generation route for locked episodes.
- **F-25 caps / Container Law — held.** Field arithmetic converges
  rather than conflicts: ≤20 words per 8-s clip, 18–20 comfortable
  per 10-s clip [`talking-story-creators-20261115/report.md`;
  `brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`].
- **Ear-test / Gate (f) — held, and they are differentiators.** The
  clone census states the field has *no* ear-test: "captions QA'd
  against script, never master transcript read cold"
  [`fruit-gen-clones-20261118/report.md` §7]. Nothing mined argues
  for weakening the only gates that read the artefact the viewer
  receives.
- **End-state pin (F-22) — held, corroborated.** Cantina's scene
  anchors and CastWorkshop's anchor-approval exist "explicitly to
  limit visual drift" [`cantina-20261118/product_report.md`;
  `cantina-20261118/clones_report.md`]; Dr. Dreams' lock line —
  "Keep Patches, Gumby, and Casey exactly matching their reference
  sheets throughout" — is the same device in prompt form
  [`lilypads/drdreams-20261118/report.md`].
- **Want announcement ban — held, with the field's one nuance
  recorded.** The fruit-soap stratum *states* wants (character =
  name + look + voice + one want, legible in the first line
  [`brainrot-soap-study-20261115/report.md`]), but no specimen
  declares "I want X" *to the counterparty* — wants surface as
  offers, self-talk with a misreading, complaints, and haggling
  (THE_STANDARD.md §3). That is want carriage as the method already
  defines it; the ban stands.
