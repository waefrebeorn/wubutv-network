# THE NEW STANDARD — WRITING FOR TASTE (2026-11-19)

**Authority:** user order, Telegram msg 704 (2026-11-19): "complete
rework of writing… this writing is so bad no taste no meme no drip
no cuh no riss no giga." Diagnosis: `TASTE_DIAGNOSIS.md` (this
directory). This document replaces the **taste standard** — what
counts as good writing, and how a scene is constructed to get it.
The fidelity floors of the 2026-11-18 method survive, demoted and
named (§5); they certify that an episode is *correct*. Nothing in
them may ever again be cited as evidence that an episode is *good*.

**The one-sentence version:** write the character before the scene,
build the scene out of bits, protect the flavor at harvest, and
judge the result by whether a stranger can quote it back.

---

## 1. THE RIZZ CARD — charisma is written at card stage, or nowhere

No character speaks in an episode until their cast card carries
all six fields below. These replace SPEECH_RULE-as-prose (retired,
§4): a rule a reader cannot *hear* is not a voice rule.

1. **CARRIER WORDS** — one to three words or phrases only this
   character says: an address habit, a verbal tic, a pet word.
   (Field form: overcookmyself's "cuh"/"homie"; Old Apple's
   "child.") A carrier word must appear in the character's lines
   at least once per episode they speak in — it is how the
   audience's ear files them.
2. **CATCHPHRASE** — one repeatable line or line-shape that is
   theirs, designed to be quoted: a verdict formula, a sworn
   stamp, a counting ritual, a greeting. It is declared on the
   card *before* the episode, and the episode must give it one
   clean instance. A catchphrase arriving after the event that
   earns it is a signature, not a recitation (see §6, the
   signature exemption).
3. **THE BRAG** — what this character thinks is cool about
   themselves, statable in one line in their own voice. Charisma
   is a character's pride made audible; a character with no brag
   has no rizz to write. The brag need not be spoken every
   episode — but the room must know what it is, because half of
   what the character says is the brag wearing a situation.
4. **AUDIBLE TELL** — how they sound when they lie, boast, panic,
   or lose, in Poptoy form: a behavior of the *voice*, not of the
   plot ("over-explains when lying — volunteers witnesses nobody
   asked for"; "goes quiet and counts when she's sure"). The tell
   must be audible in a transcript with the names removed.
5. **SPECIMEN LINES** — three lines in the character's voice,
   written at card stage, mandatory and grep-checked (B-5 existed
   and was left empty on the v6 cards; that is now a card defect
   that blocks the room). Specimens are the harvest's reference
   grain: a harvested line that matches neither the tell nor the
   specimens is re-bantered in the speaker's mouth.
6. **REGISTER DEVIATION** — the show declares a vernacular (§3);
   the card states how this character leans on it or against it
   (the invader glossier than the street; the clerk drier than
   everyone). Two designed speakers must differ in at least three
   of the six fields, audibly.

**THE SHOW-OFF.** Every episode cast designates one SHOW-OFF —
the character licensed to play to the room: brag, strut, address
the audience's delight. Default: the invader (they arrived to be
looked at; that is the occupation in miniature). The show-off's
lines may carry the target class DELIGHT (§2.3) without rationing.
Every other character gets the one licensed grievance beat the old
method allowed; the show-off's license is a *mode*, not a quota.

## 2. Construction — the pass order, revised

Passes 3.0–3.6 of DIALOGUE_METHOD.md are replaced as follows.
Passes 1–2 (storyline, breakdown) gain one input each (§2.1).

### 2.1 Story input — the petty want and the status change

- **THE ONE-SENTENCE PETTY WANT.** Before breakdown, the episode's
  story is stated in one plain sentence naming a person who wants
  something *petty* from another person: money, a name on a board,
  revenge for a slight, a seat, a meal, to be believed, to not be
  laughed at. (London: "a man who can't feel anything visits his
  dealer"; "money first, cosmic horror second.") Procedures —
  claims, counts, dockets, tests — are the world's filing system
  and may carry the plot's mechanics; they may never *be* the
  want. If the one sentence names a process instead of a desire,
  the episode is not ready to break down.
- **THE STATUS CHANGE.** The value-change pair (ST-1, kept) must
  be a change in *standing between people*, from the field's
  table: Humiliated → Striking back · Suspected → Trusted ·
  Trusting → Betrayed · Winning → Losing on a new condition ·
  Unaware → Holding a secret. A record correction (claim denied →
  claim granted) is not a status change. Someone must be able to
  **lose face** in every episode; name who, at storyline stage.

### 2.2 Banter first — kept, with the flavor locked in

Pass 3.1 stands (one continuous two-voice transcript; proof object
assigned before play; withheld line named). The banter evidence
(T-1) is that the draft is where the flavor lives. Two additions:

- **Play the show-off hot.** In the banter draft, the show-off is
  played at full strut — brags, name-spam, witness-invoking,
  playing to the room. The draft is the one place excess is free;
  a banter draft with no line the room would be embarrassed to
  lock is under-played and says why in the GATE_LOG.
- **The liar's tell is played, not implied.** Whoever lies in the
  scene lies *in their tell* during the draft (over-explanation,
  too-specific detail, volunteered witnesses), so the harvest has
  the tell on the page to protect.

### 2.3 The move ledger — amended

The ledger keeps its moves (accuse / deny / dodge / plead / offer
/ refuse / demand / expose / decide / react) and gains:

- **Target class DELIGHT** — a line aimed at the audience's
  delight (brag, strut, catchphrase instance, steal-the-insult).
  DELIGHT lines are legal for the show-off without ration, and
  for any character as the episode's one licensed grievance beat.
  The old rule — *a line whose target is the room or the audience
  is cut* — is **retired** (TASTE_DIAGNOSIS T-4: it banned
  charisma by construction).
- **FLAVOR TAG** per line, beside the move: CATCHPHRASE / TELL /
  BRAG / LADDER / INSULT / CARRIER / NONE. A line tagged NONE on
  both genre-move and flavor is presumed an orphan until defended
  (the A-1 cross-check, widened). An episode whose harvested lines
  carry no CATCHPHRASE, TELL, or BRAG tag at all is flavor-empty
  by construction and returns to banter before any gate runs.

### 2.4 THE BIT — scenes are built from named mechanisms

Each unit declares its **BIT**: the repeatable comic/dramatic
mechanism the unit runs. A bit has a setup and a payoff *inside
the scene*. The standing menu (rooms may add, with specimens):

- **THE COUNT** — accusation or verdict delivered as arithmetic
  the victim must watch being done (counting the tin, the coins,
  the bells).
- **THE SWORN STACK** — a liar escalates a claim in sworn layers
  (claim → detail → volunteered witnesses), each layer more
  convictable than the last.
- **THE TOO-SPECIFIC DENIAL** — the denial with more detail than
  the truth would need; the audience hears the lie first.
- **THE ENTRANCE RE-PRICE** — a third party enters with a line
  that re-prices everything just said.
- **THE LADDER** — a repeated call or word escalating in scope
  ("Mama." "Help." "Somebody." "Anybody."), each rung a beat.
- **THE KEY WORD LOUDER** — the licensed repetition: the core
  word said again, escalated, never rephrased ("His FAVOURITE.").
- **STEAL THE INSULT** — the strongest attack on the character or
  the show, placed in a mouth inside the scene, as a joke.
- **THE CONFESSION** — the grievance delivered to camera, in full
  sentences, restating the core fact as a complaint.
- **THE VERSUS** — two claims stated as a call-and-response the
  audience can score (numbers, counts, ranks).

A unit with no bit is a procedure. Procedures are pictured, not
spoken: if a unit's work is purely procedural, its dialogue
budget moves to the units that carry bits.

### 2.5 The harvest — KEEP LIST first, compression second

"Draft double, cut half" is **retired** as a global ratio (T-1:
the ratio was executed against the flavor). The harvest now runs:

1. **THE KEEP LIST.** Before any compression, the room marks the
   banter draft's flavor carriers: catchphrase candidates, the
   tell in action, the brag, the insult, carrier-word lines, the
   ladder's rungs. Keep-list lines are harvested FIRST and may
   not be cut for length; if the unit is over budget, compression
   falls on connective and procedural lines, and if it still
   doesn't fit, the *bit* is re-played shorter — the tell is
   never what gets trimmed.
2. **Compression targets plot, not voice.** A liar's
   over-explanation may be trimmed only to the shortest form in
   which the tell is still audible (surplus witnesses ≥1,
   surplus detail ≥1). A claim stripped to its fact has been
   harvested wrong.
3. Payload-last, names rationed, one idea per line (Pass 3.6)
   stand — they shape lines; they do not select them.

### 2.6 Fact spend, hangers, reactions — kept

Fact spend (twice, different words, verdict restatement), the
audience secret, hanger interest and the swap test, scripted
REACTION beats, proof objects, the withheld line: all stand
unchanged. They were never the flavor problem.

## 3. REGISTER — every show declares its vernacular

At bible stage, each show names its **REGISTER**: the specific
speech-world its dialogue lives in (e.g. dry municipal deadpan;
fruit-soap gloss over street slang; mock-formal minutes-speak).
The register names: the home idiom's sources, its two or three
carrier words at show level, and its temperature (deadpan against
epic visuals is the corpus's proven setting). Series flavor arc,
from the base lore: in an occupied latent space, the invaders'
register *infects* the home cast across a season — home characters
picking up invader slang is the gentrification made audible, and
the rooms should spend it deliberately, one borrowed word at a
time, never explained.

## 4. What is RETIRED (named, with reasons)

1. **The Stranger Test as headline gate** → demoted to a floor
   check (§5). *Reason:* its Q3 mechanic cannot distinguish a dead
   maxim from a live signature and executed both (T-2); three
   repair rounds under it produced a monotone flavor-loss curve
   (T-0). It remains a valid *sense* instrument. It is not a taste
   instrument and may no longer be the gate a script is said to
   have "passed."
2. **The audience-target cut rule** (Pass 3.2) → retired.
   *Reason:* T-4 — it banned charisma by construction.
3. **"Draft double, cut half" as the harvest law** → replaced by
   the keep-list harvest (§2.5). *Reason:* T-1 — the ratio was
   executed against the flavor carriers, which are surplus by
   definition.
4. **SPEECH_RULE as behavioral prose** → replaced by the RIZZ
   CARD (§1). *Reason:* T-3 — tactics are not sound; the v6 cards
   carried no carrier word, no catchphrase, no specimens.
5. **Unlimited plausibility repair rounds** → capped at ONE
   repair round under the floor check at script stage; further
   flags are adjudicated by the showrunner in writing (as the
   parent's R3 rulings did for v6), not by another re-banter.
   *Reason:* T-0 — iterated cold-reader repair is the gradient
   itself; each round must now justify what it *keeps*, not only
   what it removes.
6. **Genre-move NONE = presumed orphan, alone** → widened (§2.3):
   a line may stand on a FLAVOR tag. *Reason:* the quotable beat
   is "almost always a repetition, a one-word answer, or a
   verdict line" — shapes the four genre moves do not name.

## 5. What survives — the demoted floors

These stand, as **floor checks**: they certify correctness, and a
script's record must cite them as floors, never as quality.

- **F-25 spoken-load caps + Container Law** — arithmetic.
- **Camp B** (one speaker per unit; exchanges written as
  exchanges in the banter draft).
- **Ear-test (F-13)** — delivery fidelity of the finished master.
- **F-9 story gate + Gate E** — story sense on transcript +
  pictures.
- **Gate (f) cold run** — comprehension.
- **THE STRANGER TEST, as FLOOR CHECK** — one run at script stage
  (plausibility: would a person say this *to that person, there*),
  one at master stage. With two amendments:
  - **THE SIGNATURE EXEMPTION.** A line that is a character's
    declared CATCHPHRASE or carrier construction, arriving after
    the event that earns it, is not a STAGE line. The reader marks
    it SIGNATURE and moves on. A maxim that states a world-rule
    *before* the event that teaches it remains STAGE (D-4 stands).
  - **THE REPAIR CAP.** One repair round; a repair may not remove
    a declared flavor carrier (keep-list line, catchphrase,
    tell) without a written showrunner ruling in the GATE_LOG
    naming what the removal buys.

## 6. THE TASTE TEST — THE QUOTE-BACK TEST (the headline gate)

Run twice: on the locked script before production, and on the
finished master's transcript beside Gate (f). The reader did not
write the dialogue, has not read the brief, and gets the dialogue
only, in order, read aloud once. Then, **without looking back**,
the reader must produce three things:

1. **QUOTE BACK.** One line, verbatim or near-verbatim, from
   memory. *If no line can be quoted back, the episode FAILS* —
   nothing in it can travel, be repeated, or be memed; there is
   nothing for a viewer to carry out of the room.
2. **NAME THE SHOW-OFF.** Which character would you follow into
   another episode — and what is their verbal signature
   (catchphrase, carrier word, or tell, named in the reader's own
   words)? *If the reader cannot name a character and a
   signature, the episode FAILS* — it has no charisma carrier.
3. **GOSSIP WITH AN ATTITUDE.** Retell the scene as gossip in two
   sentences, the way you'd tell a friend — including the
   judgment you'd actually pass on the people in it (petty,
   shameless, obsessed, due). *If the material gives the retell
   no attitude to take — if it comes out as a neutral summary —
   the episode FAILS* — the story was a procedure, not a piece of
   gossip, and gossip is the genre's native transmission form.

**PASS requires all three.** A FAIL is repaired at the level that
failed — quote-back: the episode's QUOTABLE is (re)built (§2.4
bits; a catchphrase instance or ladder that lands); show-off: the
cast's RIZZ CARDs and the show-off's license, not the lines;
attitude: the story input (§2.1 — petty want, status change,
someone who can lose face). A taste FAIL is never repaired by
polishing the lines that were read; flavor is a construction
property, and construction is where it is fixed.

Beside the test, two standing production notes: every episode
declares its **QUOTABLE** (the line designed to be repeated —
catchphrase instance, verdict with a twist word, ladder rung) in
the brief, and its **STANDALONE BEAT** (the frame + line that
could be posted alone as a reaction — the torn-line test). The
social caption is written from the quotable, reacting, never
describing (A-12, kept).

*End of standard.*
