## SHE TOOK MY FACE (fruit-soap body-ledger serial)

1. **Win by re-aiming the contest, not by winning it** (0xfrosteeze). The victim should not try to out-perform the thief at being her — she forces the thief into the one contest the stolen face cannot stop playing: a mirror, a live camera, the face's original owner watching — a recognition loop the thief must keep winning forever, frozen in public while life resumes around her.
2. **The challenge that audits the fraud** (bajablstdeeznut). Nobody accuses the thief of wearing a stolen face — the victim (or a rival) challenges her to the one thing only the real face's owner can do (the signature expression, the family photo pose, the mother's recognition test), in public. And the audit must also endanger the auditor (the bar is too heavy for the challenger as well), so the test is a contest both can lose, not a clean execution.
3. **The stolen feature CRACKS on the thief** (clipcraft7, from Sweet Revenge Parts 3–7). Each episode one new crack crosses the stolen smile, readable in any frame; a stolen feature decays on the wrong body at a fixed rate, one visible stage per episode. The victim doesn't need to accuse — the crack is the accusation, a positive picture.
4. **The foundation fake** (clipcraft7, forehead-price story — ASR line: "A little foundation, a million on my forehead and I'm ready"). For the wedding photo, the thief paints a smile on the victim's mouthless face so the photo "works" — painted, not stolen. In the photo flash it holds; in the next unit it smears down her chin when she cries/laughs. A forged feature that fails in public is the cheapest possible proof shot.
5. **The tally under the makeup** (clipcraft7). Each theft leaves a small number on the thief's skin — 1, 2, 3 — which she covers with foundation every morning (shown once, in a mirror, as routine). The number sweats through during the song she stole the voice for. The victim's collection line: she doesn't argue, she wipes the thief's forehead with one thumb.
6. **Proof by performance, staged as a tasting** (clipcraft7, the donut heir verdict). The collection scene: the victim makes the thief USE the stolen feature in front of the family — sing the song with the stolen voice. Mid-line, the voice comes out in the victim's child-register / the crack crosses the smile; the table sees it happen. No deduction, no testimony: the feature misbehaves on the wrong body, in public, on demand.
7. **The seam** (clipcraft7, Sweet Revenge's knife beats). Both women carry the same faint seam line where the smile was cut. The victim holds a small knife to her own seam in the mirror — not cutting, measuring. Final-episode collection: the thief, face fully cracked, peels the smile off herself and holds it out — the peel comes off in one piece, one gesture, no gore, the whole serial in a single picture.
8. **The fraud's profit written on her own body** (diesel_lord, secondary rip — the gold-teeth close-up). The thief's stolen beauty should leave one part of her own body visibly wrong that no stolen face covers.
9. **Identity replacement taught as a training montage with a performed test line** (gavi6ista). The face-thief is shown being coached into the victim's face — practising her smile, her signature line ("read it 1000 times") — and then *examined* on the stolen identity, the exam line being the same line the episode opened on. Cold open: the thief already performing the victim's life perfectly; the season's question is "how did she get her face?"
10. **The asymmetric-fates engine** (hebertysales). After a theft, cut between the thief thriving (her photos, her glow) and the victim serving time in the diminished body — and let the victim's *preparation to collect* be bodily and visible (he practises smiling in a mirror with a stolen photograph; the face he is building toward is the episode's clock).
11. **The defect named in the first scene, with a built-in timer** (miee711). Give the face-theft a printed expiry in episode 1 (the stolen face "sets" permanently at a named date/event — a wedding, a photo day, a contract signing) so every episode is the victim racing a deadline the audience can see — "Only after they turn 18" turns a static injustice into a countdown every cruelty also ticks.
12. **The belly-clock, translated** (mviews3). The stolen feature *grows* on the thief across the episode (her borrowed smile gets wider, brighter, more photographed) while the victim's face visibly flattens — two body states moving in opposite directions, readable in any frame. Costume constant: the victim keeps ONE unstealable marker (her red scarf) in every frame, so the audience never loses her even faceless.
13. **The theft proven by playing the sale back on a screen** (shahzaibazhar08). The horn IS a stolen face feature: steal it, wear it, sell it, then have the theft proven by playing the sale back on a screen in the room — the wedding photographer's slideshow runs at the party and one frame shows the mouth mid-transfer. The victim's line is one demand — "Give me back my smile" — answered only by tears and the feature visibly moving.
14. **The sabotage escalation / the container rule** (thatchaosgirl9). The stolen feature is carried visibly ON the thief (she wears his mouth over hers). At the wedding she is fed — through his stolen mouth — the one dish he is famous for hating; *his* gag reflex fires in *her* face, in public, in front of the table. Container rule: what is put INTO a stolen feature damages the thief, not the owner — so the victim can fight back by feeding his own face.
15. **The lie-ledger** (zombieonearth_). Every lie the thief tells *wearing his stolen face* adds one more of HIS features to her face, permanently. Her face becomes the public count of her lies; the only way to stop growing his features is to confess in the stolen voice — which spends it.

## THE BIG JAR (fill-level race)

1. **Paint the running count ON the jar** (clipcraft7 — the worn number, moved to the set). After each unit, the fair judge paints the current count in big numerals on the glass itself. Any frame then reads both numbers — the FAIR DAY target line and the tally — exactly as any frame of the forehead stories reads a person's worth. The jar wears its number.
2. **The painted fake fill line** (clipcraft7 — the foundation fake, set version). The cheat escalates from re-using the tile: at night he paints a false fill line above the real fill on his side of the glass. Proof is a positive picture: rain washes the paint down the glass in streaks in front of both households — or the real fill rises past the fake line and the paint floats on top of the contents. The jar itself testifies; nobody has to make a speech.
3. **Round banners over the jar** (clipcraft7 — the challenge arena's printed rules). The fair judge's booth hangs a new banner each unit naming the round's class — "ROUND TWO: HEAVY THINGS" — so the escalation (tiles → buttons → dinner → chair → key) is printed in the world above the jar. The banner change is also the unit's opening frame: cold viewers onboard from the banner + the painted count + the two emptier houses, no recap needed.
4. **The defect is the weapon, finale inversion** (clipcraft7 — bad-breath / 0-of-100 engine). The tall neighbour must carry the full jar home on his head **through his own front door** — and his house is still full, the doorway blocked by everything he refused to give. The short neighbour's emptied house has the only clear doorway on the street. Full house = trap, empty house = road. *(Framed as ammunition for after the user's verdict on the E1 plain story — the draft itself is untouched.)*
5. **Loser's tools left on the jar** (clipcraft7, kept from Tier 1, now reinforced). What each neighbour fails to get in stays stuck on the glass where everyone can count it. Failure stays visible and countable, like the tally — nothing is ever erased between episodes.
6. **The punishment built out of the fraud's own material** (diesel_lord). The cheating neighbour's penalty should be physically built from his cheat — he must carry home, wear, or be buried under the very counterfeit goods he slipped into the jar, so the final frame is a positive picture of the fraud converted into its own sentence (the charlatan hanged from a gallows covered in the gold leaf he faked).
7. **Print the law in the world, from frame 1** (robbercat_sol + zombieonearth_ — near-identical items merged). Hang the terms over the jar as a painted banner — "FIRST TO THE LINE KEEPS THE JAR — LOSER CARRIES IT HOME" (robbercat_sol) — or carve the law on the jar's stand: "A THING PUT IN TWICE BELONGS TO THE OTHER MAN" (zombieonearth_). The fair's rule printed in the world, never spoken by a narrator; every later argument is the neighbours pointing at the banner, and the cheat beat becomes a verdict with no dialogue spent on it.
8. **The loser's victory condition** (0xfrosteeze, secondary rip). The neighbour who cannot win the fill race can still win by making the race never legally end.
9. **The ten-year contract** (creativestorytv, secondary rip). A parent states the rule, the term and the payoff day in the first scene, and the finale is simply the contract being honoured "to the letter, in public." A jar filled under a spoken contract with a named fair-day deadline inherits all of this.
10. **The gifted-sword beat, inverted** (g55628830). A neighbour "helps" by giving the short one a ladder — and the ladder is what lets the tall one steal at night. Help as the cheat's tool, shown in one handover.
11. **Stakes as spoken wages** (gavi6ista, secondary rip). A number said aloud that ladders ($15 → $50) is the cheapest legible scoreboard there is.
12. **The felled-tree rule for the cheat's comeuppance** (hebertysales). The short neighbour doesn't accuse twice; he *prepares a picture* — the marked tile laid across the tall neighbour's doorstep, so the tall one trips over his own cheat in front of the street.
13. **The daily measured distance** (justblox12, secondary rip). A recurring numeric check-in ("10 miles behind us") converts dread into a number the audience tracks episode to episode.
14. **The two-tools rule** (khalidoykq). Give each neighbour a signature vessel (the tall one's bucket, the short one's hat). What each vessel delivers into the jar is that man's character — the bucket brings roof tiles in bulk, the hat brings one button at a time, honestly.
15. **The disposal-then-jackpot intercut** (miee711, secondary rip). The family throws the stained one away and goes bankrupt in the same breath he wins; the two ledgers (theirs emptying, his filling) intercut in the finale. That intercut is exactly THE BIG JAR's jar-vs-household economy and should be stolen for its finales.
16. **Steal the weight physics for the loser's carry** (piper_mendoza5). As the jar fills, rehearse the price on his body each unit: unit 2 he lifts the jar easily for a joke; unit 4 the same lift cracks the step under him; unit 6 the real carry takes him to his knees before he rises. The jar's weight is read off posture, never off a spoken number.
17. **Wear the count** (stellan628605). Chalk the running fill number on each neighbour's chest, updated in frame whenever something goes in. The cheat beat becomes arithmetic fraud the audience catches before the short neighbour does — his chest says 40, the jar says 38, and two tiles are unaccounted for.
18. **Carry the contributions on their heads + the painted stone** (thatchaosgirl9). Each neighbour walks his offerings to the jar in a bowl on his head (roof tiles in a bowl, buttons in a bowl), so the cost of every trip is worn. Sabotage mirror: the tall neighbour slips a painted stone into the short neighbour's bowl; the stone is later found *inside the jar* and held up in an open hand — the accusation is a thing, not a speech. Also (shahzaibazhar08): when the tall neighbour is caught re-using the tile, he doesn't argue; he opens something — payment/confession as an opened container in frame, not a speech. And (zartashw3): give the short neighbour a sprained wrist in unit 1 (hurt lifting his first contribution) that *never heals* — every later contribution is one-handed, winced, slower, while the tall neighbour's cheating is two-handed and easy; effort asymmetry readable in any frame, the viewer's sympathy does the judging.

## NO TALKING AT THE TABLE (zero-word pantomime)

1. **The anthology of won encounters** (ericdushim94276). Structure episodes as a ladder of silent won encounters around one table claim (the smallest character out-sits, out-waits, out-eats every challenger), each round closed by a tableau — the loser's prop (hat, ring, wheel) ending up on the winner. Repetition-with-variation *is* the entertainment; no plot needed.
2. **The body as a thermometer** (fatosgngr35). Give one regular a literal visible gradient that tracks the season's plot — a character who slowly changes colour/coverage (sauce stains that never wash out, a tan line, flour whitening) in proportion to how much they've lost at the table, so any paused frame audits the whole feud.
3. **The withheld impact** (fightmovie41). Build each table confrontation as prop-escalation (each character answers by producing a bigger object, never a word) and cut the actual "hit" — cut from raised prop to the consequence tableau (the loser flat, the winner eating). Secondary rip: the **glasses transformation** — a silent character putting on one accessory to announce "now I am serious" is a zero-dialogue character beat the pantomime cast can use verbatim.
4. **The marked-body ending** (g55628830). The loser of the chair war leaves wearing the meal — a drumstick bone stuck in his hair, gravy handprint on his shirt. Wordless, and the audience reads the whole episode off his body in the final frame.
5. **The material transfer rule** (mviews3). 757K views with zero lines proves the slate's wordless bet is sound at scale. In the chair war, every act of war should be an object changing state on camera (a leg sawn shorter, a cushion deflating, a plate swapped) — never a gesture alone.
6. **The escort beat as a chair-war move** (nadeemjuttkojh). The winner solicitously pulls out the loser's chair, holds it, bows — and carries it out of the room. Politeness as the weapon, all mime, one unbroken shot. The deadpan salute becomes the winner's look to camera while the loser is still standing there holding his plate.
7. **The rows-of-product ledger** (robbercat_sol). When the feast is the stake, lay the food in counted rows so a stolen drumstick leaves a visible gap in the line. Theft readable as a missing tooth.
8. **Object-head casting for one invader guest** (thatchaosgirl9). His head is the dish he brought, and he keeps eating from his own head-dish under the table — visible in any frame, zero words; the table's rule (no talking) plus his rule (no sharing) collide without a single line.
9. **The vignette engine / kitchen grammar** (yuisansuk456). One course per unit, each course a whole visible process (the soup arrives as ingredients and leaves as steam), the same pair, costume constants, no words. Every unit can onboard from a raw ingredient in frame 1.
10. **A bodily, cumulative speech cost** (zombieonearth_). Every word spoken at the table costs the speaker one visible chair-inch, sawn off by the winner on camera in the same unit — a speech-cost rule needs zero explanation when the cost accrues on the body.

## ONE MILLION PETALS (arena / prize show)

1. **The mirror confession opener** (bajablstdeeznut, secondary rip). Hero alone, putting ON the fraud, telling the camera exactly what the lie is — the fastest honest cold-open in this batch.
2. **The in-world screen** (boss_bee_). Put the tally board on a TV in the seller's shop window, the number climbing behind glass while she begs outside it. The world advertises her debt back at her.
3. **The rigged audit and the unforgeable one** (creativestorytv). Let the hero be eliminated once by a visibly rigged count (petals weighed on the judge's own scale), and build the finale as the same test moved to a scale the whole town watches — the victory must be a measurement the audience trusts more than the earlier one, not just a bigger number.
4. **THE TOUCH TEST is the seller engine, ready-made** (khalidoykq). Every seller's petals get one touch in her palm, on camera — real petals stay, fake petals melt/wilt. One action, re-spent per unit, each touch a verdict: "proof is a positive picture" with a mechanic attached.
5. **The intercut bed** (mviews3). One warm shot per episode of the invader inside her chained garden, redecorating in lamplight, while she pays for petals in the cold street outside. The whole gentrification texture in a single contrast cut, never explained.
6. **The gold-heavy sack** (piper_mendoza5). One seller's petals are gold-heavy: her sack tears at the same stitch in every unit, and the tear is her tally — she mends it smaller each time, so the sack visibly shrinks as her fortune grows; in the finale she carries the last petals in her hat because the sack finally died. Cost and profit on one prop.
7. **The gold bar in the paw** (shahzaibazhar08). A seller holds the actual payment up to camera before the deal, so the audience prices the scam before she does.
8. **The count ON her, answered per move** (stellan628605 — "the biggest single rip in this group"). The number lives over the *seller's* head too (his price, counting down as she haggles), and every exchange is answered by a number moving on screen — haggling becomes arithmetic the audience can referee. Add diminishing returns: the same plea ("for the garden") buys fewer petals each time she uses it, forcing a new move per unit.
9. **The bookend rhyme for the finale** (zartashw3). Her last exit re-shoots episode frame 1 exactly — same chained garden gate, same camera — with the gate open, the sack empty, and her hands empty too. The before/after pair is the payoff picture; nothing needs to be said over it.
10. **The rules carved in stone** (0xfrosteeze, secondary rip). Print the contest's three rules as a physical object in the arena in episode 1, so the finale's loophole is fair — the audience read the same stone. (The same study's endless-contest engine — trap the never-losing creature in a contest that cannot end — is banked where the study assigns it: SHE TOOK MY FACE #1.)

## MY LANDLORD IS A BABY

1. **The posture ledger, wholesale** (boss_bee_). The tenant kneels to plead, the baby stands on the table above him. Rent pressure readable from who is higher in frame, before a single narration sentence.
2. **The prize wheel as a set piece** (ericdushim94276, secondary rip). A wheel in the hallway that the baby spins to decide this week's rent, its segments printed in the world (TOYS / NAP / DOUBLE MILK / EVICTION), converts an abstract power relation into a physical object the whole building gathers around.
3. **The question caption as the engagement engine** (fatosgngr35, secondary rip). End episodes on a verdict the comments must deliver ("do you think this is true love"), with the picture rigged so both answers are defensible.
4. **The WANTED poster** (hebertysales). Print the debt in the world: a "RENT DUE" notice on the tenant's door that grows bigger each episode — paper as the stake, readable in frame 1.
5. **A whole building organised around one hidden person's secret** (justblox12). Every tenant's absurd living arrangement (rent paid in toys, stairs replaced by slides, quiet hours at nap time) should trace back to one concealed fact about the baby landlord that a tenant eventually *tests* rather than is told. The proof method: the hero learns the truth by running an experiment the world said was fatal (standing still) and surviving it — revelation as a stunt the audience watches, never as an explanation scene.
6. **The lightbulb beat, rationed** (khalidoykq). The baby gets one idea per episode, drawn as a literal bulb over his head, and the idea is always an object he then fetches. The want made visible before the act.
7. **One rent coin impossibly heavy — for the tenant only** (piper_mendoza5). He drags it across the floor and it trenches the rug; the baby landlord picks the same coin up with two fingers and drops it in the glass. Power difference as physics, readable in any frame, no line spent.
8. **The interrogation shape for the one narration exception** (robbercat_sol). Instead of narrating *at* the viewer, let an off-camera neighbour ask the tenant short questions ("Why is a baby at your door?" — "He's come for the rent."), each answer exactly what the frame shows. Same plain-sentence law, but the lines are moves in a conversation, not captions.
9. **The diminishing-returns law for the baby's tricks** (stellan628605). The same tantrum wins less rent relief each episode (first time the tenant forgives a coin, second time half a coin), which is what forces the baby to escalate to bigger objects. A serial engine with a rule, not just a habit.
10. **The tenant's second-job montage** (zartashw3). To make one rent payment he works three jobs in one unit-chain (dish pit, night desk, roof repair), and in *every* frame the rent book rides on his back like a backpack — the debt is worn through all of it. Final crack in the landlord: in the last job the baby rides in the book's pocket, and when the tenant stumbles, the baby drops a coin of his own into the glass.
11. **The one-word law, stolen whole** (zombieonearth_). In the tenant's building, every "no" spoken to the landlord makes the *baby grow one size and the tenant's room shrink one size* (door frame pencil marks prove both). The tenant's yes-montage (handing over chair, lamp, kettle) mirrors the neck hero's homework/lunch/house-cleaning chain. Finale beat: the tenant finally says "No" to the last demand, pays the visible price (room now cupboard-sized, baby now huge) — and smiles: first honest word in the building. (The same study's companion forms for the other shows are banked at SHE TOOK MY FACE #15 and NO TALKING #10.)

## Cross-show devices (no single show named, or several)

1. **The counted-rows-of-product ledger** (robbercat_sol, found independently in yuisansuk456's kitchen grammar). Counted rows make any theft or payment readable as a gap — banked under NO TALKING #7 and #9, but the device is slate-wide: any show whose stake is countable stuff (petals, tiles, coins, courses) should lay it in rows.
2. **Printed law as set dressing** (zombieonearth_ + 0xfrosteeze + robbercat_sol). Three independent studies landed on the same device — a rule carved, painted or bannered in the world in frame 1 makes every later cheat a verdict with no dialogue spent. Banked per show at BIG JAR #7, PETALS #10; treat as a house rule for all five shows: if an episode has a rule, the rule is a physical object in the set.
3. **Diminishing returns as a serial engine** (stellan628605, twice: PETALS #8 and LANDLORD #9). The same move buys less each time it is used, forcing invention per unit — applies to any repeated gag, plea or trick across the slate.

## Craft-only — noted, NOT transplanted (per the studies' own verdicts)

- **The karaoke word-burn** (boss_bee_): "Craft only, not transplanted" — our shows already burn word-timed karaoke on MVs; for dialogue shows the one-word caption fights the ESL plain-sentence standard. Noted, not taken.
- **Twin-fountain tears** (g55628830): a reusable emotion glyph — bigger than the face, readable at thumbnail size. Craft note only in the source study.
- **The 8-minute compilation format** (yuisansuk456): dozens of micro-episodes stitched is a distribution trick, not a story device — noted for the release plan, not for the slate's writing.
