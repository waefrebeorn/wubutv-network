## What it is

A storytime fable with a one-line supernatural rule, stated in the first
seven seconds: **"In this world, everyone has a countdown over their head,
and I'm the only one who can see it. It counts the seconds until you fall
asleep. Coffee adds time, fresh air adds a little, and when it hits zero,
you're out. Instantly."** Every character wears their number in every frame.

The story is one bus ride. The narrator-kid sees the beloved driver Mr Lou
is at 900 — fifteen minutes — and the drive home is thirty. Nobody believes
him ("the weird kid who stares at foreheads"). So he fights the number with
everything the rule allows: a gas-station coffee (number jumps to 1,500),
open windows (+10), radio (+10), then jokes — and the discovery that
**laughter pays the most**: +100, +50, +30, +80. But each laugh pays less
than the last — 60, 40, 20 — diminishing returns as exhaustion wins. Zero:
head on the wheel, horn blasting, 40 kids screaming, and jokes can't reach
him anymore. The rescue is the opening anecdote harvested: on the first day
of school his sister dropped an ice cube down Mr Lou's shirt and he screamed
the roof off. The kid dumps the picnic cooler — ice and lemonade — down the
driver's back. Scream, head hits the roof, number rockets to 10,000, bus
saved. Final line, looking at the still-climbing number: "Then why is your
number still going up?" (He's still laughing.)

## Strongest devices

1. **THE NUMBER IS THE LEDGER, WORN ON THE BODY.** Every person carries
   their stake over their head; every action in the story is *answered by
   the number moving* — +10, +100, +200 pop up on screen. This is the
   legibility standard mechanised: no move goes unanswered, and the answer
   is arithmetic a child can check (900 seconds vs a 30-minute drive is the
   whole plot).
2. **Diminishing returns as a built-in escalation law.** The same trick
   pays less each time (laughs: 100 → 60 → 40 → 20), so the hero is FORCED
   to invent new moves. Re-spending a device visibly devalues it — a serial
   engine rule, not just a plot point.
3. **The cold-open anecdote is the rescue.** The ice-cube gag is planted as
   flavour in the first 40 seconds and harvested as the climax's only
   working tool. Setup and payoff are the same object.
4. **Total onboarding, zero lore.** The rule is fully taught in 7 seconds,
   then demonstrated twice on background characters (lunch lady mid-scoop,
   teacher mid-sentence) before it ever matters.
