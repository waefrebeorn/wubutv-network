## 2. "The Bigger cake" — 2125870602926109035 (boss_bee_-20261127) — NEW

Posted 2026-11-26 08:33 UTC · **174,980 views** (the batch's biggest video by
5×) · 1,947 likes · 67.6s · vertical 720×1280 · Cantina repertory cast.
Evidence: cake.mp4, 20-frame sheet, mid/end grabs, full ASR (27 segments).

**"Cake" is slang, not food.** The dreadlocked sponge lead (same design as
"Catch the circle": dreads, tattooed arms, black top, jeans) has an enormous
backside, and the episode is his shame spiral:

1. **Street (0–28s).** Walking hand-in-hand with his pink-haired girlfriend,
   crowds stare. A Patrick-type friend laughs at him; strangers follow. The
   girlfriend delivers the diagnosis: *"They probably think you're a girl
   from the back, dead ass."* Him: *"Why is my ass so damn fat, cuh?"*
2. **Mirror beat (~29–34s).** At home he turns his back to a full-length
   mirror and reads his own body in horror — the defect confirmed by
   reflection, not by anyone's say-so.
3. **The decision (38–44s).** He packs a gym bag: *"I'm going to get surgery
   to shrink my big ass, cuh."*
4. **Clinic (44–61s).** On the exam table in a hospital gown, pleading with a
   Squidward-type doctor (clipboard): *"Please, doc, cuh. You think you can
   make me normal again?"* — *"This is a tough case, cuh."* — *"I'll do my
   best to help you, cuh."*
5. **Theatre + reveal (61–67s).** Face-down on the operating table, team
   around him. Cut to the recovery room: the doctor has fled (*"Doc, where
   are you going?"*), and the final frame is the mirror again — his backside
   is **exactly as big as before**, and his mirrored face screams. Double
   *"What the f**k?"* over the credits position. The surgery changed nothing.

**Devices:**
- **THE DEFECT IS THE BODY, AND THE BODY IS IN EVERY FRAME.** No caption or
  narration is needed to state the premise; the premise walks in front of the
  camera. Every beat (stares, laughter, mirror, clinic) is the same picture
  re-read by a new person.
- **THE MIRROR BOOKEND.** The defect is confirmed twice by reflection — once
  at home (decides him), once at the hospital (convicts the fix). The mirror
  is the show's lie detector: characters can lie, the reflection can't.
- **THE PAID FIX THAT DOESN'T PAY.** He spends the episode's whole second
  half buying the cure; the cure is a scam of omission — nobody says it
  failed, the mirror shows it. Payoff withheld as a picture, not a line.

No "Made with Cantina" badge is visible in the sampled sheets/frames for
this episode (taxi's badge is burned into nearly every frame; circle's was
reported throughout) — noted as an export difference, not a different source:
cast, caption burn and register are the same factory's.

## 3. "Part 2" — 2125926850006491209 (boss_bee_-20261127) — NEW

Posted 2026-11-26 12:17 UTC · 29,344 views · 184 likes · 87.1s · vertical ·
**"Made with TrendStory.io" watermark on most frames — a different factory
from the Cantina episodes.** Evidence: part2.mp4, 20-frame sheet, full ASR
(20 segments, clean TTS narration-dialogue).

**A lion-kingdom inheritance saga where your telecom generation is stamped
on your forehead at birth.** The old white king wears **6G**; the queen wears
**4G**; the goat courtier wears **2G**. Twin princes are born: the red-maned
one stamped **6G**, the white one stamped only **G**.

1. **The disowning (0–25s).** The courtier announces the births and the law:
   *"When he turns 18 years old, his network will appear."* The king refuses
   the G twin in the delivery room: *"Only G is written on his forehead. I do
   not consider him my son."* The queen names it: *"How can you do such
   injustice to your own child?"* — and vows to love him equally.
2. **The banquet refusal (34–46s).** The 6G prince is served "special royal
   food." The G prince asks for some and is refused at the table, in public —
   and the king's refusal carries the episode's sponsor read, spoken
   in-fiction as a taunt: *"if you want to earn money like me then download
   trend story IO now."* The ad is diegetic: the villain sells the app that
   made him, mid-cruelty.
3. **The coronation block (46–68s).** The king proclaims the red son's
   coronation tomorrow. The G prince: *"Do I not have any rights at all?"*
   The queen stops the ceremony with the law the king forgot: tomorrow the
   white prince turns 18, *"and his network will appear on his forehead."*
   The king: *"I do not care about any network in my kingdom — only my word
   will stand."*
4. **The taunt and the cut (72–87s).** The red brother gloats: if no network
   appears, *"I will banish you from the kingdom."* Final line, from the
   white prince, as a glow starts at his chest/forehead in the night
   library: *"Father, something is happening to me—"* **Cut.** The episode
   ends mid-transformation; the upgrade the whole plot waits for begins in
   the last second and is not shown.

**Devices:**
- **RANK IS WRITTEN ON THE FOREHEAD, AND IT CAN CHANGE ON A KNOWN DAY.**
  Status is a body stamp every character (and viewer) can read in every
  frame — and unlike a static label, this one has a scheduled public
  revision: the 18th birthday. The entire kingdom's cruelty is indexed to a
  stamp the plot has already promised to re-roll.
- **THE REFUSAL AT THE TABLE.** Favour is served as food, in front of
  everyone; the unfavoured son's humiliation is a plate that doesn't come.
  Status you can watch being eaten by somebody else.
- **THE DIEGETIC AD.** The sponsor read is a line of dialogue, delivered by
  the villain as a boast — the commercial is a plot move (it tells the G
  prince exactly what the king values) instead of an interruption.
- **THE CUT IS THE HOOK.** A numbered part that ends mid-event — not on a
  reaction, not on a line, but inside the transformation itself.

**A stranger-danger thriller in Cantina's pigeon world.** A pigeon mother in

1. **The wrong driver (0–9s).** A rusty yellow taxi arrives; the driver is a

   *"You and I have some unfinished business."* The kid: *"How did you know
   my name?"* — the driver knew it without being told. Then the kid does the
   most ordinary thing in the world: *"I'm so bored. I better scroll some
   videos on Cantina."* (A phone in an early frame literally shows the
   Cantina app — the kid scrolls the platform that made him, inside it.)
3. **The real driver (35–43s).** Cut to the mother, still on the road, with
   a second driver — a pigeon in a flat cap and plaid, the actual taxi
   driver: *"If you're a taxi driver, then who just took Pagan-Goo?"*
   *"Quickly, see this."*
4. **The rescue device (43–53s).** The "bored scroll" was cover: the kid has
   shared his **live location**. In the impostor's car, the driver snarls at
   the back seat: *"What do you think you're doing back there? No one's
   tracking us out here."* On the road: *"She's taking him deep into the
   woods."* — *"Hold on—"* **Cut.** The chase has not happened yet; the
   episode ends at the moment the rescue becomes possible.

**Devices:**
- **THE IMPOSTOR WHO KNOWS THE NAME.** The horror is one fact — she knew his
  name — delivered in one line, and it re-reads every earlier frame (the
  grin, the reassurance, "unfinished business"). Prior history is implied,
  never shown: this cast has a past the episode spends but doesn't explain.
- **THE BORED SCROLL AS COVER.** The captive's rescue move is disguised as
  the most ordinary phone behaviour there is; the kid's lifeline is a
  live-location share sent while pretending to watch videos. The phone is
  not a prop, it's the second plot.
- **DREAD IN THE MIRROR.** The middle act is nearly wordless: the driver's
  grin caught in the rear-view mirror, rain, the kid silent and buckled in.
  Menace carried by reflection and weather, not dialogue.
- **THE HARD CLIFFHANGER CUT.** Like Part 2, the episode stops inside the
  event — "Hold on—" — with the rescue owed, not delivered.

## 5. Serial mechanics — what the full batch actually shows

**Correction to the Tier 3 verdict.** The Tier 3 study read 1 of the 4 queued
posts and called the account an "original factory account." The full batch
does not support that. All four videos were posted on **one day** (Thu
2026-11-26, 08:33 → 22:41 UTC) and they come from **at least two different
factories**:

- **Cantina** (circle, cake, taxi — badge/watermark, one-word karaoke burn):
  two grammars under one roof. (a) A **repertory-company soap**: the sponge
  cast (dreadlocked lead, pink-haired girlfriend, Patrick-type, Squidward
  type) plays self-contained stories — circle's plea, cake's shame spiral —
  with no continuing plot between episodes; continuity is the cast and the
  caption system, not the story. (b) A **recurring-character peril serial**:
  Peigengoo the pigeon kid has a past with the impostor ("unfinished
  business"), and taxi ends unresolved mid-chase — episodic peril, serial
  people.
- **TrendStory.io** (Part 2 — watermarked): a **numbered continuing saga**.
  Narration-led TTS dialogue, a forehead-rank status system with a scheduled
  public revision, diegetic sponsor reads spoken by characters, and an
  episode that ends mid-transformation. Part 2 presupposes a Part 1 that is
  not in our registered set.

And above both sits the account layer the 2026-11-18 study documented: a
~260-posts/day text engagement operation. The safe claim for the whole
account is **distributor**: in this batch boss_bee_ behaves as a same-day
multi-factory dump (4 videos, ~14 hours, 2+ factories), exactly the
aggregation pattern the text operation runs at line scale. Whether it also
operates one of the factories cannot be proven from these artifacts, and
this study does not claim it.

  day.** The judge doesn't just paint the count on the glass; at the fair's
  start he stamps each neighbour's forehead with his class (FULL / HALF /
  CHEAT), the whole street reads it in every frame, and FAIR DAY is the
  scheduled public re-stamping — the finale is a forehead changing in front
  of everyone. From cake: **the mirror bookend** — the tall neighbour opens
  the episode reading his empty roof in a reflection and closes it reading
  the same reflection after the jar is full; the picture, not a line, says
  what the contest cost him.
- **SHE TOOK MY FACE** — from Part 2: **the re-stamp ceremony.** The thief's
  crime isn't only wearing the face; it's that the face-keeper will re-write
  the forehead word in public on a known day — so the victim's whole plot is
  getting to that ceremony with the truth, and the thief's is stopping her.
  From cake: **the mirror ledger** — the victim checks a mirror once per
  unit and reads her loss off her own reflection (flatter, paler, less
  hers); nobody has to announce the theft's progress, her face in the glass
  announces it.
- **NO TALKING AT THE TABLE** — from taxi: **menace in the mirror, and the
  under-table phone.** The silent show can carry dread, not just comedy: a
  guest who smiles and says nothing, read by the hosts only in reflections
  (a serving spoon, a dark window) — and the plot's turning move is a silent
  phone gesture under the table, a photo/location sent without a word, the
  one "line" of the episode spent as a picture leaving a phone.
- **ONE MILLION PETALS** — from Part 2: **the refusal at the table** — the
  favoured seller is served the special plate at the market feast while our
  seller's plate never comes, in front of everyone; her status is a meal
  eaten by somebody else. And **the diegetic pitch**: the invader's sales
  line spoken as a taunt inside the fiction ("want petals like mine? buy
  from the chained garden") — the gentrifier advertises in dialogue, and
  the ad itself is the cruelty, never a break in the story.
- **MY LANDLORD IS A BABY** — from taxi: **the impostor collector** — a fake
  rent-collector who knows the tenant's name and history ("unfinished
  business") takes the payment/keys before the real one arrives; the baby

  2: **the baby reads foreheads, not papers** — the tenant's rank is stamped
  where the baby can see it, the lease dispute is fought over what the
  stamp says, and rent day is the scheduled re-stamping the tenant dreads
  and the baby performs with a fat marker.
