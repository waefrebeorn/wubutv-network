## The piece in one paragraph

At a school for squishy-headed children, the little sister —
a girl who wears a tied brown pouch over her head because
nobody, including her, knows what kind of squishy she is —
suddenly erupts: a beam of golden light shoots from her head
in the schoolyard (frame 1). Nice Cube, the ice-cube-headed
boy, breathes "I knew you were special." Her big sister, the
crowned sprinkle-headed reigning "queen of squishies," hears
her title slipping — "I'm supposed to be the queen of
squishies!" — and dumps a bucket of expanding foam over the
little sister in front of everyone: "Let's see you squish
your way outta this!" The foam hardens; a squishy who cannot
squish can die. Nice Cube carries her to hospital while the
parents confront the queen — "Do you have any idea how long
she's waited to find out who she is?!" — and the queen's whole
defence is "Why does she get all the attention?!" At the
hospital the doctors begin removing the foam — and stop: the
little sister's body is repairing itself, in patterns the
doctor has never seen in his entire career, her energy
radiating heart- and star-shaped lights around the bed, felt
even by the queen from another room: "Her energy is way
stronger than mine!" Nice Cube holds her hand — "Come on,
baby, wake up. Everyone's waiting to see you." Final shot:
the pouch over her head cracks open with golden light from
inside. Cut. What kind of squishy she is — the question the
whole serial asks — is still not answered.

## Premise / law

The law is biological and it is demonstrated, never lectured
(INFERRED from the artifact; every element below MEASURED in
dialogue or frames):

**A squishy is defined by one visible property — what kind
they are — and the property is body, status and life at
once.** Kind is worn on the head (sprinkles, ice, jelly,
candy) and read at a glance by everyone in frame. To be a
squishy is to stay soft: expanding foam that hardens is a
death sentence precisely because it removes the defining
property ("She could die!" 21.5 s; "Oh no, it's already
hardening," ~63 s). And kind is destiny: the reigning queen's
title is a property ranking, and the one character whose kind
is UNKNOWN — hidden under a pouch, unknown even to herself —
is the serial's bomb. The episode spends its whole length
proving she is off the top of the scale the queen rules,
without ever naming what she is.

Cast (identifications INFERRED from frames + dialogue;

## Dialogue craft — how the lines sound human (msg 1296)

This is an all-dialogue piece (no narrator), and its lines
do plot work at full speed while sounding like people
shouting in a corridor. The specimens (burned-in captions
checked against ASR at every grid point):

- **Lines are questions and accusations, not statements.**
  "What the squish?!" / "Why does Nice Cube always pick
  her?!" / "Do you have any idea how long she's waited to
  find out who she is?!" / "You poured expanding foam all
  over her?!" / "Is she okay?!" / "What do you mean?!" /
  "What kind of squishy is she?" A question forces the next
  character to answer; the scene is a chain of demands for
  an answer, and the plot is what the answers reveal.
- **Every line carries its feeling in its shape.** The
  queen's lines are claims with the stress on the self:
  "Now nobody gets to be more special than ME." The mother's
  grief collapses into fragments: "Is she okay?! … My poor
  baby!" Nice Cube's care lines are the only quiet ones —
  "I knew you were special." / "Stay with me." — the volume
  difference IS the character difference.
- **People repeat the operative word until it lands.**
  "special" is fought over by three speakers in the first
  20 s (his "you were special," her "more special than me");
  "attention" is the queen's tell, said twice; the doctor
  says "never seen anything like it" twice, escalating to
  "in my entire career" — repetition as a pressure gauge,
  not padding.
- **The exposition wears a uniform.** Everything the viewer
  must learn (foam hardens, she waited to know her kind, the
  body is self-repairing) is spoken by the person whose job
  or wound makes them say it — the doctor prices the foam,
  the mother prices the wait, the doctor again certifies
  the miracle. No line exists only to inform the audience;
  each is a move in an argument happening in the room.
- **The silent centre.** The most important character speaks
  zero lines. Her silence is the plot: she cannot name
  herself, so the whole world argues over her name. The
  episode's last spoken line is somebody else asking what
  she is — her first line is being saved for a later part,
  and the audience knows it.

exactly what it is selling: the withheld name.

   reactions; a returning viewer is paid in frame 1.

   (sprinkle/ice/jelly/unknown) is read off the head, ranks
   the school, and is the thing the attack removes — foam
   kills by un-squishying. One property, three jobs: the
   audience never needs a second system explained.

   priced in body terms, not vanity terms. → NO TALKING:
   the chair is body, rank and voice at once — it already

3. **PRICE THE WEAPON IN ONE LINE, SECONDS AFTER IT LANDS.**
   "She could die!" arrives ~11 s after the pour; the
   hardening is priced again at the hospital door ("It's
   already hardening"). The audience is never left to guess
   what the attack costs — a bystander says it while the
   foam is still wet. → BIG JAR: the re-used tile needs its

   discovered later. → LANDLORD: each taking gets its price

4. **THE SILENT CENTRE — THE MOST IMPORTANT CHARACTER SAYS
   NOTHING.** The unknown sister speaks zero lines; the
   episode is everyone else fighting over her name, and her
   withheld first line becomes serial capital. Silence is
   not absence: her body acts (erupts, heals, cracks) while
   her voice is saved. → LANDLORD: Baby Boss already never
   speaks — push further: let one whole unit be the world

   he only acts. → FACE: in E2, let the stolen VOICE be
   heard from everyone except Nino.
5. **THE REVEAL IS CERTIFIED BY THE PROFESSIONAL'S CAREER.**
   The miracle is measured against the doctor's whole
   working life — "never seen anything like this in my
   entire career" — not against a number. An expert's
   biography is a bigger ruler than any scale the show
   could draw. → PETALS: the true-petal reveal should be

   board alone. → BIG JAR: Widow Bran's judging history is
   the ruler for the final count.
6. **GRIEF HAS PHYSICS.** The mother's body melts as she
   kneels — in a squishy world, emotion deforms the body on
   camera, no line needed. The feeling is a state of the
   ledger-body, readable in a still. → ALL SLATE: give each
   show's ledger-body an emotion state (the baby's size
   already carries LANDLORD's account — let it also slump;
   FACE's stolen features should sit wrong on the thief
   when she is afraid; NO TALKING's chairs creak/sag under
   a diner suppressing a laugh).
7. **THE VILLAIN'S DEFENCE NAMES THE REAL WANT.** The
   queen's title claim collapses, under her father's
   sentence, into the true grievance: "Why does she get all
   the attention?!" — and the episode lets her say it in
   full before it sentences her. A villain whose real want
   is spoken aloud can be punished without being flattened.
   → FACE: Vella must get her "why does she get…" line in
   full before Ora revokes anything. → PETALS: Doro's
   grievance gets one full line before the disposal.
8. **END ON THE CRACK, NOT THE CREATURE.** The final frame
   is the pouch splitting with light — the answer emerging,
   never shown. The cut lands at the first visible sign,
   and the last spoken line is the question itself. A door
   the audience can see opening beats a door described.
   → ALL SLATE E2/E3 DOORS: end on the first physical sign
   of the next state (the jar's first crack of fair-day
   light, the baby's hand already bigger on the door
   frame), with the episode's question spoken over it —
   never the payoff picture itself. → MV CANNON: the
   verse-to-chorus cut can hold the first frame of the
